# Google Business Profile (GBP) setup and audit

Source: BowTiedBills, "How To Set Up An SEO Optimized Google Business Profile" (X article, 2026-11-25, https://x.com/BowTiedBills/status/2125764026529624137, companion post https://x.com/BowTiedBills/status/2125827366165402037 showing Tobin Injury Law, Atlanta: right primary category, 448 reviews at 5.0, real office photo, open 24 hours, website, booking and phone, attributes filled in). He sells this service, so his case-study results are marketing claims, not evidence. The method matches Google's published guidelines. Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors and BrightLocal's 2026 survey are his sources; we have not re-read them. One reader noted 4.9 may be trusted more than a perfect 5; unproven, so do not advise on it.

HomePowerRebate has no storefront, so never create a profile for HPR with a virtual office, mailbox or home address. The main uses: audit installers and advisors on our ranking pages and advise them, and check profiles we link to.

Why it matters (his numbers): the 3 map businesses get about 61% of clicks on a local search. Website changes take 2 to 4 weeks to show in organic results; profile changes can move map rankings in days. Whitespark studied 1.8 million profiles: moving a profile from 0 to 5 on their completeness score improved average rank by about 19 positions; a specific primary category lined up with about 36% higher top-10 presence.

## How Google picks the 3
Google's words: **Relevance** ("how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for": categories, services, description, reviews), **Distance** ("how far each business is from the customer who's searching": you can't move your office, but make sure Google knows exactly where it is), **Prominence** ("how well-known a business is": reviews, rating, links to your website, mentions across the web).

Whitespark 2026 (47 local SEO experts, 187 factors), top 10 for the map: 1 primary category, 2 distance from searcher, 3 keywords in business name, 4 physical address in the city searched, 5 open at the time of the search, 6 high Google rating (4 to 5 stars), 7 address showing on the profile (not hidden), 8 additional categories, 9 number of Google reviews with text, 10 pin placed correctly. Eight of the ten are set inside the profile or earned through it.

## Setup, in order

### Step 1: Claim, verify, lock down
- Google's first ranking tip is to get verified. Without it there is no performance data and no control over what people see.
- Claim: search the name on Google Maps and claim an existing profile; if none, create one at business.google.com; if an old agency or former employee owns it, request access through Google.
- Verify: Google picks the method and it keeps changing. Video verification is common (film signage, office, proof you run the business). In Oct 2026 Google began testing government ID verification and asking some businesses for an EIN. Have ready: signage outside and inside, business licence or registration, a utility bill or lease in the business name, EIN (US).
- Lock down: you as primary owner with an email the business controls; marketing person or agency as manager, never owner; turn on email notifications. Anyone (a competitor, a confused customer, Google's own systems) can suggest edits; since 2026 Google emails owners about suggested changes on Maps and gives about 4 days to give feedback and block. If those emails go to an unread inbox, hours or phone number can change unseen.
- To do: check who owns the profile now, move ownership to a controlled email, turn on every notification.
- More than one location: every office with its own address, staff and hours gets its own profile, linking to that office's page (never the homepage), with its own reviews, photos and posts.
- Suspended: do not create a new profile. Fix the cause (usually name, address or a category), then file a reinstatement request with proof (signage, lease, utility bills, business licence). It can take a while.

### Step 2: Name, address, pin (most weight, most suspensions)
- Name: the real name exactly as on the sign, website and paperwork. Not allowed: taglines, services or locations added to the name, phone numbers, URLs or hours, ALL CAPS words (Google's example: "Subway", never "SUBWAY"). Breaking these can get the profile suspended.
- Keywords in the name were Whitespark's #3 factor and names with a keyword rank top 3 more often (1.4 million profile study), but adding a keyword that is not part of the real name breaks the guidelines; competitors can report it. It is fine only if it is the real legal name, on the signage and paperwork first. "Smith Divorce Law" is fine if that is the door sign; "Smith Law | Best Divorce Lawyer Springfield" is not.
- Address: if clients come to you, show it (factor #7; address in the searched city is #4, so an office inside the city is an advantage). Write it as the post office writes it and use the identical version on the website and every directory.
- Service areas (plumbers, roofers, mobile services): hide the address and set up to 20 service areas (cities or postal codes) within about 2 hours' drive of base. Since June 2025 whole states or countries are not allowed. Hybrids (clients visit and you travel) show the address and add service areas.
- Addresses that cause trouble: a virtual office or mailbox service where nobody works; an old P.O. box left on the profile (flagged by Whitespark's Q3 2026 roundup as a suspension risk); a home address shown publicly when clients do not come there. The address must be a real place where you serve clients during the hours listed. Updating a hidden address does not always move the pin: check it after any change.
- Pin: where Google thinks your door is (top-10 factor). Zoom in on Maps; if it is on the wrong building or in a parking lot, drag it to the real entrance in the profile editor.
- Case claim: a traffic ticket firm with 3 California markets gave each office its own profile linked to its own page and reported #1 in 3 of 3 markets, organic clicks 1,200 to 4,800 a month, ad spend $50K to $5K a month.
- To do: compare the profile name to the sign word for word; check the address matches the website character for character; fix the pin.

### Step 3: Categories and services (moves rankings most, done wrong most)
- Primary category = the service worth most to you, as specific as possible ("Divorce lawyer" not "Law firm"; "Personal injury attorney"). Google's test: it must complete "This business IS a...", never "has a...".
- Case claim: a personal injury attorney switched "Law firm" to personal injury, added a review system and real photos; calls doubled in month 1, climbed about another 70 to 80% in month 2, about 23 new cases in 6 weeks.
- Additional categories: up to 9 more, only real parts of the business (a family law firm might add "Divorce service" or "Family law attorney"). Google says use as few as possible, most specific, never as a keyword. Whitespark: well-chosen extras lined up with gains of 6 to 17 positions.
- How to pick: search the main keyword on Maps, look at the top 3 profiles, use a free category checker (GMB Everywhere) to see their categories, match the primary category of those ranking for the service you want.
- Services: list every single thing you do, each separate with a short description (a personal injury firm lists car, truck and motorcycle accidents, slip and falls, dog bites, wrongful death...). Each is a reason for Google and its AI answers to match a specific search. Google can now link a review to the specific service, and review forms for many service categories ask "Which services did you get?" and "How much did you pay?", so a fuller services list attaches more reviews to the services you want to rank for.
- Products, if the category allows: each with photo, description, link (one more place for a matching picture and words).
- To do: check the primary category today and change it if generic; add fitting extras; list every service one by one with a description.

### Step 4: Hours, description, attributes, links
- Hours: "open at time of search" is factor #5; closed at 7 pm means less likely to show. List real hours, set special hours for every holiday before it arrives; with an evening answering service you can honestly list longer call hours.
- Description: 750 characters, only the first ~250 show. No links, no prices, promotions or sales. Describe services, what you do, history. Structure: first sentence what you do and where (service and town); second who you help and how; then main services, experience, what makes you different; then areas served. Write for a person first. His made-up example (about 330 characters): "Smith Law is a divorce and family law firm in Springfield, helping parents with divorce, child custody and child support. We've handled family cases in [County] courts for 15 years. We offer flat fee uncontested divorces, payment plans and consultations in English and Spanish. We serve Springfield, [Town], [Town] and [Town]." (The "flat fee" line is a made-up example; an installer's description must not state prices.) Description keywords rank low (115th of 187 for AI visibility), so stuffing does nothing, but AI answers still read it, so make every line true and specific.
- Attributes: small details (wheelchair accessible entrance, online appointments, women owned, veteran owned...), list depends on category and Google keeps adding (Oct 2026: gluten free, dairy free, halal, kosher for restaurants). Google's own ranking tips say add them; AI answers match against them ("which divorce lawyer near me does online appointments?"). Fill every true one; re-check every few months.
- Links: website link to the right page (homepage for one location, that office's page for several); booking link if you take appointments; social profiles.
- Messaging: Oct 2026 a WhatsApp button began showing on some profiles, Facebook Messenger links were added, and in Nov Google expanded business messaging to Maps. Only turn it on if someone answers fast; a message sitting a day is a lead lost to the next profile.
- Phone: a local number the business controls, not a central call-centre number.
- To do: set holiday hours for the rest of the year; rewrite the description so line one says service and town; fill every true attribute; check the website link goes to the right page.

### Step 5: Photos, posts, reviews (the weekly habit)
- Photos and videos that show what you offer: real photos you took, never stock; the outside (so people find you), the inside, the team, you at work; short videos; new ones regularly. Recent real photos build trust before a call.
- Posts: since Oct 2026 Google shows posts higher on the business panel. Google has not said posts improve rankings and Whitespark experts rank them near the bottom for AI visibility; he still posts a couple of times a week because people see them and it keeps the profile looking active. Ideas: recent project or result (no client details), a common question answered, team photo, event or holiday schedule, an Offers post. Phone numbers in posts must now be verified.
- Reviews hit 2 of the top 10 factors (rating, reviews with text) and decide who people call. BrightLocal 2026: 74% want reviews from the past 3 months; 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 or higher; 47% won't use one with fewer than 20 reviews; 89% expect the owner to reply.
- Velocity (new reviews in the last 90 days) matters more than the total: 300 reviews with 2 recent looks less active than 80 with 15 recent.
- Getting them inside the rules: ask every client, happy or not; ask at the moment the job ends; send a direct review link by email and text; follow up a few times; never offer anything in return; never tell clients what to write. April 2026 policy lines spotted by local SEOs: no review quotas for staff, no asking customers to mention staff by name. Google tightened enforcement in April 2026: on a spike of fake reviews it removes them, can pause new reviews, alerts the owner and shows customers a warning banner. Steady beats sudden. Pace: about 5 a month in a quiet market, closer to 20 in the busiest cities.
- Reply to every review within 24 to 48 hours, good and bad, each written fresh. Never confirm someone is a client or patient (privacy problem for lawyers and medical practices). People can now report owner replies to Google, so stay calm and professional.
  - 5 stars: "Thank you for taking the time to write this, [first name]. We're glad the process felt simple, and we appreciate you trusting us with it."
  - Bad review: "We're sorry to hear this. We take every concern seriously and would like to talk it through. Please call our office at [number] and ask for [name]." Short, calm, no case details, take it offline.
- Google says it blocked or removed more than 292 million reviews in 2025 and took down more than 13 million fake business profiles. Buying or swapping reviews is not worth the risk.
- Case claim: an immigration firm grew from 70 to 90 reviews at 5 stars by making asking feel safe and simple; calls about 32 to 104 a month in 4 months; statewide map from outside the top 3 at all 225 grid points to #1 at all 225.
- To do: upload 10 real photos this week; post twice a week; automatic review request at the end of every job; reply to every review from the last 30 days.

### Step 6: Feed the AI answers, build citations, track
- Q&A is gone: the Q&A API shut Nov 3, 2025 and the public section was phased out. AI answers now pull from the profile, website, reviews and other web sources.
- Ask Maps (Gemini assistant in Google Maps, launched March 2026) recommends businesses from listings, reviews, photos, websites, social media and even Reddit. The way in is being the best documented business in your area. BrightLocal: 45% of people now use ChatGPT or another AI tool for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier.
- Do: fill every field in steps 1 to 4 (AI can only use what is there); put the 10 most common client questions on the website with short direct answers; keep profile and website identical on services, hours and areas; ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Ask Maps who they recommend for your service in your city and fix errors at the source.
- Google now phones businesses to check hours, prices and availability. The dashboard's "Collected Info" tab shows what it picked up and lets you delete wrong items. Check it monthly; a wrong answer from whoever picks up the phone can land on the profile.
- Nov 10, 2026 (Sterling Sky): the local pack inside AI Overviews is dropping action buttons, photos and reviews, so business name, category and what AI says about you carry more weight (another reason to get steps 2 and 3 right).
- Citations (name, address, phone on directories, industry sites, chamber of commerce, Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing): Whitespark says 3 of the top 5 AI-visibility factors are citation factors. He builds clients onto 80 to 100+ directories with identical name, address and phone. Quick check: Google your exact business name in quotes and the top competitor's, compare result counts (in one market the two leaders showed about 5,000 results each against 20 to 100 for firms saying they "don't rank anywhere").
- Track: Performance section monthly (calls, website clicks, direction requests, profile views); a grid scan with a local rank tracker from points around the office; write down where you started. Case claim: a divorce lawyer went from outside the top 3 at all 169 grid points to #1 at all 169 in 90 days and profile views went about 1,840 to 9,660.
- Expect: uptick in clicks, calls, visits in 30 days; usually #1 in a 20 to 25 square mile radius around 3 months; closer to 6 months in very competitive cities; sometimes faster (one lawyer's first lead came on day 6).

## Mistakes seen most
Generic primary category; keywords added to the name; wrong hours or no holiday hours; website link on the homepage for a multi-location business; old or stock photos; a review burst then nothing for months; reviews with no replies; name, address or phone differing between website and directories; notifications going to an unread inbox.

## Checklist
1. Claimed and verified; owner email controlled; notifications on
2. Real name as on sign; address shown or service areas set (up to 20, within ~2 hours' drive); pin on entrance
3. Primary category matches the service wanted most; real extra categories; every service listed with a description
4. Regular and holiday hours; description under 750 characters with service and town first; every true attribute; website link to the right page plus booking and social; messaging answered fast if on
5. Real photos regularly; posts a couple of times a week; automatic review requests inside the rules; every review answered in 24 to 48 hours
6. Common questions on the website; profile and website match; 80 to 100+ directories with matching details; performance and grid scan monthly

## Auditing an installer (support only)
Run `python3 scripts/audit_installer_profiles.py` (see its docstring). It reads `installers/*-real.csv`, skips blocked companies (`data/installer-blocklist.json`), merges the same company across service files, and checks only what the CSV holds: name (stuffing, phone or URL, ALL CAPS), website (missing, or only a chat or social page), phone, address (missing or mailbox style), rating (under 4.0, under 4.5), review count (under 20, or far behind the city's top three). It prints up to three fixes per company, easiest first, in Grade 6 words, and lists what it cannot see (categories, hours, pin, review recency, photos, replies, services, attributes) as unknown; it never guesses. Flags: `--region` (csv prefix; no prefix = bc), `--service`, `--city`, `--recommended-only`, `--limit`, `--summary`, `--out`. First full run (2026-11-26): 3,452 companies, 1,622 with at least one fix; no website 643, review gap 487, under 20 reviews 478, no address shown 447.

## HomePowerRebate guardrails
- The audit never changes a ranking. Rankings stay ordered by Google reviews; money or help never affects them.
- Google Places ratings and reviews may be shown with attribution but must not feed LocalBusiness, AggregateRating or Review markup, and Places photos must not be stored. The script never reads the Image URL column and stores no review text.
- Installer outreach is paused (CLAUDE.md, 2026-11-08): run audits and build notes, send nothing. Never say "free" or mention price to installers (so drop pricing lines from any example description).
- Any installer-facing copy goes through the `no-ai-slop` skill, Grade 6, one em dash at most.
- Reports written to `data/reports/` are gitignored by default; do not commit per-installer audits.
