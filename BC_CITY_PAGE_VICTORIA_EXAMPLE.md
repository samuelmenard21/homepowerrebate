## Section 7: Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Will my heat pump work in Victoria's winters?
**A:** Yes. Heat pumps work down to about -15°C. Victoria's average winter low is around 5°C. Cold-climate heat pumps (which these installers specialize in) handle our weather fine. They're more expensive upfront but necessary for reliability.

### Q: What's Peak Saver and should I enroll?
**A:** Peak Saver is BC Hydro's demand response program. You pre-heat your home at off-peak times, and during peak demand (winter mornings), your system temporarily dials back. You get paid $200 upfront plus $15–30/month during winter. Enrollment is automatic with heat pumps, but these installers can explain how to optimize for your situation.

### Q: Does a heat pump replace my furnace completely?
**A:** Yes. Heat pumps are a complete replacement—they handle heating, cooling, and hot water (if you get an air-source system with DHW). You remove the old furnace and install the heat pump. Some people keep a backup electric baseboard heater, but it's not necessary in Victoria.

### Q: How much can I really save per year?
**A:** Depends on your current heating system and habits. Furnace users typically save $250–400/year on heating. Baseboard heaters see even more savings (up to $600+/year) because they're so inefficient. These savings appear in your BC Hydro bill within the first month.

### Q: Is the rebate guaranteed?
**A:** BC Hydro honors the rebates if your system is installed by a certified installer (all three here are) and passes inspection. The rebate isn't guaranteed by us—it's guaranteed by BC Hydro's program—but these installers know how to make sure the paperwork gets done right.

### Q: Can I combine a heat pump with solar?
**A:** Absolutely. Heat pump + solar is the ultimate combo for energy independence. Victoria Energy Partners specializes in this combo. Talk to them about long-term energy strategy.
