BC Hydro vs FortisBC: Which Utility Offers Better Heat Pump Rebates?

BC has two major utilities with different rebate programs. We compare heat pump incentives, service areas, and which communities can qualify for double rebates.

Which utility am I with?

Most of you are with BC Hydro — that's Lower Mainland, Vancouver Island, and interior cities like Kelowna, Kamloops, and Vernon.

Some of you are with FortisBC — that's Fort St. John, Prince George, and parts of northern BC. Smaller service area, tougher winters, higher operating costs.

Here's the interesting part: FortisBC's rebate program is way more generous than BC Hydro's. They're basically saying: "Our climate is harsher. We need you to go electric even more. So we're paying more." And they mean it.

Why FortisBC pays more

There are a few reasons:

  • It's brutal out there. Fort St. John hits -15°C regularly. Prince George gets -20°C winters. Heating costs are sky-high. FortisBC knows that heat pumps work in cold climates—but most customers think they don't. So they're paying extra to prove it.
  • The grid is fragile. Northern grids are smaller and more vulnerable to outages. FortisBC wants distributed energy (solar + battery + heat pump) everywhere. Less strain on central infrastructure.
  • They're attracting talent and businesses. Cold, remote areas lose people. Cheaper heating = more people stay. FortisBC's rebates are partly an economic development play.
  • Income support matters more. Rural northern BC has lower average incomes. FortisBC's double rebate for income-qualified customers is their way of making sure everyone can afford to upgrade, not just high-earners.

What this means for you

If you're BC Hydro: You're getting solid rebates ($4–8K for heat pumps). Interior cities like Kelowna and Kamloops do especially well because solar potential is higher—you stack heat pump + solar + battery rebates and your system pays for itself in 8–12 years.

If you're FortisBC: You're winning. Higher rebates, colder winters = bigger heating savings. And if your household income is under $175K, you get double rebates. Your 10-year total savings is legitimately $7K–$12K higher than BC Hydro customers in similar situations. Check your income qualification—it matters.

Either way: Your utility wants you to go electric. The economics work. The rebates are real. Don't leave money on the table by waiting.
