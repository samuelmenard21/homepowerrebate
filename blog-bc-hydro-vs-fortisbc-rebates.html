BC Hydro vs FortisBC: Which Utility Offers Better Heat Pump Rebates?

BC has two major utilities with different rebate programs. We compare heat pump incentives, service areas, and which communities can qualify for double rebates.

The Two Utilities

BC Hydro: Serves most of BC — Lower Mainland, Vancouver Island, interior cities (Kelowna, Kamloops, Vernon), and most southern/central BC.

FortisBC: Serves remote/northern BC — Fort St. John, Prince George, and parts of the northeastern interior. Smaller customer base, higher operational costs.

Because FortisBC operates in remote areas with higher costs and more challenging winters, their rebate program is more generous. Here's why and how much.

Heat Pump Rebates: BC Hydro vs FortisBC

*Income-qualified = household income under $175,000/year (FortisBC only)

The Why Behind FortisBC's Higher Rebates

  • Remote service area: Northern BC has smaller customer bases and higher infrastructure costs. FortisBC incentivizes efficiency upgrades to reduce grid strain
  • Harsh winters: Fort St. John and Prince George average -15°C winters. Gas heating is expensive. FortisBC wants to move customers away from fossil fuels fast
  • Grid resilience: Remote grids are more fragile. FortisBC prioritizes distributed energy (solar + battery + heat pump) more aggressively than BC Hydro
  • Income support: FortisBC's double rebate for income-qualified customers reflects higher operational costs in remote areas + climate vulnerability

The Verdict

If you're BC Hydro: You get solid rebates ($4K–$8K heat pump). Your ROI is excellent, especially in interior cities with high solar potential (Kelowna, Kamloops).

If you're FortisBC: You get higher rebates and bigger operational savings due to colder winters. Check if you're income-qualified — if you are, you get double rebates. Your 10-year ROI is superior.

Bottom line: Both utilities' programs are designed to benefit their communities. FortisBC's program is simply more generous because the need is greater in the north.
