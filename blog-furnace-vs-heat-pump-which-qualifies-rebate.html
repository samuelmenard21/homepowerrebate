Furnace vs Heat Pump: Which Qualifies for BC Rebates?

Published June 16, 2026 · 7 min read

If your furnace is getting old, you have two paths: replace it with a new furnace, or switch to a heat pump. On the surface, a furnace replacement looks cheaper. But BC's rebate programs only support heat pumps—not furnaces—so the real cost is very different.

Here's what you need to know before you decide.

Furnace vs Heat Pump: Side by Side

Item New Furnace Heat Pump
Equipment cost $2,500–$4,500 $5,000–$8,000
Installation $1,500–$2,500 $2,000–$3,500
Total upfront $4,000–$7,000 $7,000–$11,500
BC rebate $0 $4,000–$16,000
Net cost after rebate $4,000–$7,000 $0–$5,000

*Heat pump rebate depends on your utility (BC Hydro vs FortisBC), home type, and income. Use our free rebate calculator for your exact number.

Why BC Rebates Only Support Heat Pumps

British Columbia's climate targets require a shift away from natural gas heating. That's why:

  • Heat pumps get $4,000–$16,000 rebates. They replace gas heating with electric, moving homes toward zero-emission heating.
  • Gas furnaces get $0. You can replace a furnace with a new furnace, but BC won't pay you to do it.

This is true whether your current system is a furnace, a boiler, or a baseboardheater. If you're replacing gas heating with a heat pump, you qualify. If you're replacing it with another gas furnace, you don't.

Annual Heating Costs: Furnace vs Heat Pump

Natural gas furnace (90% efficient):

  • Typical BC home: 50,000–80,000 BTU/hour
  • Winter season (Oct–Apr): ~4,000 hours of use
  • Annual cost: $800–$1,400/year (at current BC gas rates)

Heat pump (300%–400% efficient):

  • Same home, same heating load
  • Annual cost: $600–$1,000/year (at current BC electricity rates)
  • Savings vs gas furnace: $200–$400/year
Why is a heat pump more efficient? A furnace burns gas to create heat. A heat pump moves heat from outside air (or ground) into your home—even when it's cold. It uses 3–4× less energy to deliver the same BTU output.

10-Year Total Cost: The Real Comparison

New gas furnace (10 years):

  • Upfront: $4,000–$7,000 (no rebate)
  • Annual heating: $800–$1,400 × 10 years = $8,000–$14,000
  • Total: $12,000–$21,000

Heat pump (10 years, after rebate):

  • Upfront: $0–$5,000 (after $4K–$16K rebate)
  • Annual heating: $600–$1,000 × 10 years = $6,000–$10,000
  • Total: $6,000–$15,000

10-year savings: $6,000–$15,000 with a heat pump

The Catch: Heat Pumps Also Cool

A furnace heats in winter. If you want summer cooling, you need a separate air conditioner.

A heat pump heats in winter and cools in summer. So when you replace a furnace + AC system with a heat pump, you're eliminating two devices and their upkeep. That's one less thing to maintain, and one less unit taking up space.

But if you don't have an AC now, and you don't want one, a heat pump is overkill—it'll do a job you don't need. In that case, a furnace replacement might make sense, though you'll miss the rebate.

What About Air Quality & Comfort?

Furnace: Burns gas, creates dry heat, requires ductwork maintenance

Heat pump: Produces consistent, humidity-balanced heating, integrates with smart thermostats, quieter operation

Many BC homeowners report that heat pumps feel more comfortable because they run at lower capacity more often (vs a furnace's on-off cycle). They're also quieter and don't produce the dry-heat feeling of gas heating.

Who Should Replace Their Furnace with a Heat Pump?

Good fit for heat pump:

  • Your furnace is 15+ years old (near end of life)
  • You want to reduce gas consumption
  • You have or want summer cooling
  • You qualify for BC rebates ($4K–$16K covers much of the cost)

Good fit for furnace replacement:

  • Your furnace is newer (5–10 years) but needs a repair
  • You only need winter heating, never want to cool
  • You're in a rental or planning to move in 3–5 years

But if your furnace is failing and you own your home? Heat pump almost always wins. The $4K–$16K rebate basically covers the difference in upfront cost, and you save $200–$400 every year after.

How Much Rebate Do You Qualify For?

It depends on:

  • Your utility: BC Hydro ($4,000–$8,000) or FortisBC ($4,000–$16,000)
  • Your income: FortisBC income-qualified rebates are double for households under $175K
  • Your home type: Single-family vs multifamily, square footage, heating fuel type
