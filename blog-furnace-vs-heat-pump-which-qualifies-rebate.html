Furnace vs Heat Pump: Which Qualifies for BC Rebates?

Published June 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Most furnaces I talk to homeowners about are 15+ years old and on their way out. When they fail (and they will), you have two choices: replace it with a new gas furnace, or go with a heat pump.

Here's the thing: a new furnace looks cheaper upfront. But BC's rebate system only pays for heat pumps. So when you do the real math—upfront cost minus rebate, then add 10 years of heating bills—the heat pump usually wins by a lot.

Furnace vs Heat Pump: Side by Side

Item New Furnace Heat Pump
Equipment cost $2,500–$4,500 $5,000–$8,000
Installation $1,500–$2,500 $2,000–$3,500
Total upfront $4,000–$7,000 $7,000–$11,500
BC rebate $0 $4,000–$16,000
Net cost after rebate $4,000–$7,000 $0–$5,000

*Heat pump rebate depends on your utility (BC Hydro vs FortisBC), home type, and income. Use our free rebate calculator for your estimate.

Why BC only pays for heat pumps (not furnaces)

BC is trying to move off natural gas heating. It's a climate commitment. So the rebates are designed to make that shift affordable.

  • Heat pump: Replaces gas with electric. Gets $4K–$16K back.
  • Gas furnace: More gas. Gets $0.

It doesn't matter if you're replacing a furnace, boiler, or old baseboard heaters. The rule is simple: swap gas for electricity, get paid. Stick with gas, don't.

Annual Heating Costs: Furnace vs Heat Pump

Natural gas furnace (90% efficient):

  • Typical BC home: 50,000–80,000 BTU/hour
  • Winter season (Oct–Apr): ~4,000 hours of use
  • Annual cost: $800–$1,400/year (at current BC gas rates)

Heat pump (300%–400% efficient):

  • Same home, same heating load
  • Annual cost: $600–$1,000/year (at current BC electricity rates)
  • Savings vs gas furnace: $200–$400/year
Why is a heat pump more efficient? A furnace burns gas to create heat. A heat pump moves heat from outside air (or ground) into your home—even when it's cold. It uses 3–4× less energy to deliver the same BTU output.

10-Year Total Cost: The Real Comparison

New gas furnace (10 years):

  • Upfront: $4,000–$7,000 (no rebate)
  • Annual heating: $800–$1,400 × 10 years = $8,000–$14,000
  • Total: $12,000–$21,000

Heat pump (10 years, after rebate):

  • Upfront: $0–$5,000 (after $4K–$16K rebate)
  • Annual heating: $600–$1,000 × 10 years = $6,000–$10,000
  • Total: $6,000–$15,000

10-year savings: $6,000–$15,000 with a heat pump

One thing to know: heat pumps cool AND heat

A furnace is winter-only. A heat pump is year-round—it heats in winter and cools in summer.

If you've got an old air conditioner too, a heat pump replaces both. Suddenly you have one device instead of two. Simpler, cheaper to maintain, takes up less space. Most BC homeowners like this upgrade.

But if you've never had AC and you don't want it, a heat pump might feel like overkill. You'd be paying for a feature you don't need. In that specific case, a furnace replacement might make sense. Just remember: you'll get zero rebate.

What About Air Quality & Comfort?

Furnace: Burns gas, creates dry heat, requires ductwork maintenance

Heat pump: Produces consistent, humidity-balanced heating, integrates with smart thermostats, quieter operation

Many BC homeowners report that heat pumps feel more comfortable because they run at lower capacity more often (vs a furnace's on-off cycle). They're also quieter and don't produce the dry-heat feeling of gas heating.

When does a heat pump make sense?

Heat pump is the right call if:

  • Your furnace is 15+ years old and failing
  • You own your home (not renting)
  • You're okay with a small upfront cost (or $0 if you get the max rebate)
  • You want to save $200–$400/year on heating bills

Furnace replacement makes sense if:

  • Your furnace is only 5–10 years old and just needs a repair (not replacement)
  • You absolutely don't want cooling in summer
  • You're renting or plan to move in 3 years

Real talk: If your furnace is dying and you own your home, a heat pump wins almost every time. The rebate covers the upfront difference, and you're saving money every single winter after that. It's not a close call.

How Much Rebate Do You Qualify For?

It depends on:

  • Your utility: BC Hydro ($4,000–$8,000) or FortisBC ($4,000–$16,000)
  • Your income: FortisBC income-qualified rebates are double for households under $175K
  • Your home type: Single-family vs multifamily, square footage, heating fuel type
