Heat Pump vs Air Conditioner: Cost, Rebates & 10-Year Savings

Published June 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Most BC homeowners I talk to who need cooling think about one thing: air conditioning. It's the obvious choice, right? Well, not quite. The real competitor is a heat pump.

An AC looks cheaper upfront. But when you add BC rebates and 10 years of heating + cooling costs, a heat pump usually saves you $7,000–$16,000. Here's why.

Annual Operating Costs: Where Heat Pumps Win

An AC cools your home in summer. A heat pump cools in summer and heats in winter—so it's working year-round in BC. That's where the real savings emerge.

Typical BC Home (1,800 sq ft, electric or gas heating):

  • AC only (summer cooling only): $400–$600/year in electricity
  • Gas heat (winter heating only): $800–$1,200/year in gas
  • AC + Gas heating (annual): $1,200–$1,800/year

Heat pump (summer + winter): $1,000–$1,400/year in electricity

Annual savings with a heat pump: $200–$400/year vs AC + gas heating

Why? Heat pumps are 3–4× more efficient than resistance heating and burn less energy than gas furnaces. Even though they use electricity (not cheap), they move heat rather than create it, so the per-BTU cost is lower.

10-Year Total Cost: The Real Picture

AC + Gas Heating (10 years):

  • Upfront: $5,000–$8,000
  • Annual operating: $1,200–$1,800 × 10 years = $12,000–$18,000
  • Total: $17,000–$26,000

Heat Pump (10 years, after BC rebate):

  • Upfront: $0–$5,000 (after rebate)
  • Annual operating: $1,000–$1,400 × 10 years = $10,000–$14,000
  • Total: $10,000–$19,000

10-year savings: $7,000–$16,000 with a heat pump

Who Qualifies for BC Rebates?

BC Hydro customers: $4,000–$8,000 rebate for heat pump installation (depending on equipment and home type)

FortisBC customers: Up to $16,000 rebate through their CleanBC Retrofit program (income-qualified)

Income-qualified (FortisBC): If your household income is under $175K (for some programs), you may qualify for double the rebate.

The one thing AC does better

An AC is simpler. One job: cool in summer. Fewer moving parts. Straightforward install.

But here's the thing—you live in BC. You need heat in winter too. If you're already running a furnace + AC, a heat pump replaces both with one unit. Bigger install upfront, but you're eliminating two systems you'd have to maintain separately.

The Math: Should You Buy a Heat Pump?

If you have an air conditioner and a gas furnace:

  • Heat pump upfront cost: $7,000–$11,500
  • Minus BC rebate: -$4,000 to -$16,000
  • Real net cost: $0–$5,000 (or free, if you're FortisBC income-qualified)
  • Annual savings: $200–$400
  • Payback period: 5–15 years (usually 8–10)

After payback, you're saving $2,000–$4,000 per decade. Over 20 years of heat pump life, that's $4,000–$8,000 in pure savings—plus a more comfortable home and smaller carbon footprint.
