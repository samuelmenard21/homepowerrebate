41 Massachusetts Towns Locked Out of Mass Save

By Sam Menard

Updated September 26, 2026. If a town light department sends your electric bill, Mass Save\'s electric rebates don\'t apply to you. Here is the full town list and what you can get instead.

Short answer: 41 town-owned electric utilities, called municipal light plants (MLPs), serve all or part of 50 Massachusetts cities and towns. If your electric bill comes from one of them, you can't get Mass Save's electric-side rebates, like the $8,500 whole-home heat pump rebate. You still have options: your light plant's own rebates, the state's 0% MLP Z-Loan (up to $25,000), and sometimes Mass Save gas-side offers.

Why MLP towns can't use Mass Save

Mass Save is paid for by a charge on the bills of the state's big utilities: Eversource, National Grid, Unitil and Cape Light Compact on the electric side, plus the gas companies. Town-owned light plants don't collect that charge. So their customers sit outside the program.

This is about your address, not your income. Your neighbour one town over may get a rebate you can't. The fastest way to check is to look at your electric bill. If it names a town light department, you're in an MLP town.

What you can get instead

1. Your light plant's own rebates

Many light plants run their own heat pump, water heater and appliance rebates. Many do this through NextZero (run by MMWEC) or Energy New England. Amounts differ a lot from town to town. Call your light department or check its website before you get quotes.

2. The DOER MLP Z-Loan (0% interest)

The state's Department of Energy Resources offers a 0% loan of $500 to $25,000, paid back over up to 5 years. It covers heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, insulation and weatherization work. You need a home energy assessment first, and only upgrades that assessment recommends qualify. Solar and EV chargers don't qualify. Apply at myz-loan.com.

3. Mass Save gas-side offers (sometimes)

If your electricity comes from a light plant but your natural gas comes from a Mass Save gas sponsor, you may still qualify for gas-side help. That can include a no-cost home energy assessment, insulation and air sealing. Mass Save also lists heat pump rebates for homes replacing gas heat, applied through a gas account. Ask your gas company.

4. State programs open to everyone

The state solar tax credit (15% of cost, up to $1,000) applies no matter who your utility is. The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended for work done after December 31, 2025, so don't count on them.

What to do next

  1. Check your electric bill to confirm who serves you.
  2. Call your light department and ask: "What heat pump and weatherization rebates do you offer this year?"
  3. Book the home energy assessment your light plant uses. You need it for the Z-Loan.
  4. Get quotes, then apply for the Z-Loan before work starts.

Common questions

How many Massachusetts towns can't use Mass Save?

41 municipal light plants serve all or part of 50 cities and towns, per the Department of Public Utilities. Those customers can't use Mass Save's electric-side programs.

Is there a 0% loan for MLP towns?

Yes. The DOER MLP Z-Loan lends $500 to $25,000 at 0% for up to 5 years for upgrades recommended in a home energy assessment.

Can I get Mass Save if my gas comes from a big utility?

Possibly. If a Mass Save gas sponsor serves your home, ask about gas-side assessments, weatherization and heat pump rebates.
