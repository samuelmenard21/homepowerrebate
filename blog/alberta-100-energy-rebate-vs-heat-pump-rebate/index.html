The $100 Alberta 'Energy Rebate' Isn't What You Think

Two different things share almost the same name in Alberta right now, and the confusion is costing homeowners real time.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Two different programs, one confusing name

The Alberta Energy Rebate launched July 1, 2026 — a one-time $100 payment from the provincial government, applied for online, available to Albertans broadly as a cost-of-living measure. It has nothing to do with heat pumps, insulation, or any home energy upgrade. It's closer to a rebate on your utility bill than an incentive to renovate.

Separately, if you're looking for money toward an actual heat pump, insulation, or solar installation, you're looking at a completely different (and much thinner) set of programs: CEIP financing, ENMAX/ATCO utility rebates, or municipal programs specific to your city.

Why the confusion matters

The Alberta Energy Rebate got real media attention — including criticism of its rollout and verification process. Some homeowners searching for heat pump help have landed on Alberta Energy Rebate coverage and assumed it applies to renovation costs. It doesn't. If you're planning a $12,000 heat pump install, this $100 payment isn't part of your math.

What actually helps with a heat pump in Alberta

  • ENMAX or ATCO utility rebate: $500-$2,000/unit, availability varies through the year
  • CEIP: 0% or low-interest financing through your property tax bill, sometimes with a small incentive
  • City-specific programs where they exist — Calgary's free Home Upgrades Program, Lethbridge's own utility rebate, St. Albert's $1,400 CEIP bonus

None of it is close to what BC or Quebec homeowners can claim. But it's the real list — separate from the unrelated $100 payment making headlines.
