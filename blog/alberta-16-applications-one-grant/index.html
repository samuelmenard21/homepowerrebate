Alberta's Heat Pump Program Got 16 Applications. One Was Approved.

Not per city. Not per month. Province-wide, total, as of January 2026.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

The number

The Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program (OHPA) is a federal program, delivered directly in provinces without their own agreement — Alberta, Manitoba, Northwest Territories, Quebec, and Saskatchewan. It offers up to $10,000 toward switching an oil furnace to a cold-climate heat pump.

As of January 2026, federal program statistics showed Alberta had received 16 applications and issued 1 grant. Compare that to provinces with active provincial delivery partners, where participation ran into the hundreds or thousands.

Why this happened

It's not that Alberta homeowners don't want heat pumps. It's that almost nobody in Alberta heats with oil in the first place — oil heating is far more common in rural Atlantic Canada and parts of Quebec, where furnace-oil delivery trucks are still a normal part of winter. OHPA was built for that population. Applying it to Alberta, where natural gas dominates home heating, means the program simply doesn't match the province it's supposed to serve.

That's the deeper problem: Alberta doesn't just have weak uptake of a federal program — it has no functioning provincial heat pump rebate of its own to fill the gap. When the one federal tool available doesn't fit the local heating mix, there's nothing else in place.

What actually exists in Alberta

  • CEIP — financing, not a rebate. A property-tax-attached loan, sometimes with a 5-10% incentive.
  • ENMAX/ATCO utility rebates — $500-$2,000 per heat pump, described by installers as changing "throughout the year."
  • Zero solar, battery, or EV charger rebate at the provincial level.

Some Alberta cities do better than the provincial baseline — Calgary runs a free Home Upgrades Program for income-qualified residents, Lethbridge has its own municipal utility rebate, St. Albert adds a $1,400 CEIP bonus. But none of that changes the province-wide picture: a single-digit application count for the one federal tool meant to help.

If you're in Alberta and heat with oil: OHPA closed to new applications July 31, 2026. If you missed it, your remaining options are CEIP financing and whatever your specific utility (ENMAX, ATCO, EPCOR) currently offers — confirm directly, since availability shifts through the year.
