11 ways to cut your energy bill in Nova Scotia

Nova Scotia Power's rate is one of the highest in Canada and keeps climbing. Here's what actually moves the number on your bill, and an update on a program that changed status this year.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

1. Know the program status before you plan around it

The federal Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program, which paired up to $15,000 in federal money with provincial top-ups, closed to new applicants on July 2, 2026 after its funding was fully allocated. If you already applied before the closure, you have until January 31, 2027 to complete the work and submit documents. If you haven't applied, that specific program is no longer an option — but up to $10,000 remains available through the Moderate Income and Home Energy Assessment Rebates, run by Efficiency Nova Scotia. (Efficiency Nova Scotia program page)

2. Budget for further Nova Scotia Power rate increases

Nova Scotia Power's rate rose 6.5% in a single step in January 2024, on top of prior increases, and the utility has a pattern of compounding hikes. Any payback calculation for a heat pump or insulation upgrade should assume the rate keeps rising, not stay flat, since a flat-rate assumption understates your real future savings.

3. If you have electric baseboard heat, get a walkthrough on efficient settings

Homeowners who switch from baseboards to a heat pump sometimes report a bill spike right after install, and the usual cause isn't the equipment, it's the setback settings not being configured for how the household actually uses the space. Ask your installer to walk you through the schedule and setback temperatures before they leave, not just the thermostat's basic operation.

4. Insulate before, or alongside, switching off baseboards

Nova Scotia's older housing stock often has electric baseboard heat installed back when electricity was cheap, paired with thin wall insulation. Baseboards run at 100% efficiency; a heat pump runs at 300%+ for the same heat output. If your walls are poorly insulated, insulating first (or at the same time as the heat pump swap) means the heat pump doesn't have to work as hard to hold the temperature, which improves the payback on both upgrades.

5. Compare heating oil against electric before your next tank fill

Heating oil prices rose roughly 70% in a single year in Nova Scotia, which is the main reason demand for heat pump conversions spiked. If you're still on oil, run the comparison against a heat pump's operating cost at current Nova Scotia Power rates before locking in another season of oil deliveries.

6. Use the Winter Payment Plan if a spike catches you off guard

Nova Scotia Power's heating season can push bills up sharply as baseboards or oil systems run harder. If a bill lands higher than expected, ask about a payment plan that spreads the cost rather than absorbing the full hit in one month.

7. Seal and weatherstrip before winter, not during it

Door sweeps, outlet gaskets, and weatherstripping around window and door frames are the cheapest lever available and pay back within a season in a province this cold. Do it in fall, before the heating season starts, rather than mid-winter when it's harder to find and fix leaks.

8. Check your eligibility for the Moderate Income and Home Energy Assessment Rebates directly

With the federal oil-to-heat-pump program closed, this Efficiency Nova Scotia program is now the main standing incentive for a heat pump or insulation upgrade. Confirm your income tier and the current rebate ceiling before you get quotes, since the amount depends on both.

9. Use window coverings actively, not just as decoration

Open south-facing coverings on sunny winter days for free solar heat, close them at night to hold it in. It costs nothing and makes a real difference in a heating-dominated climate like Nova Scotia's.

10. Wash clothes cold and run full loads only

Water heating is one of the largest loads in a typical Nova Scotia home. Cold-water washing and full loads cut that load directly, at zero cost.

11. Get your real number before you commit

With the biggest federal program now closed to new applicants, the available rebate stack looks different than it did even a few months ago. Run your specific address through an assessment to see what's actually still available, rather than budgeting around a program that's no longer open.
