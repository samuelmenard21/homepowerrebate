The Greener Homes Grant has closed — here's what replaced it

Straight talk on the federal programs in 2026, so you don't chase money that no longer exists.

By Sam Menard · Updated October 16, 2026 · 3 min read

⚠️ Both federal programs are now closed

The Canada Greener Homes Grant (up to $5,000) is closed to new applicants, and December 31, 2025 was the last day for existing applicants to submit documents. The Canada Greener Homes Loan (interest-free, up to $40,000) is also closed: no further applications can be approved. If a website, installer, or ad still promises you "$40,000 from Greener Homes," it's out of date.

We're updating this page because the old version — like a lot of the internet — described a program that no longer accepts applications. Here's the accurate picture for 2026, and more importantly, where the money actually is now.

What happened

  • The Grant gave up to $5,000 back for retrofits after pre- and post-EnerGuide evaluations. It closed to new applicants once funding was committed, and the final document deadline was December 31, 2025.
  • The Loan offered up to $40,000 interest-free over 10 years. That's the "$40,000" number everyone remembers — but it was a loan you repay, not free money, and it's now closed.
  • If you were already approved for either, you keep your funding. This only affects new applications.

What replaced it: the Greener Homes Affordability Program

In its place, the federal government launched the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program (CGHAP), with a narrower focus: low-to-median income homeowners and tenants. For households that qualify, it covers the full cost of recommended retrofits.

The key difference for BC

CGHAP is delivered through partners in each province and territory, not through a federal application form. In BC, start with Better Homes BC, which lists the income-qualified programs open to you.

Coming next: the National Heat Pump Rebate

On October 8, 2026, Ottawa announced a new federal heat pump rebate: up to $10,000 for households below the median income and $2,000 for other eligible homeowners. Ottawa expects it to open in early 2027. It is not open yet.

It works differently from the old grant. Ottawa says there is no energy audit before or after. You apply online when your heating system breaks or is near the end of its life, then install a heat pump from an approved list.

Ottawa has not published the income line, which homes qualify or the exact start date. Read everything confirmed so far.

So where's the money in 2026? (This part is good news)

Losing the federal grant sounds bad, but for most BC homeowners the provincial and utility programs are larger and still wide open:

The takeaway

Don't plan around the Greener Homes Grant or Loan — they're gone. Plan around the CleanBC and utility rebates (still open, often bigger), and if your income qualifies, the free ECAP install and the federal Affordability Program through your provincial partner.
