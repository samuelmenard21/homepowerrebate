Every BC homeowner asks the same question: "Will a heat pump actually heat my house when it's -20°C outside?" 

The short answer: **yes, modern cold-climate heat pumps work reliably in BC winters.** They'll keep your home warm without relying on backup electric resistance heating—even in Prince George or Kamloops on the coldest nights.

The longer answer matters for your wallet, though. Here's what the engineering actually shows.

## How heat pumps work (even in extreme cold)

A heat pump doesn't create heat. It *extracts* heat from outside air and moves it inside, using a refrigerant cycle. 

The myth: "When it's below freezing, there's no heat left to extract."

The reality: Even at -25°C, there's heat energy in the air. A heat pump just needs enough temperature difference to do the work. Modern cold-climate systems use:

- **Enhanced Vapor Injection (EVI) compressors** — these compress the refrigerant more efficiently, extracting more heat even when it's frigid
- **Advanced inverter technology** — speeds up and slows down the compressor based on demand, not just running on/off
- **Specialized refrigerants** — formulated to work at lower temperatures

The result: rated to work at -30°C or lower with minimal capacity loss.

## Where backup heating DOES matter

The cold-climate units still need backup for extreme outliers:

- **Prince George** (extremes to -37°C): backup electric heating recommended for the coldest 5 days/year
- **Interior (Williams Lake, Dawson Creek)** (extremes to -30°C+): backup still prudent
- **Lower Mainland, Island** (extremes to -15°C): none needed, heat pump alone will heat 100% of the time

For most BC homeowners, "backup heating" means your home has a little electric resistance coil that only kicks in if somehow the outdoor temp drops below what the heat pump is rated for. It rarely runs.

## The payback math

A whole-home heat pump costs $8,000–$12,000 installed in BC. You get:

- **BC Hydro rebate:** $4,000 (whole-home coverage) or $1,500 (partial)
- **Lower heating bills:** $1,000–$2,000 per year depending on fuel you're replacing (oil/gas saves more than electric)
- **Income-qualified CleanBC:** Up to $16,000 if you qualify (household income under ~$95k, varies by family size)

**Payback:** 4–6 years for middle-income homes, often free/negative if you're income-qualified (upgrades paid for, you just owe nothing).

## The real decision: is it worth it?

If you're heating with oil, propane, or electric resistance (baseboard/furnace), a cold-climate heat pump saves money from day one, even before the rebate.

If you're heating with natural gas, the payback is longer but still solid: 6–8 years depending on your province's gas prices.

If you're already on a cheap gas supply and rarely experience outages, the ROI is emotional (comfort, resilience) not financial. Both are valid.

The fear about BC winters? That's not backed up by the engineering or the data. Modern units work.
