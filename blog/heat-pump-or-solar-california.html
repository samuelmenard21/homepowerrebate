Heat Pump or Solar First? California Edition

Compare costs, rebates, and payback times to find out which upgrade saves you money faster.

The Short Answer: Heat Pump Wins Everywhere in California

No matter which utility you have in California, a heat pump pays for itself faster than solar. Heat pumps pay back in 3–7 years. Solar takes 10–18 years. Install the heat pump first, then add solar later when the cost comes down further.

Why? Heat pumps heat and cool. You use heating almost every day in winter. Solar only works during sunny daylight. Heating savings pile up faster than electric savings.

Real Numbers: LADWP (Los Angeles)

LADWP is California's most generous utility. Here's how heat pump and solar actually work:

Metric Heat Pump Solar
Installation cost $12,000 $22,000
LADWP rebate $10,000 $1,500
Your out-of-pocket $2,000 $20,500
Annual savings $1,200 $1,500
Payback time 2 years 14 years

The heat pump path: You pay $2,000 out-of-pocket. You save $1,200 per year. In 2 years, it's paid for. Then you get 20+ years of free heating.

The solar path: You pay $20,500 out-of-pocket. You save $1,500 per year. In 14 years, it's paid for. Then you get 20+ years of free electricity.

Heat pump is faster and cheaper to get started. Most LADWP homeowners break even by year 2–3. Most solar systems break even by year 13–15.

SMUD (Sacramento): Even Better for Heat Pumps

SMUD pays $3,000 for heat pumps and covers more upgrades. The payback is even faster:

System Cost SMUD Rebate Out-of-Pocket Annual Savings Payback
Heat pump alone $11,000 $3,000 $8,000 $1,000 8 years
Heat pump + water heater $15,000 $5,500 $9,500 $1,500 6.5 years
Solar (8 kW) $20,000 $4,000 $16,000 $1,500 11 years

Key insight: In SMUD territory, if you stack heat pump + water heater, your payback drops to 6.5 years. Add a federal IRA tax credit (up to $3,200 for heat pump alone) and your actual out-of-pocket cost drops to almost zero.

PG&E (Bay Area): Heat Pump Wins by Even More

PG&E pays less than LADWP for heat pumps ($1,500–$2,000 instead of $2,500), but electricity is more expensive, so savings are still decent:

  • Heat pump: Cost $12,000. Rebate $1,500–$2,000. Out-of-pocket $10,000–$10,500. Saves $1,100/year. Payback: 9–10 years.
  • Solar (8 kW): Cost $22,000. Rebate $2,000–$3,000. Out-of-pocket $19,000–$20,000. Saves $1,400/year. Payback: 14–15 years.

Heat pump still wins. Even with lower rebates, heat pumps break even in under 10 years. Solar takes 14–15.

SDG&E (San Diego): Heat Pump Also Wins

San Diego has mild winters, so heating savings are lower than LA. But heating bills still add up, and heat pump installation is cheap:

  • Heat pump: Cost $10,500. Rebate $1,500–$2,000. Out-of-pocket $8,500–$9,000. Saves $800/year. Payback: 11–12 years.
  • Solar (8 kW): Cost $20,000. Rebate $2,000. Out-of-pocket $18,000. Saves $1,200/year. Payback: 15 years.

Even in mild-climate San Diego, the heat pump breaks even faster. And solar is still 15 years.

Why You Should Install Heat Pump First (Not Solar)

1. Faster payback everywhere

No matter which utility, heat pump pays back in 3–12 years. Solar takes 10–18. Heat pump wins every time.

2. Lower upfront cost

Heat pump: $8,000–$20,000 out-of-pocket after rebates. Solar: $16,000–$20,500 out-of-pocket. Heat pump is cheaper to start.

3. Comfort benefit now

Heat pump replaces your old furnace or AC with a modern, quiet system that heats and cools better. You get comfort the day it's installed. Solar produces power for electricity, but your furnace still uses gas for years.

4. Foundation for solar later

Once your home uses a heat pump, your electricity demand drops (no more gas heating). When you add solar, it sizes cheaper because your home needs less electricity.

The Path Forward

Year 1–2: Install heat pump

Get your $3,000–$10,000 rebate depending on your utility. Pay $2,000–$20,000 out-of-pocket. Start saving on heating immediately.

Year 3–4: Add smart thermostat and water heater

Stack more rebates. Boost your annual savings to $1,500+. These upgrades cost $3,000–$5,000 and often pay for themselves in 2–3 years.

Year 5+: Install solar

By now, heat pump is paid off. You have cash freed up. Solar costs less per watt as prices drop. Your home uses less electricity (heat pump efficiency), so solar is sized smaller and costs less.

Bottom Line

Heat pump first. Solar second. Heat pumps pay for themselves in under a decade everywhere in California. Solar takes 10–18 years. Install the fast-payback system first. Then add solar when you're ready and the cost comes down further.
