Heat Pump or Solar First in San Diego? The Honest Answer

San Diego's mild winters and strong battery incentives point one direction more than the other. Here's the honest case.

The Short, Honest Answer

For most San Diego homeowners, solar (paired with a battery) tends to pencil out better than a heat pump alone. Here's why, and where the exception is.

Why Solar + Battery Usually Wins Here

San Diego has very low heating demand — around 1,100–1,200 heating degree days a year, among the mildest climates in our dataset. That means a heat pump's biggest advantage (efficient winter heating) doesn't save you as much here as it would somewhere colder. Meanwhile, SDG&E's territory has one of the stronger battery storage incentive programs around, SGIP, which pays $1,000+ toward home battery systems. Combine that with solar and you're building real energy independence and bill savings in a way that doesn't depend on how cold your winter gets.

When the Heat Pump Case Still Holds

If you're already replacing your air conditioner — not just heating — a heat pump does double duty as an efficient AC replacement. In that case, the SDG&E heat pump rebate ($250–$500) becomes a nice bonus on a purchase you were making anyway, rather than the main financial driver.

How to Decide

  1. If your AC is fine and your furnace is fine: prioritize solar + battery.
  2. If your AC needs replacing regardless: get heat pump quotes as your AC replacement, and treat the rebate as a bonus.
  3. If money is tight and you can only do one project this year: check current SGIP funding levels first — it's sometimes tighter than expected.
