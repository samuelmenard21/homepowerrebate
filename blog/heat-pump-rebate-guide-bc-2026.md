## BC is now the heat pump capital. Here's why.

Heat pumps are growing 3x faster than solar in Canada. BC's rebate ($4,000 standard, $16,000 income-qualified) is one of the most generous in North America. If you're replacing a gas furnace or old electric resistance heating, the payback is 4–6 years.

The biggest question people ask: "Will it actually heat my house in a BC winter?"

**Answer: Yes, definitively.**

## How heat pumps work (even in extreme cold)

A heat pump extracts heat from outside air and moves it indoors. The myth: "When it's freezing, there's no heat to extract." Reality: Even at -25°C, heat energy exists. Modern cold-climate heat pumps use advanced refrigerant cycles and inverter technology to work reliably at -30°C with 80%+ efficiency.

Kelowna's record low is around -37°C (once a decade). Vancouver's is -15°C (rare). Most BC homes will run on a heat pump alone 100% of the winter. Only remote Interior locations (Prince George, Dawson Creek) might need a backup electric coil for the coldest 5 days/year.

## The BC rebate breakdown

**Standard Stream (non-income-qualified):**
- Whole-home (100% heating at -5°C): **$4,000**
- Partial (50%+ heating at -5°C): **$1,500**

**Income-Qualified Stream (CleanBC):**
- Household income under ~$95k–$185k (varies by family size): **$8,000–$16,000** depending on tier
- Tier 3 (highest): $16,000 for whole-home systems

**How to qualify:** Household combined pre-tax annual income. Thresholds are strict but generous enough that many homeowners qualify without realizing it.

## The payback math

**If replacing gas furnace:**
- Current heating bill: ~$1,500–$2,000/year
- Heat pump heating bill: ~$800–$1,200/year
- Annual savings: $700–$1,200
- Payback: 4–6 years (even faster if income-qualified)

**If replacing electric resistance:**
- Current heating bill: ~$2,500–$3,500/year
- Heat pump heating bill: ~$1,000–$1,500/year
- Annual savings: $1,500–$2,000
- Payback: 3–5 years

## Why now?

1. **Gas prices are rising.** Natural gas in BC has doubled in the last 5 years. That trend continues.
2. **Rebates won't last forever.** These programs are 3-year commitments that may not renew at current levels.
3. **Installation backlogs are normalizing.** Heat pump installers used to have 6-month waits. Now it's 4–8 weeks.
4. **Your HVAC lifespan is probably ending anyway.** If your furnace is 15+ years old, replacement is coming. Might as well get the rebate.

## The one gotcha: Peak Saver enrollment

Heat pumps don't have the same 14-day Peak Saver deadline as batteries. But if you're pairing a heat pump with solar + battery, your installer needs to handle Peak Saver enrollment correctly for the battery to get its $5,000 rebate. Make sure they have a documented process.

## Installer checklist

Before you sign with any heat pump contractor:

- ☐ **HPCN-certified** (required as of June 1, 2026 for BC Hydro rebate)
- ☐ **Cold-climate experience** (ask for references in your region)
- ☐ **10-year workmanship warranty** on installation
- ☐ **Understands income-qualified programs** (can they help you navigate CleanBC?)
- ☐ **Handles rebate paperwork** (you shouldn't have to submit anything)
- ☐ **Knows Peak Saver if you're adding solar** (critical timing)
