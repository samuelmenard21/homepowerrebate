Heat Pump Rebates by Income: Exactly How Much You Qualify For

By Sam Menard

July 12, 2026 · 4-min read

Heat pump rebates aren't one-size-fits-all — and that's good news for you

If you've seen "$4,000–$16,000 heat pump rebate" online and wondered which end applies to you, here's the answer: it depends on your household size and income.

Lower income = higher rebate. This is the Energy Savings Program working as intended: the households that need the most help get the most financial support.

The exact income tiers (BC Hydro / BetterHomesBC)

Rebate amounts depend on two things: how many people live in your home, and your household's pre-tax income.

Here's the official table:

Household Size Level 1 Max Income Level 2 Max Income Level 3 Max Income
1 person $51,100 $63,900 $102,100
2 people $63,600 $79,500 $127,200
3 people $78,200 $97,700 $156,300
4 people $94,900 $118,600 $189,800
5 people $107,600 $134,500 $215,200
6 people $121,400 $151,700 $242,700
7+ people $135,100 $168,900 $270,200

What each level gets you

Level 1 households (lower income): $16,000 heat pump rebate (ground-oriented homes only)
This is the maximum. You get it if your household income is below the Level 1 threshold for your household size.

Level 2 households (middle income): $12,000 heat pump rebate (ground-oriented homes only)
Your income falls between the Level 1 and Level 2 thresholds.

Level 3 households (higher income): $13,000 heat pump rebate (ground-oriented homes, switching from gas/oil/propane only)
Your income exceeds the Level 2 threshold. Important: Level 3 rebates only apply if you're replacing a fossil fuel heating system (not electric baseboard).

A real example: 4-person household in Kelowna

  • If your household income is under $94,900: you qualify for $16,000
  • If your income is $94,900–$118,600: you qualify for $12,000
  • If your income exceeds $118,600 (and you're replacing gas/oil): you qualify for $13,000

The difference between Level 1 and Level 2? That's $4,000 toward your install costs.

FortisBC customers (Kelowna, Penticton): different amounts

If you're on FortisBC (not BC Hydro), heat pump rebates are $4,000 flat — not income-tiered. Same amount for everyone, no Level 1/2/3 split.

What "ground-oriented home" means

Level 1, 2, and 3 amounts apply to ground-oriented homes — detached houses, townhomes, duplexes, rowhomes. This means the heating system heats the whole home from one location.

Condo/apartment residents? You can still get $5,000 for a heat pump if your unit is electrically heated (not gas).

The assessment and approval process

  1. Pre-qualify: Go to bcenergysavingsprogram.ca and answer questions. No money yet.
  2. Get a code: Received within 3 months. It's your ticket to contractor quotes.
  3. Get quotes: Only from ESP-registered contractors (HPCN-certified).
  4. Contractor receives rebate: After installation and inspection, BC Hydro pays your contractor. You pay the remainder.

Timeline: 2–4 weeks from application to approval, then installation is up to you.

One more thing: home value matters

Homes with an assessed value over $1,230,000 may not qualify. If you're in that range, check bcenergysavingsprogram.ca to confirm.

Next steps

  1. Find your household size in the table above
  2. Estimate your pre-tax income (include all adults' earnings)
  3. See which Level you fall into — that's your rebate amount
  4. Go to bcenergysavingsprogram.ca and pre-qualify (takes 10 minutes)
  5. Get contractor quotes (we can recommend HPCN-certified installers in your city)

Sources verified: BetterHomesBC as of July 12, 2026. Program terms change — always confirm on bcenergysavingsprogram.ca before committing.
