The $575 You Save Without Doing Anything Different

Everyone talks about solar and heat pumps. Almost nobody talks about the tank in the basement quietly costing you money every day. This is the upgrade with no drama.

By Sam Menard · September 2026 · 4 min read

The most boring upgrade that actually pays off

Solar gets the photos. Heat pumps get the blog posts. Nobody brags about their water heater. That's exactly why it's worth a second look: heat pump water heaters are one of the simplest, lowest-friction upgrades a homeowner can make, and the savings are real.

A heat pump water heater doesn't generate heat directly the way a standard electric tank does. It moves heat from the surrounding air into the water instead, which uses a fraction of the electricity. That difference alone can cut your water heating costs by roughly half, saving many households up to about $575 a year.

Why this is a good first step: no roof work, no structural changes, no permit fight most of the time. It replaces your existing tank, in the same spot, connected to the same pipes.

What actually changes in your house

Almost nothing, day to day. Hot water comes out of the same taps, at the same temperature, on the same schedule. The two differences homeowners actually notice:

  • The unit is a bit larger than a standard tank, since the heat pump component sits on top.
  • It makes a quiet fan sound, similar to a dehumidifier, while it's running.

That's the whole adjustment. No new habits, no new maintenance routine, no rewiring your daily life around it.

Why this is a good place to start

If you're weighing a bigger decision like solar or a full home heat pump system, a water heater swap is a low-risk way to get comfortable with electrified upgrades before committing to a larger project. It's a smaller check to write, a shorter install, and the savings show up on your very next bill.

Many utilities and provinces also offer rebates specifically for heat pump water heaters, often covering a meaningful chunk of the install cost, since it's an easy category for programs to fund.

What to ask an installer

  • Does my current spot have enough room and airflow? Heat pump water heaters pull warmth from the surrounding air, so they work best in a space with some volume around them, like a basement or utility room.
  • What size tank do I actually need? Sizing follows your household's hot water use, same as any water heater.
  • What rebates apply to this specific model? Not every unit qualifies for every program, so confirm before you buy.
