Why Massachusetts Is Our First US State

Mass Save is one of the most generous, most coherent home energy rebate programs in the country. Here's what made it the right place to start.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

What Mass Save actually pays

A standard heat pump rebate runs $8,500 to $9,500. Income-qualified households can reach $25,000. Insulation is 75% covered for everyone, 100% free below 60% of area median income. Add solar, battery storage, water heater, windows, EV charger, and smart thermostat rebates, and a single program covers all 8 categories we track — administered consistently across the state's utilities rather than fragmented city by city or utility by utility.

That combination — genuinely large numbers, one coherent administrator, funding that's actually open right now — is rare. It's the reason Massachusetts became our first US market instead of staying a "someday" state.

What research turned up once we started building

The rebate amounts were the easy part to document. What took longer, and what most competitor content skips entirely, were two things:

  • A documented rebate-delay crisis — CBS Boston's I-Team ran multiple investigations, and the Massachusetts Attorney General's office logged more than 30 formal complaints in a single year, some homeowners waiting close to six months for a check.
  • 41 Massachusetts towns run their own municipal electric utility and are structurally locked out of Mass Save entirely — a real, common source of confusion that almost nobody explains clearly.

We built content around both, because a rebate guide that only publishes the good news isn't actually useful to someone about to spend $12,000 on a heat pump.

What's live now

Full coverage across 12 Massachusetts cities — Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Cambridge, Lowell, Brockton, New Bedford, Quincy, Lynn, Fall River, Newton, and Somerville — all 8 rebate categories on every page, plus city-specific finds along the way: Boston's Solar Loan Pilot Program, Cambridge's heat pump permit fee waiver, and Worcester/Springfield's Equity Community designation that gives every renter free weatherization regardless of income.
