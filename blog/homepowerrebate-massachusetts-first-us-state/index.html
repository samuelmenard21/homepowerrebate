Why Massachusetts Is Our First US State

California looked obvious. Vermont looked easy. Neither was the right call once we checked the actual numbers.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

Why not California

California has the reputation. It doesn't currently have the availability. Both HEEHRA and TECH Clean California — the state's two biggest heat pump rebate mechanisms — are fully reserved and closed to new single-family applicants as of early 2026. A rebate program that's functionally a waitlist isn't a program we can build honest content around.

Why not Vermont

Vermont's rebates are real and live — $2,200-$2,675 per heat pump system, actively funded. But the population is 650,000, smaller than most single cities we already cover. It didn't clear the bar to justify building out full city coverage.

Why Massachusetts won

Mass Save pays $8,500-$9,500 for a standard heat pump, up to $25,000 income-qualified — genuinely competitive with BC. It's administered as one program across the state's utilities rather than fragmented like Alberta. Population is 7.1M. And it's open and funded right now, not reserved out.

What we found once we started building

Research turned up things no competitor site was covering: a documented rebate-delay crisis with 30+ Attorney General complaints, and 41 Massachusetts towns that are structurally locked out of Mass Save because they run their own municipal electric utility. We built content around both — because an honest rebate guide should tell homeowners what actually happens, not just what a program promises.

Live now: full coverage across 12 Massachusetts cities, all 8 rebate categories, plus city-specific finds like Boston's Solar Loan Pilot Program and Worcester/Springfield's free-weatherization-for-all-renters designation.
