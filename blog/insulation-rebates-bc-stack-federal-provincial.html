BC Insulation Rebates 2026: Stack Federal + Provincial Grants for Up to $11,350

BC homeowners can combine federal, provincial, and utility rebates on the same insulation project. Here's exactly how much you can claim—and why the stack matters.

The Stack: How Much You Can Claim

A single attic insulation project can qualify for rebates from three separate programs. They don't compete—they combine.

Program Insulation Rebate Eligibility
CleanBC (Provincial) $1,500–$5,500 All BC homeowners
BC Hydro (Utility) $900 BC Hydro customers
FortisBC (Utility) $500–$2,000 FortisBC gas customers; higher if income-qualified
Total Possible Stack Up to $11,350 Income-qualified homeowners, dual utility service

Why this matters: A typical $3,000 attic insulation project nets $2,000–$3,000 in rebates under a full stack. Your out-of-pocket cost drops to $0–$1,000. That's a payback period of under 2 years from heating savings alone.

Breaking Down Each Rebate

CleanBC: The Big One ($1,500–$5,500)

This is the largest rebate and applies to almost all BC homeowners. Income doesn't matter—you qualify based on the upgrade you're making.

  • Attic insulation: Add $500 for every R-value increase up to $2,500 (R-50 max payout)
  • Wall insulation: Up to $1,500
  • Crawlspace or basement: Up to $1,500
  • Combined ceiling/wall/floor: Up to $5,500 total per home per year

Key detail: You must use a BetterHomes BC contractor (HPCN member). DIY installations don't qualify.

BC Hydro: Paid by the Square Foot, Not Flat

The rebate scales with how much R-value you're adding and how much area you're covering — it isn't one flat number. Here's the exact formula for each area of the house:

AreaFormulaCap
Attic$0.02 × R-value added × sq ft$900
Basement / crawlspace$0.09 × R-value added × sq ft$1,200
Exterior wall cavity$0.09 × R-value added × sq ft$1,200
Exterior wall sheathing$0.09 × R-value added × sq ft$1,200
Other (floors, headers)$0.07 × R-value added × sq ft$1,000

A typical 1,000 sq ft attic going from R-12 to R-50 (R-38 added) works out to $0.02 × 38 × 1,000 = $760 — under the $900 cap, so you'd get the full $760. These stack independently by area, so a full attic-plus-basement job can clear $1,900 from BC Hydro alone, on top of CleanBC.

Minimum R-values to qualify (this trips people up): attics and walls need at least R-12 added, sheathing needs R-3.8, basements need R-10, and exposed floors need R-20. Add less than that and the rebate doesn't apply at all — ask your contractor to confirm the post-upgrade R-value in writing before they start.

Key detail: Must be an HPCN-certified contractor — self-installs never qualify, no exceptions. You have six months from your paid invoice date to apply, not six months from when the work started. BC Hydro's official insulation rebate page →

FortisBC: $500–$2,000 (Gas Customers)

The wildcard. Non-income-qualified homeowners get $500; income-qualified get up to $2,000.

  • Basic rebate: $500 attic, $500 wall, $500 basement
  • Income-qualified: Up to $2,000 if you earn under ~$75K household income

Key detail: Application window is 90 days after installation. Invoice must show HPCN contractor. Processing takes 4–6 weeks. FortisBC's official insulation rebate page →

For general insulation tips (not rebate-specific), BC Hydro also runs an insulation basics page worth a skim before you get quotes.

Who Qualifies: Income & Utility Breakdown

CleanBC (Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3)

All BC homeowners qualify for at least Tier 1. Higher income = lower rebate, but you still get something.

  • Tier 1 (Under $110K): $1,500 rebate on attic insulation
  • Tier 2 ($110K–$165K): $750 rebate on attic insulation
  • Tier 3 (Over $165K): $500 rebate on attic insulation

BC Hydro

All BC Hydro customers qualify. No income requirement.

FortisBC

All FortisBC gas customers qualify for basic rebate. Income-qualified (under ~$75K) get the higher tier.

If You Have Dual Service (Both BC Hydro + FortisBC)

You qualify for CleanBC + BC Hydro + FortisBC rebates on the same project. This is rare but possible in some border regions. Stack everything.

10 Things to Do While You're Waiting on Quotes

Getting a contractor booked and the paperwork sorted takes a few weeks. None of that has to be cold weeks. Here's what actually moves the needle in the meantime — most of it costs less than a takeout order.

The uncomfortable truth about home heat: it's not really about the house. It's about you, in one room, at one moment, feeling cold. A perfectly insulated attic won't help if you're sitting three feet from a drafty window with a laptop on your knees. Fix the room you're actually in first — the whole-house stuff below is the long game.

  1. Hunt outlet drafts with the back of your hand. Exterior-wall outlets and switch plates are basically holes in your wall with a thin plastic lid. Unplug, hover the back of your hand near the gap (it's more sensitive to cold than your palm), and feel for a breeze. A pack of foam gaskets is about $5 and takes ten minutes for a whole house.
  2. Let the sun do free labour. Open south- and west-facing blinds on clear winter days — that's real heat, no thermostat involved. Close everything at night; glass loses heat fast once the sun's down. Reverse the routine in summer.
  3. Find the gaps insulation won't fix. Baseboards, where pipes and wires punch through walls, and the trim around doors are classic leak points that a caulk gun handles in an afternoon — and they'll undercut your new insulation's performance if left alone.
  4. Sleeve your exposed pipes. Hot water pipes running through an unheated basement or crawlspace lose heat the entire trip. Foam pipe sleeves run about $1/foot and mean your water heater works less to deliver the same shower.
  5. Reverse your ceiling fan. Most fans have a tiny switch on the motor housing. Clockwise in winter (on low) gently pushes the warm air that's pooled at the ceiling back down into the room, without the wind-chill effect.
  6. Seal the attic hatch specifically. It's the single most commonly missed leak in the whole house — a plywood square with a lid, often with zero insulation or weatherstripping of its own, sitting right above your heated living space. Ask your insulation contractor to weatherstrip it while they're already up there.
  7. Check the furnace filter monthly, not seasonally. A clogged filter makes your system work harder for the same heat — and it's the cheapest efficiency fix that exists.
  8. Heat yourself before you heat the room. A $30 heated throw or a good pair of wool socks lets you drop the thermostat 1–2°C without anyone actually feeling colder. That's not a consolation prize — it's free money, every single day, for the rest of the season.
  9. Get quotes now, not in January. Contractors are slower and more available in fall. Come the first real cold snap, everyone panics at once and both price and wait times jump. Booking now is arguably the highest-value item on this list.
  10. Write down where it's actually cold. Walk your home on a cold evening with a notepad. "Cold by the office window," "draft under the basement door" — specific notes like this help your contractor prioritize the areas that'll make the biggest difference to how the house feels, not just what the rebate math says.

Sources for the tips above: BC Hydro on outlet insulators, window coverings, draftproofing, pipe insulation, and BC Hydro's pieces on cold-weather prep and why home heat is personal.

Common Questions

Can I DIY insulation and still get rebates?
No. All three programs require HPCN-certified contractors. DIY installations disqualify you from every rebate. The contractor cost is worth it for the rebate stack.
What if I already got a furnace rebate—can I still stack insulation rebates?
Yes. Furnace rebates are separate from insulation rebates. You can claim both in the same year from the same utility.
Do I need a home energy audit?
CleanBC requires an EnerGuide pre-upgrade assessment (not an audit—just a quick inspection). BC Hydro and FortisBC do not. Your contractor handles this.
What's the timeline from completion to rebate check?
CleanBC: 4–8 weeks. BC Hydro: 4–6 weeks. FortisBC: 4–6 weeks after submission (which can be up to 90 days after completion). Total: 2–5 months depending on submission order.
What if I have oil or propane heat instead of gas?
CleanBC and BC Hydro still apply. FortisBC doesn't (they only service gas customers). You get up to $2,400 instead of $2,900+.
Can I claim the rebate if I already applied years ago?
Each program has a one-per-address-per-year rule. If you claimed insulation rebates in 2025, you're ineligible for 2026. Mark your calendar for next year.
What's the minimum R-value I need to add to qualify?
This is the detail most homeowners miss. Attics and walls need at least R-12 added, sheathing needs R-3.8, basements need R-10, and exposed floors need R-20. Fall short of these thresholds and BC Hydro's rebate doesn't apply at all — get your contractor to confirm the post-upgrade R-value in writing before work starts.
