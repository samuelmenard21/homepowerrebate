Mass Save Rebate Delayed? What to Do

You did everything right. You got the assessment, hired an approved contractor, installed the heat pump, filed the paperwork. Now it's been four months and your rebate check hasn't shown up.

You're not alone, and there's a real process for fixing it.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

The real timeline (not the marketing timeline)

Mass Save's official processing window is 8–12 weeks. In practice, that number is optimistic. CBS Boston's I-Team ran multiple investigations into rebate delays, and the Massachusetts Attorney General's office received more than 30 formal complaints about the program within a single year. Some homeowners waited close to six months. At least one case dragged past a year.

This isn't a rumor — it's been covered repeatedly by local news, and Mass Save brought in a new rebate-processing vendor specifically to work through the backlog.

Why delays happen

  • Paperwork gaps. Missing signatures, incomplete contractor documentation, or a mismatch between the assessment record and the installed equipment.
  • Backlog volume. Demand for heat pump and insulation rebates has outpaced Mass Save's processing capacity in recent years.
  • Old certifications. One documented case: a homeowner's rebate was denied because his 2006 weatherization certification was ruled too old, even though the home hadn't changed.
  • Contractor errors. If your contractor filed the claim on your behalf and made a mistake, you may not find out until you call to check status — which can be months in.

What to do if it's been more than 12 weeks

Step 1: Call Mass Save directly first

Before escalating, call and ask for a specific status update and a reference number for your case. Write down the date, the representative's name, and what they told you. This becomes your paper trail.

Step 2: Check your paperwork

Confirm your contractor actually submitted the claim (not just completed the work). Ask for a copy of the submission confirmation. A shocking number of "delays" are actually claims that were never filed.

Step 3: Escalate to the Department of Public Utilities

If Mass Save can't give you a resolution timeline, file a complaint with the Massachusetts DPU:

Massachusetts DPU Consumer Division
Phone: 877-886-5066
Web: mass.gov/dpu

Step 4: File with the Attorney General's office

If DPU doesn't move things along, the AG's consumer protection division has direct oversight and has pushed Mass Save publicly on this issue before.

Massachusetts AG Consumer Protection Division
Phone: 617-727-8400

Step 5: Contact local media (yes, really)

This sounds extreme, but it's documented as effective. Multiple homeowners in CBS Boston's I-Team coverage had their rebate checks arrive within days of the story airing. Local investigative reporters have an established interest in this exact issue — it's not a long shot.

Keep everything. Save your contractor's invoice, the assessment report, your rebate application confirmation, and every phone call log. If you end up escalating, this is what makes your case move faster.

How to avoid delays in the first place

  • Use a Mass Save-approved contractor with a track record — ask how many of their other customers have had rebate issues.
  • Get the submission confirmation in writing the same day work is completed, not weeks later.
  • Follow up at the 6-week mark, not the 12-week mark. Catching a paperwork problem early is much faster to fix than after the file has gone quiet.
  • Keep your own copy of every document your contractor submits on your behalf.

Mass Save's rebate amounts are genuinely some of the best in the country — up to $9,500 for a heat pump, or $25,000 income-qualified. The program works. It's the processing pipeline that needs pressure sometimes.
