Mass Save Rejected My Application. Now What?

There's a good chance this comes down to one specific, fixable rule most contractors forget to mention. Here's how to fix it.

Start Here: The Refrigerant Rule

The single most common, most avoidable reason Massachusetts heat pump rebate applications get rejected in 2026 is refrigerant type. As of January 1, 2026, units using R-410A refrigerant are no longer eligible for Mass Save rebates. Only newer refrigerants — R-32 or R-454B — qualify.

If your contractor quoted you a unit before checking this rule, or is installing older inventory, your application can be denied even though everything else about the job was done correctly. Ask your contractor directly which refrigerant your quoted unit uses.

Other Common Rejection Causes

1. Non-participating contractor

Mass Save rebates require your installer to be part of the program's contractor network. If they aren't, the rebate is denied regardless of install quality.

2. Missing insulation prerequisite

In some cases, Mass Save requires your home to meet certain insulation levels before a heat pump rebate is approved. Rules here can vary by project type, so if you're unsure whether this applies to you, ask your Mass Save energy assessor to confirm directly rather than assuming either way.

3. HEAT Loan paperwork issues

If you're financing through the Mass Save HEAT Loan program, incomplete or mismatched loan paperwork is a separate, common source of delay or rejection. Double check that your loan application and rebate application list matching contractor and project details.

How to Resubmit

  1. Confirm your unit's refrigerant type in writing — this alone resolves a large share of rejections.
  2. Confirm your contractor is in the Mass Save network.
  3. Ask your energy assessor to confirm any insulation prerequisite before resubmitting.
  4. Cross-check HEAT Loan paperwork against your rebate application for matching details.
