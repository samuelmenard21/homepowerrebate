41 Massachusetts Towns Are Locked Out of Mass Save

Mass Save is one of the best home energy rebate programs in the country. If you live in one of 41 Massachusetts towns, you can't use it — and almost nothing online explains why.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

The exclusion nobody explains clearly

Mass Save is funded through a charge on customer bills from Massachusetts's investor-owned utilities — Eversource, National Grid, Unitil. Towns that run their own municipal light plant (MLP) — a town-owned electric utility — sit entirely outside that funding structure. Forty-one Massachusetts communities fall into this category, including Concord, Shrewsbury, Reading, Wellesley, Belmont, Braintree, Hingham, Holyoke, Chicopee, Westfield, Taunton, and Middleborough, among others.

If you live in one of these towns, you cannot access Mass Save's electric-side rebates — the heat pump rebate, the insulation program, none of it — no matter how badly you want to.

Why this stays hidden

Most Mass Save marketing and third-party content talks about the program as if it covers "Massachusetts," full stop. It doesn't. Nobody selling heat pumps has an incentive to tell a Concord homeowner mid-quote that the rebate they're expecting doesn't apply to their address — by the time that comes up, they've already spent an hour on the phone.

What you can actually get instead

  • Gas-side Mass Save benefits — if your gas comes from an investor-owned utility even though your electricity doesn't, you may still get a free assessment and weatherization help.
  • Your MLP's own program — many run their own incentives through Energy New England or MMWEC. Amounts vary widely by town.
  • The Municipal Light Plant Zero-Interest Loan — a state program built specifically for this gap, up to $25,000 at 0% interest.
  • NextZero battery rebate — $100/kWh, the MLP equivalent of the investor-owned utility battery incentive.
