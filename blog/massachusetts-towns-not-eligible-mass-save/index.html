41 Massachusetts Towns Not Eligible for Mass Save

If you live in one of these towns and you've been searching for why your neighbor got a heat pump rebate and you can't find the same program, this is why: your town runs its own electric utility, and Mass Save doesn't cover it.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

Why this happens

Mass Save is funded through a charge on customer bills from Massachusetts's investor-owned utilities — Eversource, National Grid, Unitil, and a few smaller gas companies. Forty-one Massachusetts communities are served instead by a municipal light plant (MLP) — a town-owned electric utility, separate from that funding structure entirely. If your town runs its own electric department, you're not paying into Mass Save, and you can't draw rebates from it.

This isn't a penalty or an oversight. It's just a structural gap that almost nobody explains clearly, which leaves MLP-town homeowners confused about why their heat pump quote doesn't come with the rebate their friend in the next town over got.

What you CAN still get if you're in an MLP town

Gas-side Mass Save benefits (sometimes)

If your gas service comes from Eversource, National Grid, Berkshire Gas, Columbia Gas, Liberty Utilities, or Blackstone Gas Company — even though your electricity is from your town's MLP — you may still qualify for a free home energy assessment and gas-side incentives, including weatherization work and the HEAT Loan.

Your MLP's own rebate program

Many municipal light plants run their own efficiency incentives, often through Energy New England (ENE) or the Massachusetts Municipal Wholesale Electric Company (MMWEC). Amounts and categories vary significantly by town — some are generous, some are minimal. Contact your local light department directly.

The Municipal Light Plant Zero-Interest Loan

The state runs a dedicated 0% interest loan up to $25,000 specifically for MLP customers doing qualifying energy-efficiency work, including weatherization and heat pump installation. This exists precisely because MLP towns can't access the regular Mass Save HEAT Loan.

NextZero battery storage rebate

NextZero offers a $100/kWh battery storage rebate specifically for residential customers of participating municipal light plants — a direct MLP equivalent to the investor-owned utility battery incentives.

Not sure which category you're in? Check your electric bill. If it says Eversource, National Grid, Unitil, or another recognizable statewide utility, you have full Mass Save access. If it's a name like "Concord Municipal Light Plant" or "Shrewsbury Electric," you're in MLP territory and need the alternatives above.

The bottom line

MLP customers aren't locked out of every incentive — they're locked out of Mass Save specifically. The zero-interest loan, NextZero battery rebate, and your local MLP's own program are real money on the table, just under different names than the ones most Massachusetts heat pump marketing talks about.
