Mitsubishi vs Daikin: Which Heat Pump Saves More in BC?

By Sam Menard

Both Mitsubishi and Daikin are solid choices for BC homeowners. We'll compare upfront cost, winter efficiency, rebates, and which one actually makes sense for your climate zone.

TL;DR — Direct Answer

Mitsubishi edges out Daikin in extreme cold (Prince George winters), but Daikin is $1,000–$2,000 cheaper upfront. Both qualify for the same $4,000–$16,000 BC rebate. In coastal BC (Vancouver, Victoria), they're nearly identical performers. Choice: go with whichever your local HPCN installer prefers—installer support matters more than brand name.

The Head-to-Head

Real BC Numbers: What You'll Actually Pay

Vancouver / Coastal BC

Installed cost: $13,500 (either brand)

CleanBC rebate: $10,000 (middle-income household)

Your net cost: $3,500

Winter efficiency advantage: Mitsubishi ~2% better, but it won't move the needle in mild coastal winters (3,700 heating degree days). Daikin's price savings win here.

Kelowna / Interior BC

Installed cost: $14,000 (either brand)

CleanBC rebate: $12,000 (income-qualified)

Your net cost: $2,000

Winter efficiency advantage: Mitsubishi's cold-climate performance matters more here (4,900 heating degree days). If your installer specializes in Mitsubishi, go that route. Otherwise, Daikin works fine.

Prince George / Northern BC

Installed cost: $15,500 (Mitsubishi recommended)

CleanBC rebate: $16,000 (income-qualified gets full tier)

Your net cost: potential $0 or even rebate overage scenarios

Winter efficiency advantage: Mitsubishi's -20°C rating shines here (6,200 heating degree days). Heating savings in Prince George reach 40–45% vs a gas furnace—the biggest payback window in BC. Mitsubishi's cold efficiency translates directly to dollars saved every winter month.

The rebate covers most of it: In income-qualified scenarios, the full $16,000 rebate can nearly pay for the heat pump outright. Your job is choosing between brands—not financing the install.

Next step: Once you've chosen your installer, check HPCN certification requirements to verify they're legit. And understand which rebates apply to your utility—the stack is bigger than you think.

Why Mitsubishi Costs More (And When It's Worth It)

Mitsubishi's Zubadan line is engineered for extreme cold. The compressor, refrigerant, and controls are beefier to maintain efficiency when it's -20°C. That engineering costs extra—typically $1,500–$2,000 more than Daikin's standard model.

In Prince George, Kamloops, or Vernon? That premium pays itself back in heating savings within 2–3 seasons, especially if you're replacing a gas furnace.

In Vancouver or Victoria? The mild winters don't stress the system, so Daikin's efficiency is "good enough." The extra $1,500 you save buys other upgrades (better insulation, a smart thermostat) that might deliver more ROI.

Rebate Status: Both Brands Are Equal

BC doesn't play favorites. Whether you choose Mitsubishi or Daikin:

  • CleanBC rebate: $4,000–$16,000 (based on income, heating fuel)
  • BC Hydro doesn't discriminate by brand
  • FortisBC tops up another $1,500–$12,000 if you're on gas (same for both)
  • Richmond adds $2,000 (same for both)

What matters: your installer must be HPCN-certified. Brand doesn't change your rebate amount.

Installer Preference Matters More Than You Think

Here's the real secret: if your local installer is a Mitsubishi shop, buy Mitsubishi. If they're a Daikin house, buy Daikin.

Why? Installers who specialize in one brand know the quirks, stock parts faster, and give you better support long-term. A Daikin unit installed by a Mitsubishi expert can feel abandoned in year 3 when you need a service call. Conversely, a Mitsubishi system installed by a shop that's never seen one before risks a sloppy install.

Your HPCN installer's preference is worth more than a 1–2% efficiency difference.

Ask your installer outright: "Which do you install more? Which do you prefer for [your city]'s climate?" Their answer should drive your choice.

The Warranty Angle

Mitsubishi typically offers 5–12 years on compressor (depending on product line). Daikin offers 5–10 years. In practice, both are reliable—major failure rates are <1% across both brands in BC. Warranty length is a tiebreaker, not a decision-maker.

What matters: does your installer offer their own labor warranty? A 2-year labor guarantee from your installer beats a 10-year parts warranty from the manufacturer.

Bottom Line

Northern BC (Prince George, Fort St. John, Kamloops): Mitsubishi's cold-climate edge justifies the $1,500–$2,000 premium. You'll recoup it in heating savings within 2–3 winters.

Coastal BC (Vancouver, Victoria, Burnaby, Richmond): Daikin's lower price and solid efficiency make sense. The climate doesn't demand Mitsubishi's extreme-cold engineering.

Everywhere else: Let your installer decide. Both are excellent. What you don't want is a great heat pump installed by someone who doesn't know how to maintain it.
