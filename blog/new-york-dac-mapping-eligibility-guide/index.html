Are You in a New York Disadvantaged Community?

If you are, your heat pump rebate could be a lot bigger. Here's how to check, in plain English.

What "Disadvantaged Community" Actually Means

New York State has a formal process for identifying neighborhoods that face higher pollution burdens or economic hardship. These areas are officially designated as Disadvantaged Communities (DAC). If your address falls inside one of these zones, you can qualify for significantly higher rebates through New York's Clean Heat and HEAR/HOMES programs — sometimes up to $14,000 per measure for income-qualified households.

This isn't about judging a neighborhood. It's a technical designation used to direct more funding to areas that need it most, and it's worth checking even if you're not sure whether it applies to you.

The Baseline, Before Any DAC Bonus

If you're a Con Edison customer, the standard Clean Heat rebate for a primary heat source heat pump is around $10,000. A DAC designation, combined with income qualification, can push that number meaningfully higher.

How to Check If You're in a DAC Zone

  1. Use NYSERDA's official mapping tools to check your specific address — start at nyserda.ny.gov and search for the DAC viewer.
  2. Ask your Clean Heat-enrolled contractor to check your address as part of your quote — many can look this up directly during the estimate process.
  3. Cross-reference with income qualification rules, since the biggest rebate boosts typically require both DAC status and income eligibility together.

Where to Read More

For a broader look at how New York's heat pump rebate programs work together: heatpumpnetwork.com and rebateatlas.org.

Bottom Line

Don't assume the standard $10,000 Con Edison figure is your ceiling. Check your address against the DAC map before you finalize a quote — it's a five-minute check that can significantly change your numbers.
