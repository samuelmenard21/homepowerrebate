Oil at $2.16 a litre in St. John's

Oil Heating Bills Are Soaring in Newfoundland. Here Is What You Can Do.

If your home runs on furnace oil, this winter will cost more. Oil in St. John's was almost a dollar a litre higher in early October than a year ago. There are two kinds of help: money now for this winter's oil, and a much bigger incentive to stop buying oil for good. Here is how each one works, in plain words.

How big is the jump?

Canadian Press reported on October 13, 2026 that furnace oil in St. John's cost $2.16 a litre on October 3. That is almost a dollar more than on the same day in 2025. The reason is the war in the Middle East, which pushed world oil prices to a three-year high this year.

Here is what that means for one fill. A standard 900-litre tank cost about $1,950 at that price, or about $2,240 once taxes are added. One St. John's retiree in the story raised her monthly heating budget from $300 to $400 and is cutting back on groceries and travel to make it work.

Prices move every week, so your bill could be higher or lower. But most people should plan for a pricier winter than last year.

Help for this winter: the $500 Home Heating Supplement

The Government of Newfoundland and Labrador pays a Home Heating Supplement of $200 to $500 to people who buy furnace or stove oil for their main home. To qualify:

  • Your 2025 adjusted family income was $150,000 or less.
  • You bought at least 250 litres of furnace or stove oil, and you have the invoices showing your delivery address.
  • You paid for the oil yourself.

Apply online or by mail before January 15, 2027. Sign the form and check that your Social Insurance Number is right, since mistakes slow it down. If you need help, call 1-844-729-4645. The province also asks people to help older parents and neighbours apply, which is good advice. Many people who qualify never send the form.

Help for every winter after: the Oil to Electric rebate

The $500 helps, but it covers less than a quarter of one fill. The bigger lever is getting off oil. The Oil to Electric Incentive Program, run by takeCHARGE with the federal and provincial governments, pays toward switching your heat to electricity:

  • Central heat pump: up to $22,000 if your household is income-qualified, up to $9,000 if not.
  • Multi-split or several mini-splits: up to $18,000 or up to $6,500.
  • Electric furnace or boiler: up to $10,000 or up to $5,000.

Income-qualified means, for example, $73,600 or less for a two-person home or $131,100 or less for four. The program says it can cover up to 100% of the cost, and it pays your installer directly, so you may not need to front the money. It also covers a panel upgrade if needed and removing the old oil tank. Our Oil to Electric rebate guide has the full income chart and rules.

On October 16, 2026 the program page said it is still taking applications and has no closing date. It also says amounts can change with government budgets, so do not wait for a perfect moment.

Why a heat pump makes sense in Newfoundland

Two facts work in your favour. First, takeCHARGE says over 90% of the province's electricity comes from clean, renewable sources, so the switch also cuts your home's emissions. Second, your power price is not tied to world oil markets the way furnace oil is. When a war overseas pushes oil up, your electric bill does not jump with it.

A heat pump moves heat instead of burning fuel, so it gives you more heat for each unit of electricity than a baseboard or electric furnace. On the coldest days it works less well, which is why the program asks for backup heat.

Be honest with yourself about one thing. A heat pump installed this spring will not lower this winter's oil bill. It lowers next winter's and every one after. That is why we suggest doing both: apply for the $500 now, and start the heat pump paperwork now too, so the job can be done before next fall.

The rules that trip people up

  • Whole-home heat. The program pays for heating the whole house. One mini-split in the living room almost never counts. The program says fewer than 1 in 100 homes can do it with a single unit.
  • Backup heat. Central systems need a backup that is sized, wired and turns on by itself. Baseboards are only allowed as backup. The program warns that no backup heat could void your home insurance, so call your insurer first.
  • Participating installers only. Your installer must be on takeCHARGE's list. Ask before you sign anything.
  • Oil records. You need a 12-month statement from the last two years showing at least 500 litres. A tank installed since January 1, 2025 gets extra checking.
  • New homes do not qualify. The home must be your main home, on a permanent foundation and lived in all year.

What about the new federal rebate?

Ottawa announced a National Heat Pump Rebate on October 8, 2026, with up to $10,000 for households below the median income and $2,000 for others, and a launch expected in early 2027. It has not said whether you can combine it with the Oil to Electric program. With oil this expensive and a provincial program already open, most oil-heated homes should not wait for it. Your plan in four steps

  1. Find your oil invoices from 2026 and apply for the Home Heating Supplement before January 15, 2027.
  2. Find your 12-month oil statement and your latest tax assessment, and check the income chart.
  3. Get at least two quotes from participating installers. Our guide to a fair heat pump quote shows what each quote should list.
  4. Apply for Oil to Electric pre-approval before any work starts.

Common questions

How much is furnace oil in St. John's right now?

Canadian Press reported $2.16 a litre on October 3, 2026, almost a dollar more than a year earlier. Prices change weekly, so check with your oil company.

Who gets the $500 Home Heating Supplement in Newfoundland?

Residents who bought at least 250 litres of furnace or stove oil for their main home and had 2025 adjusted family income of $150,000 or less. The amount is $200 to $500. Apply by January 15, 2027.

How much can I get to switch from oil to a heat pump in Newfoundland?

Up to $22,000 for a central heat pump if your household is income-qualified, or up to $9,000 if not, through the Oil to Electric Incentive Program. Mini-split and multi-split systems get up to $18,000 or $6,500.

Is the Oil to Electric program still open?

Yes. On October 16, 2026 takeCHARGE said it is still taking applications and has not set a closing date.

Does a heat pump work in a Newfoundland winter?

Yes, with backup heat. Cold-climate heat pumps keep heating in winter but work less well on the coldest days, which is why the program requires a backup source with a central heat pump.

Should I wait for the federal heat pump rebate?

For most oil-heated homes, no. The federal rebate is expected in early 2027 and its rules are not published. The provincial Oil to Electric program is open now.
