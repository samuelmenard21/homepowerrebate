6 mistakes that get your Ontario heat pump rebate rejected

Up to $12,000 is on the table — and the program's own process has a few steps that are easy to get wrong, in ways that void the whole rebate.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

These aren't hypothetical gotchas — they come directly from the Home Renovation Savings Program's own published application process. Every one of these is avoidable if you know about it before you sign a contract.

1

Skipping mandatory pre-installation approval

The program requires written pre-installation approval before work starts — not after. Homeowners who let a contractor begin work while the paperwork is "in progress" risk forfeiting the rebate entirely, even if the installed system would otherwise qualify.

Fix: Confirm you have written approval in hand before any equipment is removed or installed — not a verbal "you're probably fine."
2

Installing a unit not on the qualified products list

There's no pre-approved brand list — the heat pump must appear on Natural Resources Canada's qualified products list for cold-climate air-source or ground-source heat pumps. A perfectly good heat pump that simply isn't on that specific list gets $0, regardless of efficiency rating.

Fix: Ask your contractor to confirm the exact model number is on the current NRCan list before you commit — it's a two-minute check on their end.
3

Miscalculating your fuel tier

The rebate splits into a lower tier (Enbridge Gas customers switching from gas) and a higher tier (electric, oil, propane, or wood) — up to a 2.5x difference in payout. Homeowners sometimes assume they qualify for the higher tier based on their electricity bill, without realizing their primary heating fuel is what determines the tier, not their electricity provider.

Fix: Confirm which tier you're actually in based on your current heating fuel — not your electricity bill — before budgeting around a specific rebate number.
4

Using a non-participating contractor

The rebate application is filed by the contractor, not the homeowner. If you hire an HVAC contractor who isn't registered as a participating contractor in the program, there's no path to file the claim after the fact — even if the equipment itself qualifies.

Fix: Confirm participating-contractor status before signing any installation contract, not after.
5

Missing the post-installation documentation window

After installation, the contractor has to submit a post-installation application with documentation. Delays or incomplete paperwork on either side can push past processing windows, and appliance-category rebates specifically require receipts within 60 days of purchase — a deadline that's easy to miss if you're not tracking it.

Fix: Ask your contractor when they'll submit the post-installation paperwork, and follow up if you haven't heard back within a couple weeks of the install.
6

Assuming a rental gets purchase-tier pricing

Heat pump rentals are eligible, but at a flat lower rate regardless of your current fuel — up to $2,000 for air-source, $3,000 for ground-source — even if you'd otherwise qualify for the higher purchase tier. Homeowners comparing rental vs. purchase sometimes budget the wrong rebate figure against a rental quote.

Fix: When comparing rental vs. purchase costs, use the rental-specific rebate figure, not your purchase-tier number.

The one-line summary

Every mistake on this list comes down to process, not equipment — get pre-approval in writing, confirm the model is on the qualified list, confirm your fuel tier, use a participating contractor, and don't let paperwork lag. Do those five things and rejection risk drops to nearly zero.
