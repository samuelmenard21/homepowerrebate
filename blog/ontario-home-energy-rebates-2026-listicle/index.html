9 Ontario home energy rebates you can claim in 2026

Ranked by typical payout, from the biggest single rebate down to the smallest free money you're probably not claiming.

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

The Home Renovation Savings Program covers nine categories, and the amounts range from $100 to $12,000. Here they are, ranked by typical payout, so you know where to focus first.

1
$2,000–$12,000

Heat pump

The biggest single rebate. Amount depends on your current fuel (gas vs. electric/oil/propane/wood) and system type (air-source vs. ground-source). See exactly how the tiers work →

2
up to $6,300

Wall, foundation & crawlspace insulation

Part of the assessment-required tier — needs a $600 home energy assessment (refunded) and at least one other qualifying upgrade alongside it.

3
up to $5,000

Solar panels

$1,000/kW, capped at 50% of cost. You have to choose this rebate or net metering — not both. How to decide which fits your home →

4
up to $5,000

Battery storage

$300/kWh, capped at 50% of cost. Stacks with the solar rebate for up to $10,000 combined on the same project.

5
up to $1,250

Attic insulation

No assessment needed — just confirm eligibility, pick a participating contractor, and get it installed. Amount scales with your starting R-value.

6
$100 per opening

Windows & doors

Per rough opening replaced with an ENERGY STAR-rated window or door. Assessment-required tier.

7
$500

Heat pump water heater

Replaces a gas, electric, or oil tank. Pairs naturally with insulation or windows to hit the 2-upgrade minimum.

8
$200–$250

Air sealing

Draftproofing your home's envelope, verified by the before/after energy assessment.

9
$100

Smart thermostat

The smallest rebate, but the easiest — instant discount before you buy, or mail-in after. Compare the eligible models →

Don't forget appliances

A separate track covers appliances ($25–$200 each: heat pump dryer, induction cooktop, washer, fridge, freezer, dishwasher) — not included above since it's per-appliance rather than a single project.

Two of these need an energy assessment

Insulation (wall/foundation), windows & doors, water heater, and air sealing require a home energy assessment first ($600, refunded as part of your rebate) and at least two of those four categories completed together. Everything else on this list just needs an eligibility form and a participating contractor or retailer. Full breakdown of both tracks →
