Ontario's Home Renovation Savings Program, explained

One program name covers nine different rebate categories, split across two very different application tracks. Here's how it actually works.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

If you've searched for Ontario home energy rebates, you've landed on the Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP) — a joint effort between Enbridge Gas, Save on Energy (IESO), and the Ontario government. It covers nine rebates, split into two tracks that work completely differently, and mixing them up is the easiest way to apply for the wrong thing or miss money you're owed.

Track 1: No assessment needed

These five categories only need an eligibility form and a participating contractor or retailer — no home energy assessment required:

CategoryAmount
Heat pump$2,000–$12,000
Solar panelsup to $5,000
Battery storageup to $5,000 (up to $10,000 with solar)
Attic insulationup to $1,250
Smart thermostat$100
Appliances$25–$200 each

This is the track most homeowners use first — it's fast, and several categories (thermostat, appliances) are worth claiming even if you're not planning a bigger renovation.

Track 2: Assessment required

These four categories pay more, but only after a professional home energy assessment confirms your baseline — and you have to bundle at least two of them together:

CategoryAmount
Wall / foundation / crawlspace insulationup to $6,300
Windows & doors$100 per rough opening
Heat pump water heater$500
Air sealing$200–$250

✅ The assessment pays for itself

The assessment costs about $600, but that's refunded as part of your rebate once you complete your upgrades. Do three or more upgrades from this track and you get an extra $500 bonus on top of everything else.

Why the tracks are split this way

The logic: heat pumps, solar, battery, and attic insulation are "point" upgrades — a single contractor visit, easy to verify. Wall insulation, windows, water heaters, and air sealing are usually done together as part of a bigger envelope retrofit, so the program bundles them and uses a professional assessment to confirm the work actually improved your home's efficiency (rather than just trusting a receipt).

Stacking example: a real Toronto retrofit

Say you're switching a gas furnace to an air-source heat pump, adding attic insulation, and doing wall insulation + new windows as part of the same project:

  • Heat pump (Enbridge Gas customer, air-source): up to $2,000
  • Attic insulation (from ≤R-12): up to $1,250
  • Wall insulation: up to $3,600
  • Windows (say, 6 openings): $600
  • 3+ upgrade bonus: $500

That's roughly $7,950 in stacked rebates — before considering city-specific financing like Toronto's HELP loan to cover the rest of the project cost.

How to apply

  1. Decide which track your upgrade falls into (see the tables above)
  2. No-assessment track: fill out the eligibility form, pick a participating contractor/retailer, get pre-approval where required, install, submit documentation
  3. Assessment track: book an initial energy assessment first, get your custom report, complete 2+ upgrades, book a follow-up assessment, submit documentation
  4. Rebates typically arrive within 60 days of approval
