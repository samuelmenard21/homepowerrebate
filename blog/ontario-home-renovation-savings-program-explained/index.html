Ontario Home Renovation Savings Program: 2026 guide

Every rebate amount, who qualifies, and how to apply, checked against the official program site.

By Sam Menard · Updated September 26, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

The Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP, sometimes typed "HRS" or "HERS") is Ontario's main home energy rebate for 2026. It is run by Enbridge Gas and Save on Energy (IESO). It pays up to $7,500 for a cold-climate air source heat pump if you heat with electricity, oil, propane or wood (up to $2,000 if you heat with Enbridge natural gas), up to $10,000 for solar plus a battery, $125 for a smart thermostat, and up to $7,700 for insulation. It does not pay for a new gas furnace.

Who can get the Home Renovation Savings rebates?

You need to own the home. It has to be a detached or semi-detached house, a row house or townhome, or a mobile home on a permanent foundation. Brand-new homes you've lived in for 6 months or less don't count. You also need to be one of these:

  • An Enbridge Gas home customer who heats mainly with a natural gas furnace or boiler, or
  • Connected to Ontario's power grid and heating with electricity, oil, propane or wood.

Which group you're in changes your heat pump amount a lot, so check this first.

Heat pump rebate Ontario 2026: how much?

No home energy assessment is needed for a heat pump. The amount depends on how you heat today and the size of the heat pump (in tons).

How you heat nowCold-climate air sourceGround source
Electricity, oil, propane or wood$1,250/ton, up to $7,500$2,000/ton, up to $12,000
Enbridge natural gas$500/ton, up to $2,000$3,000
Rented heat pump$500/ton, up to $2,000$3,000

So the "up to $12,000" you see in ads is only for a ground source (geothermal) system in a home that doesn't heat with gas. Most gas-heated homes in cities like Toronto or Ottawa will see up to $2,000 for an air source unit. See heat pump rebate tiers explained for more.

Is there an Ontario rebate for a new furnace?

Not through this program. The Home Renovation Savings Program lists heat pumps, not gas furnaces. If your furnace is dying, a cold-climate heat pump (often paired with your furnace as backup) is the upgrade that gets a rebate. If your household income is lower, the free Energy Affordability Program (below) is also worth a look.

Two catches on solar: the battery must go in with new solar (not on its own), and if you take this rebate you can't also sign a net-metering deal with your utility. Read solar rebate vs. net metering before you pick.

Rebates that need a home energy assessment

These pay more. You book an assessment before and after the work. The program gives back $600 toward the assessments, and a $500 bonus if you do three or more upgrades.

UpgradeRebate
Attic insulation (to R-50)$900 to $1,500, based on what's there now
Exterior walls$1,200 to $3,600
Basement walls$900 to $1,500

Insulation adds up to $7,700 in total. Looking for "insulation rebates"? This table is it. Our Ontario insulation guide goes deeper.

Example: a gas-heated Toronto home

  • 3-ton air source heat pump (Enbridge gas customer): $1,500
  • Attic from R-12 to R-50, with assessment: $1,500
  • Exterior walls above R-20: $3,600
  • 6 new windows: $600
  • Assessment rebate: $600, plus 3-upgrade bonus: $500

That's about $8,300 back. Same house heated with electric baseboards? The heat pump alone could be $3,750 instead of $1,500.

How to apply

  1. Check you qualify (owner, home type, gas or electric heating) on homerenovationsavings.ca.
  2. For heat pumps, solar, attic, thermostats and appliances: use a participating contractor or retailer and apply with your receipts. For a thermostat bought yourself, upload the receipt within 60 days.
  3. For walls, windows, basement, air sealing or a heat pump water heater: book the first home energy assessment before any work starts.
  4. Keep invoices showing model numbers and sizes. Missing paperwork is the top reason claims get rejected (see why Ontario rebates get rejected).

Common questions

Is "HERS rebate" the same thing?

Usually, yes. People often mean the Home Renovation Savings Program. Its official short name is HRS.

Can I get the rebate if I rent?

No. The program is for homeowners. Renters with lower incomes can look at the Energy Affordability Program.

Does the program have an end date?

The official site doesn't list one as of September 26, 2026. Amounts can change, so check before you sign a contract.

Can I get a free smart thermostat?

Only through the income-qualified Energy Affordability Program. Everyone else can get $125 back from HRS, plus $75 from Peak Perks if the thermostat runs central air or a heat pump.
