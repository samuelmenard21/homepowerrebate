7 smart thermostats eligible for Ontario's $100 rebate

Every brand accepted by the Home Renovation Savings Program, compared by what actually differs between them — not a made-up star rating.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

A quick honesty note before the list: the official program page doesn't publish pricing or independent ratings for any of these thermostats, so we're not going to invent a 1-through-7 ranking that implies we tested them. What we can do is tell you which one fits your situation, based on real, verifiable differences — heating system compatibility, voice control, and design.

Get your $100 code before you buy

The instant rebate is claimed before checkout, not after — you request a code on the official program site, then use it at Home Depot or Best Buy. Skip the code and you're stuck filing a slower mail-in claim instead.

Best for most homes

ecobee Smart Thermostat

The most complete lineup on the eligible list — five models (Smart Thermostat with voice control, Premium, Enhanced, Essential, and the budget ecobee3 lite), so there's a tier for almost any budget without leaving the brand. Built-in voice control on the flagship model is a genuine differentiator versus most competitors here.

Models: Smart Thermostat (voice), Premium, Enhanced, Essential, ecobee3 lite

See ecobee's lineup →

Best-known name

Google Nest

The most recognizable brand on this list, with the deepest third-party smart home integration (Google Home, most major ecosystems). Three models available under the rebate: the Learning Thermostat (3rd/4th gen), the standard Nest Thermostat, and the older Nest Thermostat E.

Models: Learning Thermostat (3rd/4th gen), Nest Thermostat, Nest Thermostat E

See Google Nest's lineup →

Widest model range

Honeywell Home

The broadest spread of options on the eligible list, from the T5 Gen2 (a simpler, budget-friendly unit) up to the T9 with a Smart Room Sensor for temperature-balancing across rooms — useful if one room in your home always runs hot or cold.

Models: T9 (Wi-Fi), T9 (with Smart Room Sensor), T5 Gen2, X2S, X8S (Black and White)

See Honeywell Home's lineup →

Best budget tiering

Copeland Sensi

Four models spanning a genuine budget-to-premium range within one brand: Sensi Lite at the entry point, up to Sensi Touch 2 at the top. Worth a look if you want the simplest possible option that still qualifies for the rebate.

Models: Sensi Touch Wi-Fi, Sensi Touch 2, Sensi, Sensi Lite

See Copeland Sensi's lineup →

Best minimal design

Mysa

A single model, designed primarily for homes with electric baseboard or in-floor heating rather than central forced-air furnaces — worth checking compatibility with your specific heating system before buying, since it's a narrower fit than the others on this list.

Model: Mysa Smart Thermostat

See Mysa's product page →

Best for hydronic/electric heat

Sinopé

Three models specifically built for central systems, including "Lite" and "Pro" tiers. Like Mysa, this is a Quebec-based brand with particular strength in non-forced-air heating setups — check the model matches your system type before purchasing.

Models: Smart Thermostat Lite (Central System), Pro Lite (Central System), Pro (Central System)

See Sinopé's lineup →

The 7th "option": don't overthink it

If your heating is a standard forced-air furnace and you don't have a strong ecosystem preference, ecobee or Nest cover the widest range of use cases and have the largest installed base for troubleshooting help online. The $100 rebate is the same regardless of which eligible brand you pick, so the deciding factor should be your specific system and smart-home preferences, not the rebate amount.

How the rebate works

You can get the $100 as an instant rebate before you buy through the program portal, or as a mail-in rebate after purchasing from any retailer and submitting your receipt. No pricing is published on the official program page, so compare current retail prices at your preferred retailer before deciding.

The second rebate most people miss: Peak Perks

Separately from the $100 purchase rebate, IESO's Peak Perks program pays $75 for enrolling your smart thermostat and $20 every year after, in exchange for letting the grid make small, automatic temperature adjustments during peak demand windows (June–September). It's opt-in, the adjustments are minor, and you can leave the program anytime — but it's easy to miss because it's enrolled separately from the purchase rebate, not automatically bundled with it. Check the Home Renovation Savings site for current eligibility, since Peak Perks enrollment can close and reopen by season.
