# The $3,500 Mistake: Peak Saver Enrollment and the 14-Day Window

**Published:** July 2026  
**Read time:** 4 min  
**For:** BC homeowners who just got a battery installed (or are about to)

---

## Your battery is installed. Congrats. Here's the gotcha nobody tells you.

You've got solar panels on your roof. A shiny new battery in your garage. BC Hydro sent you a rebate cheque for $5,000. You're done, right?

**Wrong.** If you don't enroll that battery in Peak Saver within 14 days of interconnection approval, your rebate shrinks from $5,000 to $1,500. 

That's a $3,500 mistake. And it happens *constantly* because nobody—not installers, not BC Hydro, not homeowners—talks about the 14-day window until it's too late.

## Here's what changed (April 1, 2026)

Before April 1, battery rebates were straightforward: install a battery, claim up to $5,000.

Starting April 1, 2026, BC Hydro restructured the rebate:

- **$5,000 rebate** — if the battery is enrolled in Peak Saver within 14 days of interconnection approval
- **$1,500 rebate** — if the battery is installed but Peak Saver enrollment is skipped or missed

Same battery. Different payout. One deadline.

## The 14-day clock starts when?

**When BC Hydro approves the interconnection,** not when the battery is physically installed.

Timeline:
1. Installer completes battery installation
2. Installer submits application to BC Hydro for interconnection approval
3. BC Hydro reviews and approves (usually 5–10 business days, can be slower)
4. **14-day clock starts** ← this is the gotcha
5. Battery owner must enroll in Peak Saver before day 15

If you miss day 14, you lose $3,500. BC Hydro won't waive it.

## Why is Peak Saver enrollment even a thing?

Peak Saver is BC's demand management program. During winter peak demand (4–9 PM, roughly), your enrolled battery can discharge to help the grid. In return, you get:

- Higher battery rebate ($5,000 vs. $1,500)
- Seasonal rewards (~$50–$250/year, varies by device type)
- Technically, you're helping BC avoid building more generating capacity

It's not a big ask: your battery discharges a few times a week in winter, you get paid. But the enrollment is *separate* from the battery installation. And if your installer doesn't enroll you, you don't get the rebate.

## Who's responsible for enrollment?

**Your installer should do this.** A good HPCN-certified installer builds Peak Saver enrollment into the handover process.

A bad installer installs the battery, hands you the paperwork, and leaves. You're left staring at a BC Hydro form you don't understand, missing the deadline, losing $3,500.

## How to make sure you don't lose $3,500

**Before you sign the contract:**
- Ask the installer: "Will you enroll my battery in Peak Saver as part of the installation?"
- Get their answer in writing. Literally ask them to put it in the contract.
- Ask them: "What's the timeline for interconnection and enrollment?"

**After interconnection approval (day 1 of the 14-day window):**
- The installer should contact you immediately to say "You've been approved, enrollment starts now"
- They should walk you through the Peak Saver enrollment portal (online at bchydro.com)
- You enroll. Takes 5 minutes.

**If your installer doesn't contact you by day 2 or 3:**
- Call them. Don't wait.
- If they're unresponsive, you can enroll yourself at bchydro.com/peaksaver
- Enroll before day 14 ends.

## But what if I already missed it?

If you installed a battery before April 1, 2026, the old rules apply—you keep the $5,000.

If you installed after April 1 and missed the 14-day window: contact BC Hydro and ask if they'll make an exception. They *might*, but don't count on it.
