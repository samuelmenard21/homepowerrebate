How to Get Pre-Approved Before You Buy

You're in PG&E territory — Berkeley, San Jose, and the rest of the Bay Area. Here's how to get approved before you spend a dollar, so you don't get rejected later.

Why Pre-Approval Matters

PG&E rebates are smaller than some other California utilities. That makes it even more important to do the paperwork right the first time. If you buy first and apply after, you risk losing the rebate entirely.

Honest heads up: PG&E's own heat pump rebate is modest — about $250 to $500. It's real money, but don't expect it to cover your install. We'll show you where the bigger money actually is.

Step-by-Step: Getting Pre-Approved

1. Confirm you're actually a PG&E electric customer

Some Bay Area cities use community choice energy providers for generation but PG&E still delivers the power and runs the rebate programs. Check a recent bill to be sure.

2. Check BayREN first

Before you touch PG&E's own site, check BayREN's incentive finder. BayREN (Bay Area Regional Energy Network) coordinates rebates across PG&E's Bay Area footprint and often has better offers than PG&E alone.

3. Ask about income-qualified programs

If your household income is on the lower end, the Climate Equity Hub can provide a heat pump water heater at no cost. This is separate from the standard rebate and worth checking even if you don't think you'll qualify.

4. Get your written pre-approval before signing a contractor agreement

Both BayREN and PG&E programs generally require pre-approval before installation starts. Get the approval letter or confirmation number in writing before you pay a deposit.

5. Confirm your contractor is enrolled

Rebates through BayREN and PG&E typically require a participating contractor. Ask directly: "Are you enrolled in the BayREN or PG&E rebate program?"

Know Before You Solar-Shop: NEM 3.0

If you're also considering solar, know that NEM 3.0 replaced the older, more generous solar export program. It pays much less for electricity you send back to the grid than the old rules did. This doesn't kill the case for solar, but it changes the math — pairing solar with a home battery now matters more than it used to.

Bay Area SunShares

Bay Area SunShares is a seasonal group-buy program that negotiates discounted installer pricing for solar and heat pumps. It's not a rebate exactly — it's a bulk discount — but it stacks with BayREN incentives.

Bottom Line

PG&E's own rebate is small. The real value is in BayREN, income-qualified programs, and getting pre-approved in writing before you commit to a contractor. Do the paperwork first, buy second.
