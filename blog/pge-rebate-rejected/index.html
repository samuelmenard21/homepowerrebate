My PG&E Rebate Got Rejected. Now What?

Don't panic. Rejections are common and usually fixable. Here's what actually causes them and how to get back on track.

First: You're Not Alone

Rebate rejections happen to a lot of Bay Area homeowners, not just you. Most of the time the reason is simple paperwork, not something wrong with your house. Here are the most common causes, in order of how often we see them.

The Usual Reasons for Rejection

1. Non-approved contractor

PG&E and BayREN programs both require your installer to be enrolled in the rebate network. If your contractor isn't on the list, the rebate gets denied — even if the install itself is perfect. Fix: ask your contractor directly if they're enrolled, or check BayREN's incentive finder for a participating contractor list.

2. You applied after installation instead of before

Most rebate programs require pre-approval before the work starts. If you installed first and applied afterward, the application can be denied on that basis alone. Fix: for any future project, get written pre-approval before signing a contractor agreement.

3. Missing SEER or tonnage documentation

Your rebate application needs exact equipment specs — the SEER (efficiency) rating and tonnage (capacity) of the unit. If your contractor's paperwork is incomplete, the application stalls or gets rejected. Fix: request the equipment spec sheet directly from your contractor and resubmit with it attached.

4. Income-qualified program funding ran out

Some programs are genuinely first-come, first-served. As of 2026, CalEnergy Smart Homes funding has been fully subscribed for the year in many areas — meaning applications are turned away simply because the money ran out, not because of anything you did wrong. Fix: ask BayREN whether the standard (non-income-qualified) track is still open, since it runs on a separate budget.

5. NEM 3.0 confusion (for solar-paired applications)

If your rejection involves a solar component, it may be a mix-up between old NEM 2.0 assumptions and the current NEM 3.0 rules. NEM 3.0 pays less for exported power, and some paperwork templates still reference the old program. Fix: confirm with your installer which NEM version your interconnection application is filed under.

How to Resubmit

  1. Call BayREN or PG&E and ask specifically why the application was denied — get it in writing or by email if possible.
  2. Fix the specific issue (contractor enrollment, missing spec sheet, timing).
  3. Resubmit with a note referencing your original application number.
  4. If funding ran out on an income-qualified track, ask to be placed on a waitlist for the next funding cycle.
