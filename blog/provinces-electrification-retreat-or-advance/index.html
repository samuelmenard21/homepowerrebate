Which Provinces Are Advancing on Electrification — and Which Gave Up

A dollar figure is a snapshot. Trajectory tells you what actually matters: is this program getting bigger, or is it being wound down while nobody's watching?

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

Advancing

British Columbia

Still the most coherent, best-funded program in the country. No signs of retreat — CleanBC remains a single, well-administered system with a realistic ~$35K total stack.

Massachusetts

Mass Save's rebate amounts have grown, not shrunk, and income-qualified tiers now reach $25,000. The processing backlog is a real problem (see our complaints coverage), but the underlying funding commitment is expanding, not contracting.

In retreat

Nova Scotia

The clearest case of a province stepping back. A program that once had a real claim to "best in Canada" closed its core rebate December 31, 2025, with the federal top-up following in July 2026. What's left (up to $5,000 general, $15,000 moderate-income) is real, but it's a program in decline, not growth.

California

HEEHRA and TECH Clean California — the two biggest state-level rebate mechanisms — are both fully reserved and closed to new single-family applicants as of early 2026. On paper California still looks generous. In practice, the money has run out and isn't being replenished at the same pace.

Never really engaged

Alberta and Saskatchewan

These aren't provinces retreating from a program — they never built one. Saskatchewan has no heat pump rebate at all. Alberta's only federal tool (OHPA) drew 16 applications province-wide. There's no trajectory to track because there was never a real starting point.

The takeaway: if you're choosing where to invest based on rebate stability, not just today's number, BC and Massachusetts are the safer long-term bets. Nova Scotia and California both illustrate how fast a "generous" program can become a historical footnote.
