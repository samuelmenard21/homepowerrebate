Smart Thermostat Buying Guide for Massachusetts Homes

Mass Save will often install a smart thermostat for free during your home energy assessment — a detail that gets lost because most homeowners book that assessment for the insulation or heat pump rebate, not realizing the thermostat comes along with it.

This guide covers the model options, real pricing, how the rebate and free-install path actually work, and how to pick the right one.

By Sam Menard · Updated August 2026 · 7 min read

The Real Choices, Straight Up

ecobee Smart Thermostat

Retail price: roughly $130-$280 depending on model. Best for: New England homes with rooms that heat unevenly, which is common in older Massachusetts triple-deckers and colonials with zoned or radiator heat.

ecobee's optional room sensors let the thermostat average temperature readings from multiple rooms instead of just the hallway where it's mounted — useful when a single sensor at the thermostat doesn't reflect how cold the back bedroom actually gets.

Google Nest (Learning Thermostat / Nest Thermostat)

Retail price: roughly $130-$280 depending on model. Best for: homeowners who want automatic scheduling without programming it manually.

Nest builds its own schedule from your actual usage patterns over the first week or two. It's the model Mass Save's free-install contractors carry most often, so if you're going the free-install route during your assessment, Nest availability tends to be more consistent than boutique brands.

How the Mass Save Rebate Applies

Mass Save funds this program uniformly across the state — the $100 rebate is the same in Boston, Worcester, Springfield, and every other Massachusetts city, since it runs through the utility-backed Mass Save program rather than a municipal one.

There are two ways to get it:

  • Free install during your home energy assessment: a qualifying smart thermostat is often installed at no cost as part of the standard Mass Save home assessment, alongside other measures like weatherstripping and LED bulbs.
  • Buy your own, get the $100 rebate: you can still claim the $100 rebate for a qualifying model even if Mass Save didn't install it — this route makes sense if you want a specific brand not offered through the assessment program.

Booking the free home energy assessment is worth doing regardless of which thermostat brand you end up with, since it also surfaces your eligibility for insulation and other Mass Save-funded upgrades in the same visit.

What to Check Before You Buy

  • Ask what brand the assessment contractor installs for free: if you have a strong brand preference, confirm it's covered before booking, or plan to buy separately and claim the mail-in rebate instead.
  • Check your heating system compatibility: older oil or steam-heat systems common in Massachusetts housing stock sometimes need a compatibility check before a smart thermostat swap.
  • Confirm your C-wire situation: older furnaces without a common wire may need an adapter, which the assessment contractor can typically handle on the spot.
  • Bundle the visit: since the assessment also unlocks insulation and heat pump rebate eligibility, book it even if the thermostat alone wouldn't justify the appointment.

What to Ask Before You Book

  1. "Which smart thermostat brand does the free-install program use?"
  2. "Is my heating system compatible, or does it need an adapter?"
  3. "What other upgrades does this assessment visit qualify me for?"
  4. "If I buy my own thermostat, how do I file for the $100 mail-in rebate?"
Booking a Mass Save home energy assessment mainly for the thermostat undersells the visit — the same appointment is your entry point to insulation, heat pump water heater, and other Mass Save rebates. Book it as a whole-home check, not a single-item errand.

The One Number That Matters

A properly scheduled smart thermostat cuts heating costs by roughly 8-12% in a typical Massachusetts home, where heating season runs long and heating costs make up a large share of the annual energy bill. On a $200 unit, the free-install path makes this effectively a no-cost upgrade, and even buying your own and claiming the $100 rebate usually pays back within the first heating season.
