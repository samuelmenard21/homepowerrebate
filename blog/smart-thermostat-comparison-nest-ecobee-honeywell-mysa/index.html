Smart Thermostat Comparison: Nest vs Ecobee vs Honeywell vs Mysa

Which smart thermostat qualifies for BC Hydro's $150 rebate? Heat pump compatibility, Peak Saver integration, and the thermostat that wins for BC.

By Sam Menard · Updated July 2026 · 4 min read

Mysa vs Ecobee, the short answer: Mysa is $130 cheaper after the BC Hydro rebate ($49 vs $179), with the same native Peak Saver support and heat pump compatibility. Ecobee's edge is a longer 5-year warranty and voice-remote room sensing. For most BC heat pump households, Mysa wins on value with no real functional gap — see the full head-to-head below.

Why your thermostat choice matters

If you're installing a heat pump or already have one, your thermostat is doing more than controlling temperature. It's optimizing Peak Saver enrollment (worth $3K+ over 10 years), managing battery charging, and scheduling around time-of-use rates.

A bad thermostat pick could cost you thousands. A great one earns it back in 2–3 years through Peak Saver rewards alone.

Our top recommendations

🏆 Best Overall: Google Nest 4 $149 after rebate

Why: Native Peak Saver, AI learning, room sensors included, and tight integration with Google Home. If you have other Google Home devices, this is the easy choice.

Tradeoff: Expensive upfront ($299), but features justify it. Google's privacy model may concern some users. Data goes to Google servers.

Best for: Heat pump + solar buyers who want the most features and don't mind Google ecosystem.

Tradeoff: No physical room sensors (voice remote is different UX). Integration with solar/battery is fair compared to Mysa.

Peak Saver enrollment bonus: All four thermostats support Peak Saver, but Nest, Ecobee, and Mysa have it built-in. That native support earns you bonuses ($100–500 at signup, then $50–250/year in seasonal rewards). With Honeywell, you'll need third-party middleware—which works but adds friction.

Mysa vs Ecobee: head-to-head

These are the two most common finalists for BC heat pump owners, so here's the direct comparison. Both have native Peak Saver support and strong heat pump compatibility — the differences come down to price, hardware, and ecosystem.

Choose Mysa if you want the lowest upfront cost, you're pairing it with solar + battery (especially Enphase or Sunrun), or you just want a simple, Canadian-made thermostat that does Peak Saver well without extra hardware.

Choose Ecobee if you want a longer warranty, prefer voice-remote room sensing over a sensor-free setup, and don't mind paying more for a more established brand with a longer track record.

For most BC heat pump households, this comes down to budget: Mysa saves $130 upfront with no real functional gap for Peak Saver or heat pump control. Ecobee's extra warranty and voice remote are worth it if you value hands-on room-by-room comfort adjustments.

Mysa vs Nest: head-to-head

Nest is the other common finalist — especially for households already inside the Google Home ecosystem. Both support Peak Saver natively and handle heat pump control well; the split is price, AI-driven scheduling, and how each fits into a solar/battery setup.

Choose Mysa if price and solar/battery integration matter most — it's $100 cheaper after rebate and pairs natively with Sunrun and Enphase systems.

Choose Nest if you want AI-driven learning and room-by-room sensors, or you're already running a Google Home setup and want everything in one app.

Peak Saver is the deciding factor

Here's the math: Peak Saver optimizes when your battery charges and discharges to match BC Hydro's peak hours. It costs you nothing to enroll, but it pays $3K+ over 10 years if your thermostat and battery talk to each other seamlessly.

Native Peak Saver (Nest, Ecobee, Mysa): Automatic enrollment incentives, seasonal rewards, no setup friction.

Third-party Peak Saver (Honeywell): Works, but requires manual integration and you lose out on automation.

If Peak Saver is part of your strategy (and it should be), eliminate Honeywell from your list.

For solar + battery: Mysa or Nest

If you're installing solar AND battery, thermostat integration is critical. Mysa has the strongest ecosystem partnerships (Enphase, Sunrun). Nest has the most features (room sensors, AI learning).

Either choice will optimize battery charging around Peak Saver, time-of-use rates, and solar production. Mysa is more straightforward; Nest offers more control.

The bottom line

  • Best overall: Nest (features) or Mysa (Canadian + cheap)
  • Best value: Ecobee ($179 after rebate, 5-year warranty)
  • Budget: Mysa ($49 after rebate) or Honeywell T9 ($99)
  • Heat pump + battery: Mysa or Nest (native Peak Saver integration)
  • Heat pump only: Any of the four (Ecobee for warranty)

Pro tip: All four qualify for the $150 BC Hydro rebate. The real savings come from Peak Saver integration—pick a thermostat with native support so you don't miss enrollment bonuses and seasonal rewards.
