Mysa vs Ecobee vs Nest (and Cielo, Hoot): Which Thermostat Fits Your Heat?

The right smart thermostat depends on one thing: how your home is heated. Here's how to pick in 30 seconds, plus the free BC Hydro thermostats most people don't know about.

By Sam Menard · Updated September 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Mysa vs Ecobee isn't really a contest. They fit different heating. Electric baseboards need a line-voltage thermostat like Mysa, Sinopé or KING Hoot. Ecobee and Nest can't run them. A furnace or ducted heat pump uses a low-voltage (24V) thermostat like Ecobee, Nest or Mysa for Central HVAC. A ductless mini-split uses a remote-style controller like Mysa for Mini-Split or Cielo. In BC, baseboard homes can get up to five Mysa or Sinopé thermostats free from BC Hydro starting October 2026.

Step 1: What heats your home?

Look at what's on your wall and what warms the room. That tells you which thermostats can work.

Your heating Thermostats that fit Won't work
Electric baseboards or wall heaters
A heater under the window in each room, with its own thick-wired thermostat (120V or 240V)		 Mysa Baseboard, Mysa Baseboard Lite, Sinopé, KING Hoot Ecobee, Nest, Honeywell T-series

One thermostat, air comes out of floor or ceiling vents		 Ecobee, Nest, Honeywell, Mysa for Central HVAC Baseboard (line-voltage) thermostats

Safety note: Never wire a 24V thermostat like Ecobee or Nest to a 240V baseboard heater. It won't work, and it can damage the thermostat. If you're not sure what you have, turn off the breaker and look at the wires. Thick wires and a label like "240V" mean line voltage. If in doubt, ask an electrician.

Mysa vs Ecobee

People compare these two most, but they rarely compete for the same home.

  • Pick Mysa if you have electric baseboards. You need one thermostat per room, and Mysa is built for that. Ecobee can't control baseboards at all.
  • Pick Ecobee if you have a furnace or ducted heat pump. Ecobee Premium ships with a SmartSensor you place in another room, so it can balance hot and cold spots. It needs a C-wire, and Ecobee includes an adapter kit if you don't have one.
  • Have a furnace but like Mysa? Mysa also makes a Central HVAC model for 24V systems. Then it really is a head-to-head, and it comes down to app, looks and price on the day you buy.

Mysa vs Nest

Same story. Nest is a 24V thermostat for furnaces and ducted systems. The Nest Learning Thermostat (4th gen) comes with one Nest Temperature Sensor and learns your schedule. It won't run baseboard heaters. If you're in the Google Home world and have central heat, Nest is an easy pick. If you have baseboards, Mysa or Sinopé is the answer.

Mysa vs Cielo (mini-splits)

Both control a ductless heat pump by sending the same signals as its remote. The main differences:

  • Looks and placement: Mysa is a minimal white unit that can sit on a shelf or mount on the wall. Cielo's Breez Max looks more like a classic wall thermostat with buttons.
  • Smart home: Mysa works with Apple Home, Alexa, Google and SmartThings, and its app has no subscription.
  • Check first: Many heat pump brands sell their own Wi-Fi adapter. It talks to the unit directly and can show more detail than a remote-style controller. Ask your installer before you buy either.

Mysa vs Hoot (baseboards)

KING's Hoot is a Wi-Fi baseboard thermostat for 120V, 208V and 240V heaters. Before you buy, check your wiring against the model's specs, because not every model fits every setup. In BC, there's one big difference. Hoot isn't on BC Hydro's Peak Saver thermostat list. Mysa and Sinopé are. That list is what earns you the $100 sign-up credit and the $50 yearly reward below.

BC homeowners: don't pay for a baseboard thermostat this fall

Free thermostats from BC Hydro: Starting October 2026, BC Hydro residential customers with electric baseboard heat can order up to five free smart thermostats from Mysa or Sinopé, worth about $350 per home, with free shipping. They arrive already signed up for Peak Saver. Register on BC Hydro's page to get notified.

A few things worth knowing:

  • Peak Saver pays you each winter. Enrolled thermostats earn a $50 reward per season, or $100 if you're income-qualified. During peak events (four hours max, November to March), BC Hydro may lower your heat by a couple of degrees. You can override any event. Skip more than half of them and you may lose that season's reward.
  • Already bought your own? If it's an eligible model (Mysa or Sinopé baseboard), enrolling it in Peak Saver earns a $100 one-time credit plus the $50 seasonal reward. That's the "up to $150" you may have seen. It's a bill credit, not money off the price.
  • Furnace or ducted heat pump? BC Hydro's thermostat offers are for electric line-voltage heating. An Ecobee or Nest on a furnace doesn't qualify, so pick on features, not rebates.
  • Kelowna, Penticton or another FortisBC area? If FortisBC sends your electric bill, BC Hydro programs don't apply to you. Check FortisBC's offers instead.

How much will a smart thermostat save?

BC Hydro says smart thermostats cut the electricity used for space heating by about 9% on average, and up to $150 a year when they run baseboards well. That's real money on a winter bill. Baseboards can be up to half of a home's electricity use.

Here's the honest part. A thermostat makes baseboards run smarter, not cheaper to run. If you have baseboards and plan to stay in your home, a ductless heat pump is the bigger win. It often uses half the electricity or less for the same heat, and it adds air conditioning. BC Hydro's heat pump rebate is up to $4,000 for whole-home systems. Get the free thermostats now. Then decide on a heat pump with real numbers for your home.

The bottom line

  • Electric baseboards: Mysa or Sinopé. In BC, get them free from BC Hydro this October.
  • Furnace or ducted heat pump: Ecobee (includes a room sensor), Nest (learning and Google Home) or Mysa for Central HVAC.
  • Ductless mini-split: Mysa for Mini-Split or Cielo. First, ask your installer about your heat pump brand's own Wi-Fi adapter.
  • Biggest savings: replacing baseboards with a heat pump, not the thermostat.

Sources, checked September 26, 2026: BC government release on free smart thermostats (July 6, 2026), BC Hydro Peak Saver device enrollment, BC Hydro smart thermostat fact sheet, and manufacturer product pages. Programs change, so confirm on the official pages before you buy.
