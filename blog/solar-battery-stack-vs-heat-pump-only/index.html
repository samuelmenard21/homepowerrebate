Solar + Battery vs Heat Pump Only: What BC Homeowners Actually Save

Heat pump cuts heating bills 25–45%. Solar + battery adds outage resilience and grid independence, but costs $25K–$40K more upfront. Here's the real math on which combo makes sense for your home.

TL;DR

Start with insulation + heat pump ($8K–$14K net after rebates). That's your biggest payback in years 1–5. Add solar + battery only if: (1) you want outage backup, (2) you live on time-of-use electricity rates, or (3) you're building net-zero as a long-term lifestyle choice. Heat pump alone is the financially smart first move.

The Regional Breakdown

Coastal BC (Vancouver, Victoria, Richmond)

Mild winters mean heat pump ROI is slower (25–30% savings vs gas). Solar payback is 13–16 years. Battery makes sense only if you value outage backup (storm season is real on the coast).

Recommendation: Heat pump + insulation first. Solar is optional, battery even more so unless you're in a flood zone.

Interior BC (Kelowna, Kamloops, Vernon)

Colder winters (4,700–5,200 heating degree days) = higher heating savings. Heat pump payback drops to 2–3 years. Solar is decent (3.8–4.0 sun hours/day). Battery makes more sense here because Peak Saver enrollment pays $500 + $250/season bonus on top of avoiding grid peak pricing.

Recommendation: Heat pump first (fastest payback). Solar is worth considering. Battery if you want the Peak Saver rebate stream.

Northern BC (Prince George, Fort St. John)

Extreme winters (6,100+ heating degree days) mean massive heating savings (40–45%). Heat pump payback is fastest in BC (1.5–2.5 years). Solar is lower potential (2.9–3.0 sun hours/day), but battery for outage resilience during winter storms is genuinely valuable.

Recommendation: Heat pump absolutely. Solar only if you have south-facing roof. Battery yes—outages are real in winter.

Why Heat Pump First, Always

Heat pumps cut heating bills year-round, immediately. A 5-ton system handles 80–90% of your heating needs, even in Prince George winters. You see the savings from month one.

Solar generates power only during daylight (less relevant in winter when you need heat most). Battery sits idle most days unless you're on time-of-use rates or experience frequent outages.

Financially: heat pump payback is 2–4 years. Solar is 11–16 years. Battery pushes it to 15+ years. The math is clear.

When to Add Solar + Battery

You want solar if:

  • You're already planning a 15+ year retrofit (want everything at once)
  • Your roof is south-facing and unshaded
  • You want to be grid-independent (ego, not economics)
  • BC Hydro rates are climbing and you expect them to accelerate

You want battery if:

  • Your area experiences regular outages (storm-prone coastal, rural Northern BC)
  • You're on time-of-use rates and can shift load to cheap hours
  • You're in BC Hydro's Peak Saver program (the $500 + $250/season bonus is real money)
  • You value resilience more than ROI (legitimate if you work from home)

The Tesla Powerwall Exception

Don't use Tesla Powerwall in BC. It's not on BC Hydro's approved battery list, so you get zero rebate ($0 instead of $3,000–$5,000). Eguana Evolve, LG Chem, and Sonnen are all approved and cheaper anyway. Not worth it.

Real Talk: Sequence Matters

Priority Install Payback Do it when?
1 Insulation + air sealing 3–5 years First. Makes heat pump 30% smaller and cheaper.
2 Heat pump 2–4 years Second. Biggest ROI. Do it in year 1.
3 Solar panels 11–16 years Year 3+. Only if heating is solved.
4 Battery storage 15+ years (economic), priceless (resilience) Year 5+. Or skip entirely if you're budget-conscious.

Bottom Line

Heat pump alone saves more money faster. Solar + battery feel aspirational and offer genuine resilience value, but they don't pencil out financially until year 10+.

If you've got $15K, spend it on insulation + heat pump. If you've got $40K, do insulation + heat pump + solar. If you've got $50K+ and you value outages prevention more than ROI, add the battery.

Don't let perfect (net-zero house with backup power) be the enemy of good (heat pump that cuts your heating bills by 30% in year one).
