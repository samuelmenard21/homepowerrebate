## Most BC homeowners don't know they can stack rebates.

They see the solar rebate ($5,000), maybe they know about heat pump rebates ($4,000), but they don't realize you can claim both. Add water heaters, income-qualified bonuses, and multi-upgrade incentives, and a single retrofit can return $15,000–$22,000 in government rebates.

Most people never ask because nobody tells them it's possible.

Here's how it works, with real numbers.

## The stacking rule

BC Hydro, BC CleanBC, and federal programs are designed to *stack*—you can claim rebates from multiple programs for the same project, as long as each program covers different upgrades.

Example: you can't claim two rebates for one solar panel. But you can claim:
- BC Hydro rebate for solar
- BC Hydro rebate for battery
- BC Hydro rebate for heat pump
- BC Hydro rebate for water heater heat pump
- CleanBC rebate for insulation or other upgrades
- Federal CGHAP (coming to BC soon) for additional retrofits

All in one year. All on one house.

## Real example: Metro Vancouver homeowner (BC Hydro customer)

Let's run the numbers for a typical middle-class BC homeowner:

**The project:**
- Install 8 kW solar system (roof + electrical upgrades)
- Install battery (5 kWh, Peak Saver enrolled)
- Replace oil furnace with cold-climate heat pump
- Upgrade to heat pump water heater
- Air sealing + insulation improvements

**Rebates claimed:**

| Upgrade | Program | Rebate | Requirement |
|---------|---------|--------|-------------|
| Solar (8 kW) | BC Hydro | $5,000 | HPCN contractor |
| Battery (5 kWh) | BC Hydro | $5,000 | Peak Saver enrolled within 14 days |
| Heat pump (whole-home) | BC Hydro | $4,000 | HPCN contractor, -5°C coverage |
| Water heater | BC Hydro | $1,000 | ENERGY STAR heat pump model |
| Insulation | Federal CGHAP* | $2,000 | Participating retrofit contractor |
| Multi-upgrade bonus | BC Hydro | $2,000 | 3+ upgrades in same year |
| **TOTAL** | | **$19,000** | — |

*CGHAP is currently being rolled out to BC; timing TBD. Once live, the rebate structure for insulation/windows will expand options.

## Timing considerations

All these rebates are processed through different pipelines:

- **BC Hydro rebates:** Apply after installation, receive cheque within 30–45 days
- **CleanBC rebates:** Apply after work is complete; processing time varies (4–8 weeks)
- **Federal CGHAP:** Will be online by late 2026 / early 2027; expect 6–12 week processing

The strategy: **Do all upgrades in the same year.** This maximizes the multi-upgrade bonus and ensures all rebates process in the same tax year.

## The income-qualified play (huge money)

If your household income is under ~$95,000 (varies by family size, indexed annually), you unlock CleanBC's income-qualified stream:

| Upgrade | Standard rebate | Income-qualified rebate | Difference |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------------|-----------|
| Heat pump (whole-home) | $4,000 | $16,000 | +$12,000 |
| Insulation | Up to $5,500 (CGHAP) | 100% covered (CGHAP) | Full cost |
| Water heater | $1,000 | $2,500 | +$1,500 |

For a household making $90,000/year, the same project could return **$28,000–$35,000** in rebates.

Most homeowners don't check if they qualify. They assume it's just for low-income households. It's not—the income thresholds go up to ~$185,000 for a family of four in the highest tier.

## What could go wrong (and how to avoid it)

**1. Missing the Peak Saver 14-day window**
- Cost: $3,500 (battery rebate drops from $5k to $1.5k)
- Avoid: Use an HPCN installer who handles enrollment

**2. Installer isn't HPCN-certified**
- Cost: $5,000 + $1,000 (solar + battery rebates void)
- Avoid: Before signing, ask "Are you HPCN-certified?" Get it in writing

**3. Choosing a non-approved heat pump**
- Cost: $4,000 rebate void
- Avoid: Use an installer with the approved heat pump list; they should know this

**4. Installing a Tesla Powerwall in BC**
- Cost: $5,000 battery rebate void
- Avoid: Tesla is not on BC Hydro's approved battery list (this keeps coming up)

**5. Claiming rebates from overlapping programs**
- Cost: Clawback or denied claim
- Avoid: Work with an installer who knows which programs layer and which don't

## The stacking checklist

Before you commit to a multi-upgrade project:

- [ ] Installer is HPCN-certified (confirmed in writing)
- [ ] All equipment is on BC Hydro's approved product lists
- [ ] Heat pump is rated to -5°C (whole-home coverage) or -5°C for partial
- [ ] Battery is Peak Saver-compatible (Eguana, SolarEdge, LG, others—not Tesla)
- [ ] You've checked income-qualification thresholds for your household size
- [ ] Installer has agreed to handle Peak Saver enrollment within 14 days
- [ ] You're doing all upgrades in the same calendar year (to maximize stacking)

## The real question: is it worth it?

For most BC homeowners, yes. A full-home retrofit (solar + battery + heat pump) costs $30,000–$45,000. Getting back $19,000–$35,000 in rebates means you're paying $10,000–$15,000 out of pocket for:

- A home that survives winter outages

- Peace of mind that's hard to price

**Payback:** 3–5 years for middle-income homes, often close to free for income-qualified households.
