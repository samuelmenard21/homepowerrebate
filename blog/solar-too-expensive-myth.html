Myth vs. Reality

"Solar is too expensive. I can't afford it right now."

By Sam Menard

I hear this one constantly. Someone gets a quote for $45K and thinks: "That's a down payment on a house. I'm out."

Here's what they didn't see: the actual cost *after* BC's rebates, financing, and ten years of lower power bills.

What the Quote Doesn't Show

When a contractor gives you a $45K solar quote, that's the gross cost. But in BC, you don't pay the gross cost. You pay what's left after rebates take their cut.

BC Hydro's solar rebate is $5,000. CleanBC's solar grant is another $4,000 (if you're income-qualified). That's $9,000 off right there—before financing.

Real example: A typical 6kW system in Vancouver costs ~$45,000 gross. After rebates? $36,000 net. That's 20% off. And that's before you factor in financing or Peak Saver credits.

The Real Monthly Cost (With Financing)

Most homeowners don't pay cash. They finance. And this is where the math gets interesting.

A $36,000 net cost (post-rebates) financed over 10 years at 6.5% comes to about $380/month.

Meanwhile, that same homeowner's BC Hydro bill drops by $120–$160/month (depending on city and consumption). Add Peak Saver rewards (~$25/month over 10 years), and the real monthly cost is closer to $220–$260.

That's what homeowners actually pay. Not $380. The credit from lower bills covers the rest.

But Wait—There's a Home Value Lift

Most installers gloss over this: homes with solar sell for 3-4% more. On a $700K home, that's $21K–$28K in added resale value.

You're not just breaking even on the investment. You're coming out ahead *and* getting 10 years of lower bills.

The actual cost to own solar in BC: Your financing payment minus the hydro bill reduction you get. Not the quote price. Not even the post-rebate price. The financing minus your savings.

Why Installers Show You the Wrong Number

I've seen it happen: an installer gives you a $45K quote and never mentions rebates until you ask. Why? Because rebates come from the government and utilities, not from them. Their job is to show gross price, explain financing, and move on.

The problem: homeowners see $45K and think "I can't afford this." They never do the math on rebates + monthly savings. They just walk away.

Information Is the Real Barrier

I've worked with hundreds of BC homeowners now. The ones who say "solar is too expensive" are usually the ones who haven't calculated their actual net cost yet. Once they do, most say yes.

The sticker shock is real. But it's the starting price—before rebates, before savings, before the home value bump.

One More Thing: Income Qualification

Some BC rebates are income-qualified (like CleanBC). If you earn under $110K household income, you qualify for bigger grants. If you're in the $110K–$165K range, you get standard rebates. Over $165K, you still get the BC Hydro rebate, just not the CleanBC grants.

Either way, you're getting help from the province.

Your next step: Get your actual rebate amount. Don't ask an installer. Ask someone who specializes in rebates. Your real cost is the quote minus rebates minus your 10-year savings. That's the number that matters.
