How to Stack BC Rebates in 2026: A Real, Itemized Example

By Sam Menard · Updated July 2026 · 3 min read

Heads up: the federal Canada Greener Homes Grant and interest-free Loan are both closed — the Grant since February 2024, the Loan since October 2, 2025. This example uses only programs that are open in 2026. If you're still counting on federal money, read what actually replaced Greener Homes first.

"Can I combine more than one rebate?" has a genuinely useful answer: often, yes — because the open programs cover different upgrades. Here's a real, itemized 2026 example instead of a vague "it depends."

Why they stack

BC Hydro's rebates cover solar panels and home batteries for BC Hydro customers specifically. CleanBC / Better Homes BC rebates cover the retrofit side — heat pumps, insulation, windows and doors, and heat pump water heaters — and are funded provincially rather than by your utility. Because these are different upgrades funded by different bodies, you can generally claim across both for the same project.

A worked example: Metro Vancouver home

Say you're going solar, swapping a gas furnace for a heat pump, and topping up your attic insulation in the same year:

BC Hydro solar rebate+$5,000
BC Hydro battery rebate+$5,000
CleanBC heat pump (from gas)+$6,000
CleanBC attic insulation+$2,500
Total rebates claimed$18,500

The exact figures depend on your fuel source, home type, and household income — these are current program maximums applied to one realistic project, not a guaranteed payout.

What's actually open in 2026

CleanBC heat pump$4,000–$16,000
Insulationup to $5,500
Windows & doorsup to $2,000
BC Hydro solar$5,000
Heat pump water heater$1,000–$3,000
ECAP free install (income-qualified)Free heat pump / insulation

The higher heat pump and water heater amounts, and the free ECAP install, are income-qualified — they scale up (or become free) for lower-to-median income households. If your income qualifies, your home evaluation checks this automatically; you don't chase a separate form.

Watch for this: income-qualified is either/or

If you go through ECAP for a free heat pump or insulation install, that's the program paying for the work — you don't also pocket the standard CleanBC rebate for the same unit on top. The standard rebates in the worked example above are for households paying for the upgrade themselves. This is the one place people get their expectations wrong.

The practical order of operations

  1. Confirm your utility (BC Hydro or FortisBC) — this determines your solar and battery options
  2. Get your solar + battery quote and BC Hydro rebate locked in first
  3. Book a home energy evaluation for the CleanBC side — heat pump, insulation, windows — which also flags any income-qualified top-ups you're owed
  4. Apply for each program on its own paperwork — BC Hydro and CleanBC don't share an application

Always confirm current amounts and stacking rules before starting work — rebate programs get updated, and income-qualified tiers change the numbers. Verify at betterhomesbc.ca and with BC Hydro before you commit.
