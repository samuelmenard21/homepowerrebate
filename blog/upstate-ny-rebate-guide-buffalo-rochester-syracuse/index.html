Your Rebate Guide: Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse, Albany

You're not in NYC. You're on National Grid, and you deal with real winter. Here's your rebate picture.

Upstate NY Winters Are No Joke

Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse, and Albany sit in some of the coldest parts of our entire dataset — roughly 6,200 to 7,000 heating degree days a year, with Buffalo also dealing with heavy lake-effect snow. Whatever heat pump you buy here needs to be properly sized and genuinely cold-climate rated. This isn't a place to cut corners on equipment.

What NYSERDA Offers

  • Heat pump rebate: $5,000 or more — the exact 2026 figure is still being finalized in some cases, so confirm the current number with your contractor or NYSERDA directly before budgeting around it.
  • Heat pump water heater rebate: around $800
  • Insulation rebate: around $2,000
  • Smart thermostat rebate: around $50
Heads up: the "Total Home Comfort" program has closed to new applications, and National Grid's upstate programs are currently restructuring. Don't assume an older program name you've heard about is still open — check current program status before applying.

Why Equipment Choice Matters More Here Than Almost Anywhere

With heating degree days this high, an undersized or non-cold-climate-rated heat pump will struggle in a real upstate winter and lean heavily on backup electric heat, which costs more to run. Ask your installer specifically for a cold-climate-rated unit and request the rated output at low outdoor temperatures, not just the standard test number.

How to Apply

  1. Check current program status and rebate amounts at nationalgridus.com.
  2. Cross-check available incentives at amplifyincentives.com.
  3. Confirm your contractor is enrolled in current NYSERDA and National Grid programs, since older program names may no longer apply.
