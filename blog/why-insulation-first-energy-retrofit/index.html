Quick quiz: how insulation works

Ten questions on the technology. Tap an answer to see why.

1. What does an R-value measure?

A higher R-value slows heat loss more.

2. In many older homes, where is a good place to start?

Warm air rises and escapes through the top of the house, and attics are often the easiest place to add insulation.

3. What does air sealing do?

Moving air carries heat. Sealing gaps before adding insulation makes the insulation work better.

4. What is a blower door test?

The fan pulls air out, and gauges show how fast outside air leaks in.

5. Which type of insulation is blown into an attic as loose fill?

Loose fill spreads over uneven spaces and around pipes and wires.

6. Before adding insulation, what should you fix first?

Wet insulation stops working and can grow mould.

7. After you air seal a home tightly, it also needs:

A tighter home holds moisture and stale air unless fresh air comes in on purpose, often through a ventilator.

8. Does adding insulation help in summer?

Insulation slows heat in both directions.

9. What can a home energy assessment tell you?

It ranks the fixes so your first dollar goes furthest.

10. Which is a common leak in an attic floor?

Small holes where things pass through the ceiling let warm air into the attic.

Why Insulation First? The Retrofit Sequence That Saves Most

Doing upgrades in the right order can mean a smaller heat pump and fewer do-overs. Here's why insulation should come before heat pumps and solar.

By Sam Menard · Updated July 2026 · 4 min read

Most homeowners get this wrong. They see the big rebates for heat pumps and solar, so they jump straight there. But this creates two expensive mistakes:

  1. Oversized heat pump cost: If your home leaks air like a sieve, you'll install a massive (expensive) heat pump to heat it. Fix the leaks first, and you need a smaller (cheaper) heat pump.
  2. Wasted solar efficiency: If your home doesn't hold heat well, you'll run heating year-round. Solar doesn't offset heating much. But an insulated + heat-pump home runs minimal heating, so solar savings appear immediately.

The right sequence? Insulation → Heat Pump → Solar.

Why order matters

  • A smaller heat pump. A heat pump replaces the heat your home loses. Cut the loss first, and a proper heat-loss calculation will call for a smaller system, which usually costs less to buy and run.
  • No oversizing. A heat pump sized for a leaky house can be too big once you seal and insulate. Oversized units cycle on and off more and can feel less comfortable.
  • Cheaper savings first. Air sealing and attic insulation are usually the cheapest way to cut heat loss.

BC rebates for doing it in this order

For electrically heated BC homes, BC Hydro's home renovation rebates cover insulation (up to $5,500), heat pumps (up to $4,000), windows and doors (up to $2,000) and heat pump water heaters (up to $1,000), with a bonus when you do more than one upgrade. Insulation and heat pump work must be done by an HPCN contractor. Read the attic insulation guide for how the insulation rebate is calculated.

Source, checked September 26, 2026: BC Hydro home renovation rebates

CleanBC Prefers This Order Too

The federal Greener Homes Grant closed to new applicants in 2024, so it no longer factors into sequencing decisions — but the logic that made insulation-first the right call still holds under CleanBC and BC Hydro's current rebate structure. Doing insulation first gives you:

  • A better EnerGuide score, if you get one done
  • A correctly sized heat pump, which still qualifies for the same rebates
  • Faster overall retrofit — efficiency work first avoids re-sizing or redoing equipment later

The math rewards reducing energy needs before adding renewable generation. Insulation is the heavyweight champion of that math, whether or not a federal grant is in the picture.

When to Skip Straight to Heat Pump

One exception: If your home is already well-insulated , your audit will tell you. In that case, go straight to heat pump + solar. Don't waste money on insulation that won't move the needle.

But this is rare. Most homes built before 2010 have significant air sealing or insulation opportunities.

The Winning Sequence (In Order)

  1. Energy assessment - Identifies what needs fixing
  2. Air sealing + attic/basement insulation - Cuts heat loss at the source
  3. Heat pump (space heating) - Now sized correctly, cheaper to run
  4. Heat pump water heater - Pairs well with space heating heat pump
  5. Solar + Battery - On an efficient home, these pay off fast
  6. EV charging + smart thermostat - Optimization layer on top of a solid foundation

💡 Pro tip: Ask your auditor for the priority list

A professional energy audit tells you exactly what to do and in what order. It's not guesswork—it's data-driven. Many CleanBC and utility programs reimburse the audit cost after you complete the retrofit.

What If You're Impatient?

If you just want to install a heat pump this year and worry about insulation later, go for it. The heat pump still saves money and reduces carbon. You'll just have a slightly oversized system and slower payback. You can always add insulation later and replace it with a smaller heat pump in 10 years (not ideal, but possible).

But if you can wait 6 months, the right sequence saves you real money.
