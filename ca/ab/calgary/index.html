Home Rebates in Calgary

Alberta has no provincial home rebates in 2026, and Calgary's own financing program is paused. That makes the free Home Upgrades program and a few small checks the main help for Calgary's 502,305 households.

What is open for Calgary homes in 2026

We re-read each program page on October 3, 2026. Alberta has no provincial home rebates, so the help is local financing and a few small offers.

Open

Calgary CEIP financing

Up to $50,000

A loan repaid on your property tax bill over up to 20 years. Intake is closed until winter 2026/2027. This is financing, not a rebate.

Open

Calgary Home Upgrades

Free

Free home upgrades for income-qualified homeowners and renters, delivered for the City by Kambo Energy Group. Applications are on hold right now.

Open

Resilient roofing rebate

Ended

The $3,000 hail-resistant roofing rebate ended in 2022 and has not been reinstated.

Open

Utility rebates

None confirmed

We found no primary source for a current ENMAX, ATCO, EPCOR or FortisAlberta heat pump or solar rebate, so we show none. Ask your utility before you buy.

Open

Federal Oil to Heat Pump Affordability

Closed

The federal program for oil-heated homes stopped taking Alberta applications after July 31, 2026.

Open

Canada Greener Homes Grant and Loan

Closed

The grant's document deadline was December 31, 2025 and the loan cannot approve new applications.

Open

Greener Homes Affordability Program

Not in Alberta

Alberta is not among the provinces taking part, as of August 31, 2026.

A house-and-owner city

The 2021 Census counts 502,305 households in Calgary. About 55% live in detached houses, 10% in row homes and 6% in semi-detached homes, and 69% own. Only 8% live in buildings of five or more storeys. That mix suits the programs Alberta does have, which are written around a homeowner who can repay a loan on the property tax bill.

How CEIP works, and why it is paused

Calgary's Clean Energy Improvement Program lends up to $50,000, up to 100% of project cost, repaid on your property tax bill over up to 20 years. The City estimates a rate of 6.00% to 6.10%. If you sell, the new owner can take over the repayment or you can pay it off. Intake is closed until winter 2026/2027, so plan quotes now and apply when it reopens. Nothing here is a rebate, and the rate is what you will pay, so compare it with your bank's.

Free help for lower incomes

Calgary Home Upgrades gives income-qualified homeowners and renters free upgrades, delivered for the City by Kambo Energy Group. The program is on hold, so check its page for the current intake before you apply. Renters are included, which matters in a city where about a third of households rent.

What ended

The federal oil-to-heat-pump program stopped taking Alberta applications after July 31, 2026, and the Greener Homes grant and loan are closed. Alberta is not among the provinces in the Greener Homes Affordability Program. If a contractor quotes a heat pump price including a federal rebate, ask for the program name and the date.

Older homes and winters

About 35% of Calgary's homes were built before 1981, and about 4.5% need major repairs. January lows average -11.9°C, and the coldest day in our records reached -39.4°C. In a cold city with an older minority of homes, insulation and air sealing come before any equipment swap. See our Calgary insulation page.

What to do in Calgary

  1. Check whether your income qualifies for Calgary Home Upgrades and apply if it does.
  2. Get quotes now so you are ready when CEIP reopens in winter 2026/2027.
  3. Ask each quote to name any rebate it assumes and the date it applies.
  4. Check your utility for new offers before you sign.
  5. Keep invoices for any future program.

Common questions

Does Alberta have a home rebate program?

Not at the provincial level in 2026. Calgary offers financing and a free upgrade program for lower incomes.

Is Calgary's CEIP open?

No. Intake is closed until winter 2026/2027, and it is financing repaid on your property tax bill.

Who qualifies for Calgary Home Upgrades?

Income-qualified homeowners and renters. The program is currently on hold, so check its page.

Is there a federal heat pump rebate in Alberta?

No. The oil-to-heat-pump program stopped taking Alberta applications after July 31, 2026.

Does ENMAX have a rebate?

We found no primary source for a current ENMAX heat pump or solar rebate.
