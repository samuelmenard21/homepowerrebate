Home Rebates in Edmonton

Edmonton has no cash home rebate program in 2026. The City's CEIP financing is the main help, and EPCOR pays a small amount for joining its thermostat pilot. Edmonton has 396,395 households and some of the coldest winters of any big Canadian city.

What is open for Edmonton homes in 2026

We re-read each program page on October 3, 2026. Alberta has no provincial home rebates, so the help is local financing and a few small offers.

Open

Edmonton CEIP financing

$3,000 to $50,000

Financing of $3,000 to $50,000, up to 100% of cost, at a fixed 6.00% in the current tranche, repaid on your property tax bill. The program is at capacity for now, so check its page for the next intake.

Open

EPCOR Peak Rewards pilot

$50, then $25 a season

A $50 prepaid Mastercard for enrolling (one per household) plus $25 for each season you take part, for homes with a qualifying smart thermostat.

Open

Utility rebates

None confirmed

We found no primary source for a current ENMAX, ATCO, EPCOR or FortisAlberta heat pump or solar rebate, so we show none. Ask your utility before you buy.

Open

Federal Oil to Heat Pump Affordability

Closed

The federal program for oil-heated homes stopped taking Alberta applications after July 31, 2026.

Open

Canada Greener Homes Grant and Loan

Closed

The grant's document deadline was December 31, 2025 and the loan cannot approve new applications.

Open

Greener Homes Affordability Program

Not in Alberta

Alberta is not among the provinces taking part, as of August 31, 2026.

Edmonton's housing

Edmonton's 396,395 households are 64% owners and 36% renters. About 50% live in detached houses, 10% in row homes and 7% in semi-detached homes, and only 7% in buildings of five or more storeys. A CEIP loan fits the owners, and the renter share is large enough that tenants should ask landlords about loans for building upgrades.

How the CEIP loan compares

Edmonton's CEIP lends $3,000 to $50,000, up to 100% of project cost, at a fixed 6.00% in the current tranche. It is repaid on the property tax bill and passes to the next owner if you sell. At 6.00% it is not cheap money, so compare it with your bank line of credit. Its advantage is that it can cover the whole job and that the debt stays with the house.

The EPCOR thermostat pilot

EPCOR's Peak Rewards pilot pays a $50 prepaid Mastercard for enrolling, one per household, plus $25 per season. It asks you to allow small adjustments to a smart thermostat at peak times. It is small, but it is the only cash offer we found for Edmonton households.

Cold winters

Edmonton's average January low is -14.3°C and the coldest day in our records is -41.2°C. About 41% of homes were built before 1981. In weather like this, sealing and insulating usually comes before choosing a heat pump. See our Edmonton insulation page and heat pump page.

What ended

The federal oil-to-heat-pump program stopped taking Alberta applications after July 31, 2026, and the Greener Homes grant and loan are closed. Alberta is not in the Greener Homes Affordability Program.

What to do in Edmonton

  1. Get quotes that name any rebate they assume.
  2. Compare the CEIP rate of 6.00% with your own financing.
  3. Check the City's CEIP page for the next intake.
  4. If you have a smart thermostat, ask EPCOR about the Peak Rewards pilot.
  5. Keep invoices for any future program.

Common questions

Does Edmonton have home rebates?

No cash rebates beyond EPCOR's small thermostat pilot. The City offers CEIP financing.

What is Edmonton's CEIP rate?

A fixed 6.00% in the current tranche, for $3,000 to $50,000, repaid on your property tax bill.

How much does EPCOR pay for the thermostat pilot?

$50 on enrolling and $25 per season, one enrollment per household.

Is there a federal heat pump rebate?

No. The oil-to-heat-pump program stopped taking Alberta applications after July 31, 2026.

Is CEIP open right now?

It is at capacity for now. Check the City's page for the next intake.
