Alberta Home EV Charger Rebates in 2026

If you own a house in Alberta and want a charger in your garage, we could not find any rebate you can apply for today. We checked the federal, city and utility programs. Here is what we found, what closed, and how to keep the cost down anyway.

What we checked for Alberta home chargers

Each card names the official page we read: October 3, 2026, and EPCOR on October 16, 2026.

Closed

Calgary ChargeYYC

Closed; never for houses

For buildings with 3 or more units on a shared electrical service: up to $6,000 per stall for wiring and up to $1,000 or $2,000 per charger. Phase 2 applications closed November 18, 2025. Detached houses were never eligible.

Closed

Federal ZEVIP

$0 for homeowners

Natural Resources Canada funds chargers in public places, workplaces, fleets and multi-unit buildings, at 50% of costs. It does not fund individual homeowners, and the owner and operator streams are closed for applications.

Open

Federal EV Affordability Program

Vehicles only

Open from February 16, 2026 until March 31, 2031 or until funds run out. It helps you buy or lease an eligible electric vehicle. It does not pay for a home charger.

Closed

Federal iZEV

Closed

The old federal EV incentive paid up to $5,000 toward a vehicle. It ended March 31, 2025.

Info

ENMAX

No rebate listed

ENMAX's EV charger guide gives advice but lists no home charger rebate. It says Level 2 chargers start at $499 before installation.

Info

EPCOR (Edmonton)

No rebate listed

EPCOR's conservation incentives are for stormwater and a smart thermostat pilot. Its home EV charging page offers no charger or lighting rebate, and the vehicle rebate it describes is the federal one that closed in 2025.

We have not confirmed every Alberta utility or town. If your bill comes from ATCO, FortisAlberta or a local co-op, ask them directly. We also found no home LED lighting rebate from the province, ENMAX or EPCOR. If any of them start a program, we will add it here.

What a home charger costs

ENMAX says a Level 2 charger starts at $499, before installation. The bigger cost is often the wiring, and sometimes the electrical panel. A full charge takes about 6 to 14 hours for a battery electric car on Level 2, so most people charge overnight.

How to avoid a panel upgrade

A panel upgrade can cost more than the charger. ENMAX suggests two ways around it. First, a smaller charger, such as 30 amps instead of 50, is often enough: ENMAX says the average driver needs under 3 hours of charging a night. Second, an EV energy management system can share power between the charger and the rest of your home. ENMAX puts that at about $1,200. Ask your electrician for a load calculation before you decide.

Permits and who can do the work

In Calgary, new wiring for an EV charger needs an electrical permit. A homeowner permit costs $116.50, but the City says homeowners cannot install EV charging wiring themselves, change the main service or do the load calculation. A licensed electrician must do that. Condo owners need a licensed contractor, and row-house units need condo board approval and a load calculation. Other cities have their own permit rules, so check with yours.

Living in a condo or apartment?

Most of the money that did exist went to multi-unit buildings, not houses. Calgary's ChargeYYC and the federal ZEVIP both funded chargers for shared parking, and both are closed for now. If your building is planning chargers, ask your board to watch for the next round.

How to add a home charger in Alberta

  1. Hire a licensed electrician and ask for a load calculation first.
  2. Choose a charger size that fits your panel; 30 amps is often enough.
  3. If your panel is short, compare an energy management system with a panel upgrade.
  4. Make sure the electrician pulls the electrical permit.
  5. Ask your utility if a charger rebate has opened since we checked.

Common questions

Is there a home EV charger rebate in Alberta?

We found none open for houses in 2026. Calgary's ChargeYYC was only for multi-unit buildings and closed in November 2025, and federal ZEVIP does not fund individual homeowners.

Does the federal EV program pay for a home charger?

No. The Electric Vehicle Affordability Program helps with buying or leasing an eligible vehicle, not the charger.

How much does a Level 2 charger cost in Alberta?

ENMAX says chargers start at $499 before installation. Wiring, permits and any panel work add to that.

Can I install the wiring myself in Calgary?

No. The City of Calgary says homeowners cannot install EV charging wiring; a licensed electrician must, with a permit.

Do I need a 50 amp charger?

Often not. ENMAX says the average driver needs under 3 hours of charging a night, so a 30 amp charger may be enough and can avoid a panel upgrade.
