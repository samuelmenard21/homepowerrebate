Alberta Home Energy Rebates 2026

Alberta doesn't have a province-wide rebate program like BC's CleanBC or Quebec's LogisVert. What exists is fragmented across utilities and financing tools. Here's exactly what's real, so you don't waste time chasing rebates that don't exist.

Why Alberta looks different from other provinces on this site

We rank Alberta near the bottom of our Canada-wide province comparison, and we're not going to pretend otherwise here. There's no single agency running Alberta's home energy rebates the way BC Hydro or Hydro-Québec do. Instead, you're working with individual utility rebates (ENMAX, ATCO Gas, EPCOR depending on your city) and a financing program (CEIP) that isn't a rebate at all.

What you can actually claim

🔥 Heat Pump

$500–$2,000/unit

Through ENMAX or ATCO Gas for ENERGY STAR cold-climate models. Availability changes through the year.

☀️ Solar

No rebate

No direct solar grant exists in Alberta. Net metering is your main mechanism.

🔋 Battery Storage

No rebate

No dedicated battery incentive currently exists.

🧱 Insulation

$1,000–$5,000

Via CEIP (up to 10% incentive) plus utility rebates. CMHC's separate 25% mortgage insurance refund may also apply.

💧 Water Heater

Financing only

No flat rebate — available through CEIP financing or utility-specific offers.

🪟 Windows & Doors

No dedicated rebate

May qualify for CEIP financing as part of a broader envelope upgrade.

🚘 EV Charger

No rebate found

No confirmed residential EV charger rebate in Alberta as of 2026.

🌡️ Smart Thermostat

No direct rebate

No standalone grant, though CEIP-financed HVAC upgrades sometimes include one.

CEIP: what it actually is

The Clean Energy Improvement Program lets you finance eligible upgrades through your property tax bill at competitive interest rates, often with a small incentive (5–10%) on top of the loan. It genuinely helps spread cost over time — but it's a loan, not free money. Compare it against a home equity line or contractor financing before assuming it's automatically the best deal.

Realistic total stack: A typical heat pump + insulation project in Alberta nets $2,000–$5,000 in actual rebates. That's a fraction of BC's ~$35,000 or Quebec's $15,000–$20,000+. If you're comparing provinces, see the full ranking for context.
