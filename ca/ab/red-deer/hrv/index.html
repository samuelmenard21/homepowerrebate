HRV & Ventilation Rebates in Red Deer

There's no standalone HRV/ERV rebate in Red Deer. Alberta's Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP) lists it as an eligible upgrade for financing — here's what that actually means and what else to check.

The honest answer: no cash rebate, but CEIP financing covers it

Red Deer sits between Alberta's two largest cities and shares their utility providers, ENMAX and ATCO Gas, along with the same province-wide CEIP financing option. We checked Alberta's Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP) eligible-upgrades documentation directly, and heat and energy recovery ventilation is listed as measure code R-HC-008 — equipment that "extracts heat and/or energy from stale air exhausted to the outside of the home" to cut the cost of heating fresh incoming air. That makes an HRV or ERV a financeable upgrade, not a rebate-eligible one.

CEIP works by financing the project — up to $50,000, repaid through your property tax bill over up to 20 years, often with a 5–10% incentive knocked off the total cost. That's a real, usable way to spread out the cost of a Red Deer installation, but it's still your money being repaid over time, not a cheque or point-of-sale discount.

$0 cash

No direct rebate or grant for HRV/ERV installation has been found for Red Deer homeowners as of 2026.

CEIP financing eligible

Listed as measure R-HC-008 on CEIP's eligible-upgrades list, financeable up to $50,000 through your property tax bill.

Closed to new applicants

The federal Canada Greener Homes Grant, which used to help fund ventilation as part of a broader retrofit, stopped taking new applications in February 2024.

What we checked

  • CEIP's own eligible-upgrades page (ceip.abmunis.ca) for heating, ventilation, and AC — confirmed HRV/ERV appears as measure R-HC-008
  • ENMAX/ATCO Gas's residential efficiency incentive pages — built around ENERGY STAR heat pumps and furnaces, no HRV/ERV line item found
  • The federal Canada Greener Homes Grant, which formerly counted HRV/ERV toward its retrofit grant — closed to new applicants since February 2024, with the final document deadline passed December 31, 2025
  • The Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program, the federal replacement for income-qualified households — as of September 2026 it operates in Manitoba, BC, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and PEI, but has not launched in Alberta

Why this matters if you're sealing up your Red Deer home

Insulation and window upgrades — both CEIP-financeable in Red Deer — tighten a home's envelope, which helps with heating bills but can trap stale air and moisture that used to leak out on its own. An HRV or ERV solves that by exchanging stale indoor air for fresh outdoor air while recovering most of the heat, which matters more in Alberta's cold winters than almost anywhere else we cover. What actually helps pay for HRV in Red Deer

  1. Apply for CEIP financing before starting any work — installing first and applying after disqualifies the project
  2. Bundle the HRV/ERV quote into the same CEIP-financed job as insulation or a heat pump to spread the labour cost
  3. Check whether your project counts as new construction or a major renovation under the Alberta Building Code, which can require an HRV regardless of financing — in that case it's a code cost, not an optional upgrade
  4. Watch for the Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program's Alberta rollout; it isn't available here yet but has launched in five other provinces

Common questions

Is there an HRV or ERV rebate in Red Deer?

There's no standalone cash rebate for a heat recovery ventilator (HRV) or energy recovery ventilator (ERV) in Red Deer. What does exist is Alberta's Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP), which lists heat and energy recovery ventilation (measure code R-HC-008) as an eligible upgrade for financing — you can borrow up to $50,000 for the project, repaid through your property tax bill, often with a 5–10% incentive off the total cost. That's financing, not free money.

Do ENMAX/ATCO Gas or Alberta offer a direct grant for HRV installation in Red Deer?

No. ENMAX/ATCO Gas's residential efficiency incentives are built around ENERGY STAR heat pumps, not ventilation equipment, and we found no province-wide HRV/ERV grant in Alberta. CEIP financing is the only confirmed program that names HRV/ERV as an eligible upgrade.
