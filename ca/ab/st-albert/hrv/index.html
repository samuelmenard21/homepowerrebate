HRV & Ventilation Rebates in St. Albert

The honest answer: no cash rebate, but CEIP financing covers it

St. Albert homeowners share EPCOR as their utility with neighbouring Edmonton, and the same CEIP financing terms apply here as across the rest of the province. We checked Alberta's Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP) eligible-upgrades documentation directly, and heat and energy recovery ventilation is listed as measure code R-HC-008 — equipment that "extracts heat and/or energy from stale air exhausted to the outside of the home" to cut the cost of heating fresh incoming air. That makes an HRV or ERV a financeable upgrade, not a rebate-eligible one.

CEIP works by financing the project — up to $50,000, repaid through your property tax bill over up to 20 years, often with a 5–10% incentive knocked off the total cost. That's a real, usable way to spread out the cost of a St. Albert installation, but it's still your money being repaid over time, not a cheque or point-of-sale discount.

What we checked

  • CEIP's own eligible-upgrades page (ceip.abmunis.ca) for heating, ventilation, and AC — confirmed HRV/ERV appears as measure R-HC-008
  • EPCOR's residential efficiency incentive pages — built around ENERGY STAR heat pumps and furnaces, no HRV/ERV line item found
  • The federal Canada Greener Homes Grant, which formerly counted HRV/ERV toward its retrofit grant — closed to new applicants since February 2024, with the final document deadline passed December 31, 2025
  • The Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program, the federal replacement for income-qualified households — as of September 2026 it operates in Manitoba, BC, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and PEI, but has not launched in Alberta

Why this matters if you're sealing up your St. Albert home

Insulation and window upgrades — both CEIP-financeable in St. Albert — tighten a home's envelope, which helps with heating bills but can trap stale air and moisture that used to leak out on its own. An HRV or ERV solves that by exchanging stale indoor air for fresh outdoor air while recovering most of the heat, which matters more in Alberta's cold winters than almost anywhere else we cover. What actually helps pay for HRV in St. Albert

Common questions

Is there an HRV or ERV rebate in St. Albert?

There's no standalone cash rebate for a heat recovery ventilator (HRV) or energy recovery ventilator (ERV) in St. Albert. What does exist is Alberta's Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP), which lists heat and energy recovery ventilation (measure code R-HC-008) as an eligible upgrade for financing — you can borrow up to $50,000 for the project, repaid through your property tax bill, often with a 5–10% incentive off the total cost. That's financing, not free money.

Do EPCOR or Alberta offer a direct grant for HRV installation in St. Albert?

No. EPCOR's residential efficiency incentives are built around ENERGY STAR heat pumps, not ventilation equipment, and we found no province-wide HRV/ERV grant in Alberta. CEIP financing is the only confirmed program that names HRV/ERV as an eligible upgrade.
