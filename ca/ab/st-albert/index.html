CEIP: financing, not a rebate

The Clean Energy Improvement Program lets you finance eligible upgrades — solar, insulation, heat pumps — through your property tax bill at competitive interest rates. It's a genuinely useful tool for spreading cost over time, but don't confuse it with free money: you're still paying for the full upgrade, just at a lower rate than a typical loan. Official CEIP program details →

Reality check: If you're comparing Alberta to BC or Quebec coverage online, don't expect the same numbers. For most Alberta homes the realistic outcome is financing, not a rebate cheque — far less than what BC or Quebec homeowners can get. See our full province ranking for the comparison.

Renting or living in a condo?

If you rent

The rebates above go to whoever owns the equipment, so as a tenant you can't use CEIP financing yourself — that's your landlord's call. If you want a heat pump or solar in your rental, point your landlord to this page; CEIP financing in particular is worth mentioning since it's repaid through the property tax bill rather than upfront cash.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you're personally responsible for — a water heater, an in-suite heat pump — can generally still go through the same programs as any homeowner, as long as your condo board signs off on anything touching shared systems or the building exterior. CEIP is tied to a property tax account, so confirm with the CEIP administrator whether your unit qualifies on its own or only through the corporation.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Honestly: Alberta's fragmented, utility-by-utility market doesn't have a confirmed dedicated multi-unit or condo-corporation rebate program the way some other provinces do. A condo board looking at a building-wide heat pump or solar project should contact ENMAX, ATCO, and the CEIP administrator directly to ask what, if anything, applies at the corporation level — rather than assume the per-unit numbers above scale up automatically.
