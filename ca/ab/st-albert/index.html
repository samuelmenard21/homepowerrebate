Home Energy Rebates in St. Albert

An honest breakdown of what St. Albert homeowners can actually claim in 2026. Alberta doesn't have a unified rebate program like BC or Quebec, so here's exactly what's real and what isn't.

An Edmonton-area city, served by EPCOR.

What St. Albert homeowners can claim

Alberta's rebate landscape is fragmented across utilities and financing programs rather than one province-wide system. All amounts below are in Canadian dollars.

🔥 Heat Pump

$500–$2,000/unit

Through ENMAX or ATCO Gas, for ENERGY STAR cold-climate models. Availability changes through the year — confirm before you buy.

☀️ Solar

No rebate

Alberta has no direct solar grant or rebate. Net metering is your main financial mechanism.

🔌 Battery Storage

No rebate

No dedicated battery storage incentive currently exists in Alberta.

🌡️ Insulation

$1,000–$5,000 + $1,400

Via CEIP (up to 10% incentive on financed cost) plus utility rebates. St. Albert adds its own $1,400 cash incentive applied directly to your CEIP tax bill — one per project, first-come first-served while city funding lasts. CMHC's separate 25% refund program may also apply.

💧 Water Heater

Financing only

No flat rebate. Available through CEIP financing or utility-specific offers from ATCO, EPCOR, or ENMAX.

🟩 Windows & Doors

No dedicated rebate

May qualify for CEIP financing as part of a broader envelope upgrade, but no standalone rebate.

🚘 EV Charger

No rebate found

No confirmed residential EV charger rebate in Alberta as of 2026.

🌡️ Smart Thermostat

No direct rebate

No standalone grant, though CEIP-financed HVAC upgrades sometimes include one.

CEIP: financing, not a rebate

The Clean Energy Improvement Program lets you finance eligible upgrades — solar, insulation, heat pumps — through your property tax bill at competitive interest rates, often with a 5–10% incentive off the total project cost. It's a genuinely useful tool for spreading cost over time, but don't confuse it with free money: you're still paying for the full upgrade, just at a lower rate than a typical loan.
