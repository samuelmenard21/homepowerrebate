How much rebate you get

Coquitlam's housing stock skews older, with plenty of homes still running the single-pane or early double-pane windows they were built with. That makes the CleanBC Better Homes window and door rebate — up to $100 per window or door, capped at $2,000 per home — one of the more useful line items for Coquitlam owners, since replacement often qualifies for the top tier here.

The rebate flows through BC Hydro's PowerSmart program for electric-heated homes, or FortisBC if your home is on gas — either way the application and U-factor rules are the same.

What it actually costs

$8,000–$15,000 for a full-home job in Coquitlam is typical for a detached house of that vintage; after the rebate, most owners are out of pocket $6,000–$13,000.

Who qualifies

  • You own your home in Coquitlam
  • Your current windows are single-pane or older double-pane
  • New windows need a U-factor of 1.40 W/m²·K or lower to qualify at all (Tier 1), and 1.22 or lower to hit the full $100-per-window Tier 2 rate — worth asking your installer to spec Tier 2 glass up front rather than settling for Tier 1.
  • An HPCN-registered installer files the rebate claim on your behalf

Worth knowing locally

Coquitlam also gets summer heat domes that push indoor temperatures uncomfortably high in older homes with single-pane glass — new windows cut that heat gain as much as they cut winter heat loss, which is part of why the city has a lot of retrofit activity even outside the heat pump rebate.

How to claim it

  1. Get quotes from installers registered with the CleanBC Better Homes program in Coquitlam
  2. Confirm the quoted windows meet at least the Tier 1 U-factor (1.40 W/m²·K)
  3. Ask your installer to price Tier 2 glass (U-factor 1.22 or lower) — the rebate difference often outweighs the cost difference
  4. Work is installed and inspected
  5. Installer files the rebate claim through CleanBC
  6. Rebate arrives in 4–8 weeks
