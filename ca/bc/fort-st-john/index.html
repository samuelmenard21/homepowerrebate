What you can claim in Fort St. John in 2026

Amounts come from the program pages we re-read on October 2, 2026. Each card is a maximum, and most depend on how your home is heated.

Open

Whole-home heat pump

Up to $4,000

Must replace electric heat such as baseboards or an electric furnace, meet 100% of the heating load at -5C and be installed by a Home Performance Contractor Network member.

Open

Partial heat pump

$1,500

For a heat pump that covers at least half of the heating load while replacing electric heat.

Open

Limited-time bonus

Up to $1,000

Extra money on heat pump installs completed between August 1 and October 31, 2026.

Open

Income-qualified switch from gas, oil or propane

$3,500 to $13,000

CleanBC pays by income level (Level 3 / Level 2 / Level 1) for a central or three-head heat pump, for invoices from July 6, 2026.

Open

Northern top-up

+$1,500 to +$3,000

Added to CleanBC Level 1 and 2 amounts for homes at or north of 100 Mile House on BC Hydro service.

Open

Insulation

Up to $5,500

Attic, wall and basement or crawlspace work, paid per R-value added per square foot. Homes heated mainly by electricity only.

Open

Windows and doors

Up to $2,000

$100 per opening, with a U-factor of 1.22 or lower.

Open

Heat pump water heater

Up to $1,000

Only when it replaces an electric water heater.

Open

Solar panels

Up to $5,000

$1,000 per kW, capped at half the cost, from an HPCN member installer.

Open

Battery storage

$1,500 or $5,000

Up to $1,500 on its own with solar, or up to $5,000 with Peak Saver enrolment.

Open

EV charger

Up to $350

50% of a new wall-mounted Level 2 charger, plus $200 for a power-management device.

Why housing condition matters here

The 2021 Census counted 8,775 households. 42% live in homes built before 1981, 50% in detached houses and 58% own, and 2,035 are owner-occupied detached houses from that era. The 7.5% reporting major repairs compares with 3.8% in Surrey.

Northern top-up

Fort St. John qualifies for CleanBC's northern top-up on BC Hydro service: +$3,000 for a central, multi-split or two mini-split system, or +$1,500 for a single head, on Level 1 and 2. The top amount for a fossil-fuel home is $16,000.

The coldest of the group

The January average low is -15.0°C and the 2015 to 2024 record was -42.2°C. Ask the installer for performance at -30°C or below and confirm backup heat.

Doing the work in a sensible order

Start with the building: air sealing and attic insulation lower the heating load that the heat pump must meet. Then size the equipment to the improved house, and ask the installer to document the load calculation. If a roof or siding job is already planned, ask the contractor whether insulation can be added while the structure is open.

Electric homes and sun

Electric-heated houses can claim BC Hydro's up to $4,000 heat pump, up to $5,500 insulation and up to $2,000 windows. Solar gets about 3.31 peak sun hours a day.
