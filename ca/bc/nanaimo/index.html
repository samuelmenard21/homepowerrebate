Honest math for Nanaimo

A typical household keeps $5,000–$16,000 in rebates — and Nanaimo lets you finance the rest interest-free (up to $15,000, repaid on your property-tax bill), so upfront cost stops being the barrier.

The old federal "Greener Homes" $40,000 grant and loan are both closed as of late 2025. Any site still advertising them is out of date. The federal money that remains flows to lower-income households through the CleanBC programs below.

Available everywhere in BC

Provincial & utility programs — what each one is

These are the backbone. They're the same whether you're in Nanaimo or anywhere else in BC — what changes is which utility runs them for you. Here's each program and how it actually works.

Through CleanBC & BetterHomes BC

Provincial

CleanBC heat pump rebate

$4,000–$16,000

The centrepiece. A cold-climate heat pump both heats and cools your home using electricity instead of gas. Everyone gets a base rebate; lower- and middle-income households get much more.

How it works: Your contractor (not you) applies through BetterHomes BC. A pre-upgrade EnerGuide home evaluation confirms eligibility. The rebate is deducted or paid after install. Higher tiers can add up to ~$21,000 when a required electrical panel upgrade is included. Official page →

Provincial

Insulation, windows & doors rebates

up to $5,500 + $2,000

Up to $5,500 for insulation (attic, walls, crawlspace) and up to $2,000 for ENERGY STAR windows and exterior doors. This is the "building envelope" — sealing the house before you size heating.

How it works: Same BetterHomes BC application as the heat pump. Do these first or at the same time — a tighter house lets you install a smaller, cheaper heat pump and cuts bills year-round.
Provincial

Heat pump water heater rebate

$1,000–$3,000

A heat pump water heater uses ~⅓ the energy of a standard electric tank. Replaces gas, electric, or oil tanks.

How it works: Rebate amount depends on your utility and income level. Pairs naturally with a space-heating heat pump on the same visit.

Through BC Hydro (your electricity utility)

BC Hydro

Home solar (self-generation) rebate

$5,000

A rebate toward rooftop solar. Excess power you generate is credited back on your bill.

How it works: As of June 1, 2026 the install must be done by a Home Performance Contractor Network member to qualify. New self-generation customers export surplus power at 10¢/kWh from July 1, 2026 — so sizing the system to your own use matters more than it used to.
BC Hydro

Home EV charger rebate

up to $550

50% of cost up to $350 for a Level 2 home charger, plus $200 for an EV power-management device that lets you charge without an expensive panel upgrade.

How it works: Apply through your utility after install. On BC Hydro, enrolling a smart charger in Peak Saver adds a $250 credit plus $50 each season you stay in. EV rebate details →

BC Hydro

Smart thermostats & in-store rebates

free–$200

Homes with electric-baseboard heating can get a free smart thermostat (a ~$350 value) starting fall 2026. At 300+ BC stores, instant in-store rebates knock $10–$200 off efficient products.

How it works: Enrolling a smart thermostat in Peak Saver adds a $100 bonus plus $50 seasonal rewards for letting your utility trim usage at peak times — you keep control and can opt out of any event.

Free programs (income-qualified)

Free

Free energy-saving kit

$0

A box of LED bulbs, a high-efficiency showerhead and other quick wins, mailed to your door to install yourself. Open to income-qualifying households.

How it works: Apply online through the income-based programs; the kit ships free. No contractor needed. Income-qualified programs →

Free

Free install + energy coaching (ECAP)

$0

The Energy Conservation Assistance Program sends an energy coach to your home and installs upgrades at no cost — which for qualifying homes can include a free heat pump, insulation, weatherization and ventilation.

How it works: Income-based. Thresholds were recently raised — a family of four earning up to ~$57,200 can now qualify. This is the single biggest opportunity for lower-income Nanaimo homeowners; the federal Affordability money flows through here.

Through FortisBC (your natural-gas utility)

FortisBC

FortisBC heat pump & water heater rebates

up to $12,000

Since most homes here also have a FortisBC gas account, you can tap Fortis rebates too: up to $4,000 for a heat pump ($1,500 partial), or up to $12,000 income-qualified, plus $5,000 for a dual-fuel (heat pump + high-efficiency gas furnace) system.

How it works: Income-qualified customers can also get up to $3,000 on a gas ENERGY STAR water heater and up to $2,000 on insulation. Apply within six months of your install invoice. Your contractor coordinates the two utilities so you don't double-claim the same measure. FortisBC rebates →
Federal

Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program

grant (income-based)

The replacement for the closed Greener Homes grant/loan. An $800M federal program for low-to-median income households, delivered in BC through the provincial CleanBC channels — covering insulation, heat pumps, windows and solar.

How it works: You don't apply separately — in BC it's folded into the income-qualified BetterHomes/ECAP streams above. If you qualify by income, it's already part of what your evaluation checks.

Local bonus: financing

Nanaimo's edge: up to $15,000, interest-free.

Nanaimo runs a Home Energy Retrofit Financing Program — up to $15,000 in interest-free financing, repaid over 10 years right on your property-tax bill, for heat pumps, solar and envelope upgrades. That turns a big upfront cost into a manageable line item.

To use it you first join the free Home Energy Navigator (an EnerGuide before and after is required). Combined with the rebates above, most of your project can be covered up front and financed at 0%.

FAQ for Nanaimo residents

Will solar actually work in Nanaimo?

Yes. Nanaimo averages about 3.7 peak sun hours — a touch better than the mainland thanks to the Island rain shadow. An 8–10 kW system offsets a solid share of a typical bill, and you can even fold solar into the City's interest-free financing.

What's the real cost of a solar system here?

An 8 kW system usually runs $25,000–$30,000 before rebates. After the $5,000 rebate your net is roughly $20,000–$25,000 — and in Nanaimo you can finance that balance interest-free over 10 years, so payback and cash flow both improve.

Why do heat pump rebates matter so much in Nanaimo specifically?

Whether you're on gas or electric, the CleanBC heat pump rebate ($4,000–$16,000) is the top move — and Nanaimo's interest-free financing covers whatever the rebate doesn't. You also gain cooling and storm-season comfort.

What if my income is lower — is there more help?

Considerably more. The Energy Conservation Assistance Program can install a heat pump, insulation and weatherization for free, and the federal Affordability money flows through it. A family of four earning up to ~$57,200 can qualify. Start with the assessment or ask us and we'll point you to the income-qualified stream.

Can I do this if I rent or live in a strata?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates (your landlord would), but the free energy-saving kit and smart-thermostat offers are open to you. In a strata, in-unit upgrades like a heat pump water heater or windows may qualify with strata approval; whole-building work goes through the strata council and a separate condo/apartment program.

When should I apply?

Get quotes now and apply as soon as you've chosen an installer — the pre-upgrade evaluation has to happen before work starts, so late applications forfeit the rebate.
