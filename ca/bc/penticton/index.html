Penticton Home Energy Rebates

Penticton buys its electricity through the City's own utility, wholesale from FortisBC. The City adds a Home Energy Loan Program of up to $10,000, repaid over ten years on the utility bill.

Energy rebates in Penticton, BC: which ones apply

FortisBC's listing covers municipal electric customers in Penticton: insulation by the per-R formula (attic up to $900, walls and basement up to $1,200 each), $100 per window or door up to $2,000, up to $1,000 for a heat pump water heater, and up to $150 for a connected thermostat on electric central heat. We did not verify a heat pump rebate, so ask FortisBC and the City before you book.

The City's loan

The City of Penticton's HELP loan finances up to $10,000 over ten years on the utility bill. It does not replace the rebates, but it can cover the cost while you wait for them. Ask the City what upgrades qualify and what the interest rate is, since we did not verify it.

Penticton's housing

The 2021 Census counted 17,360 households. 46% live in homes built before 1981, the second-highest share among the Interior cities we cover, after Kamloops. 40% live in detached houses and 63% own, and 3,440 are owner-occupied detached houses built before 1981. Major repairs were reported by 5.7%.

Questions to ask the City before you start

Ask the City three things: which upgrades the HELP loan covers, what interest or fee applies, and whether the loan balance transfers if you sell the house. The loan is repaid on the utility bill over ten years, so a buyer inherits the account. We did not verify those terms, so get them in writing.

Then ask FortisBC whether your upgrade earns a second-upgrade bonus. A project that pairs insulation with a water heater may qualify for $300 or for the EnerGuide improvement bonus.

A note on heat pumps

We could not confirm a heat pump rebate for Penticton electric customers. If a contractor quotes one, ask for the program page and the amount in writing. Penticton's cold snaps reach -24.8°C, so confirm the model's output at low temperatures regardless of rebate.

Sun and cold

Penticton gets about 3.76 peak sun hours a day, the highest in our data. The January average low is -2.5°C and the record low in 2015 to 2024 was -24.8°C.

Solar panels in Penticton

Penticton is a sunny place to put panels: about 3.76 peak sun hours a day, the highest in our data. The rebate side is thin. BC Hydro's solar rebate of $1,000 per kilowatt is for BC Hydro customers, and Penticton homes buy power from the City. We did not find a solar rebate from the City or FortisBC for homeowners.

Before you sign a solar contract, ask the City two things in writing: how it connects your panels to its grid, and what it pays or credits you for the extra power you send back. The City keeps a solar panel application package on its website. We could not read the whole file, so we are not quoting its rules.

Energy audits in Penticton, BC

An energy audit, also called an EnerGuide home evaluation, shows where your house loses heat so you spend rebate money on the right job first. FortisBC lists an EnerGuide improvement bonus of $750 to $2,000 for municipal electric customers, which is paid when your upgrades improve your home's score. We did not verify that an audit itself is paid for in Penticton, so ask what the audit costs before you book.

If you are not sure where to start, do the audit first, then choose insulation, windows or a water heater from the list above.
