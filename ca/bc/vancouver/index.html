Furnace, Heat Pump & Solar Rebates for Vancouver Homeowners

Every rebate a Vancouver homeowner can get in 2026 — heat pumps, solar, insulation, water heaters, EV chargers, and free income-qualified programs — explained plainly, with how each one works.

Updated July 2026 · Independent guide. We don't sell your info. Numbers below are money you keep (rebates), not loans.

Planning a heat pump installation in Vancouver, or just trying to make your home more energy efficient? This page covers every energy rebate in Vancouver in one place, then matches you with a vetted local installer.

In 2026, a Vancouver homeowner can claim up to $16,000 for a heat pump, $5,000 for solar panels, up to $5,500 for insulation, $1,000–$3,000 for a heat-pump water heater, and up to $550 for an EV charger. Stacked together, the rebates can total roughly $35,000.

Replacing Your Furnace in Vancouver?

If your gas furnace is aging, the rebate math favors a heat pump. You'll claim $4,000–$16,000 (depending on income), plus local financing when available. Total rebate + financing can cover 50–70% of a new heat pump system—and you gain cooling, better resilience, and lower bills.

Honest math for Vancouver

A typical household keeps $5,000–$16,000, plus Vancouver's up-to-$2,000 heat pump top-up. A lower-income deep retrofit can stack toward $27,000–$32,000 in rebates you never repay.

The old federal "Greener Homes" $40,000 grant and loan are both closed as of late 2025. Any site still advertising them is out of date. The federal money that remains flows to lower-income households through the CleanBC programs below.

The resilience play

In Vancouver, a battery is about keeping the lights on

Storm-season outages are the real driver here, not the rebate math above (already covered in the grid). A home battery keeps your essentials — fridge, lights, internet, a heat-pump circuit — running when the grid drops. It must be on BC Hydro's approved list or you get $0 for the rebate — the Tesla Powerwall doesn't qualify. Which batteries qualify → Solar's job here is mainly to recharge that battery through the day — at ~3.5 sun hours, think of it as the battery's daytime top-up, not a fast-payback play on its own.

Available everywhere in BC

Provincial & utility programs — what each one is

These are the backbone. They're the same whether you're in Vancouver or anywhere else in BC — what changes is which utility runs them for you. Here's each program and how it works.

Through CleanBC & BetterHomes BC

Provincial

CleanBC heat pump rebate

$4,000–$16,000

The centrepiece. A cold-climate heat pump both heats and cools your home using electricity instead of gas. Everyone gets a base rebate; lower- and middle-income households get much more.

How it works: Your contractor (not you) applies through BetterHomes BC. A pre-upgrade EnerGuide home evaluation confirms eligibility. The rebate is deducted or paid after install. Higher tiers can add up to ~$21,000 when a required electrical panel upgrade is included. Official page →

Provincial

Insulation, windows & doors rebates

up to $5,500 + $2,000

Up to $5,500 for insulation (attic, walls, crawlspace) and up to $2,000 for ENERGY STAR windows and exterior doors. This is the "building envelope" — sealing the house before you size heating. See our full insulation rebates breakdown →

How it works: Same BetterHomes BC application as the heat pump. Do these first or at the same time — a tighter house lets you install a smaller, cheaper heat pump and cuts bills year-round.
Provincial

Heat pump water heater rebate

$1,000–$3,000

A heat pump water heater uses ~⅓ the energy of a standard electric tank. Replaces gas, electric, or oil tanks.

How it works: Rebate amount depends on your utility and income level. Pairs naturally with a space-heating heat pump on the same visit.

Through BC Hydro (your electricity utility)

BC Hydro

Home solar (self-generation) rebate

$5,000

A rebate toward rooftop solar. Excess power you generate is credited back on your bill.

How it works: As of June 1, 2026 the install must be done by a Home Performance Contractor Network member to qualify. New self-generation customers export surplus power at 10¢/kWh from July 1, 2026 — so sizing the system to your own use matters more than it used to.
BC Hydro

Home EV charger rebate

up to $550

50% of cost up to $350 for a Level 2 home charger, plus $200 for an EV power-management device that lets you charge without an expensive panel upgrade.

How it works: Apply through your utility after install. On BC Hydro, enrolling a smart charger in Peak Saver adds a $250 credit plus $50 each season you stay in. EV rebate details →

BC Hydro

Smart thermostats & in-store rebates

free–$200

Homes with electric-baseboard heating can get a free smart thermostat (a ~$350 value) starting fall 2026. At 300+ BC stores, instant in-store rebates knock $10–$200 off efficient products. Comparing models? See Mysa vs. Nest vs. Ecobee →

How it works: Enrolling a smart thermostat in Peak Saver adds a $100 bonus plus $50 seasonal rewards for letting your utility trim usage at peak times — you keep control and can opt out of any event.
Peak Saver

Peak Saver device enrollment

$100–$500 + seasonal

Once an eligible smart device is installed, enrolling it in Peak Saver pays a one-time bonus plus a reward every winter season — and for a home battery it's what unlocks the higher self-generation rebate.

What enrolls & pays: home battery (Eguana, SolarEdge) $500 + $250/season · EV charger $250 + $50/season · smart thermostat (Mysa, Sinopé) $100 + $50/season · water heater $100. You keep control and can opt out of any event. Enroll your devices →

Free programs (income-qualified)

Free

Free energy-saving kit

$0

A box of LED bulbs, a high-efficiency showerhead and other quick wins, mailed to your door to install yourself. Open to income-qualifying households.

How it works: Apply online through the income-based programs; the kit ships free. No contractor needed. Income-qualified programs →

Free

Free install + energy coaching (ECAP)

$0

The Energy Conservation Assistance Program sends an energy coach to your home and installs upgrades at no cost — which for qualifying homes can include a free heat pump, insulation, weatherization and ventilation.

How it works: Income-based. Thresholds were recently raised — a family of four earning up to ~$57,200 can now qualify. This is the single biggest opportunity for lower-income Vancouver homeowners; the federal Affordability money flows through here.

Through FortisBC (your natural-gas utility)

FortisBC

FortisBC heat pump & water heater rebates

up to $12,000

Since most homes here also have a FortisBC gas account, you can tap Fortis rebates too: up to $4,000 for a heat pump ($1,500 partial), or up to $12,000 income-qualified, plus $5,000 for a dual-fuel (heat pump + high-efficiency gas furnace) system.

How it works: Income-qualified customers can also get up to $3,000 on a gas ENERGY STAR water heater and up to $2,000 on insulation. Apply within six months of your install invoice. Your contractor coordinates the two utilities so you don't double-claim the same measure. FortisBC rebates →
Federal

Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program

grant (income-based)

The replacement for the closed Greener Homes grant/loan. An $800M federal program for low-to-median income households, delivered in BC through the provincial CleanBC channels — covering insulation, heat pumps, windows and solar.

How it works: You don't apply separately — in BC it's folded into the income-qualified BetterHomes/ECAP streams above. If you qualify by income, it's already part of what your evaluation checks.

Vancouver-specific programs

Local bonus: real cash

Vancouver adds up to $2,000 on your heat pump.

Vancouver is one of the few BC cities that puts in its own money: the City matches the provincial heat pump incentive with up to an extra $2,000, on top of everything CleanBC and your utilities pay. It also publishes detailed green home retrofit guidance and a dedicated heat pump resource for detached homes.

Solar equipment is also exempt from the 7% provincial sales tax, and your excess generation is credited at 10¢/kWh.

FAQ for Vancouver residents

Will solar actually work in Vancouver?

Yes, though Vancouver's ~3.5 peak sun hours are on the lower side thanks to cloudy winters. Summers are productive, and across a full year an 8–10 kW system still offsets a solid share of your bill — helped by the $5,000 rebate, PST exemption, and 10¢/kWh export credit.

What's the real cost of a solar system in Vancouver?

An 8 kW system usually runs $25,000–$30,000 before rebates. After the $5,000 BC Hydro rebate (and PST exemption) your net is roughly $19,000–$24,000, paying back in about 11–14 years.

Why do heat pump rebates matter so much in Vancouver?

Vancouver's many older gas-heated homes are prime candidates: CleanBC pays $4,000–$16,000, and the City adds up to $2,000 more. You also gain cooling for increasingly hot summers — a genuine health benefit in a heat dome.

What if my income is lower — is there more help?

The Energy Conservation Assistance Program can install a heat pump, insulation and weatherization for free, and the federal Affordability money flows through it. A family of four earning up to about $57,200 can qualify. Start with the assessment or ask us and we'll point you to the income-qualified stream.

Can I do this if I rent or live in a condo?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates (your landlord would), but the free energy-saving kit and smart-thermostat offers are open to you. In a condo, in-unit upgrades like a heat pump water heater or windows may qualify with your building's approval; whole-building work goes through the condo board and a separate multi-unit program. See the full breakdown below.

When should I apply?

Get quotes now and apply as soon as you've chosen an installer — the pre-upgrade evaluation has to happen before work starts, so late applications forfeit the rebate.

A big share of Vancouver homes

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most rebate dollar figures on this page assume you own a detached or duplex home and control the whole building. That's not most of Vancouver. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

You can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, windows, solar) — those go to whoever owns the property. What's actually open to you: the free BC Hydro energy-saving kit (LED bulbs, showerhead) and the free/discounted smart thermostat offer, both above. If you want the bigger upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, in-suite windows, an in-unit heat pump — may qualify for the rebates above with your building's approval (usually required for anything touching shared systems or the outside walls). Get that approval in writing before applying; BetterHomes BC requires proof the work was authorized.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation, and solar for the whole building go through the condo board as a group decision, using a separate multi-unit rebate stream rather than the single-family numbers on this page — the per-unit amounts and process differ. Your condo board or property manager applies, not individual owners. The BetterHomes BC energy coach line can walk your condo board through the multi-unit program specifically.
