Newfoundland and Labrador Oil Rebate 2026: Get $400 to $1,000 (How to Apply)

Yes. If you heat your home with furnace or stove oil in Newfoundland and Labrador, the province pays a Home Heating Supplement of $400 to $1,000. You get $1,000 if your 2025 family income was $100,000 or less. Between $100,000 and $150,000 you get a smaller amount, at least $400. Apply by January 15, 2027. If you already applied this year, you do not need to apply again.

How much you get

The most you can get is $1,000. You get the full $1,000 if your 2025 adjusted family income was $100,000 or less. If it was over $100,000 and up to $150,000, you get a smaller amount, with a minimum of $400. The province does not say how the smaller amount is worked out, so ask the program if you are in that range.

For most people, adjusted family income is your 2025 net income (line 23600 of your tax return) plus your spouse's or partner's. Your 2025 tax return must be filed with the Canada Revenue Agency before you apply.

What does it cost to apply?

The program page lists no fee to apply. The supplement is money paid to you, not a loan. The only costs are ones you would have anyway: the oil you buy during 2026 (you need invoices for at least 250 litres) and a stamp if you mail the form. The province offers free help by phone and email, so there is no reason to pay a company to file for you.

Who can apply

You must pay for furnace or stove oil to heat your main home in Newfoundland and Labrador. You need:

  • 2025 adjusted family income of $150,000 or less.
  • Oil invoices from January 1 to December 31, 2026, in your name or your spouse's or partner's name, showing at least 250 litres and the delivery address.
  • A signed application and a correct Social Insurance Number.

Only one person per household can apply. Heat that is included in your rent does not count, and fully subsidized Newfoundland and Labrador Housing units are not eligible unless you have oil invoices. In some special cases, such as a spouse who died, a spouse in long-term care or a divorce, you may qualify even if your income looks too high. The program asks for papers to prove it. Not sure which home counts as your main home? The province links a Tax Information Bulletin on principal residence (PDF).

How to apply: online or by mail

The province asks people to apply online if they can, because it is faster.

  • Apply online: HHSP Online, the province's online form
  • Paper form: 2026 Application Form (PDF)
  • Official program page: gov.nl.ca Home Heating Supplement Program

Paper copies are at the Department of Finance and SeniorsNL office in St. John's, Jobs, Growth and Rural Development offices, Government Service Centres (not Springdale or Lewisporte) and public libraries. Mail the form to:

Home Heating Supplement Program
Department of Finance
P.O. Box 8700
St. John's, NL A1B 4J6

Make sure the form is complete: include a copy of an oil invoice with your delivery address, sign it and check your Social Insurance Number.

Already applied or already paid?

If you applied for the 2026 program, you do not need to apply again. The province raised the amounts after the program opened. If you were already paid, it says you will get a second payment later this year.

When you get paid

The province says most applications are processed within 10 weeks of arriving. Many forms at once, or forms with missing pieces, can slow that down. If your tax refund or GST/HST credit comes by direct deposit from the Canada Revenue Agency, your supplement goes to the same bank account. If not, you get a cheque.

Change your bank details with the Canada Revenue Agency before you apply, since the Department of Finance cannot change them. How to change your direct deposit (Canada Revenue Agency).

The deadline

Applications close on January 15, 2027, and late applications are not accepted. This page is about the 2026 program. We do not have 2027 dates or amounts yet, and we will update this page if the province posts them.

Keep your invoices

Save every oil invoice with your address on it. Photocopies are fine. If a delivery is missing an address, ask your supplier to reissue it before you apply. Make a copy of your signed form and note the date you applied.

How to get help

Email OilSupplement@gov.nl.ca, or call 709-729-4645 or toll-free 1-844-729-4645. The program page is on the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador site under Finance.

Think about the bigger fix

Oil heat costs a lot to run. The Oil to Electric program from takeCHARGE pays up to $22,000 for lower-income households switching to a central heat pump, and up to $9,000 for the general program. See the Newfoundland pages below for who qualifies.

How to claim it, step by step

  1. Add up the litres on your 2026 oil invoices and check that you have at least 250.
  2. Check that your 2025 tax return is filed and your 2025 adjusted family income is $150,000 or less.
  3. Apply online through HHSP Online, or fill in the 2026 form and mail it to the Department of Finance.
  4. Attach an invoice with your delivery address, sign the form and check your Social Insurance Number.
  5. Apply before January 15, 2027. If you already applied, you do not need to apply again.
  6. Look at the Oil to Electric program for a lasting fix.

Common questions

Is there an oil rebate in Newfoundland and Labrador in 2026?

Yes. The Home Heating Supplement pays $400 to $1,000 to people who buy furnace or stove oil for their main home. The most, $1,000, goes to households with 2025 adjusted family income of $100,000 or less.

How do I apply for the NL oil rebate?

Apply online through HHSP Online, which the province prefers, or use the 2026 application form and mail it to Home Heating Supplement Program, Department of Finance, P.O. Box 8700, St. John's, NL A1B 4J6. Both are linked from the official program page.

How much does it cost to apply for the NL oil rebate?

Nothing is listed. The official program page names no application fee, and the province gives free help by phone (709-729-4645) and email (OilSupplement@gov.nl.ca). You only need your 2026 oil invoices, and a stamp if you mail the form.

Where do I get the oil rebate application form?

The 2026 application form is on the official program page. Paper copies are at the Department of Finance and SeniorsNL office in St. John's, Jobs, Growth and Rural Development offices, Government Service Centres (not Springdale or Lewisporte) and public libraries.

When does the NL oil rebate close?

Applications close on January 15, 2027. Late applications are not accepted.

Is there an oil rebate in NL in 2027?

The program now open is the 2026 program, and it takes applications until January 15, 2027. We do not have 2027 dates or amounts yet.

What is the income limit?

A 2025 adjusted family income of $150,000 or less. $1,000 is paid at $100,000 or less. Above that, you get a smaller amount, at least $400.

What invoices do I need?

Invoices from January 1 to December 31, 2026 showing at least 250 litres of furnace or stove oil and your delivery address, in your name or your spouse's or partner's name.

I already applied. Do I apply again?

No. The province raised the amounts for the 2026 program and says people who already applied do not need to apply again. People already paid get a second payment later this year.

How long does payment take?

The province says most applications are processed within 10 weeks. It pays by direct deposit if your tax refund or GST/HST credit comes that way, and by cheque if not.

How do I change my bank details so I get direct deposit?

Change them with the Canada Revenue Agency before you apply. The Department of Finance cannot update them. If your tax refund or GST/HST credit does not come by direct deposit, you are paid by cheque.

Which home counts as my principal residence?

The province links a Tax Information Bulletin on principal residence from its program page. If you own more than one home, read it before you apply.
