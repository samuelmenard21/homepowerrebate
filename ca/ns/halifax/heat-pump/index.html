Heat Pump Rebates in Halifax

What Halifax homeowners can still get for a heat pump in 2026, who qualifies for more, and how to claim it.

← Back to Halifax rebate hub Appliance Rebates in Halifax Solar in Halifax Water Heater in Halifax Smart Thermostat in Halifax HRV in Halifax
Short answer: Halifax homeowners use the same province-wide Efficiency Nova Scotia rebates. Anyone can get $300 per ton for a ductless mini-split or $500 per ton for a centrally ducted heat pump through a Home Energy Assessment. Moderate-income homes that don't heat with electricity get extra. Lower-income homes can get free upgrades through HomeWarming. The oil-to-heat-pump grant (OHPA) closed July 2, 2026. HRM adds no heat pump rebate of its own.

How much is the heat pump rebate in Halifax?

WhoDuctless mini-splitCentrally ducted

Example: a 2-ton ductless heat pump gets $600 through the Home Energy Assessment. If you also qualify for the Moderate Income Rebate, that's $1,600. The Home Energy Assessment pays up to $5,000 across all your upgrades, and up to $10,000 when stacked with the Moderate Income Rebate.

What doesn't qualify: replacing an existing heat pump, ducted mini-splits, and equipment in an unheated attic. The installer must be a certified refrigeration mechanic.

What a heat pump costs to run in Halifax

Nova Scotia Power's residential energy charge was 18.324¢/kWh in its 2026 tariff, plus riders and a $20.08 monthly base charge. (Check current rates →) Most Halifax homes compare a heat pump to baseboard heat or an oil furnace. A cold-climate heat pump gives you 2 to 3 units of heat for each unit of power in most weather, so it costs less to run than baseboards on the same rate.

How to claim it

  1. If you might qualify by income, apply for HomeWarming or the Moderate Income Rebate first and wait for approval.
  2. Book a Home Energy Assessment ($199, free if approved for an income program) before you buy anything.
  3. Pick a model on Efficiency Nova Scotia's qualifying list and have a certified installer put it in.
  4. Finish within 12 months and book your final assessment. The rebate cheque usually arrives within about 90 days.

Nova Scotia Power: one utility, no competing options

Nova Scotia Power serves virtually the entire province, Halifax included, so unlike Alberta's patchwork of city-specific utilities, there's no question of who to call. It's the same company for the electricity rate math above and for any panel-capacity conversation your installer raises. Worth knowing: Nova Scotia Power's residential rate has already climbed once in 2026 (see the rate breakdown above) with more increases scheduled through the 2026–2027 rate application, so a Halifax heat pump's savings math is partly a bet on a utility whose rates are trending up, not flat.

Halifax's winters are mild by Canadian standards, with real cold nights mixed in

Ten years of weather data (2015-2024) show Halifax's average January low at a comparatively gentle -6.0°C, well milder than the Alberta cities covered elsewhere on this site. Atlantic Canada's coastal climate moderates winter substantially. But the coldest recorded night in that decade hit -26.4°C, proof that "mild on average" still allows for genuine cold snaps. A cold-climate heat pump rated well past -25°C handles Halifax's typical winter with room to spare, and still covers its rare extreme nights.
