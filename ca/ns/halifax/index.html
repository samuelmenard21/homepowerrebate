Home Rebates in Halifax

Halifax homes use the same Efficiency Nova Scotia programs as the rest of the province, plus one city program: Solar City financing. Here is what is open on October 5, 2026, what closed this year, and which one to start with for your income and your heating fuel.

What is open for Halifax homes in 2026

We re-read the Efficiency Nova Scotia and Government of Nova Scotia pages on October 5, 2026. Halifax Regional Municipality's own page could not be opened by our tools that day, so the Solar City details come from the copy the site owner read on October 3.

Open

Home Energy Assessment rebates

Up to $5,000

Open to any Nova Scotia homeowner, whatever the income or heating fuel. Book an assessment ($199), finish the upgrades within 12 months, then book the final visit. Cheques come within about 90 days.

Open

Moderate Income Rebate

Up to $5,000 more

For owners who live in the home and heat mainly with something other than electricity, under the income limits. It was cut from $10,000 after the federal oil program closed. It stacks with the assessment rebates and needs approval before any work.

Open

HomeWarming

Free

Insulation, draft-proofing and heat pumps at no charge for lower-income homeowners, with a free assessment. Limits start at $30,187 after tax for one person.

Open

Instant rebates at the till

$45 to $800

$800 off an ENERGY STAR heat pump water heater, $150 off a heat pump dryer, $45 off a smart thermostat for electric heat, $200 off a variable-speed pool pump.

Open

Free smart thermostat

Free

Installed at no cost in electrically heated homes. Other free devices such as shower heads and aerators may come with the visit.

Open

Halifax Solar City

4.75% financing

Not a rebate. The Municipality pays the contractor and you repay over 10 years on a separate charge that stays with the property.

Open

Heating Assistance Rebate

$400

A provincial cheque toward your heating bill. Apply from October 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027.

Open

Oil to Heat Pump Affordability

Closed

Closed to new applicants on July 2, 2026 when the money was spent.

Open

SolarHomes

Closed

Stopped taking homeowner applications on April 17, 2025. Only non-profits and charities can still apply.

Open

Canada Greener Homes Grant and Loan

Closed

Both ended: the grant took documents until December 31, 2025, and the loan cannot approve new applications.

Who lives in Halifax, and what that means for rebates

The 2021 Census counted 190,510 households in the Halifax Regional Municipality. Owners are 58% and renters 42%. Almost half (48%) live in single-detached houses, 15% in apartment buildings of five or more storeys and 22% in smaller apartment buildings and duplexes. About 45% of homes were built before 1981, and 6.4% need major repairs.

Every provincial rebate here goes to the homeowner. With four households in ten renting, a large share of Halifax cannot apply at all. If you rent, your best moves are the $400 heating cheque and the free smart thermostat, and a conversation with your landlord about the rest. For the 28,280 households in tall apartment buildings, a heat pump is usually a decision for the whole building.

Start with your income

Income decides which door to knock on first, and the order matters because the income programs need approval before any work starts.

Under the HomeWarming limits ($30,187 for one person, $57,075 for two to four, $79,163 for five or more, after tax): apply to HomeWarming and wait. Under the higher Moderate Income limits ($37,260 for one person up to $126,500 for five or more) and heating with oil, propane or gas: apply for the Moderate Income Rebate. Everyone else, and anyone heating with electricity: book the assessment. Both income tests use line 23600 minus line 43500 of your Notice of Assessment.

Why the oil-to-heat-pump story changed

Until July 2, 2026, an oil-heated Halifax family under the income limit could combine federal money with provincial money for a large heat pump rebate. The federal program ran out of funds, and the province cut its top-up from $10,000 to $5,000 to match. If you read an older article promising $15,000, it is out of date. Efficiency Nova Scotia says applications not yet approved are judged at the new levels, so an old application can still pay less than you expected.

Solar City, in plain words

Solar City lets a Halifax property owner put solar panels, solar hot water or solar hot air on the house without paying up front. The Municipality pays the contractor when the work is done, and you repay through a Local Improvement Charge at a fixed 4.75% over 10 years, billed apart from your regular property taxes. You can pay it off early with no penalty. When you sell, you either pay off the balance or, if both sides agree, the buyer takes it over.

It is a loan on your property, so run the numbers as you would with any loan. The old provincial SolarHomes rebate is closed to homeowners, so Solar City is now the main help with the up-front cost. Our Halifax solar page covers it in more detail.

Power costs and the cold

Nova Scotia Power charges 19.128 cents per kWh plus a $20.08 monthly base charge since May 1, 2026. That is why the electric-only upgrades, a heat pump or a heat pump water heater, tend to pay back. Halifax's average January low is about -6°C and the coldest reading in the last ten years was -26.4°C, so ask any heat pump installer what temperature the unit is rated for and whether you should keep a backup.

What to do first in Halifax

  1. Check your after-tax household income against the HomeWarming and Moderate Income limits.
  2. If you qualify, apply first and wait for approval before you book anyone.
  3. If not, book a Home Energy Assessment with an Efficiency Nova Scotia delivery partner.
  4. Choose the upgrades, get several quotes and finish within 12 months of the assessment.
  5. Book the final assessment and wait for the rebate cheque.
  6. If you rent, apply for the $400 heating rebate before March 31, 2027.

Common questions

How much can a Halifax homeowner get back in 2026?

Up to $5,000 through the Home Energy Assessment program. Homes heated mainly by something other than electricity and under the income limits can add up to $5,000 from the Moderate Income Rebate, for up to $10,000 in total.

Does Halifax have its own rebate?

Not a cash rebate that we could confirm. Halifax Solar City is financing at 4.75% over 10 years for solar, repaid on a charge that stays with the property.

Is the oil to heat pump program still open?

No. It closed to new applicants on July 2, 2026.

Can renters in Halifax get anything?

Renters who pay their own heat can apply for the $400 heating rebate. The free smart thermostat is open to electrically heated homes, and renters need the landlord's written permission.

Do I need an assessment first?

For the per-measure rebates, yes. The initial visit costs $199, waived for moderate-income households, and the final visit comes after the work.

Is there a battery rebate in Halifax?

We found no current residential battery rebate on the Efficiency Nova Scotia programs list.
