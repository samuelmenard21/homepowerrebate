Program In Transition

Nova Scotia Home Energy Rebates 2026

Nova Scotia used to run some of the most generous stackable heat pump grants in the country. That program closed at the end of 2025. Here's exactly what's still real in 2026, and what to expect if you're switching off oil or electric heat.

What changed

If you've seen Nova Scotia described online as having "the most generous heat pump rebate in Canada," that's now out of date. The core Home Heating System Rebates Program closed to new applications on December 31, 2025. The federal top-up, the Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program (OHPA), closed to new applicants on July 2, 2026. Nova Scotia moved first on this compared to most other provinces.

What's left is real but more targeted. A new federal program is reportedly returning to serve more than 35,000 low- and median-income Nova Scotia households with no-cost heat pump and insulation upgrades — worth watching if you fall into that income band.

What you can actually claim in 2026

🔥 Heat Pump

Up to $5,000 (all) / $15,000 (moderate income)

The big stackable Home Heating System Rebates Program closed Dec 31, 2025, but Efficiency Nova Scotia still offers up to $5,000 to any homeowner, up to $15,000 for moderate-income households, and free installation for income-qualified homes through HomeWarming.

🧱 Insulation & Air Sealing

Free

Still active as a no-cost upgrade program for eligible households.

☀️ Solar

$0.60/watt

A 10kW system nets roughly $6,000. Still active as of 2026.

💧 Water Heater

Up to $2,500

Still active for efficient home water heating systems.

🌡️ Smart Thermostat

Free

Free product and installation still offered.

🔋 Battery Storage

Not confirmed

No dedicated battery storage rebate found as of 2026.

🪟 Windows & Doors

Not confirmed

No standalone windows/doors rebate found — may be covered under broader insulation programs.

🚘 EV Charger

Not confirmed

No dedicated residential EV charger rebate found as of 2026.

If you're switching off oil heat: the two biggest programs that made this affordable both closed in the past year. Check directly with Efficiency Nova Scotia before assuming the old numbers still apply — several sites still quote pre-2026 figures.
