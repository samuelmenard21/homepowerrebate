What Ontario pays for new appliances in Burlington

We re-read the program's appliance page on October 4, 2026. The rebates are province-wide, so the amounts are the same in every city, and the rules about what you replace and which model you buy are what decide a claim.

The appliance page states no end date, so confirm it is still open before you buy.

Apartments are not excluded by name

With 18% of Burlington households in buildings of five or more storeys, the lack of a home type on the appliance page matters. It lists a residential customer on the Ontario grid and does not mention condos, so owners should confirm before buying.

Burlington Hydro

Burlington Hydro distributes power here, and Burlington postal codes begin with L, which the Energy Affordability Program lists for its free fridge and freezer route. The average January low is -7.2°C.

What kind of home

Unlike the thermostat rebate, the appliance page lists no home type. It asks for a residential customer on the Ontario grid. It does not address condos or apartments directly, so a Burlington condo owner or tenant should confirm with the program. 25% of Burlington households rent, and a tenant should ask the landlord before swapping a fridge or range that came with the unit.

Adding up the six

The six rebates are $200 for a heat pump dryer, $150 for an induction cooktop or range, $75 for a washer, $50 each for a fridge and a freezer and $25 for a dishwasher. A household that replaced all six would receive $550, though few Burlington homes will need to swap everything at once. The rebate is flat, so it takes the same amount off an entry model and a premium one.

Most Efficient, not just ENERGY STAR

The rebate is for ENERGY STAR Most Efficient models, which the page describes as going beyond standard ENERGY STAR requirements. A fridge with the ordinary ENERGY STAR mark may not qualify. Each appliance has its own product list linked from the program page, so find the model number there before you pay, whether you buy in Burlington or online.

Replace, do not add

The program page asks for an existing electric appliance in need of replacing. A new fridge to go in a spare corner, or an induction range where the kitchen had no range, is not a replacement. If you are switching from gas to an electric appliance, the page does not say whether that counts, so ask the program before you buy. Burlington households that already cook on an electric range are the clearest fit for the induction rebate.

How to claim in Burlington

  1. Check that the appliance you are replacing is electric and that you are on the Ontario grid.
  2. Find a new model on the program's ENERGY STAR Most Efficient list for that appliance.
  3. Buy it new from any retailer and keep the receipt.
  4. Upload the receipt on the program page within 60 days of purchase.
  5. If your household income is under the limits, call 1-844-770-3148 about free fridges and freezers first.
