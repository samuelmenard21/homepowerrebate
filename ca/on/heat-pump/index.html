Ontario Heat Pump Rebates in 2026

How much you get in Ontario depends on what heats your home today. Homes on electric baseboards, oil, propane or wood get far more than homes on Enbridge gas. We re-read the program's heat pump page on October 9, 2026 and its requirements document on October 3. This page shows the rate for your fuel, the rules that trip people up, and the steps to claim.

What Ontario pays for a heat pump, by what you heat with now

These amounts come from Home Renovation Savings, run through Save on Energy and Enbridge. A home energy assessment is not needed for this stream. A participating HVAC contractor and written pre-approval are.

Open

Air-source, electric, oil, propane or wood heat

$1,250 a ton, up to $7,500

For a cold-climate air-source heat pump that replaces electric resistance, oil, propane or wood as the main heat. A 3-ton system pays $3,750, and 6 tons reaches the cap.

Open

Air-source, Enbridge gas heat

$500 a ton, up to $2,000

For homes mainly heated by an Enbridge gas furnace or boiler. A 4-ton system reaches the cap.

Open

Ground-source

$2,000 a ton, up to $12,000

For electric, oil, propane or wood homes. Gas homes get a flat $3,000.

Open

Rented heat pump

$500 a ton, up to $2,000

Rentals qualify for any main heating fuel. Ground-source rentals get a flat $3,000.

Open

Toronto home energy loan

Up to $125,000

Toronto homeowners can borrow for upgrades and repay on the property tax bill. We have not re-checked the current rate, so confirm it on the City's page.

Open

Peak Perks

$75 to join, then $20 a year

For homes with central air or a heat pump controlled by an eligible Wi-Fi thermostat. It is a thermostat program, not a heat pump rebate.

Starts soon

National Heat Pump Rebate

Up to $10,000 or $2,000

Announced by the federal government on October 8, 2026. It is not open yet. Launch is expected in early 2027, and the rules are not published.

The gap between the two main rates is the headline. Most Ontario homes heat with natural gas, so most homeowners land on the $500 a ton rate, which is capped at $2,000. Homes on baseboards, oil, propane or wood are the ones that reach $7,500.

Which row is yours?

You heat with electric baseboards or an electric furnace. You get the higher rate: $1,250 a ton, up to $7,500. You must be on the Ontario grid. Cornwall Electric customers are on the Hydro Quebec grid, so they are not eligible unless they heat with Enbridge gas.

You heat with oil, propane or wood. Same high rate. If you heat with oil, the tank must be removed or emptied by a TSSA contractor.

You heat with natural gas from Enbridge. You get $500 a ton, up to $2,000. You must have an active Enbridge residential account and heat mainly with a gas furnace or boiler.

You already have a heat pump for space heating. You are not eligible. Replacing an old heat pump earns nothing under this stream.

How the tons work

The rebate is paid per ton of rated heating capacity. One ton is 12,000 BTU an hour, measured at 8.3C. Your contractor reads the number off the NRCan listing for your exact model. If you need more than one outdoor unit to heat the whole home, up to three can count, their tons add up, and you still get one rebate. For example, two 3-ton units on an electric-heat home reach the $7,500 cap.

The rules that trip people up

Pre-approval is mandatory. Your contractor must get written approval before the heat pump goes in. An install done first earns nothing. The heat pump must be an active cold-climate model on NRCan's list, and the contractor must be a participating HVAC contractor from the program's list.

For homes heated by electricity, oil or wood, only sizing options C and D qualify. In plain words, the heat pump has to carry most or all of the heating, not just cool the house. Ask your contractor to show you NRCan's sizing and selection form before you sign. Existing electric or wood heat can stay as backup.

You must own the home. A landlord applies on behalf of tenants. The home must be a detached house, semi, row house, townhome or mobile home on a permanent foundation, and it cannot be a new build that has been lived in for six months or less. The requirements document also allows a single unit in a duplex, triplex or stacked townhome that has its own heating and meter. The web page lists only the narrower house types, so ask the program if you are in a duplex.

Is there a deadline?

As of October 9, 2026, the program page and its requirements document show no end date for the heat pump stream. That is not a promise. Funding and terms can change, so if you are planning a job, get your pre-approval in rather than waiting. We will update this page if an end date appears.

The new federal rebate: what we know and what we do not

On October 8, 2026, the federal government announced a National Heat Pump Rebate. It would pay up to $10,000 to households below the median income and $2,000 to other eligible homeowners. The government says launch is expected in early 2027. It has not said which home types, heating fuels or income cut-off will qualify.

That means we cannot tell you if it stacks with Ontario's program, or if work done now will count. If your furnace is failing this winter, do not wait for it. Ontario's rebate is open now. We will update this page when the federal rules come out.

How to claim an Ontario heat pump rebate

  1. Work out your row: what heats the home now, and whether you own it. Gas, electric, oil, propane or wood each have different rates.
  2. Fill in the program's short eligibility form. It sends you a list of participating HVAC contractors.
  3. Get quotes from at least two of those contractors. Ask each to confirm the heat pump model is on NRCan's cold-climate list and to show the sizing form.
  4. Let your contractor file the pre-installation application. Wait for written approval before any work starts.
  5. Have the heat pump installed. Your contractor files the post-installation application within 30 days.
  6. Watch for the cheque. It should arrive by mail within 60 days of post-installation approval.

Common questions

How much is the Ontario heat pump rebate in 2026?

It depends on what you heat with now. Electric, oil, propane or wood homes get $1,250 a ton, up to $7,500, for an air-source heat pump. Enbridge gas homes get $500 a ton, up to $2,000. Ground-source systems pay up to $12,000 for non-gas homes and a flat $3,000 for gas homes.

Is there a deadline for the Ontario heat pump rebate?

We found no end date on the program page or its requirements document as of October 9, 2026. Terms can change, so file your pre-approval before you plan to buy.

Do I need a home energy assessment?

No. This stream does not require one. You need a participating HVAC contractor and written pre-approval before the install.

Can I get the rebate if I already have a heat pump?

No. A home that already has a heat pump for space heating is not eligible, and replacing it does not earn a rebate.

Can I claim if my heat pump is already installed?

No. Pre-approval is mandatory, and installs done before approval are not eligible.

Will the new federal heat pump rebate change this?

It might, but the rules are not out. The federal government announced it on October 8, 2026, with up to $10,000 or $2,000, and a launch expected in early 2027. We will update this page when it says who qualifies.
