The battery rebate means no net metering

Read this before you ask for quotes. The battery rebate only works with new solar in the same project, and a net metered system is not eligible. The Toronto Star reported on November 1, 2026 that the Ministry of Energy's own analysis found a payback of 73 to 100+ years for solar and a battery on the rebate path. A battery can still be worth it for backup power in Kitchener. Ask installers to price it both ways, and read our guide to the rebate or net metering choice.

Enova Power

Enova Power approves the distributed energy resource connection. Ask it how a load-displacement system with a battery is connected.

Coaching, not a loan

Retrofit Waterloo Region's free Energy Coach is open across the Region. Its loan is limited to GrandBridge customers in Cambridge and North Dumfries, so Enova customers in Kitchener cannot use it.

Housing

66% of households live in an eligible house type and 60% own.

Already have solar? Not eligible

The program pays for first-time installations only. A home that already has a solar or storage system is not eligible, so an owner who installed panels years ago cannot add a battery and claim this rebate. If you are planning solar and a battery, do them as one project under one pre-installation application.

The kWh arithmetic

The rebate is $300 per kWh, so a 10 kWh battery earns $3,000 and a battery of about 16.7 kWh reaches the $5,000 cap. The rebate is also limited to half the eligible cost, so if the battery costs $8,000 before HST the most you can receive is $4,000. Ask the Kitchener installer to show the capacity in kWh and the pre-HST price on the quote.

Approvals and the order of events

The contractor submits the pre-installation application with a quote dated within 60 days. You cannot buy or install before written IESO pre-approval and utility approval. The utility's distributed energy resource approval covers the battery, and the post-installation file needs the connection agreement and the ESA certificate within 30 days of installation.

What counts as eligible cost

Eligible costs cover equipment, permits, approvals and labour, including the inverter if applicable and necessary controls. HST, financing, insurance, maintenance and spare parts are not eligible costs, and combined funding from other sources cannot exceed 100% of the cost.

How to claim in Kitchener

  1. Confirm you own an eligible house type and plan solar and the battery as one project.
  2. Talk to Enova Power about the distributed energy resource approval for the battery and the maximum size it allows.
  3. Choose a licensed electrical contractor and ask for one quote covering solar and battery, showing kW, kWh and pre-HST costs.
  4. Let the contractor file the pre-installation application and wait for written IESO pre-approval and utility approval before buying anything.
  5. After the install, check that the post-installation application is filed within 30 days with the ESA certificate and connection agreement.
