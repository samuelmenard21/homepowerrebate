1. Whose programs should a Kitchener homeowner check first?

Enova Power is the utility we use for Kitchener. Rebates often depend on who supplies your power or gas, so check yours first.

2. On average, how low does the temperature fall in Kitchener in January?

Our weather sample for Kitchener (2015-2024) shows an average January low of -9 °C. Cold-climate heat pumps are tested at low temperatures, so check the rating at your coldest days, not just the average.

3. How cold did Kitchener get at its worst between 2015 and 2024?

The lowest temperature in the sample for Kitchener was -31 °C. A right-sized heat pump is chosen for your coldest design day, not the average.

4. What is the largest 'up to' heat pump figure on our Kitchener summary?

Our summary for Kitchener: $1,250/ton up to $7,500 (air source, non-gas homes); up to $12,000 ground source; gas-heated homes $500/ton up to $2,000. The amount you get depends on your home, income and utility, so use the calculator.

5. About what share of homes in Kitchener are rented?

The 2021 Census counts 40,230 renter households out of 99,815 in Kitchener. Many rebates go to owners, and some programs have separate rules for landlords and tenants.

What Home Renovation Savings pays for a heat pump in Kitchener

Kitchener housing

The 2021 Census counts 47% of Kitchener households in detached houses, 66% in an eligible type and 60% owning. 21,465 are owner-occupied detached houses built before 1981. 15% live in low-rise apartments and 3,155 in duplexes. 4.7% report needing major repairs.

Coaching and the loan

Retrofit Waterloo Region's free Energy Coach is open to homeowners across the Region. Its loan, up to $40,000 at 2.5% over 10 or 15 years, is repaid on the GrandBridge Energy bill and is limited to customers in Cambridge and North Dumfries. Kitchener is served by Enova Power, so the loan does not apply. A coach can help you decide between ductless and central systems before you ask for quotes.

Landlords and owners

The owner must own the home and pay for the installation. Where an owner has tenants, the owner applies, and a tenant cannot apply on the owner's behalf. A Kitchener landlord with a rented upper unit should confirm that the home qualifies as an eligible type and has a central heating system before applying.

Ductless or central

The program accepts centrally ducted, partially ducted and ductless cold-climate systems, including single-head and multi-head mini-splits. A multi-head system must still meet the whole-home heating requirement. Ask the contractor to show how many indoor heads are planned and which rooms they cover.

What the quote must show

The work order has to be fully itemized in the format of the program's sample invoice. For a ground-source system it must also show the loop design and the closed-loop heating capacity on which the rebate is calculated.

How to claim in Kitchener

  1. Book a free coaching session with the Region if you want a second opinion.
  2. Confirm eligibility on the program's short form and get the list of participating HVAC contractors.
  3. Choose a contractor from that list. Ask for a load calculation and the NRCan sizing tool form for each outdoor unit.
  4. Wait for written pre-approval before any installation starts.
  5. After the install, check that the contractor files the post-installation application within 30 days, with the final itemized invoice and photos.
