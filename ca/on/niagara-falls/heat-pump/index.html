Most Niagara Falls homes heat with a gas furnace, which puts a heat pump switch in the lower tier ($2,000–$3,000) unless you're actually replacing electric baseboard, oil, or propane heat, in which case you land in the $7,500–$12,000 tier instead. Your electricity comes from Niagara Peninsula Energy Inc. (NPEI), your gas from Enbridge — both matter for figuring out which rebate tier applies and who to call about a switch.

No local top-up in Niagara Falls

We checked the City of Niagara Falls directly and found no municipal loan or rebate program layered on top of this, unlike Toronto's HELP loan or Guelph and Peterborough's Better Homes financing. What's above is the whole picture — the same provincial Home Renovation Savings Program available to every Ontario homeowner.

How to claim it

  1. Complete the eligibility form on the Save on Energy portal
  2. Select a participating HVAC contractor
  3. Get mandatory pre-installation approval before work starts
  4. Installation is completed
  5. Contractor submits the post-installation application
  6. Rebate cheque arrives within 60 days of approval

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Niagara Falls?

It depends on your current heating fuel. Enbridge Gas customers get up to $2,000 for an air-source heat pump or $3,000 for ground-source. If you heat with electric, oil, propane, or wood, you get up to $7,500 (air-source) or $12,000 (ground-source). This is the same provincial structure used across Ontario.

Does Niagara Falls add anything on top of the provincial heat pump rebate?

No. We checked the City of Niagara Falls directly and found no local top-up program, unlike Toronto's HELP loan or Guelph and Peterborough's Better Homes financing. Niagara Falls homeowners work with the standard Home Renovation Savings Program numbers above.
