What Ottawa homeowners can claim — no assessment needed

In 2026, an Ottawa homeowner can claim up to $12,000 for a heat pump, $10,000 for solar + battery combined, up to $1,250 for attic insulation, $100 for a smart thermostat, and up to $200 per appliance — on top of a separate $7,700 assessment-required insulation/windows tier and the City's Better Homes Ottawa loan.

Ottawa runs on Hydro Ottawa electricity and Enbridge Gas. The provincial Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP) is identical here to the rest of Ontario — the same rebate categories and tiers as Toronto — with Ottawa's own financing program layered on top instead of a cash top-up.

Honest math for Ottawa

A typical household stacking a heat pump with solar or battery nets $4,000–$17,000 in direct rebates — identical to Toronto, since this is a provincial program. The Better Homes Ottawa loan is where Ottawa differs most from Toronto: a single 20-year/4.33% rate instead of multiple term options, a 4% admin fee, and gas upgrades explicitly excluded from what it will finance.

We could not confirm a hard expiry date for the HRSP rebates on the official site as of this writing — check homerenovationsavings.ca directly before relying on any deadline you see elsewhere.

Attic insulation pays more if you bundle it here

Attic and cathedral-ceiling insulation are available on the no-assessment path above (up to $1,250 / $750), but the same work pays more if bundled into this assessment-required tier instead: up to $1,500 for attic (from a starting point of R-12 or less) and $780 for cathedral ceiling/flat roof. If you're already doing an assessment for other upgrades, do attic insulation here rather than through the standalone no-assessment path.

Ottawa's own financing program

The Better Homes Ottawa loan — stacks on top

The Better Homes Ottawa Loan Program is financing, not a rebate — low-interest money tied to your property, repaid through your property tax bill. It's delivered by EnviroCentre, an Ottawa-based non-profit, on behalf of the City.

City of Ottawa

Better Homes Ottawa Loan Program

$10,000–$125,000

Covers thermal envelope upgrades (insulation, air sealing, windows, doors, green roofs), heat pumps (air-source and ground-source), thermostats, ventilation, water heating (including solar and heat recovery), solar panels, Level 2 EV chargers, battery storage, water efficiency measures, and some health/safety and climate-adaptation work.

Rate: 4.33% fixed over a 20-year term — a single rate/term option, unlike Toronto's HELP which offers 5/10/15/20-year choices. A 4% administrative charge applies. Loan amount is capped at $125,000 or 10% of your property's current assessed value, whichever is less; $10,000 minimum. Natural gas-powered upgrades are explicitly excluded — this program finances switching away from gas, not replacing it. Official Better Homes Ottawa loan page →

FAQ for Ottawa residents

How much does a heat pump rebate cover in Ottawa?

It depends on the heat pump type and your current fuel. Enbridge Gas customers switching from gas get up to $2,000 for an air-source unit or $3,000 for ground-source. Households switching from electricity, oil, propane, or wood get up to $7,500 for air-source or up to $12,000 for ground-source. This is the same provincial HRSP structure as the rest of Ontario.

What is the Better Homes Ottawa Loan Program?

A City of Ottawa loan of $10,000 to $125,000 (or 10% of your property's assessed value, whichever is lower), at a fixed 4.33% rate over a 20-year term, repaid through your property tax bill. A 4% administrative fee applies. It covers heat pumps, insulation, windows, solar, battery storage, and EV chargers — but natural gas-powered upgrades are explicitly excluded.

Who is my utility in Ottawa?

Most Ottawa homes are on Hydro Ottawa for electricity and Enbridge Gas for natural gas, which serves roughly 3 in 4 Ottawa homes.

Can I use the Better Homes Ottawa loan for a gas furnace upgrade?

No. The program explicitly excludes natural gas-powered upgrades — it's meant to finance the switch away from gas, not a like-for-like gas replacement. A gas furnace upgrade would need to rely on Enbridge Gas's own rebate programs instead.
