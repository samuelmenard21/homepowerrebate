What Richmond Hill homeowners can claim — no assessment needed

In 2026, a Richmond Hill homeowner can claim up to $12,000 for a heat pump, $10,000 for solar + battery combined, up to $1,250 for attic insulation, $125 for a smart thermostat, and up to $200 per appliance — on top of a separate assessment-required insulation/windows tier.

Richmond Hill runs on Alectra Utilities electricity and Enbridge Gas. The province's Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP) is identical here to the rest of Ontario — the same categories and tiers as Toronto and Ottawa.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most of the rebate figures on this page assume you own the home outright and control the whole building. That's not everyone in Richmond Hill. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

The HRSP rebates above go to whoever pays for and owns the upgrade — typically your landlord, not you. If you want a heat pump, insulation, or solar added to the property, the ask has to go to your landlord; point them to this page and the official homerenovationsavings.ca program page so they can apply directly.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, a smart thermostat, in-suite windows — may still qualify for the HRSP rebates above, but anything touching shared systems or the building's exterior typically needs your condo board's written approval first. Get that approval before applying, since the rebate paperwork can ask for proof the work was authorized.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation, or solar for an entire building are a group decision that goes through the condo board rather than an individual owner. There's no Richmond Hill-specific multi-unit retrofit program described on this page, so a condo board would be working from the provincial HRSP program alone. The safest first step for a board is the official HRSP program finder above, or contacting the program directly to ask what a multi-unit building qualifies for before assuming the single-family numbers on this page apply.

FAQ for Richmond Hill residents

How much does a heat pump rebate cover in Richmond Hill?

It depends on the heat pump type and your current fuel. Enbridge Gas customers switching from gas get up to $2,000 for an air-source unit or $3,000 for ground-source. Households switching from electricity, oil, propane, or wood get up to $7,500 for air-source or up to $12,000 for ground-source. This is the same provincial HRSP structure as the rest of Ontario.

Does Richmond Hill have its own home energy loan program?

Not yet. Richmond Hill is running a joint feasibility study with Markham on a future retrofit program, but nothing is live as of this writing — the provincial HRSP stack is what's actually available to Richmond Hill homeowners today.

Who is my utility in Richmond Hill?

Most Richmond Hill homes are on Alectra Utilities for electricity and Enbridge Gas for natural gas.
