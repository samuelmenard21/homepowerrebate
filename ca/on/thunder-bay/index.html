These are provincial HRSP amounts — the same categories and tiers apply in Thunder Bay as in Toronto, Ottawa, or any other Ontario city.

Local programs

No Thunder Bay municipal rebate program — and a historical note worth clearing up

We found no city-specific loan or rebate program

Unlike Toronto's HELP loan, we found no Thunder Bay municipal home-energy loan or rebate program. Thunder Bay homeowners rely on the province-wide Home Renovation Savings Program and the federal Canada Greener Homes Loan — the same as most Ontario cities without a local top-up.

A now-closed program used to exclude Thunder Bay — that no longer applies

An older provincial program called HomeEnergySaver once offered rebates up to $10,000 for ground-source heat pumps and $5,000 for air-source, and it excluded Thunder Bay while including nearby Kenora — a real anomaly at the time. HomeEnergySaver's enrollment is now closed, and its heat pump rebates have been folded into the current Home Renovation Savings Program, which does not exclude Thunder Bay. If you've seen a claim that Thunder Bay is shut out of a provincial heat pump rebate, it's describing this closed program, not the one available today.

Not every Thunder Bay homeowner owns a detached house

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Everything above assumes you own your home outright and control the whole building. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo in Thunder Bay:

If you rent

The structural rebates above — heat pump, insulation, windows, solar, battery — attach to the property, so they go to whoever owns it, not to you. If you want one of these upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord; point them to this page and the official homerenovationsavings.ca program details. Some smaller rebates, like the smart thermostat rebate, may not require proof of ownership at purchase — but confirm eligibility for your specific situation with the program directly before assuming.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, in-suite windows — may qualify for the rebates above, but usually need your condo board's written approval first, especially for anything touching shared systems or exterior walls. Get that approval documented before applying; the HRSP program requires proof the work was authorized.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

A heat pump, insulation, or solar project for the whole building is a group decision that goes through your condo board or property manager, not individual owners, and the per-unit rebate math is likely different from the single-family figures on this page. This page doesn't have confirmed multi-unit numbers for Thunder Bay — your board should contact the HRSP program directly at homerenovationsavings.ca to confirm what applies to a multi-unit building.

FAQ for Thunder Bay residents

How much does a heat pump rebate cover in Thunder Bay?

It depends on the heat pump type and your current fuel. Enbridge Gas customers switching from gas get up to $2,000 for an air-source unit or $3,000 for ground-source. Households switching from electricity, oil, propane, or wood get up to $7,500 for air-source or up to $12,000 for ground-source. This is the same provincial HRSP structure as the rest of Ontario.

Does Thunder Bay have its own home energy loan or rebate program?

No. We found no Thunder Bay municipal loan or rebate program like Toronto's HELP loan. Homeowners here rely entirely on the province-wide Home Renovation Savings Program and the Canada Greener Homes Loan.

Was Thunder Bay ever excluded from a provincial heat pump rebate?

Yes, historically. An older provincial program called HomeEnergySaver offered up to $10,000 for ground-source heat pumps and $5,000 for air-source, and it excluded Thunder Bay while including nearby Kenora. That program's enrollment is now closed, and its heat pump rebates have been folded into the current Home Renovation Savings Program, which does not exclude Thunder Bay. If you see this exclusion mentioned elsewhere, it's describing a program that no longer exists.

Who is my utility in Thunder Bay?

Thunder Bay's electricity comes from Synergy North, a municipally-owned utility formed in January 2019 when Thunder Bay Hydro merged with Kenora Hydro — it now serves both cities. Natural gas is supplied by Enbridge Gas.
