Heat Pump Rebates in Toronto

The rebate depends on what heats your Toronto home today: gas homes get $500 per ton, up to $2,000, and homes heated by electric resistance, oil, propane or wood get $1,250 per ton, up to $7,500.

Toronto: Federal heat pump rebate coming in early 2027: up to $10,000 for households below the median income and $2,000 for other eligible homeowners. Home Renovation Savings pays now, and Ottawa says the federal rebate can be combined with provincial programs. Read what is confirmed or decide whether to wait.

What Home Renovation Savings pays for a heat pump in Toronto

We re-read the program's heat pump page and its requirements document on October 3, 2026. No home energy assessment is needed for this stream, but a participating HVAC contractor and written pre-approval are.

Open

Air-source, electric, oil, propane or wood heat

$1,250/ton, up to $7,500

For a cold-climate air-source heat pump replacing electric resistance, oil, propane or wood as the main heat. A 3-ton system pays $3,750 and 6 tons or more reaches the cap.

Open

Air-source, Enbridge gas heat

$500/ton, up to $2,000

For homes primarily heated by an Enbridge gas furnace or boiler. The cap is reached at 4 tons.

Open

Ground-source

$2,000/ton, up to $12,000

For electric, oil, propane or wood homes. Gas homes get a flat $3,000. The system must be a full installation sized to the home's load.

Open

Rented heat pump

$500/ton, up to $2,000

Rentals are eligible for any primary heating fuel: $500 per ton for air-source, up to $2,000, or a flat $3,000 for ground-source.

Open

More than one outdoor unit

Up to three units, one rebate

If several outdoor units are needed to heat the whole home, their tons add up and one rebate is paid on the total, for example 3 + 3 tons for $7,500.

Open

One rebate, pre-approved

Cheque in 60 days

One rebate per home. The contractor needs written pre-approval before installing, files the post-installation application within 30 days, and the cheque arrives within 60 days of approval.

Open

Toronto HELP loan

Repaid on your tax bill

A City loan for energy upgrades. Current rates were not re-verified.

The requirements document lists no end date, but the program's other streams close in late 2026, so check the program page before you sign a contract. Pre-approval is mandatory: an installation done first earns nothing.

Which Toronto homes the stream accepts

The 2021 Census counts 1,160,895 households in Toronto. Only about 35% live in a single detached, semi-detached or row home, and 52% own. The requirements document also lets an individual unit in a stacked townhome, duplex or triplex apply, if it has its own heating system and meter, and Toronto has 46,465 households in duplexes. The program's web page lists only the narrower house types, so confirm with the program before relying on that. Apartments in towers (47% of households) are out.

The rate depends on what you burn now

The rebate follows the heating fuel before the heat pump goes in. A Toronto home on an Enbridge gas furnace gets $500 per ton, capped at $2,000, which a 4-ton system reaches. A home with electric baseboards gets $1,250 per ton up to $7,500. If you rent the heat pump instead of buying it, the rate is $500 per ton for any fuel. Check which category you are in before comparing quotes.

Replacing an existing heat pump does not count

A home that already uses a heat pump for space heating is not eligible. Replacing an old heat pump with a newer one earns nothing under this stream. A home with an air conditioner only is a different case, because a cooling-only unit is not a heat pump used for space heating, so ask the program how your system is classed.

HELP loan

Toronto's Home Energy Loan Program lets owners repay an upgrade on the property tax bill. A heat pump is the sort of project it is built for, and the rebate arrives by cheque within 60 days of approval. We have not re-verified the current rate, so confirm it on the City's page.

Sizing: the program checks it

For electric, oil and wood homes, only NRCan sizing options C and D qualify, which means the heat pump supplies most or all of the heating. Your contractor must submit NRCan's sizing and selection form for each outdoor unit. Ask to see it before you sign, because a system sized mainly for cooling will not pass.

How to claim in Toronto

  1. Find out what your main heat is today: gas, electric resistance, oil, propane or wood.
  2. Confirm eligibility on the program's short form and get the list of participating HVAC contractors.
  3. Choose a contractor from that list. Ask for a load calculation and the NRCan sizing tool form for each outdoor unit.
  4. Wait for written pre-approval before any installation starts.
  5. After the install, check that the contractor files the post-installation application within 30 days, with the final itemized invoice and photos.
