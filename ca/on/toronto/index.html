What Toronto homeowners can claim

In 2026, a Toronto homeowner can claim up to $7,500 for a heat pump, $5,000 for solar, up to $5,000 for a battery (up to $10,000 combined with solar), plus a separate $125,000 low-interest loan from the City to help pay for it all.

Toronto runs on Toronto Hydro electricity and Enbridge Gas. The province's Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP) is the backbone rebate here — and unlike BC, the heat pump amount depends on what fuel you're switching from, not your household income.

🔥 Heat Pump

$1,500–$7,500

$500/ton up to $2,000 if you're an Enbridge Gas customer switching from gas (avg. $1,500). $1,250/ton up to $7,500 if you currently heat with electricity, oil, propane, or wood (avg. $3,750).

$1,000/kW, capped at 50% of total cost. You must choose this rebate or a net-metering agreement with your utility — not both.

🔋 Home Battery

Stacks with the solar rebate for up to $10,000 combined, for a behind-the-meter storage system.

🏦 Toronto HELP Loan

up to $125,000

City-run financing, repaid via your property tax bill over up to 20 years. Covers heat pumps, insulation, windows, solar, battery, and EV chargers — stacks on top of the rebates above.

Honest math for Toronto

A typical household nets $3,000–$10,000 in direct rebates (heat pump + solar or battery), with the HELP loan available to finance the rest of the project cost — not free money, but a much lower rate than a typical renovation loan.

Both the HRSP heat pump and solar rebates are scheduled to expire November 30, 2026 — if you're planning an upgrade, applying before then matters.

Available across Ontario

Provincial & utility programs — what each one is

These are the backbone. They're the same whether you're in Toronto or elsewhere in Ontario — what changes locally is the City of Toronto's own HELP loan, below.

Through the Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP)

Provincial

HRSP heat pump rebate

$1,500–$7,500

A cold-climate heat pump both heats and cools your home. The rebate splits by what you're replacing: $500/ton (up to $2,000) if you're an Enbridge Gas customer switching from gas, or $1,250/ton (up to $7,500) if you're switching from electric baseboards, oil, propane, or wood.

How it works: Apply through the Save on Energy portal after installation by a participating contractor. Official program page →
Provincial

HRSP solar rebate

up to $5,000

$1,000 per kW of installed capacity, capped at 50% of total project cost, for a behind-the-meter home solar system designed for load displacement (using the power you generate, not exporting it).

How it works: You must choose between this rebate or a net-metering agreement with your local distribution company — not both. If you plan to export surplus power, net metering may be the better fit; talk to your installer about which suits your usage.
Provincial

HRSP battery rebate

up to $5,000

For a behind-the-meter home battery storage system. Combine it with the solar rebate for up to $10,000 total.

Expires: Both the solar and battery HRSP rebates are scheduled to end November 30, 2026.

Through Enbridge Gas (your natural-gas utility)

Enbridge Gas

Fuel-switching consideration

affects your rebate tier

If you're currently an Enbridge Gas customer heating with a gas furnace, switching to a heat pump puts you in the lower HRSP tier ($500/ton, avg. $1,500) rather than the higher one. This isn't a separate rebate — it's baked into how the HRSP heat pump amount is calculated above.

Why it's structured this way: The program incentivizes switching off electricity/oil/propane/wood more heavily, since gas furnaces are already relatively efficient and lower-emission than oil.
Toronto's own financing program

The Toronto HELP loan — stacks on top

The Home Energy Loan Program (HELP) isn't a rebate — it's low-interest financing tied to your property, not you personally, repaid through your property tax bill. It's how Toronto homeowners cover the cost that's left over after the HRSP rebates above.

City of Toronto

Home Energy Loan Program (HELP)

up to $125,000

Covers air-source heat pumps, window/door replacement, insulation, air sealing, geothermal, high-efficiency water heaters, furnaces/boilers/AC, tankless water heaters, drain-water heat recovery, toilets, solar hot water, rooftop solar PV, EV chargers (Level 2), and battery storage.

Rates (fixed, valid through Dec 31, 2025 — confirm current rates before applying): 5-year term 3.08% · 10-year 3.92% · 15-year 4.41% · 20-year 4.67% (20-year term available for solar, windows, geothermal, and heat pump projects). A 2% administrative charge applies. Loan amount is capped at 10% of your property's current value assessment or $125,000, whichever is less. Official HELP program page →

FAQ for Toronto residents

How much does a heat pump rebate cover in Toronto?

Ontario's Home Renovation Savings Program pays $500 per ton up to $2,000 if you're an Enbridge Gas customer switching from gas (average about $1,500), or $1,250 per ton up to $7,500 if you currently heat with electricity, oil, propane, or wood (average about $3,750).

Can I get both a solar rebate and net metering?

No. The Home Renovation Savings Program pays up to $5,000 for solar, but you have to choose between that rebate or a net-metering agreement with your local utility — not both on the same system.

What is the Toronto HELP loan?

A City of Toronto loan of up to $125,000, repaid through your property tax bill over up to 20 years at a fixed rate. It covers heat pumps, insulation, windows, solar, battery storage, EV chargers and more, and stacks on top of the provincial and utility rebates rather than replacing them.

Who is my utility in Toronto?

Most Toronto homes are on Toronto Hydro for electricity and Enbridge Gas for natural gas. Your rebate amount for a heat pump depends on which fuel you're switching from, not which company delivers your power.
