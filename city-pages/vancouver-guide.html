Why Vancouver Homeowners Are Choosing Heat Pumps

Vancouver's mild, wet winters mean heat pumps operate at peak efficiency. Your furnace burns gas. A heat pump moves ambient heat into your home—even in Vancouver's 3–5°C winters. That's efficient.

  • Annual heating savings: $200–$400/year vs gas furnace
  • Summer cooling: One unit handles both; no separate AC needed
  • Grid benefit: Lower Mainland grid stress peaks in January. Your battery (enrolled in Peak Saver) helps stabilize the grid while you earn rewards
  • Upfront cost: $7K–$11K installed, minus $4K–$8K rebate = often free or near-free

The Eguana Battery: Why It Matters for Vancouver

Eguana batteries qualify for $5,000 BC Hydro rebate when paired with solar. They pair perfectly with Vancouver's solar + heat pump strategy:

  • Unlocks Peak Saver: $300–$500/year for sharing battery power during winter peak demand
  • 10-year payback: Battery saves $3,000–$5,000 through avoided peak charges + Peak Saver rewards
  • Grid support: Vancouver's grid is integrated and stable, but winter demand is real. Your battery helps.
Vancouver advantage: Bundle heat pump + solar + Eguana battery. Total net cost after rebates: $1K–$5K. Total 10-year value: $20K+.

Common Questions Vancouver Homeowners Ask

Will Peak Saver reduce my comfort?

No. Peak Saver only draws from your battery during peak windows (4–8pm on cold evenings). Your heat pump maintains your set temperature using grid power.

What if Vancouver has an outage?

Your battery provides 4–6 hours of backup for essentials (fridge, lights, water heater, heat pump). Vancouver outages are rare due to grid stability.

Do I need solar if I'm doing heat pump + battery?

No. Heat pump alone gets $4K–$8K. Battery requires solar for full $5K rebate. For most Vancouver homeowners, bundling all three makes economic sense.
