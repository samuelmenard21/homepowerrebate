What an energy advisor does

A registered advisor is a third-party expert who does not sell equipment, so the advice is about your house, not a product. These are the standard services. Ask this company which ones it offers.

EnerGuide evaluation

Shows where your house is losing energy and which upgrades will make the biggest difference, so you spend money in the right order.

You get: an EnerGuide rating and a report to take to a rebate program.

Heat load calculation

Works out how much heating and cooling the house actually needs, room by room, so the heat pump is sized properly.

You get: a number any installer can quote against.

Airtightness testing

A blower door test pulls air through the house to measure how leaky it is.

You get: a before and after measure of how airtight the house is.

New builds

BC: Step Code energy modelling and airtightness testing show a new build meets the BC Energy Step Code.

Why the visit matters

A heat load calculation done without visiting the house is an estimate. Equipment sized without one is often bigger than the house needs, which costs more up front and runs less efficiently.

Questions people ask

Do I need an energy advisor?

British Columbia: Not for every project. BC Hydro's full heat pump rebate needs a heat load calculation showing the unit meets 100% of your heating at -5 °C, and some add-on rebates need an EnerGuide evaluation by a program-qualified advisor. Ontario: Not for every project. Ontario's Home Renovation Savings assessment stream needs one: a registered energy advisor at an authorized service organization does the pre-audit and the post-audit. The attic-only stream does not need an assessment.

How is an advisor different from an installer?

An advisor tests and measures your home and does not sell equipment. An installer fits it. The advisor's numbers help the installer size it properly.

What does it cost?

NRCan says both evaluations together average $600 to $1,000, depending on the provider and province, and are usually paid after the service. Ask this company for its own price. NRCan, read October 17, 2026. Ontario: The program also pays a $600 audit rebate in its assessment stream, so ask the advisor how that shows up for you.

Can I book one after the install?

British Columbia: For the full BC Hydro heat pump rebate, the heat load calculation decides the size of heat pump that qualifies, so get it before you buy. Check the page for any add-on rebate you want, since some need an evaluation first. Ontario: For the assessment stream, no. The pre-audit has to come before any work starts, and the post-audit must be done by December 31, 2026.

How do I find a registered advisor?

British Columbia: Better Homes BC lists advisors registered with NRCan who also finished BC rebate training. It tells homeowners to interview and choose their own. Energy coaches answer questions at 1-844-881-9790. Ontario: Homeowners book through an NRCan licensed service organization, which sends a registered advisor. Ontario's assessment stream needs an advisor at an authorized service organization. NRCan lists them.
