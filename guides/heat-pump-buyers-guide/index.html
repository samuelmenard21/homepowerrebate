Heat Pump Buyer's Guide for BC 2026

Everything you need to know before choosing a heat pump, getting quotes, and claiming up to $21,000 in rebates

At a Glance

Question Answer
Do heat pumps work in BC winter? Yes. Modern units rated to -20°C work fine. Some models go to -25°C.
How much do they cost? $12K–$16K installed. After $9K–$16K rebates: $3K–$7K net.
How much will I save? $150–$250/month on heating. Payback: 3–7 years.
Installation time? 3 days. Minor electrical work. You stay in your home.
Best brands? Bosch, Mitsubishi, Daikin, Fujitsu. All HPCN-certified in BC.

Why Heat Pumps?

Heating is your biggest bill. Most BC homes spend $1,200–$2,000/year on heating (gas or electric).

Heat pumps replace furnaces and use 60–75% less energy to do the same job. A 10-year investment saves $15K–$25K in heating costs.

Plus: AC in summer, backup heating in winter, qualify for $9K–$21K in government rebates.

The catch: Upfront cost is real. But after rebates, you're paying $3K–$7K for a system that saves you $2K+/year.

Brand Comparison: What Matters in BC

Brand Efficiency Cold Climate Typical Cost Notes
Mitsubishi 9.0–10.5 HSPF -25°C ⭐⭐⭐ $13K–$16K Best for Interior BC, cold climates
Bosch 8.5–9.5 HSPF -20°C ⭐⭐ $12K–$15K Best value, solid reliability
Daikin 9.0–10.0 HSPF -20°C ⭐⭐ $13K–$16K Quieter operation
Fujitsu 9.0–10.0 HSPF -20°C ⭐⭐ $13K–$16K Premium brand, excellent warranty

What is HSPF? Heating Seasonal Performance Factor. Higher = more efficient. 9+ is excellent for BC.

Cold climate rating matters. If you're in Prince George or Kamloops, Mitsubishi's -25°C rating gives you peace of mind on extreme days. If you're in Vancouver or Victoria, -20°C is fine.

Hidden Costs (What Your Quote Might Miss)

Electrical panel upgrade
If your panel is old: $1,000–$3,000
This is usually separate from the system cost.
Refrigerant lines
Running new lines from outdoor unit: $500–$1,500
Depends on distance.
Thermostat replacement
Most systems need a new smart thermostat: $300–$500
May qualify for separate rebates.
Furnace removal
Removing old furnace: $300–$800
(Recycled/hazmat disposal)
Ductwork modifications
If converting from baseboard: $1,000–$3,000
Some homes skip this.
Permits
Municipal permits: $200–$500
HPCN installer handles this.
Pro tip: Ask for an "all-in" quote that includes electrical, permits, and removal. If they quote just the unit + install, you're missing 30–40% of the real cost.

Your Rebate Stack (Up to $21,000)

  • BC CleanBC (Provincial): $4,000–$16,000 (income-qualified, income under $110K gets higher amount)
  • BC Hydro (Utility): $2,000–$5,000 (depends on system size)
  • Federal backup (varies): $0–$3,000 (if CleanBC has limits)
  • Thermostat bonus: $50–$150 (if you upgrade to smart thermostat)

Timeline: Submit within 90 days of install. Rebates arrive 4–8 weeks after approval. Plan 4–6 months from install to rebate in hand.

Vetting Your Installer (7 Questions to Ask)

Are you HPCN-certified?
Non-negotiable as of June 2026. Required to process BC rebates.
Can you show me 3 recent installations in my area (with photos)?
Look for before/after photos, customer permission. Avoid generic pictures.
What's your timeline? (Actual dates, not "next month")
Professional installers book 4–8 weeks out. Beware of "we can start next week."
Who handles the rebate applications? Can I keep copies?
Good installers submit on your behalf AND give you copies for your records.
What happens if something breaks after install?
Ask about warranty, service availability, and their track record on service calls.
Is there an electrical panel upgrade needed? (You want to know now, not day 1)
Good installers do an assessment upfront. Surprises on install day = red flag.
Can I see your warranty document? (In writing, not verbal)
10-year compressor, 5-year parts is standard. Get it in writing before signing.

Red Flags (Walk Away If You See These)

  • ❌ Pressure to decide today. Good installers give you time. Bad ones use urgency.
  • ❌ Quote doesn't match others by $3K+. Either they're cutting corners or they're lying about what's included.
  • ❌ They push a battery you don't want. Batteries are optional. "You need one for rebates" is false.
  • ❌ No HPCN certification. They can't process rebates legally as of June 2026.
  • ❌ They can't explain cold climate ratings. If they don't know the difference between -20°C and -25°C, they're not experienced.
  • ❌ No references. No case studies. No reviews. Legitimate installers have work to show.
  • ❌ Full payment upfront. Standard is 25% deposit, 75% on completion. Full upfront = risk.

Final Tips

Get 2–3 quotes. You'll see huge variation. Compare all-in cost after rebates, not just the sticker price.

Don't DIY. Heat pump installation requires refrigerant certification and electrical work. HPCN installer is required for rebates anyway.

Track your rebates yourself. Keep copies of invoice, rebate confirmation, and approval emails. Installers disappear sometimes.

Learn your thermostat. Heat pumps work best when you use the smart thermostat correctly. Ask your installer for 30 minutes of training.
