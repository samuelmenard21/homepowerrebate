What Is Rebate Stacking?

Combine federal, provincial, and utility rebates to cut your home energy upgrade costs by thousands of dollars.

The Basic Idea

Rebate stacking means you can qualify for multiple rebates from different sources at the same time for the same upgrade. Instead of choosing one program, you get money from several.

Think of it like stacking gift cards. If you get a $50 card from one store and another $30 card from a different store, you use both on the same purchase. Rebates work the same way—you can combine them to dramatically lower your out-of-pocket cost.

Where Do Rebates Come From?

Three main sources stack together:

  • Federal government — Canada Greener Homes Initiative and other national programs
  • Provincial/state government — BC Hydro Power Smart 2.0, provincial income-based programs, and others
  • Utility company — Your local electric utility (BC Hydro, FortisBC, etc.) often adds its own incentives on top

Each program has its own eligibility rules, but many homeowners qualify for all three at once.

Real Example 1: Vancouver Solar + Battery

A Vancouver homeowner installing a 5 kW solar system with a 10 kWh battery

BC Hydro solar rebate $5,000
BC Hydro battery rebate $5,000
Peak Saver incentive (10 years) ~$3,000
Federal Greener Homes grant (if eligible) $5,000
Total Rebate Stack
$18,000+

Without stacking, this homeowner might only think about the BC Hydro rebate ($10,000). But by understanding that federal and utility incentives stack, they could reduce the net cost of a $32,000 system to around $14,000—more than half the price.

Real Example 2: Kamloops Heat Pump

A Kamloops homeowner installing a whole-home heat pump

BC Hydro heat pump rebate $4,000
Federal Greener Homes grant (if eligible) $5,000
CleanBC income-based top-up (if qualified) $2,000–$8,000
Total Rebate Stack (base case)
$9,000–$17,000

A heat pump system typically costs $8,000 to $12,000 installed. With stacking, a homeowner could walk away paying little to nothing out-of-pocket—or even get paid back in some cases if incentives exceed costs.

Important Rules for Stacking

1. Programs Must Allow It

Not every rebate can be combined. Most government programs explicitly allow stacking with other programs, but you need to check each one. For example, BC Hydro rebates stack with federal programs, but some provincial income-based programs do not.

2. You Must Be Eligible for Each One

You can't just claim rebates blindly. Each program has its own eligibility rules. For example:

  • BC Hydro rebates require you to be a BC Hydro customer (not FortisBC)
  • Federal programs may have income limits or specific product requirements
  • Some programs require installation by a certified contractor

3. The Equipment Matters

Not all brands and models qualify for all rebates. For example, Tesla Powerwall is not on BC Hydro's approved battery list, so you get zero rebate. But Eguana Evolve, LG Chem RESU, and Enphase IQ all qualify for BC Hydro rebates.

4. Timeline Matters

Some programs are closing (the original Canada Greener Homes Grant is no longer accepting applications). Others have annual caps or funding limits. If you wait, you might miss the window.

The Bottom Line

Rebate stacking can cut your upgrade costs by 30% to 60%. The difference between claiming one rebate and stacking three is often $10,000 or more. But you have to know the rules, check eligibility, and act before deadlines change.
