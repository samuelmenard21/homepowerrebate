Which rebate should I claim first?

You have 8 categories to choose from. Here's how to prioritize based on your climate, budget, and goals.

You probably don't have $50,000 to upgrade everything at once. So which home energy rebate should you claim first?

The answer depends on three things: your climate, your utility bill pain point, and your timeline. Here's how to think through it.

The Quick Answer: By Payback

1
Solar (if you're sunny)
6–12 year payback

Best ROI in Kamloops, Kelowna, and other interior regions. Rebates cover 30%+ of cost. Even in cloudier areas, 10-year payback beats alternatives.

2
Heat pump (if you heat with oil or gas)
8–15 year payback

Replacing heating fuel is your biggest savings lever. Rebates cover 30–50% of cost. In cold climates, heating is 40% of your energy bill.

3
Battery (only after solar)
12–20 year payback

Batteries amplify solar savings but cost $10K+. Claim solar first, then add battery if you want outage protection or peak-shaving rewards.

Decision Tree: Your Situation

🔥 "My heating costs are killing me"

Priority: Heat pump first. Replace oil or natural gas heating. You'll cut heating costs by 30–50% immediately. Then add solar/battery if budget allows.

☀️ "My area gets tons of sun, and I pay for electricity"

Priority: Solar first. Sunny interior BC (Kamloops, Kelowna, Penticton) sees 8–11 year payback on solar alone. Stack with battery later.

🌧️ "I'm in a rainy climate (coast, Vancouver, Victoria)"

Priority: Heat pump first. Rainy climates mean longer solar payback (12–15 years). Heating is your bigger lever. Solar still makes sense after, but heat pump wins on speed.

⚡ "I want to go all-in on electricity (solar + battery)"

Priority: Solar, then battery. Solar first for ROI. Battery amplifies savings but only works with solar. Stack rebates to get $10K+ combined.

🏠 "I want outage protection"

Priority: Solar + battery together. Batteries alone don't protect you (no output without solar, in most systems). Solar + battery combos cost more but give you resilience + savings.

What About the Others?

Water Heater

Low priority unless your current water heater is failing. Heat pump water heaters have 10–12 year payback. Smaller rebates than solar/heat pump. Claim first only if replacement is urgent.

Insulation

DO THIS FIRST if your home is poorly insulated (drafty, high heating bills). Insulation amplifies heat pump and heating savings. In poorly insulated homes, insulation + heat pump beats heat pump alone. But measure first—skip if your walls are already efficient.

EV Charger

Lowest priority for rebates (tiny incentives, $350–$1,050). Only if you already own an EV or planning to buy one soon. Not a standalone money-saver.

Smart Thermostat

Ultra-low priority. Rebates are small or free. Do it as a no-cost add-on, not a primary decision. But it amplifies heat pump and heating savings, so claim it after a heat pump.

HRV (Heat Recovery Ventilation)

Niche product. Only relevant if you're doing major insulation work or have serious air quality concerns. Rebates are modest and income-qualified.

Stacking Strategy

Claim multiple rebates from the same source for the same upgrade? No. You choose one rebate per upgrade.

Claim rebates from different sources for different upgrades? YES. This is rebate stacking. Example:

  • BC Hydro pays for solar ($5K)
  • Federal program pays for heat pump ($5K)
  • Provincial program pays for insulation ($2K)
  • Total: $12K across three upgrades

Plan your sequence to hit all stacking windows. Some rebate programs have deadlines or annual caps.

Urgency: Deadline Watch

Check these deadlines before prioritizing:

  • Federal Greener Homes — Closed to new applicants (no longer available)
  • Provincial income-qualified top-ups — Often annual caps; apply early in the fiscal year
  • Utility rebates — Usually rolling, but check your utility's sunset dates

Tight deadline for a program you qualify for? Prioritize that one, even if it's not your #1 ROI pick. You can't get that money back later.

The Real Priority: Start Somewhere

The best rebate is the one you actually claim. Analysis paralysis costs money. Pick your #1 based on this guide, get a quote, apply, and move to #2 next year.
