What an energy advisor does in Brampton

A registered energy advisor visits your home and runs an EnerGuide evaluation. They check your insulation and windows, test how much air leaks out with a blower door, and look at your heating and hot water. You get a report with a rating and a list of upgrades in order of value.

If your rebate program needs an evaluation, book the advisor first. Some programs pay only if an advisor sees the home before any work starts, and again after it is done; hire a contractor first and you can lose the rebate.

In Ontario, the Home Renovation Savings assessment stream needs a registered energy advisor at an authorized service organization. The advisor does a pre-audit before any work starts, then a post-audit after you finish at least two qualified upgrades. The program pays a $600 audit rebate, plus $500 more for three or more upgrades. In 2026, post-audits must be booked in the program's system by November 30 and done by December 31. Total rebates are capped at $5,000 for Enbridge gas-heated homes and $10,000 for homes heated by electricity, oil, propane or wood.

Source: Home Renovation Savings assessment stream requirements.

Before you hire an energy advisor

  1. Ask whether they are a registered energy advisor with Natural Resources Canada and which service organization they work with.
  2. Ask whether their evaluation counts for the rebate program you plan to use, and how soon they can book the first visit.
  3. Ask what the visit costs, what the report includes, and whether the follow-up visit after your upgrades costs extra.
  4. Ask how long the report takes and whether they will explain which upgrades pay back best in your home.
