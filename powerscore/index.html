Read this first: how reliable is this ranking? (tap to read)

PowerScore is built from the rebate numbers on our own city pages, not from a separate survey. That makes it transparent, but it also has limits you should know about.

  • One yardstick for every city. A city's dollar score is its top rebate in that category, in US dollars, compared with what the best-funded tenth of cities offer. It is no longer measured against the best city in its own province or state.
  • Currency is approximate. Canadian amounts are converted at 0.73 US dollars per Canadian dollar, a rough rate used only to put the two countries on one scale.
  • Rebate potential is not a promise. It adds up the top open rebate in each upgrade category. Most homes will not qualify for all of them, and some programs cannot be combined.
  • Ties are real. Only 45 different overall scores exist across 109 cities, because most rebate programs are province-wide.
  • Not every region has been verified. Regions marked below as "Not yet" (Colorado, Pennsylvania) have not had each program checked against its official page, so treat their scores as a rough guide.
  • Data last rebuilt 2026-10-02. Amounts change; the rebate tracker lists what has ended or moved.

How PowerScore is calculated

Every city gets a score from 0–100 in each of 8 categories, built entirely from the rebate numbers already published on each city's page — nothing here is estimated or invented.

  • 60% Dollar value — the top rebate amount available in that category, in US dollars, compared with a common yardstick: the 90th-percentile amount across every city we cover (shown below). Matching the yardstick earns full marks.
  • 25% Program status — Open programs get full credit, Funding Limited/Unclear programs get partial credit, and Closed or nonexistent programs get zero.
  • 15% Stackability — how many distinct funding layers apply (federal, provincial/state, utility, municipal) — more layers that stack together score higher.
