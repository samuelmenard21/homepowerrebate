Avista Home Rebates in Washington

Avista is the electric and gas utility that serves Spokane homes. Its rebates lean toward insulation and windows, which suits cold winters, and its heat pump money comes as a discount through your contractor.

What Avista pays Washington homes in 2026

We read Avista's Washington single-family rebate pages on October 6, 2026. Equipment has to run on Avista electric or natural gas service. Idaho and Oregon customers have different tables, so make sure you are looking at the Washington one. Buildings with five or more units use Avista's business rebates.

Open

Heat pump contractor discount

$300 to $600

Per outdoor system under about 5 tons. $300 for efficiency level HSPF2 8.1, $400 for 8.5 and $600 for 9.5. Do-it-yourself installs are not eligible.

Open

Heat pump water heater

$500

A contractor discount on a retrofit unit under 120 gallons with an efficiency rating (UEF) of 2.1 or better. New construction gets $150.

Open

Home Insulation Program

$1.75 per square foot

For attics, floors and walls that start thin. Duct insulation and duct sealing pay $400 each per home. Approved installers take it off your bill.

Open

Windows and sliding glass doors

Up to $225 per window

$180 or $225 per window with a contractor by U-factor, $480 or $600 per sliding glass door. Self-install pays half.

Open

Free weatherization for lower incomes

No cost

Insulation, air sealing and door and window work for income-qualifying customers.

A quick example

Say a Spokane home has an attic with R-11 insulation and 1,200 square feet of attic. Avista's example for 1,200 square feet of qualifying insulation is about $2,100, at $1.75 per square foot. Add replacing six windows with a rating of 0.22 or lower at $225 each with a contractor, which is $1,350, and the two rebates come to about $3,450. Neither needs a new furnace or a heat pump.

Insulation is Avista's strongest offer

Avista's Home Insulation Program does not need an income test. You book a free assessment with an approved installer, who checks how much insulation you have, tells you your out-of-pocket cost, and takes the rebate off the bill. Avista lists ten approved installers on its page as of July 2026, and most of them serve Washington. To qualify you must be an existing Avista residential customer insulating your main home, which can be a single-family house, duplex, triplex or fourplex. New construction does not qualify. The home must heat with Avista electricity or natural gas.

The rebate depends on how thin your insulation is today. Attics must start at R-11 or less and reach R-49 or more, floors R-11 or less to R-30, and walls from none to R-11. Avista says only about 10 to 30 percent of homes in its area qualify on existing insulation, so a newer home may get nothing. That is why the free assessment comes first.

No-cost help in named communities

Avista says some homes in what it calls Named Communities can get insulation with no out-of-pocket cost, paid from the Named Communities Investment Fund. A participating contractor checks whether your home qualifies during the assessment. If it does not, you can still get the regular rebate. Avista's separate Home Weatherization Program also offers free work to income-qualifying customers, but we did not read the income limits, so confirm them with Avista.

Windows pay by performance

Avista's window rebate pays more for tighter windows. A U-factor measures how fast heat leaks through a window, and a lower number is better. A window between 0.29 and 0.23 earns $180 with a contractor. A window at 0.22 or below earns $225. A sliding glass door pays $480 or $600 on the same scale. Storm windows pay $5.00 per square foot with a contractor and $4.00 if you install them. You must be replacing existing windows, and the final invoice must show the U-factor of each one. Apply within 120 days of the install, and note that bids and estimates are not accepted as proof.

Heat pumps and water heaters come through your contractor

Avista does not mail you a check for a heat pump. Instead, participating suppliers give your contractor a discount at purchase. The contractor can choose to pass it to you, so ask for it in writing. Avista lists about twenty supply companies on its page. Avista also lists a gas furnace discount of $650 and a gas boiler discount of $550 for Washington homes, but we do not feature those in our guides because they keep homes on gas.

How to claim Avista rebates

  1. Check that Avista sends your electric or gas bill. Rebates only count for equipment that runs on Avista service.
  2. For insulation, call one of the approved installers on Avista's Home Insulation Program page and book the free assessment.
  3. For windows, replace existing ones, keep the final invoice showing each U-factor, and apply within 120 days.
  4. For heat pumps and water heaters, ask your contractor in writing to include the Avista discount in the price.
  5. Email rebates@myavista.com if you cannot tell which table applies to your home.

Common questions

Who is Avista's Washington rebate for?

Existing homes, mostly single-family, that heat with Avista electricity or natural gas. Multifamily buildings with five or more units use Avista's business programs.

How much does Avista pay for insulation?

$1.75 per square foot for attic, floor and wall insulation that meets Avista's before and after levels, plus $400 each for duct insulation and duct sealing.

Does Avista have a heat pump rebate?

It gives your contractor a discount of $300, $400 or $600 per outdoor system depending on efficiency, which they may pass on to you.

How long do I have to apply for the window rebate?

Avista says to apply within 120 days of the installation, with the final invoice showing the number of windows, each U-factor and the cost.

Is there a limit based on income?

The insulation program has no income test. The Home Weatherization Program for free upgrades is for income-qualifying customers.
