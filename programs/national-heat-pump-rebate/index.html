Canada's National Heat Pump Rebate: What We Know

On October 8, 2026 the Government of Canada announced a $2 billion heat pump rebate. It is not open yet. This page keeps one list of what Ottawa has confirmed, what it has not said, and what that means for your home, and we update it every time the government publishes something new.

How Ottawa says it will work (plans, not final rules)

  1. Apply online when your heating breaks or gets old
  2. Approved in 24 hours no home energy audit needed
  3. Payment approved in 5 days sent by direct deposit after you upload receipts

How it will work, as announced

The steps the government has described are simple and have no energy audit before or after the install.

  1. You apply in a secure online portal when your heating system breaks down or is close to the end of its life.
  2. The government confirms approval within 24 hours.
  3. You buy and install a pre-approved heat pump from the Eligible Products List, which the government says has over 100,000 options.
  4. You upload your receipts in the portal.
  5. Payment is approved within five days and paid by direct deposit.

The design follows what the government says happens in real life: about eight in ten furnace and heating replacements happen after something breaks. If your heating works fine today, this program is not built for you yet.

What the government has not said yet

  • The income line. "Below the median income" has no published number, household size rule or proof requirement.
  • Which homes and fuels. The savings figure names oil, propane and electric resistance. Natural gas homes, condos, rentals and mobile homes are not addressed in the text we read.
  • Retroactivity. Reported by CBC as being considered. Not confirmed.
  • The product list. Which models and what cold-climate rating are not published yet.
  • Installer rules. Ottawa says it is consulting the HVAC industry so contractors have stock ready, so installer requirements may come out of that.

We will not guess at any of these. When a fact is published we add it with its source and date, and this page changes with it.

Why the quote matters more than the rebate

Many homeowners worry that contractors raise prices by the amount of any rebate. Whether or not that happens near you, you can protect yourself by getting a written load calculation, three itemized quotes and a price for the same equipment with and without the rebate. Our guide on how to get a fair heat pump quote walks through it.

Should you wait or buy now?

If your system still works, waiting a few months costs little and lets you see the real rules. If it has failed and you need heat this winter, provincial money available today may be worth more than a federal rebate that is not open yet. We lay out the deadlines in Should you wait for the federal heat pump rebate?

What each province already pays

The federal money is meant to sit on top of provincial programs. Here is what is live today, each from the program's own page.

  • British Columbia: BC Hydro pays up to $4,000 for a whole-home heat pump replacing electric heat. CleanBC pays $13,000, $7,000 or $3,500 by income level for gas, propane or oil homes. See the BC hub.
  • Ontario: Home Renovation Savings pays $1,250 per ton up to $7,500 for homes heated with electricity, oil, propane or wood, and $500 per ton up to $2,000 for gas homes. See the Ontario hub.
  • Nova Scotia: Efficiency Nova Scotia pays per ton, and HomeWarming installs upgrades free for lower incomes. See the Nova Scotia hub.
  • Alberta: we found no province-wide heat pump rebate, and the earlier federal Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program closed on July 31, 2026, so the new federal rebate may matter most here. See the Alberta hub.

The full breakdown, with sample stacks, is in federal and provincial heat pump rebates: what stacks.

What to do before the rebate launches

  1. Sign up for updates on the Canada.ca campaign page, which is where launch dates will be posted.
  2. Find out what you heat with today. Oil, propane and electric baseboard homes are the clearest fit.
  3. Ask an installer for a written heat load calculation and an itemized quote.
  4. Check your province's live rebate and its deadline on the hub for your province.
  5. Keep quotes, invoices and photos of the old equipment in one folder in case Ottawa makes the rebate retroactive.
  6. Run your numbers in our rebate calculator for your province.

Common questions

How much is the National Heat Pump Rebate?

Up to $10,000 for households below the median income and $2,000 for other eligible homeowners. The government has not yet published the income line.

When can I apply?

Not yet. The government expects to launch in early 2027. Sign up on the Canada.ca campaign page to hear launch dates.

Will it cover a heat pump I already installed?

Not confirmed. CBC reports that Ottawa is looking at making the rebate retroactive to fall 2026, but the government text we read does not promise it. Keep your invoices either way.

Can I combine it with BC Hydro, Home Renovation Savings or Efficiency Nova Scotia?

The government says the rebate can be combined with provincial programs. Each provincial program has its own rules, so confirm with the program before you buy.

Do I need an energy audit?

No. The government says no pre- or post-installation audit is required. Some provincial programs still ask for one, such as the Ontario assessment stream.

Does a natural gas home qualify?

The government has not said. Its quoted savings of more than $1,400 a year are for homes heating with oil, propane or electric resistance.

Is this the same as the Canada Greener Homes Grant?

No. The old Greener Homes Grant is closed. The Greener Homes Affordability Program is a separate free-retrofit program for lower incomes, and this is a new rebate.
