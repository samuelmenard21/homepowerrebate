Heat pump rebate income limits

On this page
  1. British Columbia (checked September 26, 2026)
  2. Federal programs
  3. Other provinces and states
  4. Key Takeaway

Most base heat pump rebates have NO income limit. You can earn any amount and still claim them. However, some programs offer extra rebates if your household income is below a certain threshold.

British Columbia (checked September 26, 2026)

ProgramHeat pump rebateIncome test?
BC Hydro (replacing electric heat)Up to $4,000 whole-home, or $1,500 partial. Bonus up to $1,000 for installs done Aug 1 to Oct 31, 2026.No
CleanBC Energy Savings ProgramUp to $13,000 / $7,000 / $3,500 (income levels 1 / 2 / 3) to switch from gas, oil or propane; +$3,000 in northern areas. Electric-to-heat-pump: level 1 only, up to $5,000.Yes. Limits depend on household size; see Better Homes BC.

Federal programs

Canada: the Canada Greener Homes Grant and Loan are closed. The Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program serves low-to-median income households through local delivery partners. United States: the federal 25C heat pump tax credit ended on December 31, 2025.

Other provinces and states

Programs and income limits change often, so we don't list numbers we can't keep current here. See your city's page for what's open now.

Sources, checked September 26, 2026: BC Hydro heat pump rebates · Better Homes BC Energy Savings Program · NRCan Greener Homes · IRS 25C

Key Takeaway

Apply anyway. Most people qualify for the base rebate regardless of income. Even if you don't meet an income-qualified top-up threshold, you'll still get the main rebate. The worst outcome is "no," and you'll know to apply elsewhere.

Income limits are set by household annual income—usually total household before taxes. Each program defines it slightly differently (some include spouse, some don't). When you apply, they'll ask for proof of income (tax return, pay stub).
