Do renters qualify for heat pump rebates?

Short answer: No. Heat pump rebates are for homeowners only, because the landlord owns the equipment and benefits from it.

The Details

Structural upgrades like heat pumps, solar panels, and insulation belong to the building owner (your landlord). Rebate programs won't give money to a renter for equipment they don't own. The landlord would need to apply.

This rule applies across federal, provincial, and utility programs in Canada and most U.S. states.

What Renters CAN Claim

Some programs allow renters to claim portable or non-structural upgrades:

  • Smart thermostats — If you install your own, some programs reimburse (you take it with you)
  • Weatherstripping and caulk — Small efficiency upgrades you do yourself
  • Window treatments — Thermal curtains or insulated blinds
  • Portable heaters — In limited cases (check local programs)

But these are rare and usually modest rebates ($50–$200). Check your province or state's program directly to see if renter-friendly incentives exist.

What Your Landlord CAN Claim

If your landlord installs a heat pump or other structural upgrade, they can claim the full rebate. Encourage them to—it may lead to better utilities or comfort for tenants.
