What to do next

  1. Book a home audit. Any HPCN-certified installer can do this for free. They'll assess your home, confirm what rebates apply, and give you a price. The audit cost is refunded after your retrofit anyway.
  2. Double-check your income. Your household income tier determines the biggest numbers here. If you're under $110K, you might qualify for a $16K heat pump (instead of $4K). That matters.
  3. Confirm your electrical panel. If your home was built before 1980 and you want heat pump + EV charging, you might need a $3K panel upgrade. The $500 rebate helps.
  4. Get a quote from a trusted installer. We'll show you every certified installer in your city — you choose. No auctions, no spam calls. Just one person to talk to.
  5. Compare and decide. You've got the numbers. Your installer will give you a binding quote. Then it's your call.

FAQ

Do I have to do all these upgrades at once?

No. You can do them in phases. Recommended sequence: electrical panel (if needed) → heat pump → solar → battery → EV charger. Each stands alone for rebates, but stacking them multiplies savings.

What if I'm not income-qualified for the bigger rebates?

Standard income tier rebates are still substantial ($4K heat pump, $5K solar, $5K battery). You're not locked out—you just get the base amount. Tier 1/2/3 households can get up to $16K heat pump rebates.

Do I have to use Peak Saver to get the battery rebate?

No, but you should. Peak Saver is free to enroll in and returns $3,000+ over 10 years through seasonal rewards. Miss the 14-day enrollment window after interconnection, and you lose $3,500 of value.

What if my electrical panel is already upgraded?

Great—check that box off. The $500 rebate only applies if you need upgrades.

Can I mix installers?

Yes. One installer for solar, another for heat pump—no problem. But using one HPCN-certified installer for the full retrofit usually means better coordination and warranty coverage.
