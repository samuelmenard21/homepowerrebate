# BC Category Research Verification 2026 — Re-Verify Against Published City Pages

Research date: August 22, 2026. Scope: re-verify the 6-category rebate stack (heat pump, insulation, solar, battery, home/heat-pump water heater, EV charger) published on all 18 BC city hub pages (ca/bc/{city}/) against current live sources — BC Hydro, FortisBC, CleanBC/BetterHomes BC — plus a per-city local/municipal top-up check, mirroring the NY research format (scripts/ny-category-research-2026.md). This is RESEARCH ONLY; no HTML was edited.

**Methodology note / limitation:** This pass was done via WebSearch + targeted WebFetch, not a manual crawl of every linked PDF. Google's index for several betterhomesbc.ca and BC Hydro pages appears stale in places (search summaries and this session's own WebFetch attempts on 3 specific betterhomesbc.ca URLs — Richmond, Kamloops, and Vancouver municipal top-up pages — returned HTTP 404 even though those exact URLs showed up in a fresh Google search moments earlier). That 404 pattern is itself a signal worth flagging (pages removed/restructured, consistent with "these rebates have now closed" language found in the same search results) but it also means several of the findings below rest on search-result snippets rather than a direct fetch of the primary page, and are flagged accordingly. Read this report's confidence notes carefully before publishing any correction — do not silently upgrade a 1–2 source finding to "fully verified."

Already-published baseline reviewed directly: `ca/bc/vancouver/index.html`, `ca/bc/kelowna/index.html`, `ca/bc/kamloops/index.html`.

## Per-City Findings

The provincial/utility baseline above applies identically to all 18 cities. Below are ONLY city-specific local/municipal additions found, or an explicit "no local top-up found" negative where a real search was done.

- **Vancouver** — Already published and independently re-confirmed as still accurate: City of Vancouver's own $2,000 heat pump top-up, plus a Vancouver-specific **Heat Pump Water Heater Top-Up** (a page distinct from the general $2,000 heat-pump top-up) was found indexed on betterhomesbc.ca (`vancouver-municipal-heat-pump-water-heater-top-up`) — **this second, HPWH-specific top-up is not currently mentioned on the published Vancouver page**, which only lists the $2,000 space-heating top-up. Direct fetch of that specific URL 404'd during this pass (consistent with the pattern of several betterhomesbc.ca municipal-offer pages returning 404 even though Google's index still shows them — see methodology note), so this is flagged as a **new-finding-to-verify**, not confirmed. 1 indirect source (search snippet) only.
- **Richmond** — Search-indexed betterhomesbc.ca page shows a **$350 municipal heat pump top-up** for Richmond (switching from fossil-fuel space heating to an electric air-source heat pump). A direct fetch of that URL 404'd, and a separate search summary states "these rebates have now closed... most ended April 11, 2025" — so this may be a **stale/closed program**, not a live one. **Do not add this to the Richmond page without confirming current status directly** — flagged explicitly as unresolved (closed-vs-still-listed), 1 source, low confidence.
- **Kamloops** — Same pattern as Richmond: a **$350 municipal heat pump top-up** page is indexed for Kamloops, but the direct fetch 404'd and the same "rebates have now closed" language applies. Also confirmed (higher confidence, direct fetch) a live, still-active **EV charger local top-up of +$150** (75% combined, up to $500 total) via BC Hydro's own top-ups page — this one is NOT flagged as closed and is a genuinely new, unpublished finding for Kamloops's EV-charger subpage.
- **Squamish** — New finding, direct-fetched and reasonably confident: BC Hydro's own top-up page lists a live **EV charger top-up of +$150** for single-family homes in the District of Squamish (75% combined, up to $500), plus separate multi-unit-building top-ups (not relevant to a single-family homeowner page). Not currently on the published Squamish EV-charger subpage (not read this pass — flag for direct check).
- **Nanaimo** — Confirmed accurate and current: the **PACE-style interest-free Home Energy Retrofit Financing Program**, up to $15,000, repaid over 10 years via property tax, funded partly by a $458,000 FCM Green Municipal Fund grant, applications opened April 1, 2025. This matches the skill's prior "$15,000 interest-free financing" claim — still accurate. 3 sources (city.nanaimo.ca news release, citygreen.ca, nanaimobulletin.com), high confidence. Separately, the Regional District of Nanaimo also appears on BC Hydro's EV-charger local top-up list (+$150, separate application process) — a smaller, additional, previously-unpublished finding.
- **Penticton** — Confirmed the skill's prior "own Home Energy Loan Program (HELP)" claim is still accurate: up to $10,000 loan, repaid over 10 years via the electric utility bill, interest rate currently prime + 0.5%. Note: the program was reported as being **under active redesign** ("staff working on redesigning the framework... changes to Council early 2025") — worth flagging that the terms found may be mid-transition rather than fully settled for 2026. 1 primary source (penticton.ca), moderate confidence on the exact current terms given the redesign note.

This PNG-specific detail does not appear to currently be on the FSJ page (unconfirmed, page not read this pass).

## New/Additional Categories or Structural Findings Discovered (outside the original 6-category framing)

1. **BC Hydro's EV-charger "local rebate top-ups" program** — a distinct, utility-administered layer of small ($150) municipality-specific top-ups (confirmed live for Kamloops, Squamish, and Regional District of Nanaimo among our 18) sitting on top of the base $350+$200 EV charger rebate. This is structurally different from the City-of-Vancouver-style $2,000 heat-pump cash top-up (province/utility-run vs. city-run) and isn't currently reflected anywhere on the site's EV-charger city subpages. Mirrors NY's finding of Con Edison's SmartCharge/PSEG's DAC top-up being a distinct incentive layer from the base hardware rebate.
2. **Battery eligibility gate (Peak Saver mandatory for the top tier)** — not a new category, but a structural eligibility change (effective April 1, 2026) that changes *who* can get the $5,000 vs. $1,500 battery tier. Recommend the battery category copy explicitly state that a standalone, non-Peak-Saver battery now gets $0, not just "up to $1,500–$5,000" as a range implying any qualifying battery gets something.
3. **CleanBC ESP restructuring (ESP1/ESP2/ESP3 tiers, discontinued ESP2-Electric)** — the underlying tier structure behind the province's income-qualified heat pump rebate changed materially on July 6, 2026. This isn't a "new category" but is a big enough mechanical change (discontinued stream, new tier ceilings) that it may deserve its own explainer note on the site, similar to how NY's research flagged NYSERDA's EmPower+ as a distinct income-qualified umbrella worth its own subsection.
4. **Municipal RARA-style property tax exemptions for rental retrofits (Victoria)** — mirrors NY's finding of NYC's solar property-tax abatement: a genuine local incentive that isn't a cash rebate and doesn't fit the existing dollar-amount card format. If the site starts covering landlord/rental-specific incentives, this needs its own treatment distinct from the homeowner rebate cards.
5. **PACE-style municipal financing (Nanaimo's Home Energy Retrofit Financing Program, Penticton's HELP)** — both already partially reflected in prior BC verification passes per the skill file, re-confirmed here, but worth explicitly flagging as a structurally distinct "financing, not rebate" category — same pattern NY's research called out for Westchester's Energize NY PACE financing.
6. **PNG (Pacific Northern Gas) as a third utility administrator** — the site's copy currently frames BC as a two-utility world (BC Hydro + FortisBC), but Fort St. John-area customers can also access Pacific Northern Gas's own dual-fuel top-up. This is a genuinely new administrator not currently named anywhere on the FSJ page (unconfirmed — page not read this pass).
