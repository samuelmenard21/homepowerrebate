# MA Category Research Verification 2026 — Mass Save Statewide Programs + 12-City Local Layer

Research date: August 22, 2026. Scope: re-verify the 8 published rebate categories (heat pump, insulation, solar, battery, water heater, windows/doors, EV charger, smart thermostat) that appear identically across all 12 published MA city hub pages (Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Cambridge, Lowell, Brockton, New Bedford, Quincy, Lynn, Fall River, Newton, Somerville), plus check for local/municipal top-ups and unexpected utility structures, matching the rigor bar set by the NY/BC/ON research passes.

**Methodology note / limitation:** Done via web search (no manual crawl of every masssave.com PDF, no direct fetch of masssave.com pages — search snippets and secondary-source aggregation only, same limitation the NY pass flagged). Every category below has 3+ independent secondary/aggregator sources (NuWatt, Boston Solar, EnergySage, HVAC contractor guides, etc.) but **zero direct masssave.com page fetches succeeded/were attempted as primary sources** — this is a real gap vs. the "check masssave.com directly" instruction in the task; flagging explicitly rather than claiming primary-source verification. Where masssave.com URLs appeared in search results, they are listed but their content was not independently fetched and read in full.

## 3. Municipal Light Plants — Checked, None of the 12 Cities Affected

Massachusetts has ~41 municipal light plants (MLPs) covering roughly 50 municipalities that operate **outside** the Mass Save program entirely, running their own independent rebate structures (e.g., Wellesley Municipal Light Plant offers up to $8,500 heat pump rebates + 50% off weatherization; Braintree Electric Light Department offers up to $3,000; Concord Municipal Light Plant runs a $3,000/ton, up to $10,000/year heat pump program). This is a structurally important gotcha — MLP customers get **zero** Mass Save rebates, a completely separate program.

**Verified: none of the 12 published MA cities are MLP-served.** Boston, Worcester, Springfield, Cambridge, Lowell, Brockton, New Bedford, Quincy, Lynn, Fall River, Newton, and Somerville are all confirmed as Eversource or National Grid territory (investor-owned, Mass Save-participating utilities). The named MLP towns (Belmont, Braintree, Reading, Wakefield, Concord, Hingham, Holyoke, Mansfield, Marblehead, Middleborough, Norwood, Peabody, Shrewsbury, Taunton, Wellesley, Westfield, Ipswich, and ~25 others) do not overlap with the 12 cities this site covers.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** This was a search-based cross-check, not a parcel-level utility lookup for each of the 12 cities. Given that all 12 are among the largest MA cities/cities by population, and MLPs are consistently described in sources as concentrated in smaller towns, the risk of one of the 12 secretly being MLP-served is low — but this wasn't confirmed via mass.gov's official MLP list document directly (the list URL surfaced but wasn't fetched and cross-referenced line by line).

**Sources:** 3 (mass.gov municipally-owned-electric-companies page listed in results, NuWatt municipal utility pages, greenenergyconsumers.org heat pump incentive one-pager) — confidence as stated above.

## 4. New Categories Discovered (Outside the 8-Category Taxonomy)

1. **HEAT Loan (0% financing, up to $25,000)** — This is not itself a rebate but a financing product referenced across nearly every category above. It's currently folded into the heat pump page's income-qualified figures, but it's actually a **cross-category, income-agnostic financing mechanism** available for insulation, water heater, windows, and other measures too — not heat-pump-specific. Recommend treating as its own line item or clearly-labeled sidebar rather than folding into the heat pump $ figure, since the current page structure may be conflating "$25,000 ground-source rebate cap" with "$25,000 HEAT Loan ceiling" (see Section 1 heat pump row).
2. **Federal HOMES program (via MassCEC)** — Separate from Mass Save, MassCEC administers this federal program: up to $8,000 for households <80% AMI, up to $4,000 for 80–150% AMI, explicitly designed to stack with Mass Save. Not currently mentioned anywhere in the published MA content. This is a real, distinct program (not a Mass Save rebate) and would be a legitimate "new category" or at least a stacking-benefit callout.
3. **ConnectedSolutions equipment brand restriction (batteries)** — Not a new category, but a new eligibility constraint: as of 2026 the battery capacity rebate is limited to Duracell, Emporia, and Tesla equipment. Single-sourced in this pass, needs confirmation.
4. **Boston's Healthy and Green Retrofit Pilot / EEIF / Retrofit Resource Hub** — arguably a "new category" in the sense that BC/ON found local top-up *rebates*; Boston's local layer is instead advisory/financing/grant programs. Doesn't map cleanly onto the existing 8-category rebate-table format and may need a different content treatment (a "Boston-specific programs" callout box rather than a 9th category row).
