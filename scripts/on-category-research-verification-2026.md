Scope: re-verify the 9-category rebate stack (heat pump, insulation, solar, battery, water heater, smart thermostat, windows & doors, EV charger, appliances) published across all 18 Ontario city hub/category pages against the Home Renovation Savings Program (HRSP, homerenovationsavings.ca, delivered by Enbridge Gas + Save On Energy with Government of Ontario support) plus per-city municipal top-up checks, mirroring the BC/NY research format (`scripts/bc-category-research-verification-2026.md`, `scripts/ny-category-research-2026.md`). ## Per-City Findings (municipal/local top-ups beyond the province-wide HRSP baseline)

- **Toronto** — Real, substantial, and NOT currently reflected on the site's Toronto pages (not read this pass, but not mentioned in the task's baseline description either). City of Toronto + Toronto Hydro run the **BetterHomesTO Furnace Upgrade Program**: free energy coaching, guidance connecting homeowners to a heat pump installer, and — per the City's 2025 budget — an extra $20M allocated toward low-interest heat pump loans delivered through the **Home Energy Loan Program (HELP)**, up to $125,000 borrowable at 3.25% (5-year term) to 4.72% (20-year term). A time-limited incentive (book a Toronto Hydro energy coaching appointment by July 31 for a chance to win $5,000 toward a heat pump) was also found but reads as a limited contest/promo, not a standing rebate. 2026 expansion specifically targets low-to-moderate-income households with additional incentives (amount not specified in sources found). 3 sources: [toronto.ca press release](https://www.toronto.ca/news/city-of-toronto-provides-low-cost-financing-and-free-coaching-to-help-homeowners-switch-their-furnaces-to-heat-pumps/), [toronto.ca Furnace Upgrade Program page](https://www.toronto.ca/services-payments/water-environment/environmental-grants-incentives/and-rebates-to-green-your-home/furnace-upgrade-program/), [mechanicalbusiness.com](https://mechanicalbusiness.com/2025/10/03/toronto-launches-low-interest-loan-program-for-heat-pumps/). **High confidence this is real and current, and it's the strongest genuine local-layer finding of this whole pass** — matches the task's hunch that Toronto (largest city) would have its own real program. Recommend adding a Toronto-specific note to the heat-pump (financing via HELP) and general hub pages once directly reviewed.
- **Ottawa** — Real municipal financing program confirmed: **Better Homes Ottawa Loan Program (BHOLP)**, a 3-year pilot offering 0%-to-low-interest financing for energy retrofits (heat pumps, insulation, renewable energy), tied to the property tax bill rather than personal credit, transferable to a new owner on sale, 20-year term. 2 sources: [betterhomesottawa.ca](https://betterhomesottawa.ca/rebates-incentives-financing/), WebSearch summary citing City of Ottawa program description. **Medium confidence — real program confirmed via its own dedicated site, but the underlying City of Ottawa page itself wasn't directly fetched this pass, and exact current loan cap/rate wasn't isolated from the search summary.**
- **Hamilton** — Real municipal financing program found: **Better Homes Hamilton**, pilot PACE-style financing at an introductory 0% interest rate fixed for a 10–15 year repayment term, up to $20,000 per property (program-wide pilot pool of $1M). 2 sources: [dynamicheatandcool.ca Hamilton HVAC rebates roundup](https://dynamicheatandcool.ca/hamilton-hvac-rebates-2026-the-complete-list-for-heat-pumps-furnaces/), [hamilton.insauga.com](https://hamilton.insauga.com/hamiltons-new-energy-retrofit-program-could-help-lower-income-residents-upgrade-their-homes/). Medium confidence — not directly fetched from hamilton.ca itself.
- **Mississauga / Brampton** (also covers neighboring Caledon, not one of our 18) — A regional program was found: the **Home Energy Retrofit Program** / **Centre for Community Energy Transformation (energytransform.ca)**, serving the Region of Peel (Mississauga, Brampton, Caledon), launched 2024, focused on energy coaching, audits, and "maximized rebates/incentives" plus zero-upfront-cost options — reads as a concierge/coaching layer connecting residents to existing HRSP and utility rebates rather than a distinct cash top-up of its own, similar to the "navigation hub" pattern flagged for several BC cities (Burnaby, Surrey) in the prior BC pass. 2 sources: [insauga.com](https://www.insauga.com/save-money-on-energy-bills-with-mississauga-brampton-caledon-home-energy-retrofits-program/), [energytransform.ca](https://energytransform.ca/). **Do not present as an additional cash rebate without more direct confirmation — flag as coaching/concierge, not confirmed cash top-up.**
- **Vaughan, Richmond Hill, Barrie, London, Windsor, Oakville, Oshawa, Whitby, Burlington, Cambridge, Greater Sudbury, Markham** — **Not individually searched this pass** due to time/scope constraints after prioritizing Toronto, Ottawa, Hamilton, and the Peel region (Mississauga/Brampton) per the task's explicit call-out of Toronto as most likely to have a real local layer. **Explicit gap, not a negative finding** — these 12 cities need their own direct search-and-fetch pass before concluding whether they have or lack a municipal top-up; do not assume "no program" for any of them.

## New Categories Discovered (outside the existing 9-category taxonomy)

1. **Air sealing** — a distinct HRSP line item (up to $250) that exists as its own sub-benefit within the insulation bundle but isn't currently broken out as its own card on the site's insulation page. Not a new page-worthy category on its own (too small, naturally belongs under insulation), but worth adding as an explicit line item for completeness. Low priority.
2. **Pre/post-retrofit energy assessment rebate ($600)** — HRSP pays $600 toward the mandatory NRCan-registered energy advisor assessments required for the bundled path, paid out after program completion. This is a process cost reimbursement, not a home-upgrade rebate, so it doesn't fit any of the 9 categories — but it's a real, sourced dollar figure homeowners should know about since the bundled path (needed to unlock the $7,700 insulation cap and combine categories toward the $12,000 overall max) requires it. Recommend a short mention on the insulation or hub page. 1 source (claimrebate.ca) — moderate confidence, not cross-verified against homerenovationsavings.ca directly.
3. **Municipal PACE-style low-interest financing (Toronto HELP, Ottawa BHOLP, Hamilton Better Homes Hamilton)** — mirrors the BC pass's finding that Nanaimo/Penticton run financing-not-rebate municipal programs. This is a structurally distinct "loan, not rebate" layer that doesn't fit the existing dollar-amount card format, present in at least 3 of Ontario's 18 cities (with Toronto's being the largest and best-documented). Recommend the site eventually carry a distinct "local financing" note per city, separate from the province-wide HRSP rebate cards, similar to how BC's report flagged this same pattern.
4. **Overall HRSP household cap ($12,000)** — not a new category, but a structural fact not obviously reflected on the site (at least not on Kitchener's category-by-category pages, which each seem to treat categories somewhat independently): all HRSP rebates combined across categories cap at $12,000 per household under the bundled path. Worth a sitewide callout so homeowners don't assume stacking a heat pump + insulation + windows all separately adds up past that ceiling. 2 sources (claimrebate.ca guide, buildersontario.com) — moderate-high confidence.
