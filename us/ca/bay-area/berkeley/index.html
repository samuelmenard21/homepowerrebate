What Berkeley homeowners can claim

Berkeley is entirely PG&E territory for electricity and gas. Unlike some BC or Ontario cities, no Bay Area city currently stacks its own cash on top of PG&E's rebates — the dollar amounts below are what PG&E itself pays, and they're the real numbers to plan around.

☀️ Solar Panels (6kW)

$0 utility rebate

NEM 3.0 (California's current net-metering rule) cut the value of exported solar power, so PG&E no longer pays a separate cash rebate. A 6kW system runs $15,000–$18,000 and still saves about $1,400/yr, a ~10-year payback, mostly by offsetting your own daytime usage rather than selling power back.

Source: CPUC NEM 3.0 / PG&E

Honest math for Berkeley

PG&E's utility rebates for a heat pump, insulation, water heater, EV charger and thermostat add up to roughly $2,475 in direct cash back — modest next to what BC or Massachusetts pay, because California shifted its incentive strategy toward net metering and financing rather than large upfront rebates. The real savings case here is the $1,200/yr in avoided gas costs from switching to a heat pump, and the $1,400/yr from solar, not the rebate checks themselves.

Two programs worth knowing about: PG&E's Climate Equity Hub provides a free heat pump water heater for income-qualified households, and Bay Area SunShares runs a seasonal group-buy that gets participating homeowners a discount through vetted installers (separate from any utility rebate). The state's CalEnergy Smart Homes 2026 funding round is fully subscribed as of this writing — if you see it advertised as open, verify directly before counting on it.

NEM 3.0 changed the math

In Berkeley, a battery is about self-consumption, not a rebate check

Since NEM 3.0 took effect, PG&E pays much less for power you export to the grid — so a home battery's job has shifted from "sell it back later" to "use your own solar power at night instead of buying it back at retail rates." A 13.5kWh battery (about $12,000 installed) has no separate PG&E cash rebate, but it's what makes a 6kW solar system's $1,400/yr savings actually materialize instead of leaking away as low-value exports. Berkeley-specific context

Local context

Berkeley's own climate push

Berkeley was one of the first US cities to adopt a Climate Action Plan and has pushed hard on building electrification (its 2019 natural-gas ban in new construction was later struck down in court, but the city's ambition on retrofits hasn't changed). There's no separate city cash rebate stacked on top of PG&E's — Berkeley's role is mostly permitting speed and community outreach through its Office of Energy & Sustainable Development, not extra dollars.

Permitting & timeline

FAQ for Berkeley residents

What's the heat pump rebate in Berkeley?

PG&E offers $250–$500 for a qualifying air-source heat pump in Berkeley. A typical 3–4 ton system costs $13,000–$22,000 installed and saves roughly $1,200/yr versus gas heat, with a 13–15 year payback on the equipment.

Does solar still pay off in Berkeley under NEM 3.0?

There's no PG&E cash rebate for solar anymore — NEM 3.0 reduced the value of power you export to the grid. A 6kW system still costs $15,000–$18,000 and saves about $1,400/yr, roughly a 10-year payback, mainly by offsetting your own usage rather than selling excess power back.

Is there help if my income is lower in Berkeley?

PG&E's Climate Equity Hub provides a free heat pump water heater for income-qualified households. Bay Area SunShares also runs a seasonal group-buy that gets participating homeowners a discount through vetted installers, separate from any utility rebate.

Can I do this if I rent or live in a condo?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates — those go to the property owner — but income-qualified renters may still access PG&E's free heat pump water heater program with landlord permission. In a condo, in-unit upgrades may qualify with HOA approval; whole-building work goes through the HOA as a group decision. See the full breakdown below.

When should I apply?

Get quotes and apply as soon as you've chosen a contractor — PG&E requires pre-approval before work starts on most rebate categories, so late applications can forfeit the rebate.

A big share of Berkeley households

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most rebate dollar figures on this page assume you own a single-family home and control the whole building. That's not every Berkeley homeowner. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

You can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, windows, solar) — those go to whoever owns the property. What's genuinely open to you is PG&E's Climate Equity Hub free heat pump water heater program if you're income-qualified and your landlord agrees to the install. If you want the bigger upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, in-suite windows, an in-unit heat pump — may qualify for the rebates above with your HOA's approval (usually required for anything touching shared systems or exterior walls). Get that approval in writing before applying; PG&E's pre-approval process may ask for proof the work was authorized by the building.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation, and solar for the whole building go through the HOA or property manager as a group decision, using PG&E's separate multifamily program rather than the single-family numbers on this page — the per-unit amounts and process differ. Your HOA or property manager applies, not individual owners.
