Heat Pump Rebates in San Jose

San Jose Clean Energy pays a flat $1,500 for a heat pump heating and cooling system that replaces gas, and $2,500 for homes in Environmental Justice communities. It is not a per-ton amount, so the size of your system does not change it.

What San Jose Clean Energy pays

These figures come from the EcoHome Rebate page of San Jose Clean Energy (SJCE), which we read on October 1, 2026. You must be a current SJCE customer, and the system must replace an existing gas-powered heating system.

What a typical project can add up to

A heat pump HVAC system ($1,500), a panel upgrade ($1,000) and attic insulation ($700 at the 1,000 square foot cap) come to $3,200 in rebates on one project. In an Environmental Justice community the same project can reach $5,500: $2,500 + $2,000 + $1,000. Each add-on needs the heat pump to go in first or alongside it.

A flat rebate changes how you shop

Most Los Angeles-area utilities pay by the ton, so a larger system earns more. SJCE pays one amount, so there is no reason to buy a bigger unit for the rebate, and a small home can earn as much as a large one. Put your effort into the right size and into the add-ons that stack on top, namely the panel upgrade and the attic insulation.

Reserve first, then install

You apply and wait for approval before installation begins. SJCE emails a confirmation within 2 to 4 business days and, if approved, holds the funds for 120 days. For an emergency replacement, email SJCEPrograms@smud.org before applying to confirm you can still qualify. Ask your installer to schedule the work after you hold the confirmation, and to start the permit in the meantime.

Permits in San Jose

The City treats duct, furnace and other mechanical equipment replacement as mechanical work that needs a mechanical permit, with an online option for some single-family and duplex properties through SJPermits.org. See the City's mechanical projects page. The Permit Center is at 200 E Santa Clara Street, and its phone number is (408) 535-3555. Insurers can deny claims tied to unpermitted work, so do not skip the permit.

Your utility: San Jose Clean Energy

SJCE's flat $1,500 is much lower than Glendale's $1,000 per ton up to $5,000 (Glendale) or LADWP's tiers (Los Angeles), but it comes with the panel and insulation add-ons and with a payment plan for the upfront cost. SJCE's EcoHome Payment Plan and its Go Electric Advisor can help you plan. San Jose's average January low over 2015 to 2024 was 6.7°C (about 44°F), with a coldest recorded day of -1.3°C, so a standard heat pump heats the house, and cooling matters in summer.

Other money

BayREN's EASE Home program helps income-qualified PG&E customers in Santa Clara County with weatherization (BayREN). The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended after December 31, 2025. TECH Clean California and the federal HEEHRA program for single-family homes are fully reserved and take new requests on hold only.

How to claim the SJCE rebate

  1. Confirm that you are a current SJCE customer and that the system replaces gas heating.
  2. Check whether you are in an Environmental Justice community or on CARE, FERA or SJ CARES.
  3. Apply on the EcoHome Rebate page, with a photo of your existing equipment and agreement to the Applicant Agreement.
  4. Wait for the confirmation email, which SJCE sends within 2 to 4 business days. Funds are held for 120 days.
  5. Install under a City mechanical permit, and add the panel and attic insulation rebates if they are part of the job.
  6. Submit your claim within the 120 days.

Common questions

How much does SJCE pay for a heat pump?

$1,500, or $2,500 in an Environmental Justice community. It is a flat amount, not per ton.

Can I add other rebates?

Yes. Add-ons for a panel upgrade ($1,000, or $2,000 in an EJ community) and attic insulation ($0.75 per square foot, capped) need a heat pump HVAC or water heater.

When do I apply?

Before installation. SJCE must approve the application first, and approved funds are held for 120 days.

Does the old system have to run on gas?

Yes. The new heat pump must replace an existing gas-powered appliance.

Is there help with the upfront cost?

SJCE offers an EcoHome Payment Plan, and its Go Electric Advisor can help assess your needs.
