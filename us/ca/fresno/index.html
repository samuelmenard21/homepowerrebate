Heat Pump & Solar Rebates for Fresno Homeowners

Every rebate a Fresno homeowner can get in 2026 through PG&E — heat pump, insulation, water heater, EV charger, and more — explained plainly, with honest math on why the heat pump rebate here is modest.

Updated August 2026 · Independent guide. We don't sell your info. Numbers below are money you keep (rebates), not loans.

Central Valley, served by Pacific Gas & Electric (PG&E). No SMUD access here.

⚡PowerScore: 46.9/100 — #11 of 24 in California

In 2026, a Fresno homeowner served by PG&E can claim $250–$500 for a heat pump, $1,600 for insulation, and $500 for a heat pump water heater. There's no dedicated PG&E solar or battery rebate, but Fresno's strong Central Valley sun still makes solar pay back faster than the heat pump here.

Honest math for Fresno

Fresno's heat pump rebate is small compared to other parts of California — $250–$500 versus $3,000+ in SMUD territory. The real savings here come from lower energy bills over time (roughly $1,000/year), not the rebate itself. Solar, with no rebate at all but strong Central Valley sun, actually pays back faster than the heat pump.

Why the numbers look different here

Fresno has about 2,500 heating degree days a year — moderate, more than Bakersfield but less than the Bay Area. A heat pump here saves an estimated $1,000 a year, and with a 6kW solar system saving around $1,200 a year, solar actually pays back faster (roughly 13 years) than the heat pump (about 15 years) once you account for the modest PG&E heat pump rebate. Neither is a bad investment — the equipment needs replacing eventually regardless, and a heat pump also adds cooling capacity for the Central Valley's hot summers.

PG&E programs — what each one is

PG&E

Heat pump rebate

$250–$500

PG&E's standard heat pump rebate. Modest compared to municipal utilities, but still reduces your net cost.

How it works: Pre-approval is required before work begins. Choose a PG&E-approved contractor, and the rebate is applied as a bill credit or check within 4–8 weeks of completion. Read our PG&E pre-approval guide →
PG&E

Heat pump water heater rebate

$500

Replaces a gas or electric tank water heater with a heat pump model that uses roughly a third the energy.

How it works: Same pre-approval process as the space-heating heat pump rebate above.
PG&E

Insulation rebate

$1,600

Attic, wall or crawlspace insulation — do this first or alongside the heat pump for a smaller, cheaper system.

How it works: Apply through a PG&E-approved contractor before work starts.
PG&E

EV charger rebate

$500

Toward a Level 2 home charging station.

How it works: Apply after installation with your receipt and contractor documentation.
Federal

Residential Clean Energy & efficiency tax credits

stacks with PG&E

Federal tax credits for solar, battery storage, and qualifying efficiency upgrades are separate from and stack on top of any PG&E rebate above — especially relevant here since PG&E has no dedicated solar or battery rebate.

How it works: Claimed when you file taxes for the year the equipment was placed in service.

FAQ for Fresno residents

How much can I get for a heat pump in Fresno?

PG&E's Fresno-area heat pump rebate is modest, $250–$500. A typical 3-4 ton system costs $13,000–$22,000 installed, saving roughly $1,000/year, for a payback of about 15 years — the rebate helps, but the real payoff is the ongoing bill savings.

Is solar worth it in Fresno?

There's no dedicated PG&E solar rebate, but Fresno's strong Central Valley sun makes a 6kW system ($15,000–$18,000) pay back in about 13 years from roughly $1,200/year in savings, faster than the heat pump payback here.

Why are Fresno's rebates smaller than other California cities?

Fresno is served by PG&E, an investor-owned utility whose heat pump rebate ($250–$500) is much smaller than municipal utilities like SMUD in Sacramento ($3,000–$4,000). PG&E also offers no dedicated solar or battery rebate at this time.

Does PG&E require pre-approval before starting work?

Yes. PG&E requires pre-approval before work begins on most rebate-eligible upgrades. Starting work first can forfeit your eligibility, so confirm pre-approval with your contractor before signing a contract.

Can renters or condo owners in Fresno get any rebate?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates directly since those go to the property owner. Condo owners can pursue in-unit upgrades like a heat pump water heater with building approval; whole-building retrofits go through the HOA or property manager. See the full breakdown below.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most rebate dollar figures on this page assume you own the property and control the whole building. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

You can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, water heater) — those go to whoever owns the property. If you want the bigger upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, an in-unit heat pump — may qualify for the rebates above with your building's approval, usually required for anything touching shared systems or exterior walls.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation and solar for a whole condo or apartment building go through the HOA or property owner as a group decision, not the single-family numbers on this page. Contact PG&E directly to ask about multifamily program options.
