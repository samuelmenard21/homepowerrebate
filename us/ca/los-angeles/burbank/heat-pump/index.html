Heat Pump Rebates in Burbank

Burbank Water and Power pays $1,000 per ton of heat pump when it replaces a gas system, but the standard cap of $2,500 means a typical 3-ton system leaves money on the table. Low-income customers get a cap of $5,000. The program has firm rules about who does the work.

What Burbank Water and Power pays

These figures come from the Electrify Your Home page of Burbank Water and Power (BWP), which we read on October 2, 2026. BWP says new construction does not qualify and that at least one new item must replace a gas-powered equivalent.

Open

$1,000 per ton

Up to $2,500, for a heat pump HVAC system or mini-split replacing gas.

Open

$1,000 per ton

Up to $5,000, for customers in the Burbank Utility Service Subsidy (BUSS) or Lifeline program.

Where the cap bites

At $1,000 per ton, a 2.5-ton system reaches the $2,500 cap. A 3-ton or 4-ton system earns the same $2,500, so the extra tons are not rebated. A low-income customer with a 3-ton system earns $3,000 and one with a 5-ton system reaches $5,000. Size the system for the house first, and do not buy a bigger unit because of the rebate.

The rule that decides whether you qualify

BWP's rebates are for electrification, not for upgrades in general. One new item on your project must replace a gas-powered equivalent, and the gas line has to be capped. A heat pump that replaces an old electric heater, or an air conditioner swap with no gas involved, does not meet that test. New construction does not qualify either. If you are not sure whether your project counts, ask BWP before you sign a quote.

Who has to do the work

BWP wants a licensed electrician to plan and install the work, including any panel or circuit upgrade and the capping of the gas line. The electrician must pull the permits and the installation must pass inspection from Burbank's Building and Safety Division. That is stricter than many utilities, so confirm early that your installer will work this way. A general handyman, or a homeowner doing the job alone, does not meet the program's steps.

BWP also asks for before and after photos of the old and new equipment, the nameplates with make, model and serial number, and a mark from a recognized testing lab such as UL or ETL. Take the photos on installation day.

Your utility: Burbank Water and Power

BWP is the city's own utility and it sets these amounts. Its standard $1,000 per ton, capped at $2,500, sits between Pasadena's $170 per ton (Pasadena) and Glendale's $1,000 per ton capped at $5,000 (Glendale). BWP's own site is clear that bigger incentives exist for income-qualified customers: enroll in BUSS or Lifeline before you apply, and note that BWP wants the enrollment approved within the past 18 months.

The call center is (818) 238-3700, and the office is at 164 W. Magnolia Boulevard.

Climate and sizing in Burbank

Burbank's average January low over 2015 to 2024 was 8.4°C (about 47°F), with a coldest recorded day of -0.6°C. A standard heat pump handles that without a cold-climate rating. Where homeowners get caught is oversizing: because the cap is flat, a larger unit costs more and earns nothing extra above 2.5 tons. Ask your installer for a written load calculation and the tonnage they recommend.

Other money

The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended for spending after December 31, 2025. Statewide heat pump help from TECH Clean California and the federal HEEHRA program is fully reserved and takes new requests on hold only. BWP points customers to the Home Energy Resource Hub and the Switch Is On site for anything else.

How to claim the BWP rebate

  1. Check that BWP is your electric utility, and that at least one new item will replace a gas-powered equivalent.
  2. If you want the low-income amount, enroll in BUSS or Lifeline first and make sure it was approved within the past 18 months.
  3. Hire a licensed electrician and have them obtain the permits.
  4. Take before photos of the old gas equipment and photos of the new nameplates.
  5. After the install passes inspection from Burbank's Building and Safety Division, send BWP the application, itemized invoices with proof of payment, a W-9, and the finaled permits.
  6. Submit by email to BWPConservation@burbankca.gov, by mail, or in person at 164 W. Magnolia Boulevard.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Burbank?

$1,000 per ton, up to $2,500. Low-income customers in BUSS or Lifeline, and certain affordable housing providers, can get up to $5,000.

Does the heat pump have to replace gas?

Yes. BWP says at least one new item on the project must replace a gas-powered equivalent, and new construction does not qualify.

Can I do the install myself?

No. BWP requires a licensed electrician to install the equipment and any panel work, with permits and an inspection by Burbank's Building and Safety Division.

Can I claim a panel upgrade rebate alone?

No. The $750 panel upgrade rebate cannot be claimed on its own.

What proof does BWP ask for?

Itemized invoices and proof of payment, a signed W-9, the finaled permits, before pictures of the old equipment and nameplate pictures of the new equipment.
