Burbank Home Energy Rebates 2026

Burbank runs its own city utility, Burbank Water and Power (BWP), so Burbank rebates come from BWP and not from Southern California Edison. BWP has two tiers: standard rebates, and much larger ones for customers in its BUSS and Lifeline programs. We read BWP's pages on October 2, 2026.

Two tiers: standard and income-qualified

BWP pays standard amounts to every customer and bigger ones to customers enrolled in the Burbank Utility Service Subsidy (BUSS) or Lifeline, and to certain affordable housing providers. A low-income customer can get up to $5,000 for a heat pump, $2,000 for an induction cooktop and $600 for a heat pump dryer. Enroll before you apply, and have the approval be within the past 18 months. If you qualify, check the assistance programs first, since they change the math more than any single product.

Rules that apply to the electrification rebates

New construction is excluded and at least one new item must replace a gas-powered equivalent. A licensed electrician does the work, obtains the permits, caps the gas line and passes an inspection from Burbank's Building and Safety Division. BWP also asks for before and after photos with nameplates, itemized invoices, proof of payment, a W-9 and the finaled permits. Applications go to BWPConservation@burbankca.gov, by mail, or in person at 164 W. Magnolia Boulevard.

How BWP compares nearby

Glendale pays $1,000 per ton up to $5,000 and $4,000 for a gas water heater swap (Glendale). Pasadena pays $170 per ton and $500 for a water heater (Pasadena). LADWP pays up to $2,500 per ton and up to $2,500 for a water heater (Los Angeles). BWP's standard amounts sit in the middle, and its low-income amounts are the largest of the four. Check your electric bill, because the utility named on it decides what you can claim.

What we did not find

BWP's pages list no solar or battery rebate and no windows rebate. We also found no electric range rebate, despite a $500 figure in some older notes, which we removed. BWP does not publish per-square-foot insulation amounts or per-model thermostat amounts on its pages, so we do not quote them. If you find a current BWP program we missed, tell us at hello@homepowerrebate.com.

Money that ended or is full

The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended for spending after December 31, 2025. Statewide heat pump help from TECH Clean California and the federal HEEHRA program is fully reserved and takes new requests on hold only. BWP points customers to the Switch Is On California site for other incentives.

How to start

  1. Look at your electric bill and confirm that BWP supplies your power.
  2. If you may qualify for the income-based amounts, enroll in BUSS or Lifeline first.
  3. Pick the upgrade that fits your next purchase and open its page above for the rules.
  4. Hire a licensed electrician and ask who handles the permits and the inspection.
  5. Keep the invoice, the nameplate photos and the finaled permits, and send them to BWP.

Common questions

Does Burbank have its own electric utility?

Yes. Burbank Water and Power serves Burbank, and its rebates are separate from Southern California Edison's. Check the name on your electric bill.

Which BWP rebate pays the most?

For standard customers, the heat pump at up to $2,500. For low-income customers, the induction cooktop at $2,000 and the heat pump at up to $5,000.

Do I need a licensed electrician?

For the electrification rebates, yes. BWP requires permits and an inspection from Burbank's Building and Safety Division.

Is there a solar rebate in Burbank?

We found no solar rebate on BWP's pages.

How do I qualify for the low-income amounts?

Enroll in BUSS or Lifeline before you apply, with an approval from within the past 18 months. Certain affordable housing providers can also qualify.
