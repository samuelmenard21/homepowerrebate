Appliance Rebates in Glendale

Glendale homeowners can claim a real appliance rebate through Glendale Water and Power on select ENERGY STAR refrigerators, clothes washers, and related appliances.

How much you get

Glendale's appliance rebate comes from Glendale Water and Power at up to $400. The exact amount depends on which appliance you're replacing and, in some cities, where you buy it.

Glendale runs its own municipal utility, Glendale Water and Power, independent of both LADWP and Southern California Edison. GWP's Home Energy and Water Saving Rebates Program covers ENERGY STAR home appliances alongside a notable $400 bill-credit rebate for an electric wall oven, relevant if you're pairing an induction cooktop with a matching electric oven during a kitchen swap. Because GWP is a small, city-owned utility rather than a statewide program, rebate amounts and qualifying models are set locally and can shift year to year, so confirm the current list before buying.

Buying the right appliance for a Glendale household

A heat pump clothes dryer is worth prioritizing over a standard model if your dryer runs several loads a week. It moves heat rather than generating it from resistance coils, which typically cuts drying energy use by close to half, and many models don't need to vent to the outside at all, which is useful in a unit or older home where adding exterior ducting is difficult or impossible. The tradeoff is cycle time: expect 20 to 40 minutes longer per load than a conventional dryer.

When shopping ENERGY STAR, know there are two tiers. Standard ENERGY STAR certification is the baseline efficiency bar; ENERGY STAR Most Efficient is a smaller, stricter list published annually that represents the top-performing models in each category. Rebate programs sometimes require Most Efficient certification specifically for the larger incentive tier, so check which list applies before you buy.

If you're considering an induction cooktop or range, the single most common mistake is buying one before checking your cookware. Induction heats through a magnetic field, so it only works with magnetic pots and pans: cast iron and most stainless steel qualify, but all-aluminum and copper cookware won't heat at all on an induction surface. How to claim it

  1. Confirm your appliance model is on the current qualifying list before you buy: not every ENERGY STAR-labeled unit clears every program's bar
  2. Purchase from a retailer that carries a qualifying model, keeping your dated receipt
  3. File the claim the way Glendale Water and Power requires: some programs apply the discount instantly at checkout, others need an online or mailed application after purchase

Common questions

Are appliance rebates available in Glendale?

Yes, through Glendale Water and Power (up to $400). Amounts and eligible models vary by appliance category, and some programs pay instantly at the register while others require a mail-in or online claim after purchase.

What appliances save the most on a Glendale bill?

A refrigerator running around the clock and a clothes dryer used weekly tend to have the biggest impact on your electricity bill. Swapping either for an ENERGY STAR model is usually where the rebate dollars pencil out fastest.

Is the $400 electric wall oven rebate the same as a range rebate?

It's specifically for a built-in electric wall oven, not a full range. If you're installing an induction cooktop with a separate wall oven as part of the same kitchen project, that oven can potentially qualify for this credit on top of the cooktop itself.
