Heat Pump & Solar Rebates for Glendale Homeowners

Every LADWP rebate a Glendale homeowner can get in 2026 — heat pumps, solar, battery storage, water heaters, and EV chargers — explained plainly, with real installed costs and payback.

Updated August 2026 · Independent guide. We don't sell your info. Numbers below are LADWP's own published rebate figures.

Glendale is a hillside city bordering LA with Spanish Revival and mid-century housing climbing into the Verdugo foothills. This page covers every LADWP rebate available to Glendale homeowners in one place.

In 2026, a Glendale homeowner in LADWP territory can claim $2,500 per ton on a heat pump (typically $7,500–$10,000+ for a 3–4 ton system), $5,400 for a home battery, and $2,500 for a heat pump water heater. LADWP does not currently rebate rooftop solar, insulation, or windows directly, and the federal solar tax credit expired at the end of 2025 — so those are savings-only decisions now, not rebate ones.

What Glendale homeowners can claim

Glendale sits in LADWP (Los Angeles Department of Water and Power) territory for electricity. Unlike investor-owned utilities such as SCE or PG&E, LADWP runs its own rebate programs directly — and its heat pump rebate is genuinely strong, but solar and building-envelope work currently carry no utility rebate here.

🔥 Heat Pump

$2,500/ton

3–4 ton systems typically qualify for $7,500–$10,000+. Installed cost usually runs $13,000–$22,000, with roughly $800/year in heating savings and an ~18-year simple payback on the unrebated portion.

Source: LADWP residential heat pump rebate

☀️ Solar Panels (6 kW)

$0 utility rebate

A 6 kW system typically costs $15,000–$18,000 and saves about $1,200/year, paying back in roughly 13 years — on savings alone, since LADWP does not currently offer a solar installation rebate.

Source: LADWP program listing; no residential solar rebate currently offered

🔋 Home Battery (13.5 kWh)

$5,400

Against a typical installed cost around $12,000. Keeps essential circuits running during an outage, on top of the electricity-bill savings.

Source: LADWP battery storage rebate

🌡️ Insulation

$0

No dedicated LADWP rebate currently offered for insulation. Still worth doing first — it shrinks the heat pump you need and cuts bills year-round.

Source: LADWP program listing

Replaces a gas or electric tank with a unit that uses a fraction of the energy.

The resilience play

In Glendale, a battery is about keeping the lights on

Southern California's grid is generally reliable, but wildfire-related public safety power shutoffs and heat-driven demand spikes are real considerations. A 13.5 kWh home battery, with LADWP's $5,400 rebate against a roughly $12,000 installed cost, keeps essential circuits — fridge, lights, internet, medical equipment — running when the grid drops. Solar's role here is mostly to recharge that battery through the day; on its own, at $0 utility rebate and a ~13-year payback, it's a savings decision rather than a fast-payback one.

LADWP program detail

How each LADWP rebate actually works

These are LADWP's own residential programs. Here's what each one covers and how you claim it.

LADWP

Residential heat pump rebate

$2,500/ton

Paid per ton of installed heat pump capacity. Most single-family homes need a 3–4 ton system, which puts the rebate at $7,500–$10,000 before any additional incentives. Installed cost typically runs $13,000–$22,000, so the rebate is a real offset but not the whole project.

How it works: Your contractor typically applies through LADWP's rebate portal after installation, with proof of purchase and system specs (tonnage, efficiency rating). Confirm your contractor is comfortable filing LADWP paperwork before you sign a contract. LADWP rebate programs →
LADWP

Home battery storage rebate

$5,400

Covers a portion of a home battery system (typically sized around 13.5 kWh) against a roughly $12,000 installed cost. Provides backup power during outages in addition to the rebate itself.

How it works: Applied for after installation through LADWP, generally alongside or after a solar or standalone battery install. Confirm your specific battery model is on LADWP's qualifying equipment list before you buy.
LADWP

Heat pump water heater rebate

$2,500

A heat pump water heater uses a fraction of the energy of a standard electric tank, and can replace a gas, electric, or oil unit.

How it works: Pairs naturally with a space-heating heat pump install on the same visit — ask your contractor to quote both together.
LADWP

EV charger & smart thermostat rebates

$1,000 & $75

$1,000 toward a Level 2 home EV charger installation, and $75 for a qualifying smart thermostat.

How it works: Apply through LADWP's rebate portal with proof of purchase and installation. Both are among the simplest LADWP rebates to claim since they don't require a pre-installation inspection.
Federal (expired)

Federal solar tax credit & HEAR program

no longer available

The federal 30% solar investment tax credit (ITC) expired December 31, 2025. The federal HEAR (Home Electrification and Appliance Rebates) program for single-family homes closed in February 2026. Neither is available to new applicants as of this writing — any site or contractor still advertising them is out of date.

What's left: The LADWP rebates on this page are the current, real numbers for Glendale homeowners. There is no federal backstop right now for solar or whole-home electrification.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most of the dollar figures on this page assume you own a home outright and control the whole property. That's not everyone in Glendale — a large share of Glendale households live in apartments and condos rather than detached homes. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

The LADWP heat pump, solar, and battery rebates go to whoever owns and installs the equipment — typically your landlord, not you. We don't have a confirmed LADWP or state program specifically for renter-initiated upgrades to point you to right now, so the honest advice is: if you want these upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord or property manager, and you can point them to this page and LADWP's own rebate portal.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, an in-unit heat pump, a smart thermostat — may qualify for the LADWP rebates above with your HOA's approval, particularly for anything touching shared walls, exterior units, or common electrical systems. Get that approval in writing before applying. We don't have confirmed details on a separate LADWP multi-unit or whole-building rebate stream, so if your building is considering a group retrofit, contact LADWP directly to ask what applies to multi-family or condo properties specifically.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

For an entire apartment or condo building, decisions typically run through the HOA or property owner rather than individual unit owners, and LADWP may handle multi-family properties differently than single-family rebate applications. We don't have verified specifics on Glendale-area multi-family LADWP terms to state here confidently — if this applies to you, the most reliable next step is calling LADWP's customer programs line directly rather than relying on the single-family figures on this page.

FAQ for Glendale residents

How much can a Glendale homeowner get for a heat pump?

LADWP pays $2,500 per ton installed, and residential systems typically run 3–4 tons — so the rebate alone is usually $7,500–$10,000, and can run higher depending on your exact system and any additional LADWP incentives layered on top. Installed cost typically runs $13,000–$22,000, so the rebate covers a meaningful share, not the whole project.

Is there a solar rebate in LADWP territory?

No — LADWP does not currently offer a utility rebate for residential solar installation. A typical 6 kW system in LADWP territory costs roughly $15,000–$18,000 and saves around $1,200 a year, paying back in about 13 years on electricity savings alone, with no rebate offsetting the upfront cost. The federal solar investment tax credit (ITC) that used to help here expired December 31, 2025, so this is now a savings-only decision, not a rebate one.

What about a home battery?

LADWP offers a $5,400 rebate on a home battery (typical size 13.5 kWh), against a typical installed cost around $12,000. That's a meaningful offset, and a battery also keeps essentials running during an outage — a real consideration in a region with periodic wildfire-related power shutoffs.

Did the federal heat pump tax credit go away?

The federal HEAR program for single-family homes closed in February 2026, and the separate federal solar ITC expired December 31, 2025. Neither is available to new applicants now. What's left is the LADWP rebate stack described on this page — that's the real, current number to plan around.

Can I get any of this if I rent or live in a condo in Glendale?

The LADWP rebates on this page are structured for whoever owns and controls the equipment being replaced — typically the property owner. Renters generally can't claim the heat pump, solar, or battery rebates directly since those upgrades belong to the building. See the renters & condo section below for what actually applies to you.
