Los Angeles Home Energy Rebates 2026

Most of the City of Los Angeles gets its power from the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP), which runs its own rebates apart from Southern California Edison. Its programs changed in the past year, so older pages that quote a single heat pump or insulation figure are out of date. We read LADWP's pages on October 2, 2026.

What LADWP pays in 2026

Each card links to a page with the rules, the paperwork and the local detail. LADWP says funding is limited and first come, first served, and that rebates are not guaranteed.

What changed in the past year

LADWP raised its heat pump and heat pump water heater rebates for equipment bought and installed on or after November 1, 2025, and it replaced single amounts with tiers by efficiency and tank size. It also added an Attic Insulation Rebate Program for projects from September 1, 2025. If you saw a figure like $2,500 for a heat pump or a flat $2,500 for a water heater on another site, those are the top of the scale, and most homes will earn less. Check the tier for the model you plan to buy.

Rules that apply across most programs

You must be an LADWP residential customer. Most rebates need a final approved Building and Safety permit, a paid itemized invoice, and an application postmarked or submitted within 12 months. LADWP can inspect the installation before it pays, and new construction and accessory dwelling units are excluded from the heat pump, water heater and window rebates. Rebates are not guaranteed until LADWP verifies them. The program line is (866) 484-0433, weekdays 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.

Other LADWP programs we saw

LADWP also pays for variable-speed pool pumps ($500, or $750 if bought between June 1 and September 30, 2026), whole house fans ($200) and cool roofs ($0.20 to $0.60 per square foot). We found no LADWP solar rebate on its residential programs pages, which list feed-in tariff programs instead, and no general home battery rebate. The state's SGIP equity program, which LADWP administers, can help households at or below 80% of area median income add solar and storage.

How LADWP compares with neighbors

Glendale pays $1,000 per ton up to $5,000 and $4,000 for a gas water heater swap (Glendale). Burbank pays $1,000 per ton up to $2,500 and $1,500 for a water heater (Burbank). Pasadena pays $170 per ton and $500 for a water heater (Pasadena). LADWP's top per-ton rate is the highest of the four, and its EV charger and insulation rebates are the largest too. Check your electric bill, since the utility named on it decides what you can claim.

Money that ended or is full

The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended for spending after December 31, 2025. LADWP's pages still mention a federal credit of up to $2,000 for heat pumps, which does not apply to 2026 spending. Statewide heat pump help from TECH Clean California and the federal HEEHRA program is fully reserved and takes new requests on hold only. The Switch Is On California site lists other incentives.

How to start

  1. Look at your electric bill and confirm that LADWP supplies your power.
  2. Pick the upgrade that fits your next purchase and open its page above for the rules and tiers.
  3. Ask your installer who pulls the LADBS permit, and get it finaled.
  4. Keep the paid invoice, certificates and photos LADWP asks for.
  5. Apply online through your LADWP account or by mail within 12 months of purchase.

Common questions

Does Los Angeles have its own electric utility?

Most of the City of Los Angeles is served by LADWP, whose rebates are separate from Southern California Edison's. Check the name on your electric bill.

Which LADWP rebate pays the most?

For a typical home, the heat pump (up to $2,500 per ton for a very efficient mini-split) and the heat pump water heater (up to $2,500). The EV charger can reach $1,500.

Is there an LADWP solar rebate?

We found none on LADWP's residential programs pages. It lists feed-in tariff programs, and SGIP equity help for households at or below 80% of area median income.

Do I need a permit?

For most of the rebates, yes. LADWP asks for a final approved Building and Safety permit.

Are LADWP rebates guaranteed?

No. LADWP says funding is limited, first come, first served, and rebates are not guaranteed.
