The largest single opportunity here

SMUD's battery enrollment incentive — up to $6,000

SMUD's My Energy Optimizer Partner+ program pays a one-time $300 per kWh, up to $6,000, when you enroll a qualifying battery and let SMUD draw a little stored power at peak times. SMUD always keeps 20% in reserve for outages. You must be on the Solar and Storage Rate and enroll within 90 days of permission to operate. Projects submitted by Sept. 22, 2026 got the older $500 per kWh (up to $10,000). It's the single largest dollar opportunity on this page for households considering both solar and storage together.

Available everywhere in SMUD territory

SMUD programs — what each one is

These are the backbone. They're the same whether you're in Sacramento, Folsom, Roseville, or Rancho Cordova — SMUD serves the whole metro area under one utility, unlike much of California which is split across investor-owned utilities.

SMUD

Heat pump rebate

Up to $3,000

For a two-stage or variable-speed heat pump installed by a SMUD Contractor Network installer. Replacing a gas furnace can add up to $2,000 through SMUD's Go Electric bonus.

How it works: Apply through an SMUD-participating contractor, ideally before work starts. SMUD rebates & incentives →
SMUD

Solar (self-generation) rebate

$0 utility rebate

SMUD doesn't pay a cash solar rebate. New systems go on SMUD's Solar and Storage Rate, so size solar to your own use and pair it with a battery if you can.

How it works: Your solar installer typically handles the SMUD paperwork and interconnection process on your behalf.
SMUD

Home battery rebate

Up to $6,000

SMUD has no flat battery rebate. Its My Energy Optimizer Partner+ program pays a one-time $300 per kWh (up to $6,000) to enroll a qualifying battery on the Solar and Storage Rate. Projects submitted by Sept. 22, 2026 got $500 per kWh.

How it works: Must be an SMUD-approved battery model. Ask your installer to confirm eligibility before purchase.
SMUD

Heat pump water heater rebate

$4,000

Replaces a gas or electric tank water heater with a heat pump model that uses roughly a third the energy.

How it works: Pairs naturally with a space-heating heat pump project on the same visit.

Insulation rebate

SMUD's Seal & Insulate rebates cover air sealing, attic insulation and ducts, up to $3,000, through a Home Performance Program contractor.

How it works: Same SMUD contractor application process as the heat pump rebate.

Federal tax credits (25C / 25D)

Ended

The federal solar, battery and efficiency tax credits ended for installs after December 31, 2025. SMUD's own rebates are what's left to stack.

FAQ for Folsom residents

How much can I get for a heat pump in Folsom?

SMUD's heat pump rebate is up to $3,000 for a qualifying two-stage or variable-speed system. Switching from a gas furnace can add up to $2,000 more through SMUD's Go Electric bonus for panel and circuit work.

Is solar worth it in Folsom?

Folsom gets strong, reliable sun most of the year, which is why the payback here is faster than coastal California. A 6kW system runs $15,000–$18,000, and after the $2,000 SMUD rebate the payback is about 11 years from roughly $1,400/year in savings.

What is SMUD's storage enrollment bonus?

SMUD's My Energy Optimizer Partner+ program pays a one-time $300 per kWh, up to $6,000, to enroll a qualifying battery (projects submitted by Sept. 22, 2026 got $500 per kWh, up to $10,000). There is no separate flat battery rebate.

Can I still claim a federal tax credit with SMUD rebates?

Not anymore. The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended December 31, 2025. SMUD rebates still stack with each other — for example a heat pump rebate plus the Go Electric bonus.

Can renters or condo owners in Folsom get any rebate?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates directly since those go to the property owner, but SMUD's My Energy Optimizer program offers free or discounted efficiency devices open to renters. Condo owners can pursue in-unit upgrades like a heat pump water heater with building approval; whole-building retrofits go through the HOA. See the full breakdown below.

When should I apply for SMUD rebates?

Apply through an SMUD-participating contractor before work begins wherever possible — some SMUD rebates require pre-approval, and starting work first can forfeit eligibility.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most rebate dollar figures on this page assume you own the property and control the whole building. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

You can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, solar, battery) — those go to whoever owns the property. SMUD's My Energy Optimizer program does offer free or discounted efficiency devices open to renters. If you want the bigger upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, an in-unit heat pump — may qualify for the rebates above with your building's approval, usually required for anything touching shared systems or exterior walls.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation and solar for a whole condo or apartment building go through the HOA or property owner as a group decision, not the single-family numbers on this page. Contact SMUD directly to ask about multifamily program options.
