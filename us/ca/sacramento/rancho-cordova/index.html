The largest single opportunity here

SMUD's storage enrollment bonus — up to $10,000

Beyond the standard $5,400 battery rebate, SMUD's My Energy Optimizer program pays homeowners to enroll a qualifying battery in demand-response — letting SMUD tap a small amount of stored power during grid stress in exchange for an enrollment bonus that can reach up to $10,000. This stacks with the base rebate and with the federal residential clean energy tax credit, and it's the single largest dollar opportunity on this page for households considering both solar and storage together.

SMUD programs — what each one is

These are the backbone. They're the same whether you're in Sacramento, Folsom, Roseville, or Rancho Cordova — SMUD serves the whole metro area under one utility, unlike much of California which is split across investor-owned utilities.

SMUD

Heat pump rebate

$3,000–$4,000

A cold-climate heat pump both heats and cools your home using electricity. Amount depends on system size and efficiency tier.

How it works: Your solar installer typically handles the SMUD paperwork and interconnection process on your behalf.

FAQ for Rancho Cordova residents

How much can I get for a heat pump in Rancho Cordova?

SMUD's heat pump rebate runs $3,000–$4,000 depending on system size and efficiency tier. A typical 3-4 ton system costs $13,000–$22,000 installed, so most Rancho Cordova homeowners net $9,000–$19,000 out of pocket, paying back in roughly 14 years from the $900/year in savings.

Is solar worth it in Rancho Cordova?

Rancho Cordova gets strong, reliable sun most of the year, which is why the payback here is faster than coastal California. A 6kW system runs $15,000–$18,000, and after the $2,000 SMUD rebate the payback is about 11 years from roughly $1,400/year in savings.

What is SMUD's storage enrollment bonus?

Beyond the $5,400 battery rebate, SMUD's My Energy Optimizer program pays up to $10,000 in additional incentives for enrolling a qualifying battery in demand-response, on top of the standard rebate and any federal credit you claim.

Do SMUD rebates stack with federal incentives?

Yes. SMUD rebates are separate from and stack with the federal residential clean energy and energy-efficiency tax credits, so you claim both without either one reducing the other.

Can renters or condo owners in Rancho Cordova get any rebate?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates directly since those go to the property owner, but SMUD's My Energy Optimizer program offers free or discounted efficiency devices open to renters. Condo owners can pursue in-unit upgrades like a heat pump water heater with building approval; whole-building retrofits go through the HOA. See the full breakdown below.

When should I apply for SMUD rebates?

Apply through an SMUD-participating contractor before work begins wherever possible — some SMUD rebates require pre-approval, and starting work first can forfeit eligibility.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most rebate dollar figures on this page assume you own the property and control the whole building. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo:

If you rent

You can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, solar, battery) — those go to whoever owns the property. SMUD's My Energy Optimizer program does offer free or discounted efficiency devices open to renters. If you want the bigger upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, an in-unit heat pump — may qualify for the rebates above with your building's approval, usually required for anything touching shared systems or exterior walls.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation and solar for a whole condo or apartment building go through the HOA or property owner as a group decision, not the single-family numbers on this page. Contact SMUD directly to ask about multifamily program options.
