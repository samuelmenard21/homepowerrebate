Most Common Question

Why is SDG&E rebate so much lower than LADWP?

Short answer: Different utility, different priorities. LADWP offers $4,000–$5,000. SDG&E offers $250–$500. Both still deliver solid payback, just different timelines.

SDG&E path:

You pay $14,000–$16,000 after rebate for a heat pump. You save $1,200–$1,500 per year. Payback in 9–13 years. Still a good deal—just takes longer than LADWP areas.

Why the difference?

LADWP is publicly owned and prioritizes aggressive rebates. SDG&E is investor-owned with different incentive strategies. San Diego also has high solar adoption already, so rebate focus differs.

Quick Questions

Can I move to LADWP area to get their better rebates?
No. You can't choose which utility serves your address. If you're in SDG&E territory, that's your utility. You can explore other local/state incentives, but utility choice isn't available.
Does SDG&E have stacking or other programs?
Limited stacking. You might combine SDG&E rebates with California state rebates or city programs, but amounts are modest. Check your specific city for local programs.
What other California rebates can San Diego homeowners use?
California has no unified state heat pump rebate (federal IRA programs ended). You might qualify for income-based programs or GRID Alternatives rebates if eligible. See all alternatives.
How long until I get my SDG&E rebate?
SDG&E processing varies. Typical timeline: Application approval 2–6 weeks, work 3–7 days, rebate payment 4–12 weeks after completion as bill credit or check.
