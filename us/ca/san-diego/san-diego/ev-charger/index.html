EV Charging Rewards in San Diego

San Diego Community Power does not rebate a home charger. Its EV Flex Connect pilot pays you for when you charge: $50 upfront per eligible vehicle and $5 a month for letting an app schedule charging away from peak hours. It works best for Tesla drivers right now.

What San Diego Community Power pays

These figures come from the EV Flex Connect page of San Diego Community Power (SDCP), which we read on October 2, 2026. Incentives are issued as digital payments, which may change over time. You must be an active SDCP residential customer.

Open

Upfront incentive

$50 per vehicle

For each eligible EV. Multiple vehicles can be enrolled per household.

Open

Ongoing incentive

$5 a month

For staying active in the program.

Open

Eligible vehicles

Mostly Tesla

Cybertruck Crew (2024+), Model 3 (2017+), Model S (2012+), Model X (2016+), Model Y (2020+). Other makes are in a demonstration phase and may not charge as expected.

What it adds up to

$50 + 12 x $5 = $110 in the first year for one vehicle, then $60 a year. It is a small reward for something many people do anyway, charging overnight. It is not a charger rebate, so any charger or circuit cost is on you.

How enrollment works

You join through the Optiwatt app or website and connect your eligible EV, share your charging needs and schedule, and enroll in EV Flex Connect from the app's programs section. SDCP says it takes a few days to review the application. After approval, plug in at home and the program charges the vehicle to the level you set by the time you need it, shifting charging to lower-demand hours. You can override the schedule, but frequent overrides may affect your incentives.

Connecting your car to a third-party app means sharing vehicle data with it, so read the terms and conditions that SDCP links from its program page before you enroll. If you change cars or move, ask SDCP whether your enrollment carries over.

The eligibility rules

You must own or drive an eligible EV and charge at home at least 50% of the time, have Wi-Fi or cellular service at the charger, use a dedicated charger in a spot used by your household only, and not be in another demand response program such as the Emergency Load Reduction Program or Demand Side Grid Support. SDCP calls it an active pilot primarily optimized for Tesla drivers, and warns that non-Tesla vehicles may not always charge as expected, and recommends removing in-vehicle charging schedules to avoid conflicts.

Your charger and the wiring

SDCP does not pay for a charger or its circuit. The City of San Diego issues simple no-plan permits for minor mechanical, electrical and plumbing work, normally within two business days, and single-family and duplex projects choose the Combination permit (see the City's simple permit page). Ask your electrician which permit they plan to pull. San Diego's average January low over 2015 to 2024 was 8.8°C, so a garage charger runs fine year-round.

Compared with other utilities

Burbank pays $200 to $500 for the charger itself (Burbank), LADWP up to $1,000 plus a rate discount (Los Angeles), and SMUD up to $850 (Sacramento). San Diego's EV program pays for behavior instead, and it is the smallest of them. SDG&E's rebates page does not list an EV charger rebate (SDG&E).

How to join EV Flex Connect

  1. Confirm that San Diego Community Power supplies your electricity.
  2. Check that your EV is on SDCP's eligible list and that you charge at home at least half the time.
  3. Join through the Optiwatt app or website and connect the vehicle.
  4. Enter your charging needs and schedule.
  5. Enroll in EV Flex Connect from the app's programs section.
  6. After approval in a few days, plug in at home and collect $50 plus $5 a month.

Common questions

How much does EV Flex Connect pay?

$50 upfront per eligible vehicle and $5 a month while you stay active.

Does SDCP rebate a home charger?

Not that we found. EV Flex Connect pays for charging behavior, not equipment.

Which cars qualify?

Primarily Tesla models listed on the page. Other makes are in a demonstration phase and may not always charge as expected.

What do I need at home?

A dedicated charger used only by your household, Wi-Fi or cellular service at the charger, and at least half your charging done at home.

Does it work in Escondido?

No. Escondido is not an SDCP member.
