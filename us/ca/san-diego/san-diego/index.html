Home Energy Rebates in San Diego

Complete guide to SDG&E rebates for San Diego homeowners in 2026 — heat pumps, solar, water heaters, and realistic payback expectations.

San Diego's anchor city, served by San Diego Gas & Electric Company (SDG&E).

What San Diego homeowners can claim

SDG&E rebates are lower than other California utilities. But the payback is still solid. All amounts below are in US dollars.

Heat Pump

$500–$1,500

SDG&E's standard heat pump rebate. Lowest in California, but real savings still exist.

Solar

Per-kWh adder

Solar incentives vary by program. SDG&E offers ongoing generation credits.

Water Heater

$200–$500

Heat pump water heater rebate from SDG&E.

Battery Storage

Limited

SDG&E battery incentives are modest compared to solar.

Home Energy Assessment

Free

First step to confirm eligibility.

No Pre-Approval

Different from PG&E

SDG&E doesn't require pre-approval before work. Different process than Bay Area.

How to claim it

  1. Schedule free energy assessment with SDG&E
  2. Choose a local contractor (can be any qualified installer)
  3. Work is completed
  4. Submit rebate documentation to SDG&E
  5. Rebate processed (4–12 weeks typical)
Realistic numbers for San Diego: $500–$1,500 rebate + $1,200–$1,500/year savings = 9–13 year payback. Not as fast as Bay Area, but still a solid investment. Run your free assessment →

Why is SDG&E rebate lower?

SDG&E is investor-owned (unlike publicly-owned LADWP). Its priorities and funding structures differ. San Diego also has high solar adoption already, so rebate focus is spread across multiple programs rather than concentrated on heat pumps.
