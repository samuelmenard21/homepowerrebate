Honest math for San Diego

SDG&E's own cash rebates here are modest — $250–$500 on a heat pump, no direct check for solar or battery. The real payback comes from stacking the federal 30% solar/battery tax credit, California's SGIP battery incentive, and the ongoing bill savings from lower gas/electric use, not from utility rebates alone.

Real installed costs and payback

What a heat pump, solar, and battery actually cost in San Diego

A 3–4 ton system typically runs $13,000–$22,000 installed, saving around $600/yr a year — a payback of roughly ~22 years on the rebate alone (bill savings do the real work) on the SDG&E rebate alone, faster once you count bill savings and the federal tax credit. A 6 kW system runs $15,000–$18,000, saving about $1,200/yr/yr for a payback of ~13 years. A 13.5 kWh battery runs about $12,000 before any SGIP incentive. San Diego has some of the best solar potential in the state — more sun hours than almost anywhere else we cover — but SDG&E pays no cash rebate for it. The payoff is federal tax credit plus a long runway of sunny-day generation.

Available across San Diego

SDG&E & statewide programs — what each one is

These programs are the same across San Diego — what changes city to city is mostly local climate and permitting, not the rebate amount. Here's each program and how it works.

SDG&E

SDG&E heat pump rebate

$250–$500

A rebate toward a qualifying cold-climate or standard heat pump that both heats and cools your home using electricity instead of gas.

How it works: Confirm eligibility with SDG&E before work starts, then have your contractor complete the installation and submit paperwork. Official SDG&E rebate page →
SDG&E

SDG&E heat pump water heater rebate

$250

Replaces a gas or electric tank water heater with a heat pump model that uses roughly a third the energy.

How it works: Apply through SDG&E after your contractor completes the install; pairs naturally with a space-heating heat pump on the same visit.
SDG&E

SDG&E home EV charger rebate

$500

A flat rebate toward installing a Level 2 home EV charger.

How it works: Apply through SDG&E after installation with your purchase and install receipts. EV rebate details →
SDG&E

SDG&E smart thermostat rebate

$50

A modest rebate on qualifying smart thermostat models, on top of any manufacturer or retailer promotions.

How it works: Usually claimed as an instant in-store or online discount, or a mail-in rebate depending on the retailer.
California SGIP

Self-Generation Incentive Program (battery)

$1,000+ (SGIP)

California's statewide incentive for home battery storage, available regardless of which utility serves you. Equity and equity resiliency tiers (for wildfire/PSPS-prone or lower-income households) pay significantly more per kWh than the general market rate.

How it works: Your installer typically applies on your behalf through the SGIP program administrator for your utility territory. SGIP program details →
Federal

Federal solar & battery tax credit (ITC)

30% of cost

A federal tax credit equal to 30% of the cost of a qualifying solar and/or battery system, claimed on your federal tax return — not a rebate check, but it reduces what you owe dollar-for-dollar.

How it works: No pre-approval needed; your tax preparer applies IRS Form 5695 using your installer's documentation. Confirm current-year eligibility rules with a tax professional, as federal credit rules can change.

FAQ for San Diego residents

Does solar actually work in San Diego?

Yes — San Diego gets ~7.1 peak sun hours/day, among the strongest solar resource of any region we cover. SDG&E pays no direct cash rebate for solar, so the return comes from the 30% federal tax credit and net billing credits on your utility bill, not a utility check.

How much does a heat pump rebate save in San Diego?

SDG&E pays $250–$500 toward a qualifying heat pump. A typical 3–4 ton system costs $13,000–$22,000 installed and saves around $600/yr on energy bills — the rebate is a modest dent, so bill savings and the federal tax credit do most of the payback work.

Is there a battery rebate in San Diego?

California's Self-Generation Incentive Program (SGIP) pays toward home batteries statewide, including SDG&E territory — equity and resiliency tiers can pay significantly more.

Can I get these rebates if I rent or live in a condo in San Diego?

Renters can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, solar, insulation) since those go to the property owner, but EV charger and smart thermostat offers may still apply to what you control. Condo owners can pursue in-unit upgrades with building approval; whole-building retrofits go through the HOA or property manager as a separate process. See the full breakdown below.

Does San Diego have its own city-level rebate on top of SDG&E?

Not currently — we have not confirmed a San Diego-specific municipal top-up program. The figures on this page are SDG&E and California statewide/federal programs available to every San Diego homeowner in its service area.

When should I apply for SDG&E rebates?

Apply as soon as you've chosen an installer — most SDG&E rebate programs require eligibility confirmation or an application before work starts, so applying after installation can forfeit the rebate.

A significant share of San Diego households

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most rebate dollar figures on this page assume you own a single-family home and control the whole building. Here's what changes if you rent or live in a condo in San Diego:

If you rent

You can't claim the structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, solar) — those go to whoever owns the property. If your unit has its own EV parking or you can install a plug-in smart thermostat with landlord permission, those smaller rebates may still be reachable. For the bigger upgrades, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, an in-unit heat pump, a smart thermostat — may qualify for the rebates above with your building's approval, especially for anything touching shared systems, exterior walls, or the roof (solar). Get written approval from your HOA before applying; most SDG&E programs ask for proof of authorization on shared property.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, solar, and battery storage for an entire multi-unit building typically go through the HOA or property manager as a group decision, often under different program terms than the single-family numbers on this page. Ask SDG&E directly about multifamily/multi-unit incentive programs before assuming the per-home figures above apply.
