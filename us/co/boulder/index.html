Home Rebates in Boulder

Boulder County's EnergySmart program retired its standard rebates in late 2025 and now pays half the cost, up to $2,000 a year, for households at or below median income. For everyone else, the money comes from Xcel Energy, Power Ahead Colorado and the state discount.

Boulder's homes

The American Community Survey (2020 to 2024) counts 47,465 homes in Boulder. About 53% of households rent and 47% own. Only 39% of homes are detached houses, and about 9% are in buildings of two to four units. About 55% were built before 1980 and only 18% in 2000 or later.

Boulder has a smaller number of homes than the other four cities, an older stock and a renter majority. Many of the offers below start with the owner, so tenants should hand their landlord the Xcel and EnergySmart links.

Start with income

The first question in Boulder County is whether your household earns at or below 100% of area median income. If it does, EnergySmart pays half the cost of insulation, air sealing, heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, induction cooktops, panel upgrades and cellular shades, up to $2,000 a year, for site-built and manufactured homes. Funds are first come, first served, and work must be done and invoiced in 2026. If your income is higher, go to Xcel. See our Boulder heat pump page and insulation page for examples.

Xcel and others in the county

Xcel Energy customers get the Xcel offers on this page. EnergySmart's list of other rebate sources also names Longmont, Lyons, Erie, Poudre Valley REA and United Power, plus the Colorado RENU loan, so check your bill before relying on Xcel amounts. About 63% of Boulder households heat with utility gas and 33% with electricity.

What ended in 2026

The federal 30% credits ended after 2025. The Colorado Energy Office closed its single-family income-based rebate program in August 2026, and Boulder County sits in its Front Range region. The state's invoice discount remains but is at least $333 for an air-source heat pump, down from $499.95.

Boulder winters

Our climate file, built from ten Januaries (2015 to 2024), gives Boulder an average January low of about 17°F and a coldest day near minus 24°F. Cold-climate equipment that holds output at 5°F is what the Xcel and Power Ahead Colorado heat pump offers ask for.

How to claim Boulder rebates

  1. Check your household income against 100% of area median income at energysmartyes.com.
  2. If you qualify, talk to an EnergySmart advisor before you hire a contractor.
  3. Check your bill to see whether Xcel supplies your electricity and gas.
  4. Choose a contractor who is Xcel participating, Power Ahead Colorado verified and registered for the state discount.
  5. Finish and invoice EnergySmart-funded work before December 31, 2026.

Common questions

What rebates can a Boulder homeowner get in 2026?

EnergySmart's income-qualified rebate (50% up to $2,000 a year), Xcel Energy offers, the $1,500 Power Ahead Colorado heat pump rebate, and the state invoice discount.

Did Boulder County end its rebates?

It retired its standard rebates in late 2025 and kept the income-qualified ones.

Does the city of Boulder have its own rebate?

We did not find a separate city rebate for homes. EnergySmart's list of other rebate sources names Xcel and other utilities, not a city program.

Are the Xcel heat pump amounts open yet?

Xcel's 2026 rebate summary lists them from October 1, 2026. Its page would not load for us, so confirm with Xcel.

Is the state's income-based program open?

No. The single-family program is closed.
