Home Rebates in Denver

Denver has about 359,000 homes, and more than half of the households rent. Most rebate money here comes from Xcel Energy, the Power Ahead Colorado regional program and the state's invoice discount. Two big pots closed this year, and we could not confirm a Denver city rebate for homes.

1. Whose programs should a Denver homeowner check first?

Xcel Energy is the utility we use for Denver. Rebates often depend on who supplies your power or gas, so check yours first.

2. In January, Denver nights usually bottom out near:

Our weather sample for Denver (2015-2024) shows an average January low of 15 °F. Cold-climate heat pumps are tested at low temperatures, so check the rating at your coldest days, not just the average.

3. About how many peak sun hours a day does Denver get on average?

Our 2020-2024 sunshine sample for Denver averages about 5.0 peak sun hours a day. Fewer hours means less solar power per panel, so the size of the system matters.

Who lives in Denver's homes

The American Community Survey (2020 to 2024) says about 51% of Denver households rent and 49% own. Only 40% of homes are detached houses, and about 5% are in buildings of two to four units, so the rest are larger apartment and condo buildings. About 55% were built before 1980 and 31% in 2000 or later.

That mix means a lot of Denver rebate decisions belong to landlords, condo boards and building owners. Several of the programs above reach them: Power Ahead Colorado takes multifamily and commercial buildings, and the state discount lets a contractor claim one credit per unit a heat pump serves. If you rent, use this page to start a conversation with the owner.

How the pieces stack

Denver homes are served by Xcel Energy, and Xcel's heat pump rebate can be combined with the $1,500 Power Ahead Colorado rebate and the state discount, according to Colorado Public Radio's reporting on the program. The best order is to choose a contractor who does all three, get Power Ahead Colorado's approval before work starts, and ask for the model's output at 5°F so you can check Xcel's per-ton math. Our Denver heat pump page works through an example.

Gas or electric

About 61% of Denver households heat with utility gas and 36% with electricity. Xcel pays higher amounts for gas-heated homes on heat pumps, water heaters and weatherization, so the gas majority has the larger rebates. The electric-heat 36% saves the most on bills after a switch. If your home is in that group, start with the heat pump and the insulation, and see the Denver insulation page.

What ended in 2026

The federal 30% credits ended for spending after 2025. The Colorado Energy Office closed its single-family income-based rebate program in August 2026, which could pay up to $8,000 for a cold-climate heat pump and $14,000 in total. The state's invoice discount is still running, but it fell from $499.95 to $333 per air-source heat pump in 2026, and it is scheduled to fall again in tax year 2029.

Group buying and city programs

The city runs a group-buy program with Switch Together Denver. Homeowners sign up, join a group wanting similar equipment, and an installer prices the job; the city says savings can reach 10%. It does not replace any rebate here. The city's pages also list rebates for energy audits, building electrification grants and affordable housing, which are aimed at larger buildings, not single-family homes.

How to claim Denver rebates

  1. Check your Xcel bill to see whether you get gas and electric from Xcel.
  2. Decide which project comes first, usually insulation and air sealing, then the heat pump.
  3. Ask contractors whether they are Xcel participating, Power Ahead Colorado verified and registered for the state discount.
  4. Have the contractor submit heat pump projects to Power Ahead Colorado before any work begins.
  5. Keep every invoice and check that the state discount shows as its own line.

Common questions

What rebates can a Denver homeowner get in 2026?

Xcel Energy heat pump, water heater, insulation, thermostat, EV charger and battery rebates, the $1,500 Power Ahead Colorado heat pump rebate, and the state's invoice discount. The state's income-based program and the federal credits have closed.

Does Denver have its own rebate for homes?

We could not confirm one. The city's homeowner page lists a group-buy program and links to other rebate sources.

Can renters get rebates?

Rebates usually go to whoever pays for the equipment. A tenant can ask the landlord to apply, and some programs cover multifamily buildings.

Are the Xcel heat pump amounts open yet?

Xcel's 2026 rebate summary lists them from October 1, 2026. Its page would not load for us, so confirm the status with Xcel.

Is the state's income-based program open?

No. The Colorado Energy Office says the single-family program is closed.
