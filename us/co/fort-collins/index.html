What Fort Collins homeowners can claim

Amounts below reflect the most recently confirmed Fort Collins Utilities figures, plus statewide Colorado Energy Office programs. All amounts are in US dollars. Programs marked "confirm status" don't have a single published current amount — call the program directly before assuming a figure is live.

🔥 Heat Pump

Retrofit rebate + $2,000 bonus

Fort Collins Utilities' Efficiency Works Retrofit Rebate program pays for qualifying heat pumps and, when a heat pump and an insulation/envelope upgrade are both completed within six months of each other, adds a $2,000 bundling bonus (heat pump water heaters excluded from the bonus). Combined with Colorado's $1,000 state heat pump tax credit. Efficiency Works — Fort Collins Utilities rebates → Colorado Dept. of Revenue — DR 1322 Heat Pump Tax Credit →

🟫 Insulation & Air Sealing

Retrofit rebate + $2,000 bonus

Efficiency Works' Retrofit Rebate program covers building insulation, air/duct sealing, windows, and doors. Pair it with a qualifying heat pump within six months for the $2,000 bundling bonus. Rental properties served by Fort Collins Utilities get a doubled rebate on any qualifying measure. Efficiency Works — Fort Collins Utilities rebates →

☀️ Solar

Confirm status

Fort Collins Utilities runs its own net-metering and interconnection rules rather than Xcel's Solar Rewards program. Ask about a current solar rebate or net-metering credit directly — the federal 30% solar tax credit (Section 25D) expired for installations after December 31, 2025, under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, so no federal credit applies to new 2026 installs. How to claim it

  1. Confirm the current Fort Collins Utilities rebate amount and that funding is still open
  2. Get quotes from local licensed contractors
  3. Work is installed by a qualified contractor
  4. Submit the utility rebate application, typically within 60–90 days of installation
  5. If a contractor is registered with the Colorado Energy Office, they claim the $1,000 state heat pump tax credit on your behalf at point of sale

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

If you rent

Utility rebates go to whoever owns the equipment — usually your landlord. Ask your landlord about a free home energy audit through Fort Collins Utilities, which can come with a no-cost smart thermostat. Point them to this page if they want to pursue the bigger upgrades.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control — a heat pump water heater, an in-suite mini-split — can qualify for the rebates above, usually with your association's sign-off for anything touching shared walls or exterior equipment. Get that approval in writing before your contractor applies.
