Illinois Solar Incentives in 2026

Illinois does not give a flat solar rebate. It pays for the credits your panels create through a program called Illinois Shines, and the payment depends on your area and your system. The federal home solar credit ended after 2025, so this state payment is now the main help. Here is how the price works, what it can mean for your home, and what to ask a solar company before you sign. We read the 2026-27 prices on October 6, 2026.

One credit is one megawatt-hour of solar power, which is 1,000 kilowatt-hours. A system that makes 10,000 kilowatt-hours a year makes about 10 credits a year, and the contract counts the credits over 15 or 20 years.

Reading the price

The price per credit depends on your location group and on your system size. Group A covers Ameren Illinois, MidAmerican and some co-ops and city utilities in the MISO grid region. Group B covers ComEd and some co-ops and city utilities in the PJM grid region. For 2026-27 a system of 10 kW or less gets $70.37 in Group A and $80.77 in Group B. Naperville runs its own electric utility, so ask an approved vendor which group applies to you.

Illinois Shines pays your vendor, and your vendor must pass the value to you as the contract says. Ask whether you will be paid in a lump sum, in parts, or as a lower price.

How and when the money arrives

For contracts approved on or after June 1, 2026, half of the payment is made when the system turns on and the rest is spread over six years. Earlier contracts were paid all at once. The state requires a disclosure form from your vendor that explains how any promised payment will pass to you. Read it before you sign.

The loss of the federal credit

The federal credit covered 30 percent of a home solar system for years. It ended for spending after December 31, 2025. Illinois answered with the $20 adder for customer-owned systems. We cannot say if it fully replaces the lost credit. Ask your vendor to show the full cost, the expected yearly output and the expected payments side by side in a simple table.

What we did not confirm

We did not read the 2026 Illinois net metering rules, which decide what your utility pays for extra power you send to the grid, so we state no amount. We did not confirm a ComEd or Ameren Illinois battery rebate. If a vendor promises one, ask for the program name and check it.

Leases and loans

A lease or power purchase agreement is not eligible for the $20 adder. The page says the adder is for a project the customer owns. If a vendor offers a lease, ask how the credit payment is handled and who gets it.

How to get the Illinois solar incentive

  1. Check your electric bill to find your utility and group.
  2. Collect at least three quotes from Illinois Shines approved vendors.
  3. Ask each how many credits the contract has and how you will be paid.
  4. Let the vendor submit the application and keep every contract page.
  5. If your income is low, ask Illinois Solar for All first.

Common questions

Is there an Illinois solar rebate?

Illinois Shines pays for the credits your panels create, rather than a flat rebate. For a home system of 10 kW or less it is $70.37 or $80.77 per credit in 2026-27.

Is the federal solar credit available?

No. It ended for spending after December 31, 2025.

What is the $20 adder?

An extra $20 per credit if you own your system and do not take a federal credit, through the 2026-27 year.

Who gets paid?

Illinois Shines pays your vendor, and your vendor must pass the value to you as the contract says.

Is there help for lower-income households?

Yes. Illinois Solar for All offers solar with required bill savings and little or no cost up front.
