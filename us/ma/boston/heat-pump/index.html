Heat Pump Rebates in Boston

No city gets a bigger or smaller cut of Mass Save than another — Boston's number is the same as the rest of the state. What differs here is the housing stock and the panel-capacity questions that come with it, which the sections below get into.

Short answer: Boston homes (Eversource) get Mass Save's 2026 heat pump rebate. Whole-home air-source systems get $2,650 per ton, up to $8,500. Partial-home systems get $1,125 per ton, up to $8,500, plus up to $1,000 in bonuses. Income-qualified households can get up to $16,000, or no cost through Turnkey. Mass Save's own example: a $22,000 whole-home install costs $13,500 after the standard rebate. The federal 25C tax credit ended December 31, 2025. The 0% HEAT Loan (up to $25,000, up to 7 years) can cover the rest.

How much you get

Up to $8,500 — $2,650 per ton, capped at $8,500 for whole-home systems (partial-home systems can add up to $1,000 in weatherization and sizing bonuses). Income-qualified households can get up to $16,000 for air-source or $25,000 for ground-source. Only R-32 or R-454B refrigerant systems qualify — R-410A heat pumps stopped being eligible as of January 1, 2026.

What you'll actually save each month

A rebate lowers the price, but your monthly bill decides whether a heat pump pays off. Your electric rate has two parts on your Eversource bill: delivery and Basic Service (supply). Both change during the year, so use the numbers on your own latest bill rather than an average. Massachusetts utilities also offer a seasonal heat pump rate that lowers winter prices for homes heated by heat pumps; ask your utility once your system is in.

Efficiency is what makes that rate work in your favor: a modern cold-climate heat pump has a coefficient of performance around 2.5 to 3.5, so it returns 2.5 to 3.5 units of heat for every unit of electricity it consumes, beating plain electric resistance heat even at Eversource's prices. A side-by-side with gas furnaces isn't in here on purpose — Eversource has an open 2026 gas rate case right now, and publishing a payback number against a rate that's about to move isn't something we're willing to do.

How to claim it

Here's how the Mass Save heat pump rebate works in 2026. For a whole-home rebate, your home must be weatherized first. A no-cost home energy assessment is the usual way to show that, and it also unlocks 75–100% off insulation.

  1. Book a no-cost Mass Save Home Energy Assessment (masssave.com or 866-527-7283) and do the recommended insulation and air sealing.
  2. Get 2–3 quotes from contractors in the Mass Save Heat Pump Installer Network. Only they qualify.
  3. Install an ENERGY STAR Cold Climate unit from Mass Save's qualified list during 2026. High-GWP refrigerants like R-410A were dropped from the list on January 1, 2026.
  4. Apply online or by mail with your invoice by February 28, 2027. For a whole-home rebate, include the signed Whole-Home Heat Pump Verification Form.

Common questions

How much is the Mass Save heat pump rebate in Boston?

Boston's rebate is the standard statewide $2,650 per ton, up to $8,500 for most households. Income-qualified households can get up to $16,000 for an air-source system or $25,000 for ground-source. Only equipment running R-32 or R-454B refrigerant qualifies as of January 1, 2026; older R-410A systems are no longer eligible.

How cold does it get in Boston, and will a heat pump keep up?

Boston's 10-year average January low (2015-2024) is -5.2°C, and the coldest single night measured in that stretch was -26.5°C. A cold-climate heat pump — the only kind that qualifies for the rebate above — is rated to keep delivering heat efficiently down to around -25°C or colder, so it covers essentially everything Boston has thrown at it in the last decade.

Boston is Eversource's biggest single city — panel capacity is the thing to plan for

Boston is the largest city in Eversource's Massachusetts territory, and a lot of its housing stock is the classic triple-decker: three units, one electrical service, often a panel that was sized decades before anyone was running central heat off it. If your installer flags a panel upgrade, that request goes to Eversource, and it's worth asking your electrician early rather than after your Mass Save assessment is already booked, since a panel upgrade can be the actual pacing item on a Boston triple-decker install, not the heat pump itself. The rebate dollar amounts above don't change based on this because they're set by the state program, but a stalled service upgrade is the most common reason a Boston install runs long.

What the last 10 winters actually looked like near Boston

The climate data above comes from the long-running weather station near Logan Airport, which is about as close to sea level and to Boston Harbor as any station in the state. The harbor's moderating effect is part of why Boston's average January low sits close to, rather than well below, its inland Massachusetts neighbors. A modern cold-climate heat pump is built for colder than that.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Massachusetts in 2026?

Mass Save pays $2,650 per ton up to $8,500 for whole-home air-source systems, $1,125 per ton up to $8,500 for partial-home, and up to $16,000 or no cost for income-qualified households.

Is the Boston heat pump rebate different from the rest of Massachusetts?

No. Boston homes on Eversource get the same statewide Mass Save amounts.

Do I still get a federal tax credit for a heat pump?

No. The federal 25C credit ended for work done after December 31, 2025.

What's the best HVAC rebate in Massachusetts for homeowners?

For most homes heated with oil, propane or electric baseboard, it's the Mass Save whole-home heat pump rebate, paired with 75-100% off insulation and the 0% HEAT Loan.
