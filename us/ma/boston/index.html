What's different about rebates in Boston

Boston's electricity comes from Eversource, so every Boston address can use Mass Save. That includes Dorchester, Roxbury, Jamaica Plain, East Boston, Hyde Park and West Roxbury. Boston also has something most cities don't: its own solar program. The Boston Home Center's Solar Loan Pilot offers a 0% loan of up to $25,000 for rooftop solar. It is fully forgiven if you live in the home for 20 years. It's for owner-occupants of 1–4 family homes with income at or below 135% of area median income and less than $100,000 in assets. It's a pilot for about 20 households, so apply early.

Many Boston homes are two- and three-family buildings. If you own one, ask Mass Save about a whole-building assessment. When every owner in a 1–4 unit building joins, the insulation discount can go above the usual 75%. Renters in Boston can book their own no-cost assessment too. Braintree, Norwood and Belmont nearby run their own town electric utilities, so friends there won't see the same Mass Save offers.

How to claim it

  1. Schedule your free Mass Save home energy assessment
  2. Get quotes from Mass Save-participating contractors in Boston
  3. Work is installed
  4. Contractor or you file the rebate paperwork with Mass Save
  5. Rebate is paid after Mass Save approves it. Some offers (like heat pump water heaters) are instant; mailed rebates can take months, so keep copies of everything

Boston-specific programs

Boston runs its own Solar Loan Pilot Program — a 0% interest, forgivable loan of up to $25,000 specifically for solar installations, stacking on top of Mass Save and SMART 3.0. Boston also runs Solarize Eastie, a targeted program with GreenRoots for low-income, immigrant, and renter households in East Boston.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most of the numbers above assume you own a single-family or multi-family home and control the whole building. That's not every Boston homeowner. Here's what changes:

If you rent

You can't claim the heat pump, insulation, or solar rebates — those go to whoever owns the property. The free Mass Save home energy assessment is still worth requesting through your landlord, since it often comes with a free smart thermostat and other no-cost measures. If you want the bigger upgrades done, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, in-suite windows, an in-unit heat pump — can qualify for the Mass Save rebates above, usually with your condo association's approval for anything touching shared walls or exterior systems. Get that approval in writing before your contractor applies.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation, and solar for the whole building go through the condo association as a group decision, and Mass Save's multi-family program handles the paperwork rather than the single-unit process on this page. The HEAT Loan and Mass Save's free assessment are both still available — your association or property manager coordinates the application, not individual owners.
