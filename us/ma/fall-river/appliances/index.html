Mass Save's fridge and freezer program is structured as a recycling rebate with free pickup, not a purchase rebate — you get paid $75 to have your old, working unit hauled away, whether or not you're buying a replacement. Induction range and cooktop rebates run as retailer instant discounts rather than a fixed mail-in amount, so check the price at checkout.

Buying the right appliance for a Fall River household

Fall River's mill-worker housing wasn't designed around a modern dryer vent, so a heat pump model that doesn't need one solves a layout problem a lot of triple-decker owners here already deal with. Fall River's granite-mill triple-deckers, many built directly for textile-mill workers, tend to have compact utility spaces with limited or no exterior venting for a dryer.

The $200 heat pump dryer rebate is worth planning around if your current dryer is on its way out. These units condense moisture internally instead of exhausting hot air outdoors, so many don't need an exterior vent at all, and they run on roughly half the electricity of a standard resistance dryer because they recycle heat instead of wasting it. The tradeoff is real: cycles typically run 20 to 40 minutes longer than a conventional dryer. Browse heat pump dryers at Home Depot →

Induction cooking is worth considering too if you're replacing a range. Induction heats the pan directly through a magnetic field rather than heating an element first, so water boils faster and the cooktop surface stays cooler, a real safety advantage in a household with kids. The catch is cookware: induction needs magnetic pots and pans, generally cast iron or certain stainless steel, so an all-aluminum or all-copper set won't work without swapping a few pieces. Browse induction cooktops at Home Depot → or induction ranges →

Not every appliance carrying an ENERGY STAR label clears Mass Save's bar for the higher rebate tiers — ENERGY STAR Most Efficient models qualify for more than standard ENERGY STAR units on some categories, so check the current qualified product list before buying rather than assuming the label alone is enough. Worth saying plainly: this rebate is for replacing an electric appliance already in your home, not for adding one where there wasn't one before. If your current fridge or washer still works fine, the smaller rebate amounts here rarely justify swapping it out ahead of schedule.

How to claim it

  1. Purchase your appliance from any retailer — Fall River has a Home Depot on William S Canning Blvd, and a Best Buy a short drive away in Dartmouth, both stocking qualified models
  2. Recycle the old appliance through Fall River's bulk-item pickup program instead of setting it out with regular trash
  3. Apply online at masssave.com with your dated receipt, or claim the instant discount at checkout for induction cooktops and ranges where offered

Common questions

Are appliance rebates available in Fall River?

Yes. Mass Save funds rebates on ENERGY STAR clothes washers, clothes dryers, heat pump clothes dryers, and refrigerator/freezer recycling for Fall River residential National Grid customers.

What appliance rebate is worth prioritizing in Fall River?

The $200 heat pump clothes dryer rebate is the largest single appliance rebate Mass Save offers, and it also cuts drying costs roughly in half compared to a standard electric dryer.

Is there a Fall River-specific appliance program on top of Mass Save?

No separate city program exists — the appliance rebate available to Fall River homeowners runs entirely through Mass Save and National Grid. Citizens for Citizens and other local community-action agencies run separate weatherization help for income-eligible households, worth checking in parallel.

Your utility: National Grid

National Grid is Fall River's electricity distributor and funds the Mass Save appliance rebates through ratepayer efficiency charges. National Grid serves Fall River, and is who to call if your triple-decker's older electrical service needs a capacity check before adding an induction range circuit.
