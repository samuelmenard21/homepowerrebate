How much is the Massachusetts heat pump rebate in 2026?

Short answer: Through Mass Save, a whole-home air-source heat pump gets $2,650 per ton, up to $8,500. A partial-home system (you keep your old boiler or furnace as backup) gets $1,125 per ton, up to $8,500, plus up to $1,000 in bonuses. Income-qualified households can get up to $16,000, or no cost. Ground-source systems get $13,500, or up to $25,000 with enhanced rebates. The federal tax credit ended December 31, 2025.
OptionMass Save example (2026)
Whole-home air-source, standard$22,000 install − $8,500 = $13,500
Whole-home air-source, income-qualified$22,000 install − $16,000 = $6,000 (or no cost via Turnkey)
Whole-home ground-source, standard$44,000 install − $13,500 = $30,500

To qualify, you need a Mass Save Heat Pump Installer Network contractor and an ENERGY STAR Cold Climate unit from the qualified list, installed in 2026. Whole-home rebates also need a weatherized home and a signed verification form. The 0% HEAT Loan covers up to $25,000 over up to 7 years. Towns with a municipal light plant (like Concord, Wellesley, Braintree or Taunton) aren't part of Mass Save's electric programs.

Should I get a heat pump or solar first?

If you heat with oil, propane or electric baseboard, a heat pump usually cuts your bills more. It also has the bigger upfront rebate. Solar in Massachusetts has no upfront Mass Save rebate. Instead, SMART 3.0 pays small home systems $0.03 per kWh they make ($0.06 for low-income), and the state tax credit covers 15% of cost, up to $1,000. Your payback depends on your fuel, your roof and your bills, so we don't quote a single number. Read the full comparison →

Heat pump

Mass Save heat pump rebate

Up to $8,500

$2,650 per ton, capped at $8,500 for most households (down from $10,000 in 2024; regulators also trimmed the 2025–2027 Mass Save budget by $500 million). Income-qualified households can reach $16,000 for air-source or $25,000 for ground-source. Only R-32 or R-454B refrigerant systems qualify as of January 1, 2026 — older R-410A units are no longer eligible. Same statewide, not just Boston.

Quick Questions

Read the questions and answers
Will a heat pump heat my Massachusetts home in winter?
Yes. Massachusetts winters are milder than Canada's. Boston averages -2°C to +5°C. Heat pumps work great. When cold snaps hit -15°C to -20°C, backup heat kicks in. Read more about heat pump performance in Massachusetts winter.
I live in one of the 41 excluded towns. What do I do?
You can't use Mass Save, but alternatives exist. Check your town and see your options.
What if my Mass Save rebate gets rejected?
Common reasons: Your contractor isn't on the Mass Save list, your heat pump uses the now-banned R-410A refrigerant, or your income is too high. See all rejection reasons.
How long until I get my Mass Save rebate?
About 5–7 months total. Application approval takes 2–4 weeks. Work gets done in 1–5 days. Rebate payment arrives in 4–8 weeks after completion.
Can I get multiple installer quotes?

Common questions

How much is the Massachusetts heat pump rebate?

Mass Save pays $2,650 per ton up to $8,500 for whole-home air-source heat pumps, and $1,125 per ton up to $8,500 for partial-home systems. Income-qualified households can get up to $16,000 or no cost.

Is there a federal heat pump tax credit in 2026?

No. The federal 25C and 25D credits ended for work done after December 31, 2025.

Who can't use Mass Save?

Homes in towns served by a municipal light plant, like Concord, Wellesley, Braintree or Taunton. They can use their light plant's programs and the state's 0% MLP Z-Loan.

Is there a 0% loan for heat pumps in Massachusetts?

Yes. The Mass Save HEAT Loan offers up to $25,000 at 0% over up to 7 years.
