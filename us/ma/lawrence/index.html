Yes, Lawrence is eligible for Mass Save

Lawrence isn't a Municipal Light Plant town, so it isn't shut out of Mass Save the way some Massachusetts towns are. Lawrence's electric utility is National Grid, an investor-owned utility that helps fund the statewide Mass Save program. Homeowners here qualify the same way homeowners in Boston or Worcester do.

Lawrence has a split utility setup worth knowing about: National Grid delivers electricity and Eversource delivers gas (Eversource took over the former Columbia Gas of Massachusetts territory in 2020 and runs a local office on Marston Street). Because Mass Save is jointly administered by all the state's investor-owned utilities, the rebate amounts below apply whether your project touches the electric side (heat pumps, EV chargers, smart thermostats) or the gas side (insulation, some water heater work) — you don't need to figure out which utility "owns" a given rebate before applying.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most of the numbers above assume you own a single-family or multi-family home and control the whole building. Lawrence has a large share of renters and multi-unit triple-deckers, so this section matters for a lot of homeowners here. Here's what changes:

If you rent

You can't claim the heat pump, insulation, or solar rebates — those go to whoever owns the property. The free Mass Save home energy assessment is still worth requesting through your landlord, since it often comes with a free smart thermostat and other no-cost measures. If you want the bigger upgrades done, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a unit in a multi-family building

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, in-unit windows, an in-unit heat pump — can qualify for the Mass Save rebates above, usually with approval from your co-owners for anything touching shared walls, the roof, or exterior systems. Get that approval in writing before your contractor applies.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation, and solar for the whole building go through Mass Save's multifamily program rather than the single-unit process on this page, and that program handles the paperwork differently. The HEAT Loan and Mass Save's free assessment are both still available — your property manager or the building's owner coordinates the application, not individual unit owners.

Heat pump

Mass Save heat pump rebate

Up to $8,500

$2,650 per ton, capped at $8,500 for most households (down from $10,000 in 2024; regulators also trimmed the 2025–2027 Mass Save budget by $500 million). Income-qualified households can reach $16,000 for air-source or $25,000 for ground-source. Only R-32 or R-454B refrigerant systems qualify as of January 1, 2026 — older R-410A units are no longer eligible. Same statewide, not just Lawrence.

Source: Mass Save (statewide program)
Solar

SMART 3.0 solar incentive

Per-kWh incentive

Not a flat rebate — SMART 3.0 locks in a per-kWh production payment for a fixed term once you're enrolled, on top of your own electricity savings. Same program statewide.

Source: Mass Save (statewide program)
Battery

Battery storage rebate

ConnectedSolutions pay-for-performance

There's no upfront Mass Save battery rebate. You earn ConnectedSolutions payments (MassCEC example: about $1,375/yr for a 5 kW battery), plus the SMART storage adder if paired with solar.

Source: Mass Save (statewide program)
Insulation

Insulation & air sealing

75–100% off

Mass Save covers 75% of the cost for every Massachusetts homeowner, including Lawrence. If your household is under 60% of area median income, it's covered at 100% — free.

Source: Mass Save (statewide program)
Water heater

Heat-pump water heater rebate

$750

A flat $750 for a heat pump water heater. The unit needs to be ENERGY STAR certified to qualify. Same flat amount statewide.

Source: Mass Save (statewide program)

Mass Save pays $75 per window when you replace single-pane windows with ENERGY STAR Most Efficient certified windows.
