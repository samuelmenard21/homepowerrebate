Home Energy Rebates in Lowell

Complete guide to Mass Save rebates for Lowell homeowners in 2026 — heat pumps, insulation, solar, and battery storage, with exactly how each one works.

A former mill city in the Merrimack Valley, served by National Grid.

What Lowell homeowners can claim

Mass Save funds nearly all residential energy rebates across Massachusetts, including Lowell. All amounts below are in US dollars.

🔥 Heat Pump

$8,500–$9,500

$2,650/ton, capped at $8,500, plus sizing and weatherization bonuses. Income-qualified: up to $16,000 (air-source) or $25,000 (ground-source). Only R-32 or R-454B refrigerant systems qualify — R-410A heat pumps are no longer eligible as of January 1, 2026.

🟫 Insulation & Air Sealing

75–100% off

75% covered for all Lowell homeowners. Income-eligible households (under 60% AMI) pay $0.

☀️ Solar (SMART 3.0)

Per-kWh incentive

Locked-in production payment for 10–20 years once enrolled.

🔌 Battery Storage

$100/kWh + ongoing

Capacity rebate plus SMART storage adder and annual ConnectedSolutions payments.

💧 Water Heater

$750

Heat pump water heater rebate. ENERGY STAR certified equipment required.

🟩 Windows & Doors

$75/window

Replace single-pane windows with ENERGY STAR Most Efficient certified windows. Windows & insulation →

🚘 EV Charger

$250

Toward purchase and installation of a qualifying Level 2 home EV charger. EV charger rebates →

🌡️ Smart Thermostat

$100

Often installed free during your home energy assessment.

💰 HEAT Loan

0% interest

Up to $25,000, 0% interest, 0% fees, 7-year term.

📋 Home Energy Assessment

Free

Required first step to confirm what you qualify for.

Renting or living in a condo? Here's what actually applies to you

Most of the numbers above assume you own a single-family or multi-family home and control the whole building. That's not every Lowell homeowner — a former mill city with a lot of multi-family housing. Here's what changes:

If you rent

You can't claim the heat pump, insulation, or solar rebates — those go to whoever owns the property. The free Mass Save home energy assessment is still worth requesting through your landlord, since it often comes with a free smart thermostat and other no-cost measures. If you want the bigger upgrades done, the ask has to go to your landlord — point them to this page.

If you own a condo unit

In-unit upgrades you control yourself — a heat pump water heater, in-suite windows, an in-unit heat pump — can qualify for the Mass Save rebates above, usually with your condo association's approval for anything touching shared walls or exterior systems. Get that approval in writing before your contractor applies.

If it's a whole-building retrofit

Heat pumps, insulation, and solar for the whole building go through the condo association (or, for multi-family rentals, the property owner) as a group decision, and Mass Save's multi-family program handles the paperwork rather than the single-unit process on this page. The HEAT Loan and Mass Save's free assessment are both still available — the owner or property manager coordinates the application, not individual tenants.
