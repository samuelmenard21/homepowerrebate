What's different about rebates in Somerville

Somerville is served by Eversource, so its homeowners, landlords and renters can all use Mass Save. The city is packed with two- and three-family houses. That makes the landlord side of Mass Save worth a look: owners of 1–4 unit buildings have their own assessment path. If every owner in the building takes part, insulation can be covered at more than the usual 75%. Tenants can also book a no-cost assessment for their own unit.

Tight lots and shared walls matter when you plan a heat pump. Outdoor units need a spot that meets setback rules, and older buildings often share one electrical service. Ask your installer early about panel capacity, since Eversource handles any service upgrade. Nearby, Belmont runs a town-owned light plant, so Mass Save's electric offers don't reach it. If you heat with gas, Mass Save lists heat pump rebates for gas-to-heat-pump switches through your gas account.

How to claim it

  1. Schedule your free Mass Save home energy assessment
  2. Get quotes from Mass Save-participating contractors in Somerville
  3. Work is installed
  4. Contractor or you file the rebate paperwork with Mass Save
  5. Rebate is paid after Mass Save approves it. Some offers (like heat pump water heaters) are instant; mailed rebates can take months, so keep copies of everything

Renting or living in a condo in Somerville? Here's what applies to you

Most of the rebate figures above assume you own a single-family home and control the whole building. That's not most of Somerville, one of the densest cities in Massachusetts. Here's what changes if you rent or own a condo unit:

  • If you rent: The structural rebates (heat pump, insulation, windows, solar) go to whoever owns the property, not you. A free smart thermostat installed during a home energy assessment is often still available to renters — ask when you book. For the bigger upgrades, point your landlord to this page.
  • If you own a condo unit: In-unit upgrades you control yourself, like a heat pump water heater or an in-suite heat pump, may still qualify for the rebates above, usually with your building's approval for anything touching shared systems or exterior walls.
  • If it's a whole-building retrofit: Heat pumps, insulation, and solar for an entire condo or multi-family building go through a separate Mass Save multi-unit program rather than the single-family numbers above. Your condo board or property manager applies, not individual owners.
