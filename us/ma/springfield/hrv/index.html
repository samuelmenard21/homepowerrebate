The honest answer: no standalone HRV program in Springfield

Springfield homeowners searching for a standalone "HRV rebate" on Mass Save's site will come up short. The real incentive lives in two narrower places.

The first is an instant discount: Mass Save pays qualifying equipment vendors and contractors up to $500 off a qualifying HRV or ERV unit at the point of sale, no separate rebate application needed on your end. The second is a weatherization-linked rebate, cited by contractor guides tracking Mass Save's 2026 program at $400 to $800, available when the ventilation unit is installed as part of a broader insulation or air-sealing project through Mass Save. As an Eversource customer, your Mass Save home energy assessment and any resulting HRV/ERV paperwork route through Eversource's program administration.

$0

What the equipment has to meet

  • Energy recovery ventilators (ERV) need an enthalpy recovery ratio of at least 75% at both heating and cooling design conditions, tested to AHRI Standard 1060
  • Heat recovery ventilators (HRV) need a sensible energy recovery ratio of at least 75% at heating design conditions, tested to the same AHRI standard
  • Installation has to go through a Mass Save-authorized contractor who can apply the instant discount and, if applicable, file the weatherization-linked rebate

Why Springfield homes end up needing HRV anyway

A tightly sealed Springfield home holds heat better, but it also holds humidity, cooking odors, and stale air that used to escape through gaps in the building envelope. HRV and ERV systems solve that by continuously swapping indoor and outdoor air while recovering most of the heat, which is why the rebate shows up attached to insulation work rather than standing alone.

How to claim it in Springfield

  1. Book a free Mass Save home energy assessment — it's the same required first step as every other Mass Save rebate, including this one
  2. Ask your assessor or contractor whether an HRV or ERV makes sense for your project, especially if you're already sealing the attic, walls, or basement
  3. Confirm the equipment meets the AHRI 1060 recovery-ratio thresholds before you buy — your contractor should know which units qualify
  4. Have your Mass Save-authorized contractor apply the instant discount at purchase, and file the weatherization-linked rebate if the ventilation work is bundled with an insulation or air-sealing project

Common questions

Is there a standalone Mass Save HRV rebate in Springfield?

No. Mass Save has never built HRV/ERV into a dedicated standalone rebate program the way it has for heat pumps. Springfield homeowners can still get a real instant discount of up to $500 on qualifying equipment at the point of purchase, or $400 to $800 when a heat or energy recovery ventilator is installed as part of a broader Mass Save insulation or weatherization project.

What equipment qualifies for the Springfield HRV/ERV discount?

The unit has to meet Mass Save's efficiency threshold under AHRI Standard 1060: an energy recovery ventilator needs an enthalpy recovery ratio of at least 75%, and a heat recovery ventilator needs a sensible energy recovery ratio of at least 75%, both at design heating and cooling conditions. Installation has to go through a Mass Save-authorized contractor.

The dollar amounts here are small next to Springfield's heat pump program, and that's by design — Mass Save is subsidizing the equipment that makes a sealed home livable, not funding ventilation as its own project.
