Heat Pump Rebates in Worcester

Worcester has the coldest Januarys of any city we cover in Massachusetts. A cold-climate heat pump still works here, and Mass Save pays up to $8,500 for one. Sizing is what decides whether you get that full amount.

What heat pump rebates pay in Worcester

We re-read MassCEC's heat pump page on October 3, 2026. Mass Save's own site blocks our checks, so its rebate table comes from a July 16, 2026 snapshot. Worcester is on National Grid's own list of the towns it serves, a Mass Save sponsor.

Open

Whole-home heat pump

Up to $8,500

$2,650 per ton. The heat pump must be your main heat, and the home needs enough insulation.

Open

Partial-home heat pump

$1,125 per ton

Up to $8,500, for heat pumps that heat part of the home alongside your old system. Integrated controls are required.

Open

Income-based Enhanced Rebates

Up to $16,000

Or no cost, for households that qualify by income. This is not the standard amount.

Open

Alternative Energy Certificates

About $250 to $1,000

A one-time state payment. MassCEC's example is a 2,000 square foot home with a whole-home system.

Open

Mass Save HEAT Loan

Up to $25,000 at 0%

Terms up to 7 years, to spread the rest of the cost.

Closed

Federal tax credit (25C)

Ended

Ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025.

Working it out

A 3-ton whole-home system: 3 x $2,650 = $7,950. A 4-ton system would be $10,600 by the math, but the cap is $8,500. The same 3 tons as a partial-home system: 3 x $1,125 = $3,375. MassCEC's cost example for a 2,000 square foot home is $22,000 before incentives and $12,500 after the $8,500 rebate and $1,000 in certificates.

Worcester's winters

Worcester's average January low is about -6.3°C (21°F), and the coldest reading in the last ten years was -27.2°C. Both are the coldest of the 13 Massachusetts cities we cover. Cold-climate heat pumps still heat at those temperatures, but they make less heat as it gets colder. Ask your installer for the unit's output at -15°C, and for a heat-loss calculation for your home.

If the heat pump can't carry the house on the coldest nights and your old system becomes the main heat, you get the partial-home rate. That is $1,125 per ton instead of $2,650.

How Worcester heats

The US Census Bureau's survey (2020 to 2024) says 51% of Worcester households heat with utility gas, 29% with electricity and 14% with fuel oil. Electric and oil homes, more than four in ten, are the ones that save the most.

What Worcester homes look like

Worcester has 85,876 homes, the most of any city on our list after Boston. About 43% of households own. Some 32% of homes are single-family detached and 34% are in two- to four-unit buildings. Three in four (75%) were built before 1980.

The rules that trip people up

Mass Save's heat pump rebates for 2026 have a few conditions that decide whether you get the big number or a small one. The system must be installed during 2026 by a contractor in Mass Save's Heat Pump Installer Network, and the equipment must be ENERGY STAR Cold Climate rated. Since January 1, 2026, heat pumps that use refrigerant with a global warming rating over 700, including the common R-410A, are off the qualified list. Ask your installer which refrigerant the model uses before you sign.

The whole-home rebate also asks that the home is insulated well enough, and you sign a form saying the heat pump will be your main heat. If you keep your old boiler or furnace running as the main heat, you are in the partial-home tier instead. Mass Save lists a $500 weatherization bonus and a $500 sizing bonus for partial-home systems. You apply after the install, and the deadline for 2026 installs is February 28, 2027.

How to get a heat pump rebate in Worcester

  1. Book a free Mass Save Home Energy Assessment. It tells you whether your home needs insulation first, which the whole-home rebate asks for.
  2. If your income might qualify, ask about Enhanced Rebates before you get quotes. They can pay up to $16,000 or the full cost.
  3. Get at least two quotes from Heat Pump Installer Network contractors. Ask about tons, refrigerant and whether the system is sized to be your main heat.
  4. Have the system installed in 2026, then apply for the rebate by February 28, 2027.
  5. Claim your Alternative Energy Certificates through the state program, and use the 0% HEAT Loan for what is left if you need it.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Worcester?

Mass Save pays $2,650 per ton for a whole-home system, up to $8,500, or $1,125 per ton for a partial-home system. Households that qualify by income can get up to $16,000 or a no-cost system.

Do heat pumps work in Worcester winters?

Yes, if you choose a cold-climate model and size it for your home. Mass Save only pays for ENERGY STAR Cold Climate heat pumps.

Who is the electric utility in Worcester?

National Grid. Worcester is on National Grid's list of Massachusetts towns.

Is there still a federal tax credit for heat pumps?

No. The federal 25C credit ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025. The Mass Save rebate, the state's Alternative Energy Certificates and the 0% HEAT Loan are still open.

Will Mass Save pay for an R-410A heat pump?

No. Since January 1, 2026, heat pumps using refrigerant with a global warming rating over 700, including R-410A, were removed from Mass Save's qualified list.

How much does a whole-home heat pump cost after rebates?

MassCEC's example is a 2,000 square foot home: about $22,000 before incentives and about $12,500 after the $8,500 Mass Save rebate and $1,000 in certificates. Your quote will differ.
