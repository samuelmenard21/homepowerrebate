The honest answer: no standalone HRV program in Worcester

There's no flagship HRV or ERV rebate on Mass Save's site for Worcester homeowners the way there is for heat pumps. What exists is smaller and tied to other work.

The first is an instant discount: Mass Save pays qualifying equipment vendors and contractors up to $500 off a qualifying HRV or ERV unit at the point of sale, no separate rebate application needed on your end. The second is a weatherization-linked rebate, cited by contractor guides tracking Mass Save's 2026 program at $400 to $800, available when the ventilation unit is installed as part of a broader insulation or air-sealing project through Mass Save. As a National Grid customer, your Mass Save home energy assessment and any resulting HRV/ERV paperwork route through National Grid's program administration — National Grid customers can also reach Mass Save directly at 1-866-527-7283.

Up to $500

Instant discount at point of sale on qualifying HRV/ERV equipment, applied by the vendor or contractor.

$400–$800

Rebate when HRV/ERV is installed as part of a Mass Save insulation or weatherization project.

$0

Ventilation installed with no other Mass Save-eligible project underway — the instant discount still applies to qualifying equipment, but nothing else does.

What the equipment has to meet

  • Energy recovery ventilators (ERV) need an enthalpy recovery ratio of at least 75% at both heating and cooling design conditions, tested to AHRI Standard 1060
  • Heat recovery ventilators (HRV) need a sensible energy recovery ratio of at least 75% at heating design conditions, tested to the same AHRI standard
  • Installation has to go through a Mass Save-authorized contractor who can apply the instant discount and, if applicable, file the weatherization-linked rebate

Why Worcester homes end up needing HRV anyway

Once a Worcester home gets air sealed as part of a Mass Save weatherization job, it stops "breathing" the way older, leakier houses do. That's good for the heating bill and bad for indoor air quality unless something replaces the airflow — which is the job an HRV or ERV does, recovering heat from the outgoing air in the process.

How to claim it in Worcester

  1. Book a free Mass Save home energy assessment — it's the same required first step as every other Mass Save rebate, including this one
  2. Ask your assessor or contractor whether an HRV or ERV makes sense for your project, especially if you're already sealing the attic, walls, or basement
  3. Confirm the equipment meets the AHRI 1060 recovery-ratio thresholds before you buy — your contractor should know which units qualify
  4. Have your Mass Save-authorized contractor apply the instant discount at purchase, and file the weatherization-linked rebate if the ventilation work is bundled with an insulation or air-sealing project

Common questions

Is there a standalone Mass Save HRV rebate in Worcester?

No. Mass Save has never built HRV/ERV into a dedicated standalone rebate program the way it has for heat pumps. Worcester homeowners can still get a real instant discount of up to $500 on qualifying equipment at the point of purchase, or $400 to $800 when a heat or energy recovery ventilator is installed as part of a broader Mass Save insulation or weatherization project.

What equipment qualifies for the Worcester HRV/ERV discount?

The unit has to meet Mass Save's efficiency threshold under AHRI Standard 1060: an energy recovery ventilator needs an enthalpy recovery ratio of at least 75%, and a heat recovery ventilator needs a sensible energy recovery ratio of at least 75%, both at design heating and cooling conditions. Installation has to go through a Mass Save-authorized contractor.

Treat this as a secondary rebate. Worcester homeowners doing a heat pump or insulation project should ask their contractor about it, but it's not worth chasing on its own.
