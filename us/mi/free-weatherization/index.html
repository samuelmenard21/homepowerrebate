Free Home Weatherization in Michigan

If your household income is lower, Michigan has real help that costs nothing. The state's weatherization program, DTE's Energy Efficiency Assistance and the MiHER rebates can cover insulation, air sealing and sometimes new equipment. Rules and waiting times vary, and MiHER is paused for new income-qualified applications in five districts. We read each official page on October 6, 2026.

Where lower-income Michigan households can get help in 2026

Each card names the fact we keep on file. Programs are first come, first served, so apply early.

Open

State Weatherization Assistance Program

Free

Wall, attic and foundation insulation, air leakage reduction, smoke detectors and dryer venting. Income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, or on cash assistance or SSI. Run by local community action agencies.

Open

DTE Energy Efficiency Assistance

Free

Weatherization, heating system tune-up or replacement, a high efficiency water heater, a refrigerator and lighting, for households at or below 250% of the federal poverty level. About 88% of electric and 87% of gas slots were open.

Paused

State home energy rebates (MiHER)

Up to $14,000 or $20,000

Caps are $14,000 for electric equipment and $20,000 for whole-home efficiency, with up to the full project cost for eligible lower-income households. New income-qualified applications are paused in the Detroit, Warren, Grand Rapids, Lansing and Kalamazoo districts.

Open

Consumers free home energy analysis

Free

A free visit and report for homeowners in Consumers Energy's service area, with free small upgrades. No income test is listed.

Open

Michigan Saves loan

6.24% to 6.99% APR

For work you pay for yourself. Fixed-rate loans from eight credit unions, $1,000 to $100,000.

Closed

Federal 25C and 25D tax credits

Ended

The federal home energy credits ended for work finished after December 31, 2025.

Which program first

Start with the state weatherization program if your income is at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, or if you get cash assistance or SSI. It is free and run by local agencies. DTE's program reaches higher, at 250%, and the page lists $80,375 as the limit for a family of four. If you fall between 200% and 250% of poverty and use DTE, try DTE first.

About MiHER

The state's rebate program is funded by federal money and run by Michigan's environment department (EGLE). It covers heat pumps, water heaters, insulation, air sealing and electrical wiring, and pays an approved contractor directly, so you do not pay upfront for the covered amount. Its page says applications are open for low- to moderate-income households. But new income-qualified applications in the Detroit, Warren, Grand Rapids, Lansing and Kalamazoo districts are temporarily unavailable. The state's news release in August 2026 said the Detroit district was nearing its funding cap. The page names districts, not counties, so use the state's district map to find yours.

What to bring

Expect to show income. The state's rebate FAQ names pay statements, and it also accepts proof that you get certain benefits. Keep a recent utility bill ready. DTE's program also needs your DTE account to be active and in good standing.

Renters

The state weatherization program is open to renters as well as owners. A landlord's approval is usually needed for work on the building. Ask the local agency what it needs.

How to ask for free help in Michigan

  1. Check your household income against 200% and 250% of the federal poverty level.
  2. Call DTE at (833) 627-2300 if DTE is your utility, or contact your county's weatherization agency through the state.
  3. Gather income proof and a recent utility bill.
  4. Ask if the agency can also use MiHER money once it reopens.
  5. Keep a copy of every form you sign.

Common questions

Who qualifies for Michigan's free weatherization?

Households with income at or below 200% of the federal poverty level, or those on cash assistance or SSI.

How do I apply?

Contact the local weatherization operator, usually a community action agency, that serves your county.

Is MiHER open?

Applications are open to low- to moderate-income households, except new income-qualified ones in the Detroit, Warren, Grand Rapids, Lansing and Kalamazoo districts, which are paused.

What does DTE's program give?

No-cost upgrades such as weatherization, a heating system tune-up or replacement, a water heater and a refrigerator, for households at or below 250% of the federal poverty level.

Does the federal tax credit help?

No. It ended for work finished after December 31, 2025, and it was never a free program.
