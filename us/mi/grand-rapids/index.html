Consumers Energy: furnace, insulation, free visit

Home Energy Rebates in Grand Rapids

Most Grand Rapids homes heat with gas, and most buy their gas and power from Consumers Energy. Consumers pays $100 to $300 for a high efficiency furnace, up to $475 for insulation, and offers a free home energy visit. Its heat pump rebates are narrower than people expect. We read Consumers' own pages on October 6, 2026. Check your bill to confirm your company.

How Grand Rapids heats its homes

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020 to 2024) counts 84,894 housing units in Grand Rapids. Utility gas heats 82.5% of homes and electricity heats 15.3%. Bottled gas is 1.5% and fuel oil is under one tenth of one percent. That is the highest gas share of the four Michigan cities we cover.

Older homes and many renters

75.7% of Grand Rapids homes were built before 1980, and 37.5% before 1940, which is the highest share of pre-1940 homes of our four cities. Renters are 46.0% of households. About 16.1% of homes are in buildings of two to four units, more than in Detroit, Ann Arbor or Lansing. Consumers' rebates cover single-family homes and buildings with fewer than three units, so many small rentals qualify. The tenant should get the landlord's approval first.

Heat pump rebates are narrow

Consumers' 2026 chart limits air source and ground source heat pump rebates to customers replacing an existing heat pump. A gas home switching to a heat pump does not fit that wording. A mini-split rebate is for replacing a primary electric heat system. If you plan to switch from gas, expect no Consumers heat pump rebate, and look at the state program and Michigan Saves instead.

The state program is on pause here

The state's MiHER program can pay up to the full cost of work for lower-income households, with caps of $14,000 for electric equipment and $20,000 for whole-home efficiency. Its page says new income-qualified applications are temporarily unavailable in the Grand Rapids district, along with four others. Check your county on the state's map and check again before you rely on it.

What to do in Grand Rapids

  1. Book the free Consumers home energy analysis online.
  2. Ask for quotes from contractors on Consumers' participating list. Only they can file the rebate.
  3. Ask the contractor to file within 30 days of finishing. Consumers' chart says that is the deadline for HVAC paperwork.
  4. For insulation, check the R-value you reach, since the amount grows with it.
  5. If your income may be low, ask about free weatherization through your county's community action agency.

Common questions

Does Consumers Energy pay for a heat pump in Grand Rapids?

A little, and only for replacing an existing heat pump or electric heat. The amounts are $200 to $350. Gas homes switching to a heat pump are not covered on the chart we read.

How much does Consumers pay for a furnace?

$100 for 95% to 96.99% efficiency and $300 for 97% or better, for gas customers.

Is the free home energy analysis really free?

Consumers' page says it is free, with no income test listed.

Is MiHER open in Grand Rapids?

New income-qualified applications are paused in the Grand Rapids district, as of October 6, 2026.

Does the federal tax credit still apply?

No. It ended for work finished after December 31, 2025.
