Short answer: Minnesota rebates come from your gas and electric companies. Minnesota Power pays $1,000 for a cold-climate heat pump, Rochester Public Utilities up to $2,200, and CenterPoint Energy $1,100 to add a heat pump to a gas furnace. Xcel Energy pays up to $3,000 for insulation and its heat pump amounts could not be confirmed on a current page. Minneapolis adds up to $5,000. The state's own HOMES and HEAR rebates have not launched, and we could not confirm any federal tax credit for 2026.

How Minnesota's rebates are organized

Minnesota has no single statewide rebate office that pays for most upgrades. Your rebates come from the companies that send your bills. We read the pages of five programs: Xcel Energy, CenterPoint Energy, Minnesota Power, Rochester Public Utilities and the City of Minneapolis. A sixth, the state's own Save Energy Minnesota program, has not launched yet.

Start by looking at your two bills. Your electric company and your gas company are often different, and each may pay for different things. For example, a home might get a heat pump rebate from its electric company and a hybrid rebate from its gas company. The calculator on this site asks for both company names and then adds up only what is open. Our Minnesota rebate calculator does that for you.

Cold-climate heat pumps in Minnesota

A cold-climate heat pump is built to keep heating when the air is far below freezing. Minnesota Power defines it as a unit that stays efficient down to at least 5 degrees Fahrenheit, and Xcel Energy uses a similar test. Each company pays more for these units than for standard ones. Here is what we found, each from the program's own page or form:

  • Minnesota Power: $1,000 for a cold-climate heat pump and $400 for a standard one.
  • Rochester Public Utilities: up to $2,200 when the heat pump replaces working electric heat, and $100 to $200 when it replaces gas.
  • CenterPoint Energy: $1,100 to add a ducted heat pump to a home that keeps a high-efficiency gas furnace.
  • Xcel Energy: $1,500 to $2,000 on its older rebate sheet. Its live page shows no amount, so confirm the figure on your contractor's quote.
  • City of Minneapolis: up to $5,000, or $14,000 in a Green Zone, across all your projects.

Which route is best depends on your house. A hybrid system keeps your furnace as backup and is the lowest-risk choice for a very cold winter. A full system removes the furnace and can use Xcel's lower electric heating rate of $0.06537 per kilowatt hour from October through May, which applies to homes whose main heat is electric. A smaller heat pump costs less, so tighten the house first. Our Minneapolis insulation page shows how.

Statewide programs that apply no matter your utility

The Department of Commerce runs Save Energy Minnesota, which combines federal home rebates and state programs. As of the notice dated June 12, 2026 on its page, it has not launched and has no estimated launch date. We list it as Paused and do not count it in any total. It names a whole-home rebate called HOMES that may pay $2,000 to $8,000 or more per home, and an income-based rebate called HEAR. It also lists a state heat pump rebate and a panel upgrade program, but those pages were behind a bot check and we could not read them. Read our Save Energy Minnesota guide for what we know.

Federal tax credits are not listed. We could not confirm that any federal home energy credit is open for work done in 2026.

Old homes and cold winters

Minnesota's biggest cities have old housing. In the Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 survey, 78% of homes in Duluth, 78% in Saint Paul and 72% in Minneapolis were built before 1980. In Rochester it is 41%, since 35% went up in 2000 or later. Natural gas is the main heat in all four: about 75% of Rochester homes, 72% of Duluth, 70% of Minneapolis and 67% of Saint Paul. Electric heat runs from about 19% in Duluth to 29% in Saint Paul. Fuel oil is 4% in Duluth and almost nothing elsewhere.

Because most homes use gas, the money that most homeowners can reach today is for insulation, air sealing, hybrid heat pumps, high-efficiency gas equipment and, in Minneapolis, the city rebate. Homes with electric heat can reach the larger electric-company heat pump rebates.

Common questions

Read the questions and answers

Which company pays my rebate?

It depends on who sends your bills. Look at both your electric and your gas bill. Xcel Energy, CenterPoint Energy, Minnesota Power and Rochester Public Utilities each have their own programs.

Is there a Minnesota state rebate for heat pumps?

The state lists a Residential Heat Pump Rebate Program, but its page was behind a bot check and we could not read the amount. Its federal HOMES and HEAR rebates have not launched.

How much does a cold-climate heat pump rebate pay in Minnesota?

$1,000 from Minnesota Power, up to $2,200 from Rochester Public Utilities when replacing electric heat, $1,100 from CenterPoint for a hybrid system, and $1,500 to $2,000 on Xcel's older sheet. Minneapolis adds up to $5,000 or $14,000 for all projects.

Are federal tax credits still available?

We could not confirm any federal home energy credit is open for 2026 installs, so we do not list one. Ask a tax professional.

How often do you check?

We read each program page on the date shown on the page, and show the date. When a program closes we change its status.
