Saint Paul Home Energy Rebates

Saint Paul homeowners can use the same kinds of utility rebates as the rest of the Twin Cities area. We read Xcel Energy's and CenterPoint Energy's pages. We could not confirm a city rebate for Saint Paul homes, and the Minneapolis city rebate is not open to Saint Paul addresses.

What Saint Paul homes look like

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 counts 133,051 homes in Saint Paul. About 53% of households own and 47% rent. Nearly half, 49%, are detached houses, which is more than in Minneapolis. About 10% are in buildings of two to four units. 78% were built before 1980 and 42% before 1940, while only 11% were built in 2000 or later. About 67% of homes heat with natural gas from a utility line, 29% with electricity and 1% with bottled gas.

Compared with Minneapolis, Saint Paul has a higher share of owners and of single-family houses and a higher share of electric heat. That makes the utility programs the main tool, since owners can apply directly and many houses fit the insulation rules.

No city rebate that we could confirm

Minneapolis has a city program called Green Cost Share. It is for homes inside Minneapolis, so a Saint Paul home cannot use it. A search turned up city and county programs for Saint Paul homes, but we did not read their pages, so we do not list amounts or say whether they are open. If you live in Saint Paul, ask the city or your county housing office what help exists, and tell us if you find a program that should be on this page.

Homes with electric heat have another lever

About 29% of Saint Paul homes heat with electricity. If your main heat is electric, Xcel offers a reduced electric rate: $0.06537 per kilowatt hour from October through May and $0.13069 from June through September, and it applies to all the electricity in your home. A heat pump counts as electric heat. Xcel says that if you got a heat pump rebate and are not yet on the rate, you can sign up through your online account starting in September 2026. For a home with baseboard heaters, a heat pump plus this rate can change the bill a lot, and your contractor can estimate it.

Old houses first

Because 78% of Saint Paul homes were built before 1980, a good order is: air sealing and insulation first, then the heating system. Xcel's rebate sheet pays a $600 bonus if you add a qualifying heat pump within two years of qualifying insulation work. With a tighter house you may need a smaller heat pump. A smaller unit usually costs less, which can matter more than the rebate. The census table does not say which homes have been insulated, so book an energy visit to find out where yours stands. Xcel's visit costs $70 or $100, and income-qualified customers can get it free.

How to get started in Saint Paul

  1. Read your electric and gas bills and write down the two company names.
  2. Book a Home Energy Squad visit if your home has Xcel electric service and gas from Xcel or CenterPoint.
  3. Choose a contractor from the right utility's list and get a written quote with the rebates listed.
  4. Finish work by the deadline. Xcel wants heat pump work invoiced by December 31, 2026.
  5. File each rebate form with the invoice, or let your contractor do it.

Common questions

Can I use the Minneapolis Green Cost Share rebate in Saint Paul?

No. The program is for homes in Minneapolis.

How much does Xcel pay for a heat pump in Saint Paul?

Its older rebate sheet lists $1,500 to $2,000 for a cold-climate heat pump. The live page shows no amount, so ask your contractor for the number on the quote.

Is there a hybrid option if I have a gas furnace?

Yes. CenterPoint pays $1,100 for a ducted heat pump added to a home with a high-efficiency gas furnace, if you are a CenterPoint gas customer.

Are state rebates open?

No. The state says its federal rebate program has not launched and has no date.

What to do next

Compare with Minneapolis, which has an extra city rebate, or read the CenterPoint guide and the smart thermostat page for small, quick savings.
