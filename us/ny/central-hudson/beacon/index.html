Beacon Heat Pump & Solar Rebates 2026

Complete guide to Central Hudson rebates for Beacon homeowners

What rebates can you get in Beacon?

Beacon, a scenic Dutchess County riverfront city (population 15,000), sits in Central Hudson Gas & Electric's service territory in the Mid-Hudson Valley. Central Hudson offers some of the most generous rebates in the Northeast: $8,000 for full heat pump replacement, $1,250 for heat pump water heaters, and direct solar rebates up to $0.80 per watt for income-qualified households. Unlike utilities on Long Island, Central Hudson actively incentivizes residential solar.

This page covers verified 2026 rebate amounts from Central Hudson's official website. All figures are current as of August 2026, but utility rebate programs change — verify with Central Hudson directly before committing to a project.

2026 Rebate Amounts by Category

Heat Pump

$3,000–$8,000
Depends on replacement scenario

Central Hudson pays $8,000 if your heat pump fully replaces fossil-fuel heating with no backup system. $5,000 if you keep a backup system (for backup-heat scenarios), and $3,000 if you're adding heating to an existing forced-air cooling system. Most Beacon homeowners converting from oil or gas qualify for the full $8,000. Installed cost typically runs $13,000–$22,000.

Verify your specific scenario with Central Hudson; rebate tiers depend on backup fuel configuration.

Heat Pump Water Heater

$1,250
Replaces gas or electric resistance

One of the highest HPWH rebates in the region. A heat pump water heater costs $4,000–$6,000 installed and reduces water heating energy use by 50% or more. Combined with a whole-home heat pump, this is one of the highest-impact upgrades a household can make.

Solar Rebate

$1,200–$2,880
$0.20–$0.40 per watt (standard); $0.80 per watt (income-qualified)

Central Hudson is one of few utilities actively rebating residential solar. Standard-income households receive $0.20–$0.40 per watt (varies by program tier). Income-qualified households receive $0.80 per watt. A typical 6 kW system (roughly $15,000–$18,000 installed) qualifies for $1,200–$2,880 depending on income. Annual savings: ~$1,400 per year, payback: 10–13 years.

Verify income thresholds and current per-watt rates directly with Central Hudson.

EV Charger

$25
Level 2 home charging equipment

Central Hudson's EV charger rebate is modest ($25), and the program does not activate until 3 months after charger installation. A Level 2 charger costs $500–$2,000 installed and cuts charging time from 24+ hours to 6–8 hours overnight.

Smart Thermostat

$50
Programmable WiFi-connected control

A smart thermostat enables fine-grained temperature control and typically saves 1–2% on heating/cooling costs. Central Hudson's rebate is conservative but covers roughly 10% of a typical device's cost.

Real Beacon Example: Historic Homeowner with Solar Interest

Case Study: $135K Household

Starting situation: Historic 1900s Victorian with oil heating, window air conditioning, electric water heater. Annual energy bill: ~$2,900 (old home, poor insulation).

Retrofit plan: Heat pump ($18K installed) + heat pump water heater ($5K installed) + solar (7 kW system, $16,800 installed).

Rebates: $8,000 (heat pump) + $1,250 (HPWH) + $1,400 (solar at $0.20/W) = $10,650 total.

Out-of-pocket: $39,800 − $10,650 = $29,150 (before any financing; may qualify for income-based solar bonus).

Annual savings: ~$1,900 (50% reduction in both heating and generation needs).

Payback period: ~15 years with standard rebates; ~12 years if income-qualified for higher solar tier.

Central Hudson Service Territory

Beacon is served by Central Hudson Gas & Electric, which covers the Mid-Hudson Valley including Newburgh, Poughkeepsie, Kingston, Saugerties, and other communities. All municipalities within Central Hudson's territory share the same rebate schedule. Central Hudson's rates are moderate (approximately 16–18¢ per kWh), making electrification projects significantly more cost-effective than in higher-rate regions like Long Island.

Renters & Condo Owners

If you rent or live in a condo, most rebates apply only to the building owner or management company. Tenants can pursue smart thermostat or EV charger rebates (if permitted by the lease), but major retrofits require landlord/board approval. Multi-unit buildings may qualify for larger central-building rebates through Central Hudson's commercial programs.

Look for contractors with 5+ years of heat pump and solar experience in the Northeast, clear warranties (10+ years on major components), and references from similar-age homes in the Mid-Hudson Valley.

Next Steps

1. Get a home energy audit. A professional audit ($200–$400, sometimes free through utility programs) identifies where your home loses energy and prioritizes retrofits by impact. Historic homes like many Beacon residences often have major air-leakage and insulation gaps.

2. Gather multiple quotes. Call 3–5 local contractors and ask specifically about Central Hudson rebates. Most will handle the rebate application, but confirm this upfront.

3. Verify current Central Hudson rebate amounts. Visit www.chenergystar.com or call 1-800-752-6633 to confirm 2026 rebate levels and income thresholds before finalizing your budget.

4. Explore financing. If upfront cost is a barrier, Central Hudson offers financing options; New York State also offers low-interest Property Assessed Clean Energy (PACE) loans for energy upgrades.
