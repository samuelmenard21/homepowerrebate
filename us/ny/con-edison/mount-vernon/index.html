Mount Vernon Heat Pump Rebates 2026: Con Edison

What Mount Vernon homeowners can really get in 2026, checked against Con Edison and NYSERDA on September 26, 2026.

Short answer: In Mount Vernon, the main heat pump rebate is NYS Clean Heat (run by Con Edison): up to $10,000 ($11,000 in a Disadvantaged Community) for a whole-home system, taken off your contractor’s bill. It is one rebate — not several that add up. Lower-income households can get much more through EmPower+, and the federal tax credits ended on December 31, 2025.

Con Edison takes the incentive off your contractor’s invoice, so you never wait for a cheque. Your home must pass a “service adequate” check first.

What makes Mount Vernon different

Mount Vernon borders the Bronx in southern Westchester and is served by Con Edison. Parts of the city are mapped as Disadvantaged Communities, so check your address — DAC status raises the Con Edison amount and the cost cap (85% instead of 70%).

Mount Vernon is one of the most densely built cities in Westchester, with many older two-family homes and small apartment buildings. Owner-occupants of 1–4 family buildings can use the Con Edison program for every unit.

Because parts of the city are Disadvantaged Communities, the same project can earn a higher Clean Heat amount and a higher cost cap depending on the exact address. Two neighbours on different blocks can get different numbers.

Lower-income owners and renters should look at EmPower+ first, since it can pay for insulation and air sealing at no cost and make the heat pump smaller.

What does not stack

Clean Heat is the Con Edison rebate, so never add the two together. Clean Heat is capped at 70% of cost (85% in a DAC). There is no separate federal HEAR cheque — New York delivers it through EmPower+. The federal 25C/25D tax credits ended December 31, 2025.

Income-qualified? EmPower+ can cover far more than the utility rebate. Check the state DAC map too — it can raise your amount.

EV chargers in Mount Vernon

Con Edison has no rebate for buying a home charger. Its SmartCharge New York program pays about $400 a year on average for charging off-peak. PowerReady stopped taking new Level 2 applications on April 22, 2026.

What to do next in Mount Vernon

  1. Look up your exact address on the DAC map before you compare quotes.
  2. If money is tight, apply to EmPower+ first, then plan the heat pump.
  3. Get 2–3 quotes and ask each contractor to show the rebate tier on the quote.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Mount Vernon?

NYS Clean Heat (run by Con Edison) pays up to $10,000 ($11,000 in a Disadvantaged Community) for a whole-home heat pump in Mount Vernon, depending on home size, whether you remove the old system, and DAC status.

Why did my neighbour get a bigger rebate than my quote shows?

Disadvantaged Community status is set by census tract, so homes a few blocks apart can fall on different sides of the line. DAC homes get higher Con Edison amounts and an 85% cost cap instead of 70%.

Is the federal tax credit still available for heat pumps?

No. The federal 25C and 25D credits ended for anything installed after December 31, 2025.

What you'll actually save each month

Rebates cover the upfront cost, but the number that decides whether this is worth it is the payback period — how long until the monthly savings pay back what you spent out of pocket. Here's the real math using Con Edison's actual current electricity rate:

  • Con Edison electricity (2026): roughly 30–35.7¢/kWh depending on rate plan and time of use — among the highest residential electricity rates in the country. (Check current Con Edison rates →)

A modern cold-climate heat pump typically moves 2.5–3.5 units of heat per unit of electricity it uses (its "coefficient of performance"), which is why it beats electric resistance heating on cost even at the same per-kWh rate. Con Edison's high electric rate means a heat pump's cost advantage over gas heat is narrower here than in most of the country — the real number depends heavily on your specific home. We didn't find a reliable current per-therm gas rate for Con Edison territory to compare against — rather than guess, we're leaving gas-furnace payback math out until we can source it properly.
