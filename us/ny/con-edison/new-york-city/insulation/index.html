Insulation Rebates in New York City

Con Edison's Weather Ready insulation program has stopped taking applications, so the big insulation rebate many New York City homeowners heard about is gone for now. There is still real help, mostly for lower- and moderate-income households, and a smart reason to insulate before a heat pump goes in.

Why insulate before a heat pump

Since September 1, 2026, NYS Clean Heat pays a higher Weatherized Tier rate to homes that took part in an approved weatherization program. For a Con Edison single-family home replacing and removing its old system, that is $10,000 instead of $7,000. In a Disadvantaged Community it is $11,000 instead of $8,000. The higher tier does not apply to Con Edison projects that keep the old system with integrated controls.

What New York City homes look like

New York City has old buildings. The US Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020 to 2024) counts about 3.68 million homes. 79% were built before 1980 and 38% in 1939 or earlier. About two out of three households rent, and only 9% of homes are single detached houses. Another 21% are in buildings of 2 to 4 units.

That 2 to 4 unit group matters most here. EmPower+ and Comfort Home are built for 1 to 4 family homes. Large apartment buildings use different programs run for building owners.

How heating fuel changes the math

The same Census survey shows 64% of city homes heat with piped gas, 17% with electricity and 11% with fuel oil. Insulation saves the most where heat costs the most. If you pay for oil, or heat with electric baseboards, air sealing and attic insulation tend to pay back faster. If you plan to switch to a heat pump, a tighter house also means a smaller, cheaper system.

Comfort Home in the city: check first

NYSERDA's Comfort Home pays $2,500 for a Good package (seal and insulate the attic and rim joist) and $3,000 for a Better package (Good plus walls and floors). Windows can add $2,000 once the sealing and insulation are done. Contractors are listed by county. Con Edison's own page points Westchester homes to Comfort Home but does not say the same for the five boroughs, so check the contractor list for your county before you plan around it. If no contractor serves your borough, EmPower+ is your main route.

Renters

Most New Yorkers rent. EmPower+ is open to income-eligible renters of 1 to 4 family homes, with the landlord's okay. If you rent in a bigger building, ask your landlord about NYSERDA's multifamily programs. Plastic window kits and door sweeps are cheap fixes you can do yourself while you wait.

How to get insulation help in New York City

  1. Check your income against EmPower+ limits on NYSERDA's site. The limits depend on county and household size.
  2. If you qualify, apply through the MyEnergy portal. A contractor does a free home assessment and recommends work.
  3. If you do not qualify, check the Comfort Home contractor list for your county and book a free consultation.
  4. If a heat pump is next, keep your weatherization paperwork. It is your proof for the Weatherized Tier.

Common questions

Is Con Edison's Weather Ready program still open?

No. On October 3, 2026, Con Edison's page said Weather Ready is no longer accepting applications and that 2027 weatherization incentives will be announced.

Can renters in New York City get free insulation?

Income-eligible renters of 1 to 4 family homes can apply to EmPower+, with landlord approval. Renters in larger buildings should ask their landlord about multifamily programs.

How does insulating raise my heat pump rebate?

NYS Clean Heat pays a higher Weatherized Tier to homes that took part in an approved weatherization program, such as Comfort Home. For a Con Edison single-family home with decommissioning, it is $10,000 instead of $7,000.

Is there a federal tax credit for insulation in 2026?

No. The IRS says the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit does not apply to work placed in service after December 31, 2025.

What counts as downstate for EmPower+?

NYSERDA lists the Bronx, Kings, New York, Queens and Richmond counties as downstate, along with nearby counties. Downstate homes get the higher caps.
