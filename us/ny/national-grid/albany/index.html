What Albany homeowners can claim

Albany is in National Grid territory (most of the metro area). Like other National Grid cities, you get utility + state + federal stacking. Some areas may have NYSEG or other utilities—check your bill first.

Real Albany Example

Albany homeowner, $135K household income, National Grid territory:

  • National Grid rebate: $2,500
  • NYS Clean Heat: $8,000

Before you apply: Verify your utility

Critical first step: Check your electric bill. Albany metro has National Grid coverage in most areas, but parts may have NYSEG or other utilities. If you're on NYSEG, utility rebates ended June 2025—but state + federal still work. National Grid service gives you the utility rebate tier too.

How to claim

  1. Check your electric bill to confirm utility (National Grid vs. NYSEG)
  2. If National Grid: Get pre-approval (2–4 weeks)
  3. Apply to state Clean Heat and federal HEAR simultaneously
  4. Contractor performs work (1–3 days)
  5. Rebates arrive 4–8 weeks post-completion

What you'll actually save each month

Rebates cover the upfront cost, but the number that decides whether this is worth it is the payback period — how long until the monthly savings pay back what you spent out of pocket. Here's the real math using National Grid (Upstate NY)'s actual current electricity rate:

  • National Grid (Upstate NY) electricity (2026): roughly 21.4–22.1¢/kWh, plus a $17.00 monthly fixed charge; delivery rates rose about 7.3% in April 2026. (Check current National Grid (Upstate NY) rates →)

A modern cold-climate heat pump typically moves 2.5–3.5 units of heat per unit of electricity it uses (its "coefficient of performance"), which is why it beats electric resistance heating on cost even at the same per-kWh rate. Upstate National Grid territory has meaningfully lower electricity rates than the NYC/Long Island utilities, which generally makes a heat pump's payback period shorter here. We didn't find a reliable current per-therm gas rate for National Grid (Upstate NY) territory to compare against — rather than guess, we're leaving gas-furnace payback math out until we can source it properly.
