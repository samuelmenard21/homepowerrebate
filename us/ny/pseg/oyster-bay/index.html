Oyster Bay Heat Pump Rebates 2026: PSEG Long Island

What Oyster Bay homeowners can really get in 2026, checked against PSEG Long Island and NYSERDA on September 26, 2026.

Short answer: In Oyster Bay, the main heat pump rebate is PSEG Long Island’s heat pump rebate: $4,000 (or $5,000–$7,500 if you qualify on income or live in a DAC) for a whole-home system, taken off your contractor’s bill. It is one rebate — not several that add up. Lower-income households can get much more through EmPower+, and the federal tax credits ended on December 31, 2025.

How much is the PSEG Long Island heat pump rebate in Oyster Bay?

Amounts for a single-family home. You get one line from this table, not several.

What you installStandardIn a Disadvantaged Community
Whole-home heat pump, market rate$4,000$5,000
Whole-home heat pump, moderate income (under 80% AMI)$5,000$5,000
Whole-home heat pump, low income (under 60% AMI)$7,500$7,500

PSEG Long Island is not part of NYS Clean Heat. The heat pump must be on the NEEP cold-climate list, sized for the whole home, installed by a participating contractor, and pre-approved before work starts.

What makes Oyster Bay different

The Town of Oyster Bay is in Nassau County and is served by PSEG Long Island for electricity and National Grid for gas. Most homes are on gas, so the main reason to switch is cooling plus heating in one system — savings versus gas are smaller than versus oil.

The Town of Oyster Bay stretches from the North Shore to the South Shore across Nassau County, with a mix of large older estates and dense post-war developments like Levittown-style capes and ranches.

Post-war capes and ranches are often easy candidates: simple layouts and existing ducts in many of them.

What does not stack

Long Island is not part of NYS Clean Heat, so there is no second state rebate. There is no separate federal HEAR cheque — New York delivers it through EmPower+. The federal 25C/25D tax credits ended December 31, 2025.

Income-qualified? EmPower+ can cover far more than the utility rebate. Check the state DAC map too — it can raise your amount.

What to do next in Oyster Bay

  1. If your home is a post-war cape or ranch, ask about a ducted system using your existing ducts.
  2. Compare your gas bill to a heat pump estimate before deciding.
  3. Get 2–3 quotes and ask each contractor to show the rebate tier on the quote.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Oyster Bay?

PSEG Long Island’s heat pump rebate pays $4,000 (or $5,000–$7,500 if you qualify on income or live in a DAC) for a whole-home heat pump in Oyster Bay, depending on home size, whether you remove the old system, and DAC status.

Is a heat pump worth it in Oyster Bay if I already have gas?

It can be, especially if you also need new AC, since one system does both. The savings versus gas are smaller than versus oil, so ask for a bill estimate with your actual rates.

Is the federal tax credit still available for heat pumps?

No. The federal 25C and 25D credits ended for anything installed after December 31, 2025.

What you'll actually save each month

Rebates cover the upfront cost, but the number that decides whether this is worth it is the payback period — how long until the monthly savings pay back what you spent out of pocket. Here's the real math using PSEG Long Island's actual current electricity rate:

  • PSEG Long Island electricity (2026): approximately 23.6¢/kWh (up 12–13% in early 2026), plus a $14.49 monthly fixed charge. (Check current PSEG Long Island rates →)

A modern cold-climate heat pump typically moves 2.5–3.5 units of heat per unit of electricity it uses (its "coefficient of performance"), which is why it beats electric resistance heating on cost even at the same per-kWh rate. PSEG Long Island is electric-only in most of its territory; Long Island gas customers are typically National Grid, a separate bill. We didn't find a reliable current per-therm gas rate for PSEG Long Island territory to compare against — rather than guess, we're leaving gas-furnace payback math out until we can source it properly.
