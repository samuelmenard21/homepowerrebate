Smithtown Heat Pump Rebates 2026: PSEG Long Island

What Smithtown homeowners can really get in 2026, checked against PSEG Long Island and NYSERDA on October 3, 2026.

Short answer: In Smithtown, the main heat pump rebate is PSEG Long Island’s heat pump rebate: $4,000 (or $5,000–$7,500 if you qualify on income or live in a DAC) for a whole-home system, taken off your contractor’s bill. It is one rebate — not several that add up. Lower-income households can get much more through EmPower+, and the federal tax credits ended on December 31, 2025.

What makes Smithtown different

Smithtown is a Suffolk County town served by PSEG Long Island. Many of its 1950s–70s homes still heat with oil, which is where a whole-home heat pump saves the most each winter.

Smithtown has many split-levels and high ranches from the 1960s and 70s, often with ducts for central air. These layouts usually work well with a ducted or mixed ducted-and-ductless setup.

Plenty of homes still burn heating oil, and oil-to-heat-pump switches are where Long Island homeowners see the biggest drop in heating costs.

What does not stack

Long Island is not part of NYS Clean Heat, so there is no second state rebate. There is no separate federal HEAR cheque — New York delivers it through EmPower+. The federal 25C/25D tax credits ended December 31, 2025.

Income-qualified? EmPower+ can cover far more than the utility rebate. Check the state DAC map too — it can raise your amount.

What to do next in Smithtown

  1. If you have oil heat, ask for a whole-home switch quote.
  2. Ask if your existing AC ducts can be reused.
  3. Get 2–3 quotes and ask each contractor to show the rebate tier on the quote.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Smithtown?

PSEG Long Island’s heat pump rebate pays $4,000 (or $5,000–$7,500 if you qualify on income or live in a DAC) for a whole-home heat pump in Smithtown, depending on home size, whether you remove the old system, and DAC status.

Can I keep my oil boiler as backup and still get the PSEG rebate?

PSEG's rebate is for a heat pump sized to heat the whole home. Ask your participating contractor how PSEG treats backup systems on your project before you sign.

Is the federal tax credit still available for heat pumps?

No. The federal 25C and 25D credits ended for anything installed after December 31, 2025.

What you'll actually save each month

Rebates cover the upfront cost, but the number that decides whether this is worth it is the payback period — how long until the monthly savings pay back what you spent out of pocket. Here's the real math using PSEG Long Island's actual current electricity rate:

  • PSEG Long Island electricity (2026): approximately 23.6¢/kWh (up 12–13% in early 2026), plus a $14.49 monthly fixed charge. (Check current PSEG Long Island rates →)

A modern cold-climate heat pump typically moves 2.5–3.5 units of heat per unit of electricity it uses (its "coefficient of performance"), which is why it beats electric resistance heating on cost even at the same per-kWh rate. PSEG Long Island is electric-only in most of its territory; Long Island gas customers are typically National Grid, a separate bill. We didn't find a reliable current per-therm gas rate for PSEG Long Island territory to compare against — rather than guess, we're leaving gas-furnace payback math out until we can source it properly.
