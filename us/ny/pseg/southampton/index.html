Southampton Heat Pump Rebates 2026: PSEG Long Island

What Southampton homeowners can really get in 2026, checked against PSEG Long Island and NYSERDA on October 3, 2026.

Short answer: In Southampton, the main heat pump rebate is PSEG Long Island’s heat pump rebate: $4,000 (or $5,000–$7,500 if you qualify on income or live in a DAC) for a whole-home system, taken off your contractor’s bill. It is one rebate — not several that add up. Lower-income households can get much more through EmPower+, and the federal tax credits ended on December 31, 2025.

How much is the PSEG Long Island heat pump rebate in Southampton?

Amounts for a single-family home. You get one line from this table, not several.

PSEG Long Island is not part of NYS Clean Heat. The heat pump must be on the NEEP cold-climate list, sized for the whole home, installed by a participating contractor, and pre-approved before work starts.

What makes Southampton different

Southampton is on Long Island’s East End in Suffolk County, served by PSEG Long Island. Gas lines are limited out here, so many homes heat with oil or propane, and seasonal homes should ask whether the rebate’s whole-home sizing rules fit how the house is used.

Large homes and second homes are common here. A bigger house means a bigger system and a higher price, but the PSEG rebate is a flat amount per home, so the rebate covers a smaller share.

Salt air near the ocean and bays can shorten the life of outdoor units, so ask about coastal-rated coatings.

What does not stack

Long Island is not part of NYS Clean Heat, so there is no second state rebate. There is no separate federal HEAR cheque — New York delivers it through EmPower+. The federal 25C/25D tax credits ended December 31, 2025.

Income-qualified? EmPower+ can cover far more than the utility rebate. Check the state DAC map too — it can raise your amount.

What to do next in Southampton

  1. Ask about coastal-rated outdoor units if you are near salt water.
  2. For a second home, ask how PSEG treats seasonal occupancy before you sign.
  3. Get 2–3 quotes and ask each contractor to show the rebate tier on the quote.

Common questions

How much is the heat pump rebate in Southampton?

PSEG Long Island’s heat pump rebate pays $4,000 (or $5,000–$7,500 if you qualify on income or live in a DAC) for a whole-home heat pump in Southampton, depending on home size, whether you remove the old system, and DAC status.

Is the PSEG rebate bigger for a big Southampton house?

No. PSEG Long Island pays a flat amount per home ($4,000 at market rate), so on a large house it covers a smaller share of the cost.

Is the federal tax credit still available for heat pumps?

No. The federal 25C and 25D credits ended for anything installed after December 31, 2025.

What you'll actually save each month

Rebates cover the upfront cost, but the number that decides whether this is worth it is the payback period — how long until the monthly savings pay back what you spent out of pocket. Here's the real math using PSEG Long Island's actual current electricity rate:

  • PSEG Long Island electricity (2026): approximately 23.6¢/kWh (up 12–13% in early 2026), plus a $14.49 monthly fixed charge. (Check current PSEG Long Island rates →)

A modern cold-climate heat pump typically moves 2.5–3.5 units of heat per unit of electricity it uses (its "coefficient of performance"), which is why it beats electric resistance heating on cost even at the same per-kWh rate. PSEG Long Island is electric-only in most of its territory; Long Island gas customers are typically National Grid, a separate bill. We didn't find a reliable current per-therm gas rate for PSEG Long Island territory to compare against — rather than guess, we're leaving gas-furnace payback math out until we can source it properly.
