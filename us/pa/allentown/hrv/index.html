HRV & ERV Rebates in Allentown

PPL Electric doesn't fund a heat recovery ventilator or energy recovery ventilator rebate in Allentown. Here's what actually applies in 2026.

How much you get

PPL Electric's residential rebate catalog is specific and itemized: air-source heat pumps ($350–$450), ductless mini-split heat pumps ($400), central air conditioners ($200–$300), smart thermostats ($50 self-install, $100 professional install), plus attic and basement insulation and air-sealing rebates up to $575 combined. HRV and ERV equipment doesn't appear anywhere on that list.

Lehigh Valley homes range from older Allentown rowhouses to newer suburban builds, and PPL's own air-sealing rebate encourages homeowners to tighten up their houses — which is precisely when a fresh-air ventilation system starts to matter. The rebate exists for the tightening; it doesn't exist for the ventilation that should follow it.

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C) expired December 31, 2025 and never covered HRV or ERV equipment even when active, so there's no federal credit filling the gap. An HRV or ERV in Allentown is currently a full out-of-pocket cost.

Source: PPL Electric Savings, rebates.energy program database, IRS Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C)

Common questions

Does PPL Electric offer an HRV or ERV rebate in Allentown?

No. PPL Electric's residential rebate catalog covers other equipment, but heat recovery ventilators (HRVs) and energy recovery ventilators (ERVs) aren't a funded category as of this writing.

What should a Allentown homeowner do instead of waiting for an HRV rebate?

Treat it as a straight equipment-and-install cost, get quotes from a couple of local installers, and fold it into a broader retrofit plan rather than delaying for a rebate that doesn't currently exist. See our general guide to HRV and ERV systems for how the equipment works and what it costs.
