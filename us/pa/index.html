Find Your Pennsylvania Energy Rebate

Rebate amounts in Pennsylvania depend heavily on your electric utility. Pick your utility below to see what heat pumps, insulation, solar, and more actually cost after rebates.

Short answer: Pennsylvania has no statewide home rebate in 2026, so your utility matters. PECO pays $200 to $300 for a heat pump, $350 for a heat pump water heater and $500 for rooftop solar. PPL Electric pays $225 to $500 for a heat pump and $400 for a heat pump water heater. Duquesne Light's rebate store says energy rebates are not available yet, and we could not confirm Penelec's amounts. The state Sunshine solar rebate closed in 2013 and the federal solar credit ended after 2025.

Statewide & Federal Programs (Every PA City)

PA Sunshine Solar Program (no longer active) — this state solar rebate stopped accepting new applications years ago once its funding was exhausted and has not reopened. If you see it referenced as a current rebate, that information is outdated. PA DEP energy savings programs →

Keystone HELP Home Energy Loans — low fixed-rate financing (administered through PHFA-affiliated lenders) for insulation, heat pumps, and other efficiency upgrades, meant to cover costs after rebates. PA DEP energy savings programs →

Penn Energy Savers (federal HOMES + HEAR) — $258.9M allocated ($129.2M HEAR, $130M HOMES). Not yet publicly launched; DEP expects activation Q3–Q4 2026. When live, HEAR is expected to cover up to 100% of costs (heat pump capped near $8,000) for households under 80% area median income, and 50% for 81–150% AMI. PA DEP IRA rebates page →

LIHEAP — federally funded home heating bill assistance for income-qualified PA households, administered by the PA Department of Human Services.

Weatherization Assistance Program (WAP) — free weatherization (insulation, air sealing) for income-qualified households, run through PA's network of community action agencies.

Federal 25C / 25D tax credits — 30% of cost up to $2,000/year for heat pumps and heat pump water heaters, up to $1,200/year for insulation/windows/doors, and 30% uncapped for solar and battery storage (Section 25D), claimed on your federal tax return. Confirm current eligibility windows before purchasing, as federal tax credit rules have changed multiple times in recent years. ENERGY STAR federal tax credits →

Heat pump

PECO Act 129 heat pump rebate

Up to $1,950

PECO's Act 129 rebate for Philadelphia, the highest confirmed PA utility amount we've found — the higher figure requires stacking with income-qualified (EAP) offers. PPL Electric (Allentown) pays up to $450, and Penelec (Erie) up to $500. Confirm your own utility's current Phase V number before assuming it matches.

Source: /us/pa/philadelphia · PECO Act 129
Smart thermostat

PPL Electric smart thermostat rebate

$50–$100

PPL Electric (Allentown) pays $50 for a self-installed ENERGY STAR smart thermostat, $100 if professionally installed. PECO (Philadelphia) and Penelec (Erie) both offer a flat $50 instant rebate at participating retailers, no application required.

Source: /us/pa/allentown · PPL Electric Act 129
Water heater

PPL Electric heat pump water heater rebate

Up to $400

PPL Electric's Act 129 rebate for a qualifying heat pump water heater. PECO and Duquesne Light (Pittsburgh) also cover water heaters under their broader Act 129 programs, but haven't published a confirmed Phase V dollar figure as of this writing — call ahead.

Source: /us/pa/allentown · PPL Electric Act 129
Insulation

Keystone HELP Home Energy Loans

Low fixed-rate financing

Financing, not a rebate: PHFA-affiliated lenders offer low fixed-rate loans through Keystone HELP to cover insulation, heat pumps, and other efficiency upgrades after your utility rebate. Statewide, not tied to any one utility.

Source: /us/pa · PA DEP / Keystone HELP

Solar has no active statewide PA rebate as of 2026 — PA Sunshine stopped accepting new applications years ago and hasn't reopened. Battery storage relies on the federal 30% Section 25D credit only; no PA utility has a confirmed battery-specific rebate.

Quick Questions

Read the questions and answers
Which utility do I have?
Check your electric bill. PECO covers Philadelphia and its suburbs. Duquesne Light covers Pittsburgh and Allegheny County. PPL Electric covers Allentown, the Lehigh Valley, and much of central PA. FirstEnergy's four Pennsylvania utilities (Met-Ed, Penelec, Penn Power, West Penn Power) cover Erie, Reading, and most of the rest of the state.
Is Pennsylvania's federal HEAR rebate available yet?
Not yet as of this writing. DEP is finalizing its Penn Energy Savers program design after new federal guidance in June 2026, with activation expected Q3–Q4 2026. Check DEP's page for the current status before counting on it.
Are utility Act 129 rebates still open?
Most Pennsylvania utilities closed their Phase IV Act 129 rebates around May 31, 2026 and are transitioning to Phase V (June 2026–May 2031). As of this writing, not every utility has published new Phase V rebate amounts — confirm directly with your utility.
What's the Pennsylvania solar rebate?
There isn't one right now, statewide. The PA Sunshine Solar Program used to rebate residential solar installs, but it stopped accepting new applications years ago once its funding ran out, and it hasn't reopened since. Check your specific utility (PECO, PPL Electric, Duquesne Light, or your FirstEnergy company) for any solar-specific incentive they run.
Is there still a PA Sunshine solar rebate in 2026?
No. PA Sunshine was funded starting in 2011 and stopped taking new applications once that money ran out — it has not been refunded or relaunched since. If a contractor advertises a "PA Sunshine rebate" today, that's outdated. For solar in Pennsylvania now, check whether your utility offers anything, and confirm current program status directly with PA DEP before counting on a state rebate.
Is my contractor's rebate quote a scam?
It can be, if the promised rebate is doing most of the work to make an inflated price look reasonable. Check the real rebate cap before you sign →
