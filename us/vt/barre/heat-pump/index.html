Heat Pump Rebates in Barre, Vermont

Barre is the most oil-dependent of the five Vermont cities we cover: 58% of occupied homes burn it. It is also split evenly between owners and renters, so whether you can claim a rebate often depends on which side of the lease you are on.

What Barre heat pump buyers can get

We re-read Efficiency Vermont's heat pump pages and Green Mountain Power's heat pump page on October 5, 2026. Barre is served by Green Mountain Power. Amounts are maximums for qualifying cold-climate equipment.

Open

Ductless heat pump

$375 or $475 off

An instant discount from Efficiency Vermont: $375 for systems up to 2 tons and $475 over 2 tons, applied at purchase by your contractor.

Open

Ducted heat pump

$1,200 to $2,200 off

$1,200 up to 2 tons, $1,700 over 2 and under 4 tons, $2,200 at 4 tons or more, for qualifying cold-climate equipment.

Open

Integrated controls

Up to $600

Cash back for controls that let a ductless heat pump run as the main heat and signal the old system only when needed.

Open

Green Mountain Power income-eligible rebate

$2,000 per condenser

For households at or below 80% of area median income. A household of four in Washington County must be at or under $91,200. The rebate is combined with Efficiency Vermont's in one application on Efficiency Vermont's site.

Open

Efficiency Vermont income bonus

$200 to $2,200

$2,200 for Green Mountain Power low-income customers, $200 for most other utilities' customers. Rental property owners are not eligible. Income limits changed on July 1, 2026.

Open

Ground source heat pump

Up to $2,100 per ton

Plus $500 for income-eligible households. New systems up to 10 tons, an Efficiency Excellence Network contractor, and not for wood-heating customers.

Barre sits in Washington County, so the four-person limit is $91,200. We did not pull income data for Barre, so use Green Mountain Power's published table rather than guessing whether your household qualifies.

Oil country

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey counts 4,094 occupied homes in Barre. 2,385 (58%) heat mainly with fuel oil or kerosene, 627 (15%) with propane, 569 (14%) with electricity, 201 (5%) with piped gas and 175 (4%) with wood. A household with an oil boiler and radiators and no ductwork usually starts with a ductless system for the main living area, then decides whether to keep the boiler for the coldest nights.

Half the city rents

2,062 Barre households own their home and 2,032 rent, almost exactly 50% each. 1,426 housing units sit in two-, three- and four-unit buildings, and 496 more in five-to-nine-unit buildings. For a landlord, Efficiency Vermont's rebates are open to rental properties, but the income bonus is not. For a tenant, a heat pump is a conversation with the landlord, who also decides whether the old system stays as backup.

If you rent and pay your own heating bills, ask whether the landlord would share the cost. A heat pump that cuts the oil you buy may be worth the rebate paperwork for both sides.

Old buildings

2,012 of Barre's 4,335 housing units, 46%, were built in 1939 or earlier, a higher share than Montpelier's. Weatherization comes first in these buildings. Efficiency Vermont pays 75% of qualifying insulation and air sealing up to $3,000, or up to $7,000 for income-eligible households. See our Barre insulation guide.

How cold Barre gets

Barre's average January low is -11.9°C, and the coldest day between 2015 and 2024 reached -30.9°C. That is the second-coldest record in our five Vermont cities, behind Rutland. Efficiency Vermont's rebates are for qualifying cold-climate units, and the specification sheet for the model you choose will say what it delivers at the bottom of its range.

Ground source heat pumps

Efficiency Vermont pays up to $2,100 per ton on qualifying ground source heat pumps, plus $500 for income-eligible households, for new systems up to 10 tons. Wood-heating customers are not eligible. Whether your lot suits a ground loop is a site question for a licensed installer, and we did not verify any Barre-specific ground conditions.

How to claim a heat pump rebate in Barre

  1. Add up a year of oil or propane deliveries.
  2. If you rent, ask the landlord who will pay and who will apply.
  3. Ask contractors for quotes with model numbers and check them against Efficiency Vermont's qualifying list.
  4. If your income may qualify, apply through Efficiency Vermont so Green Mountain Power's rebate is included.
  5. Keep the old system working until the new one has been through a cold week.

Common questions

Is Barre eligible for Green Mountain Power's income-eligible heat pump rebate?

Yes, if your household is at or below 80% of area median income. In Washington County that is $91,200 for a household of four.

I rent in Barre. Can I get a heat pump rebate?

The rebate goes to whoever buys the equipment, so your landlord needs to be involved. Rental owners cannot claim the income bonus.

Why do most Barre homes use oil?

The Census counts oil as the main fuel for 58% of homes. We did not research why, but the effect is that a heat pump replaces oil more often here than gas.

Do I need to weatherize before a heat pump?

It is not a rebate condition we found, but a tighter house needs a smaller system. Efficiency Vermont pays up to $3,000, or $7,000 if income-eligible, for weatherization.

How are Barre and Montpelier different for rebates?

The offers are the same because both are Green Mountain Power territory in Washington County. The housing differs: Barre has more oil, more old buildings and a 50-50 owner-renter split.
