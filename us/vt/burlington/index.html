Burlington rebates by category

Burlington is served by Burlington Electric Department (BED). Most efficiency rebates (heat pumps, water heaters, insulation, thermostats) run through the statewide Efficiency Vermont program, with BED running its own separate menu since it is a municipal utility outside Efficiency Vermont territory for heat pumps and EV chargers. Battery storage and EV charger incentives run directly through Burlington Electric Department (BED).

One side note on energy costs: Burlington Electric Department's rates run roughly 7–9% below Green Mountain Power, which serves the rest of Vermont on this site (South Burlington, Rutland, Barre, Montpelier). It's a real gap, just not large enough on its own to change Burlington's PowerScore energy-cost estimate from the statewide figure shown on the PowerScore leaderboard.

Common questions

What's the heat pump rebate in Burlington?

Burlington Electric Department pays up to 75% of installed cost for a qualifying cold-climate heat pump, and income-qualified households installing a high-performance ducted system can reach up to $7,950 total. Because Burlington runs its own municipal utility, BED customers use BED's own heat pump program rather than Efficiency Vermont's.

What's the battery storage rebate in Burlington?

We found no confirmed battery storage rebate for Burlington Electric Department customers as of this writing. Green Mountain Power's Bring Your Own Device program pays $850–$950 per kW, but it's only open to GMP customers, and Burlington runs its own separate municipal utility.

What's the solar rebate in Burlington?

Vermont has no flat cash rebate for solar. Homeowners benefit from net metering (roughly $0.184/kWh blended credit through their utility), a state sales tax exemption on the system, and in some towns a property tax exemption. The federal 30% solar tax credit expired after December 31, 2025 and does not apply to 2026 installs.
