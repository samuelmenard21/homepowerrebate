Heat Pump Rebates in Rutland, Vermont

Rutland is an oil-heat city. 5,124 of its 7,414 occupied homes (69%) burn fuel oil, a fuel delivered by truck at a price that changes, and Rutland also has the coldest record low of the five Vermont cities we cover. That is exactly the household Efficiency Vermont's rebates were built for.

What Rutland heat pump buyers can get

We re-read Efficiency Vermont's heat pump pages and Green Mountain Power's heat pump page on October 5, 2026. Rutland is served by Green Mountain Power. Amounts are maximums for qualifying cold-climate equipment.

Open

Ductless heat pump

$375 or $475 off

An instant discount from Efficiency Vermont: $375 for systems up to 2 tons and $475 over 2 tons, applied at purchase by your contractor.

Open

Ducted heat pump

$1,200 to $2,200 off

$1,200 up to 2 tons, $1,700 over 2 and under 4 tons, $2,200 at 4 tons or more, for qualifying cold-climate equipment.

Open

Integrated controls

Up to $600

Cash back for controls that let a ductless heat pump run as the main heat and signal the old system only when needed.

Open

Green Mountain Power income-eligible rebate

$2,000 per condenser

For households at or below 80% of area median income. A household of four in Rutland County must be at or under $89,200. The rebate is combined with Efficiency Vermont's in one application on Efficiency Vermont's site.

Open

Efficiency Vermont income bonus

$200 to $2,200

$2,200 for Green Mountain Power low-income customers, $200 for most other utilities' customers. Rental property owners are not eligible. Income limits changed on July 1, 2026.

Open

Ground source heat pump

Up to $2,100 per ton

Plus $500 for income-eligible households. New systems up to 10 tons, an Efficiency Excellence Network contractor, and not for wood-heating customers.

Rutland County is not listed by name in Green Mountain Power's limits, so it falls under the "other counties" figure of $89,200 for a household of four. Smaller households have lower limits. Rental property owners cannot claim the income bonus, which matters in a city where almost half of households rent.

What Rutland burns, and why it matters

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey puts 5,124 Rutland households (69%) on fuel oil, 1,111 (15%) on propane, 605 (8%) on electricity, 184 (2%) on wood and only 366 (5%) on piped gas. Oil and propane together heat 84% of homes. A family replacing part of an oil load with electricity from a cold-climate heat pump is swapping a delivered fuel for a metered one.

One approach is a single ductless unit in the main living area, controlled by an integrated controller so the oil boiler fires only when the heat pump cannot keep up. Efficiency Vermont's integrated-controls cash back of up to $600 exists for that arrangement.

An old housing stock

Half of Rutland's housing units (4,287 of 8,517, or 50%) were built in 1939 or earlier, and another 1,166 date from the 1940s and 1950s. Efficiency Vermont's weatherization offer pays 75% of a qualifying project up to $3,000, and a tighter house needs a smaller system. Our Rutland insulation guide covers that order of work.

Owners, renters and two-to-four-unit houses

55% of Rutland households own and 45% rent. 49% of housing units are detached houses, and 2,500 sit in two-, three- and four-unit buildings. An owner of a small multi-unit building can use Efficiency Vermont's rebates for rental properties, but not the income bonus. A tenant who pays their own electric bill will want the landlord's agreement before a heat pump goes on the wall.

The coldest record of the five

Rutland's average January low is -10.2°C, and the coldest day recorded between 2015 and 2024 was -34.8°C. At that temperature even good cold-climate units lose output, which is why Efficiency Vermont ties its rebates to qualifying cold-climate models and why many Rutland homes keep a backup. Ask your contractor what the unit delivers at -25°C, not only at -15°C, and what your backup will do on those nights.

Wood and pellet households

184 Rutland households heat mainly with wood. Efficiency Vermont says wood-heating customers are not eligible for its ground source heat pump rebate, which is worth knowing before you plan a geothermal project. The ductless and ducted offers carry their own conditions, so confirm yours with Efficiency Vermont.

How to claim a heat pump rebate in Rutland

  1. Collect your last two winters of oil or propane delivery totals.
  2. Ask two or three contractors for a load calculation and a quote with model numbers.
  3. Check the models against Efficiency Vermont's qualifying list and choose an Efficiency Excellence Network contractor.
  4. If your income may qualify, apply through Efficiency Vermont so Green Mountain Power's $2,000 is included.
  5. Keep your oil tank filled until the heat pump has proven itself through a cold snap.

Common questions

Is a heat pump a good fit for an oil-heated Rutland home?

Often yes. Oil arrives by truck at a price that changes, and replacing part of it with a cold-climate heat pump reduces that exposure. Weatherizing first makes the heat pump smaller and cheaper.

What income limit applies in Rutland County?

Green Mountain Power's 2026 limit for a four-person household in counties not listed by name is $89,200, which is 80% of area median income. Check its page for other household sizes.

Can I keep my oil boiler as a backup?

Yes. Efficiency Vermont's integrated controls rebate, up to $600, is for ductless systems that run as the main heat and call on the old system only when needed.

Do rental owners in Rutland get the income bonus?

No. Efficiency Vermont says rental property owners are not eligible for the income bonus, though they can use the standard heat pump rebates.

Does Rutland have a gas utility rebate?

Only 5% of Rutland homes heat with piped natural gas, and Vermont Gas serves northwest Vermont, so most Rutland readers should look at Efficiency Vermont and Green Mountain Power instead.
