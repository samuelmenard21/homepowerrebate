Water Heater Rebates in Rutland, Vermont

A heat pump water heater is one way a Rutland household can cut fuel use without touching the main heating system. Efficiency Vermont pays $600 toward it, and up to $1,000 for income-eligible households.

What pays for a heat pump water heater in Rutland

We re-read Efficiency Vermont's heat pump water heater page, the utility pages that link to it and Burlington Electric's pages on October 5, 2026. Amounts are maximums.

Open

Efficiency Vermont heat pump water heater

$600, up to $1,000

A $600 discount or rebate, plus $400 more for income-eligible households. Applies to qualifying heat pump water heaters (effective August 16, 2025).

Open

Green Mountain Power

Up to $600

Green Mountain Power's page sends you to Efficiency Vermont for the rebate. GMP says a heat pump unit runs at about 550 watts while a standard electric water heater uses 4,500 watts.

Open

Home Energy Loan

From 0% interest

Efficiency Vermont lists a Home Energy Loan from 0% interest alongside its small-appliance rebates. Ask whether a water heater project qualifies.

The rebate shown here is the most we found. Efficiency Vermont lists it as up to $1,000, which includes the extra $400 for income-eligible homes.

Rutland heats with oil, so hot water may too

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey counts 5,124 of Rutland's 7,414 occupied homes (69%) heating mainly with fuel oil, 1,111 (15%) with propane and 605 (8%) with electricity. It does not count water heating fuel, so check yours. If your water is heated by the same oil boiler as your house, replacing it with a heat pump unit means the boiler no longer runs in summer just to make hot water. If your tank is propane or electric, a heat pump unit still cuts that fuel or electricity.

Old basements

50% of Rutland's housing units were built in 1939 or earlier. Green Mountain Power describes a heat pump water heater as a water heater and dehumidifier in one, because it removes moisture as it pulls heat from the air. That can suit a basement that feels damp in summer. What we cannot tell you is whether yours has the clearance and a drain, so ask the installer to look before you order.

Landlords and two-to-four-unit buildings

45% of Rutland households rent, and 2,500 units sit in two-, three- and four-unit buildings. In a small building the owner usually decides the water heater. Efficiency Vermont's heat pump water heater rebate is listed for residential properties, so ask whether a landlord-owned building with several tanks can claim one rebate per unit, which we did not verify.

The power draw, in plain numbers

Green Mountain Power says a heat pump water heater runs at about 550 watts, while a standard electric water heater uses 4,500 watts. That is roughly eight times less power while the unit is running. Running power is not the same as yearly energy use, because a heat pump unit may run longer, but it explains why GMP says these units lower electricity use and energy cost. In a city with a January average low of -10.2°C and a record of -34.8°C, a unit that takes heat from a basement is also taking it from the same space your boiler warms, so keep that in mind if the basement is a cold one.

Pair it with weatherization or a heat pump

A water heater is a small project that fits alongside others. If you are already insulating (see the Rutland insulation guide) or installing a heat pump (Rutland heat pump guide), ask for a single quote and a single visit.

How to claim a water heater rebate in Rutland

  1. Check which fuel heats your water today, and the age of the tank.
  2. Choose a heat pump model on Efficiency Vermont's qualifying list and ask the installer to apply the discount on the invoice.
  3. Confirm the installation spot has the space and drainage the unit needs.
  4. If your income may qualify, ask Efficiency Vermont about the extra $400 before you order.
  5. Keep the invoice and model number for your records.

Common questions

Is Rutland covered by the $600 Efficiency Vermont water heater rebate?

Yes. It is a statewide offer, and Green Mountain Power's page sends customers to Efficiency Vermont for it.

What if my hot water comes from my oil boiler?

A heat pump water heater lets you shut the boiler off in summer. Ask your installer how the old boiler's hot water coil will be handled, because it depends on your system.

Do I get more if I qualify by income?

Yes, $400 more, up to $1,000 total.

Can I claim it if I rent?

Your landlord owns the equipment, so ask them. We did not verify whether tenants can claim.

Does the unit need a drain?

Heat pump water heaters make condensate, so ask the installer about drainage.
